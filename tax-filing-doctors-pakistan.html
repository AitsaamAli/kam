Tax Filing for Doctors in Pakistan
Complete 2026 Guide

📅 May 31, 2026 ⏱ 9 min read ✍️ Kamboh Associates

Doctors in Pakistan face a unique tax situation — many earn from multiple sources simultaneously: a salaried hospital position, a private clinic, specialist consultation fees, and medical writing or teaching. Each income stream is taxed differently under the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, and understanding the rules can save you tens of thousands in legitimate deductions.

Key fact: Pakistani doctors are subject to a 7.5% withholding tax on specialist fees paid by hospitals and clinics. This is adjustable — not final — meaning it gets credited against your total annual tax liability when you file your return.

Who Must File?

Under FBR rules, every doctor earning more than Rs 600,000 annually is required to file an income tax return, regardless of employment type. This includes:

  • Salaried doctors in government or private hospitals
  • Doctors running private clinics or chambers
  • Consultant doctors receiving hospital fees
  • Doctors earning from medical education, writing, or research
  • Doctors with foreign remittances or investment income

Deductible Expenses for Doctors

If you run a private clinic, chamber, or medical practice, these expenses reduce your taxable income:

  • Clinic rent: Monthly rental payments for your chamber or clinic
  • Staff salaries: Receptionist, nurse, lab technician wages
  • Medical equipment depreciation: ECG machines, ultrasound, diagnostic tools (15–25% per year)
  • Medicines and supplies: Consumables used in patient treatment
  • Utility bills: Electricity, gas, internet for the clinic
  • Professional development: CME conferences, medical journals, books
  • Medical indemnity insurance: Malpractice insurance premiums
  • Vehicle expenses: Proportionate to business use (keep log)

Pro tip: Keep all receipts and invoices for clinic expenses. FBR can audit your records for the past 5 years. A well-maintained expense ledger can reduce your tax liability by 20–40% legally.

Wealth Statement Requirement

Doctors earning substantial income must file a Wealth Statement (Form WS) alongside the tax return. This declares:

  • All assets (property, vehicles, bank accounts, investments)
  • All liabilities (loans, mortgages, credit cards)
  • Net worth and how it reconciles with your declared income

Inconsistencies between declared income and lifestyle (property purchases, vehicle upgrades, foreign travel) can trigger FBR audit notices. Always ensure your wealth statement matches your income declarations.

How to File — Step-by-Step

  1. Get your NTN: Register on FBR IRIS portal at iris.fbr.gov.pk
  2. Gather documents: Salary slips, clinic income records, withholding tax certificates, bank statements
  3. Log in to IRIS: Navigate to Declaration → Income Tax Return
  4. Fill Form IT-2: For individuals with business/professional income
  5. Enter all income heads: Salary, business income, capital gains separately
  6. Claim deductions: Add all documented clinic expenses under the business income section
  7. Claim withholding credits: Enter the 7.5% hospital withholding as adjustable tax
  8. Pay balance tax: If any balance after credits, pay via PSID (Payment Slip ID)
  9. Submit return: Before September 30 deadline each year

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not declaring hospital consultation fees (hospitals report these to FBR)
  • Filing as salaried when significant clinic income exists — use business income form
  • Forgetting to claim withholding tax deducted at source as an adjustable credit
  • Not filing a wealth statement when required
  • Missing the September 30 annual filing deadline

Frequently Asked Questions

Do doctors need to file income tax in Pakistan?
Yes. All doctors earning above Rs 600,000 annually must file an income tax return with FBR, regardless of whether they are salaried or in private practice.
What tax rate applies to a doctor's private practice income?
Private practice income is taxed under the normal tax slab rates (0%–35%). Hospitals deduct 7.5% withholding tax on payments to specialist doctors, which is adjustable against total tax liability.
Can doctors deduct clinic expenses from taxable income?
Yes. Doctors running a private clinic can deduct rent, staff salaries, medical equipment depreciation, utilities, and professional fee expenses as business costs.
When is the tax return deadline for doctors?
The annual income tax return for individuals (including doctors) is due by September 30 each year for the preceding tax year (July 1 – June 30).
