Photographer Website Guide: Fast Portfolio Galleries, Client Proofing and Booking Deposits

Kamboh Associates Digital team
Updated 30 October 2026
5 min read
Lahore, Pakistan
Short answer

A photographer website needs fast, beautiful galleries above all, organised by type such as weddings, portraits or products, plus clear packages, a booking form with date and deposit details, and private client galleries for proofing and delivery. Optimise every image for the web; 1 heavy gallery can lose visitors. Sites start from Rs 40,500.

Key Takeaways

  • Your galleries are the product; speed and presentation matter more than anything else.
  • Serve web-sized, compressed images; keep full-resolution files for client delivery only.
  • Watermarks deter casual copying but can distract; use them lightly.
  • Private, password-protected galleries suit proofing and delivery.
  • A deposit secures dates and reduces last-minute cancellations.

What Makes a Good Photographer Website?

For a photographer, the website is the portfolio, the shop window and often the delivery system. Couples choosing a wedding photographer in Lahore, brands looking for product shots and families booking portraits all judge by what they see. A photographer website succeeds when galleries load quickly and look stunning on a phone, the photographer's style is obvious within seconds, and booking is straightforward. Social media brings attention; the website turns attention into bookings.

How Do You Make Galleries Fast?

Never upload full-resolution camera files to public galleries. Resize images for display, typically to around 2,000 pixels on the long side for full-screen viewing, compress them carefully, and use modern formats such as WebP where supported. Load images as visitors scroll rather than all at once. Test galleries on a mid-range phone over mobile data. Google's image guidance also recommends descriptive file names and alt text, which help your work appear in image search.

Should You Watermark Your Photos?

Watermarks deter casual copying and remind viewers whose work it is, but large watermarks distract from the photo and can make a portfolio look defensive. A small, subtle mark in a corner is a reasonable compromise for public galleries. Protecting web-sized images, rather than full-resolution files, is the bigger safeguard: a copied web image is far less useful for printing.

How Do Client Galleries Work?

Private, password-protected galleries let clients choose favourites for albums and download final images. They save endless WhatsApp file transfers and look more professional. Set expiry dates for download links, and agree in your contract how long you will keep files available.

How Should Bookings and Deposits Work?

Ask for the date first, then event type, location, coverage hours and contact details. Explain your deposit policy clearly: how much secures the date, when the balance is due and what happens if the event is cancelled or moved. Online deposits need a payment gateway; bank transfer with manual confirmation is a simple start. Our booking system guide covers more advanced options.

How Do Clients Find Photographers?

Local searches such as "wedding photographer in Lahore" depend on your Google Business Profile, reviews and pages mentioning the areas and events you cover. Name featured stories descriptively, for example "Barat at a Gulberg marquee", without revealing private details.

Should Photographers Write Blog Posts?

Short posts about real shoots, such as a venue, the light, the story of the day, help families imagine their own event and give search engines more to index. Keep them genuine and permission-based. A few good stories a year are worth more than frequent filler posts.

Should the Website Mention Contracts?

Briefly, yes. Saying that every booking comes with a written agreement covering coverage hours, delivery times, usage rights and cancellation reassures clients that you are professional, and sets expectations before the first call.

How Should Clients' Photos Be Protected?

Get written permission before showing clients publicly, especially for weddings, families and children. Keep client galleries private, delete files after the agreed period, and respect requests to remove images.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a photographer website include?
Fast portfolio galleries organised by type, a few featured stories, clear packages with deliverables and starting prices, an about page, password-protected client galleries for proofing and delivery, and a booking form that asks for the date first and explains deposits.
How do I make my photography website load faster?
Upload web-sized images rather than camera originals, typically around 2,000 pixels on the long side, compress them carefully, use modern formats such as WebP where supported, and load images as visitors scroll. Test on a mid-range phone using mobile data, not office Wi-Fi.
Should photographers watermark website images?
A small, subtle watermark is a reasonable compromise for public galleries. Large watermarks distract from the work. The stronger protection is only publishing web-sized images and keeping full-resolution files for client delivery, because small web images are far less useful for printing.
What are client galleries for photographers?
Private, password-protected galleries where clients view, select and download their photos. They are used for proofing album choices and delivering final images, replacing large file transfers over WhatsApp or USB drives. Set download expiry dates and agree in the contract how long files stay available.
How should photographers handle booking deposits?
Explain the deposit amount that secures the date, when the balance is due and what happens if the event is cancelled or moved. Collect deposits by bank transfer or a payment gateway, and confirm bookings in writing with the date, coverage and package.
Do I need permission to show client photos in my portfolio?
Yes. Get written permission before showing clients publicly, especially at weddings and with families or children. Many photographers include a portfolio permission clause in the contract, with an option for clients who prefer privacy. Remove images promptly if a client asks.
