How Should You Choose Among Developers?

People search for the "best website developer in Iqbal Town", but nobody can honestly claim that title. Judge any developer, including us, on things you can check:

  1. Live work: websites you can open on your phone today.
  2. A real address: an office you can visit, listed on Google Maps.
  3. Written scope and fixed price: before any deposit.
  4. Ownership: domain, hosting and code in your name.
  5. Reply speed: how quickly they answer your first WhatsApp message.
  6. Support terms: what happens after launch, in writing.

What Should You Bring to the First Meeting?

  • your business name, logo files and a short description of what you sell
  • three websites you like, and one thing you like about each
  • your list of services or products, with prices if you are happy to show them
  • any existing domain, hosting or Facebook page logins
  • a rough budget and the date you need the site

We will ask who your customers are, how they contact you now and what you want the website to do. You leave with a clear next step.

What Happens After the Meeting?

  1. Written scope and price: usually within one business day, listing pages, features, timeline and the fixed price.
  2. Deposit: typically 30% to 40%, paid only after you approve the scope.
  3. Design: we show the home page and key pages; you can come to the office to review them on a big screen if you like.
  4. Build: we build on a staging link you can open on your phone, and you send one list of changes per round.
  5. Launch and handover: the site goes live, and you receive the logins, with the domain and hosting already in your name.

Because the office is a short drive from most of Iqbal Town, many clients drop in for the design review and the handover, and handle everything else on WhatsApp.

Which Mistakes Do Local Businesses Often Make?

  • Paying a freelancer in full upfront, then losing contact before the site is finished.
  • Letting someone else register the domain, which makes it hard to move later.
  • Building a large website when a one-page site with WhatsApp would bring the same enquiries.
  • Skipping a Google Business Profile, which often brings more local customers than the website itself.
  • Publishing no prices, so families compare and choose a shop that does.
