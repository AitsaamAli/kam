Web Application Maintenance: What Web App Maintenance Cost Covers After Launch

Published 30 October 2026
Updated 30 October 2026

Web app maintenance cost covers hosting, security and framework updates, monitoring, backups, bug fixes and small changes after launch. It is ongoing and depends on traffic, integrations and how often you want changes. Plan a yearly budget from these 6 parts instead of a rule-of-thumb percentage, and agree response times in writing.

Key Takeaways

  • Every web app has running costs after launch; budget for them before you build.
  • Hosting, third-party services and developer time are the three main cost groups.
  • Security updates are not optional; unpatched apps are a common route to breaches.
  • Separate maintenance (keeping it working) from new features (changing what it does).
  • Agree response times, included hours and what counts as a new feature in writing.

What Does Web App Maintenance Cost Cover?

A school system, clinic portal or dealer ordering app is not finished on launch day. Servers need paying for, frameworks release security fixes, payment providers change their requirements, browsers update and users find bugs nobody saw in testing. Web app maintenance cost is the price of keeping the application secure, working and useful. Owners who skip it usually pay more later, in emergency fixes or a premature rebuild.

How Should You Plan a Yearly Budget?

Rather than applying a percentage of the build price, add up the parts:

  1. Hosting: take the current plan cost for 12 months, plus room to grow if users are increasing.
  2. Services: estimate monthly volumes of messages, emails and transactions, multiplied by each provider's current official rates.
  3. Developer time: agree a monthly block of hours for updates, monitoring and small fixes, or a support plan with defined response times.
  4. Feature budget: a separate amount for planned improvements, approved one by one.
  5. Contingency: a reserve for unexpected changes, such as a payment provider updating its integration.

Writing it out this way shows which parts you control and which depend on usage.

What Is Maintenance and What Is a New Feature?

Disputes after launch usually come from this line. A useful rule: maintenance keeps the app doing what was agreed, securely; a new feature changes what it does. Fixing a report that shows wrong totals is maintenance. Adding a new report is a feature. Updating the payment integration because the provider changed its system is maintenance. Adding a second payment provider is a feature. Put examples like these in the support agreement.

Why Can't Security Updates Wait?

Web applications depend on many libraries, and vulnerabilities are found in them regularly. The OWASP Top 10 lists vulnerable and outdated components among the major risks. A maintenance plan should include checking for updates monthly, applying critical ones quickly and testing that nothing breaks. Our security checklist covers related basics.

What Should a Support Agreement Include?

  • Response times by severity: app down, major feature broken, minor issue.
  • Hours of support and how to report problems.
  • Included hours per month and the rate for extra work.
  • Who holds hosting and code accounts (they should be yours).
  • Backup frequency and a yearly restore test.
  • Notice periods for ending the agreement and a handover process.

What Are the Signs an App Is Under-Maintained?

Watch for pages that slowly get slower, error messages users have learnt to ignore, a hosting bill nobody has reviewed in a year, security certificates that expire, and a developer who is the only person able to deploy changes. Any of these suggests the app needs a maintenance review. Our guide to backups for small businesses explains why a restore test should be part of that review.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is included in web app maintenance cost?
Hosting, third-party services such as messaging and payment fees, security and framework updates, monitoring, backups, bug fixes and small changes. New features are usually budgeted separately. The total depends on traffic, integrations and how often the business needs changes to the application.
How much should I budget each year for web app maintenance?
Add up the parts: twelve months of hosting, expected service usage at providers' current rates, a monthly block of developer hours, a separate feature budget and a contingency reserve. This gives a more realistic figure than a percentage of the build price, and shows which costs depend on usage.
What is the difference between maintenance and a new feature?
Maintenance keeps the app doing what was agreed, securely and correctly. A new feature changes what it does. Fixing wrong report totals is maintenance; adding a new report is a feature. Write examples like these into your support agreement to avoid disputes later.
Do web apps really need regular security updates?
Yes. Applications rely on many libraries, and vulnerabilities are found in them regularly. The OWASP Top 10 includes vulnerable and outdated components among major risks. Check for updates monthly, apply critical patches quickly and test that the application still works afterwards.
What should a software support agreement include?
Response times by severity, support hours, included monthly hours and the rate for extra work, account ownership, backup frequency with a yearly restore test, and notice periods with a handover process. Clear terms prevent arguments when something goes wrong at a busy time.
