Why We Publish Prices At All

A published starting price doesn't commit us to building every project at that exact figure — scope varies, and we'll always confirm the exact price before any work starts. What it does is remove the single biggest piece of friction in hiring a web developer you've never met: not knowing if you're even in the right ballpark before you spend time on a conversation.

How Payment Works

We don't ask for full payment upfront. A typical project runs on a simple milestone structure: a deposit to begin (usually 30–40%), a payment at a mid-project checkpoint, and a final payment on delivery. Payment is taken through standard, recognisable channels, not a request to wire money to an unfamiliar account.

What Can Change the Final Price

  • Page count beyond the stated package — extra pages are quoted per page, not folded in silently
  • Custom copywriting or professional photography, if you'd like us to arrange either
  • Non-standard integrations (a specific booking tool, a specific payment provider, a specific CRM)
  • A compressed timeline, if you need something delivered faster than our standard schedule

Anything that changes the price is agreed with you in writing before it's added — never discovered on a final invoice.

A Couple of Worked Examples

A five-page business site for a small trades business, using mostly client-provided text and photos, typically lands right at the Standard Business Website price. A twelve-product online store using a hosted e-commerce platform with standard payment methods typically lands near the E-commerce Store starting price, slightly above if custom product photography is needed. These are illustrative, not guaranteed.

A Note for Businesses With a Tight Budget

If our starting price is still a stretch, say so when you message us. We'd rather talk honestly about a smaller, phased version of the project — launching with fewer pages or features now, adding more once the business can support it — than have a tight budget be a silent reason you never reach out at all.

How This Compares to What You’d Pay Elsewhere

For context, not as a sales pitch: most SME-focused web development agencies internationally start an entry-level business site somewhere between $500 and $900, with mid-tier agencies often running considerably higher once overhead and account-management layers are factored in. Our starting prices sit at or below that typical band for comparable scope — you're welcome to get a second quote elsewhere and compare directly before deciding.

A Word on Prices That Seem Too Low

You'll find web developers quoting far less than even our starting prices — sometimes a fraction of them. We'd rather be honest: at that price, something is usually being cut, whether it's genuine mobile testing, real SEO basics, accessibility consideration, or simply the time needed to build something that doesn't need fixing six months later. Our prices reflect what it actually costs to do this properly, not the lowest number that will get a deposit paid.

What’s Not Included in the Starting Price

Domain registration and hosting costs are paid directly by you to the registrar/host (usually a small annual fee, in your own name so you're never locked in). Custom photography or professional copywriting, if you'd like us to arrange either, is quoted separately. Ongoing maintenance after launch is optional and separately priced. We list these plainly here rather than let them surface as a surprise later.

If a Project Isn’t Working Out

If, after the deposit, it becomes clear a project genuinely isn't going to work — for either side — we handle it fairly: you're only charged for work actually completed against the agreed milestones, never for undelivered work. This is written into the agreement before any deposit is taken, not negotiated after the fact if something goes wrong.

Currency and Local Quoting

While the reference prices above are shown in USD for a simple comparison point, we're happy to quote in your local currency, calculated against current exchange rates at the time of quoting, so there's no ambiguity about what you're actually agreeing to pay.

A five-page business site for a small trades business, using mostly client-provided text and photos, typically lands right at the Standard Business Website price. A twelve-product online store for a small retailer, using a hosted e-commerce platform with standard payment methods for their market, typically lands near the E-commerce Store starting price, slightly above if custom product photography is needed. These are illustrative, not guaranteed — your exact quote depends on your actual project.

Why We Don’t Push Monthly Subscription Pricing

Some web agencies structure everything as an ongoing monthly fee, which can quietly cost more over two or three years than simply paying to build and own the site outright. We default to a one-time build price with site ownership handed to you, and offer maintenance as an optional add-on rather than a requirement — because we think that's the fairer structure for a small business, even though a recurring fee would be more predictable revenue for us.

The Initial Quote Conversation Is Free

There's no charge for describing your project and getting a fixed quote back — that conversation costs you nothing and commits you to nothing. We only ask for a deposit once you've seen and approved a written scope and price you're actually happy with.

If You Get a Lower Quote Elsewhere

We won't pretend to match any price — that race to the bottom is exactly how corners get cut. What we will do is explain plainly what's included in our price versus what a much lower quote elsewhere is likely leaving out, so you can compare on substance, not just the headline number, and decide for yourself.

Still Not Sure Which Package Fits?

Send us a description of your business and what you're trying to achieve online, and we'll tell you honestly which service and price band actually fits — including telling you if what you need is simpler, and cheaper, than you assumed.

The Philosophy Behind All of This

A lot of small businesses have been burned once by a vague quote that grew well beyond what was first discussed. Every pricing decision on this page — publishing starting prices, explaining what changes a price, being upfront about what isn't included — is built around not being the cause of that experience for anyone. If you've been quoted vaguely elsewhere and it made you uneasy, that instinct was probably right.

One More Thing Worth Saying

We'd rather lose a project to a client who simply can't stretch to our price than win one by quietly cutting corners to hit a lower number. That's a trade-off we're comfortable with, and it's worth knowing about us before you ask for a quote.

A Note for Businesses With a Tight Budget

If our starting price is still a stretch, say so when you message us. We'd rather talk honestly about what a smaller, phased version of the project could look like — launching with fewer pages or features now, adding more once the business can support it — than have a tight budget be a silent reason you never reach out at all.

One Price, Clearly Explained, Beats a Cheaper One You Don’t Understand

If any part of a quote we send you is unclear — why a figure is what it is, what exactly a line item covers — ask us to explain it before you agree to anything. A quote you fully understand, even if it's not the cheapest you've seen, is worth more than one that looks good on the surface and leaves you guessing what's actually included.

A Final Word

Pricing built around honesty, not around getting the lowest number in front of you first. That's the whole philosophy on this page, and we're happy to defend it in conversation if you'd like to ask us more.
