An Online Store Built to Actually Sell

A product catalogue alone isn’t an online store. What makes the difference is the checkout flow, the payment methods your actual customers use, and whether the site loads fast enough that nobody abandons their cart. That’s where we focus.

What’s Actually Included

  • A product catalogue with categories, search and filtering — sized to however many products you actually have
  • A checkout flow tested end-to-end, on both desktop and mobile, before launch
  • Payment methods matched to your actual market and customers
  • Order management you can run yourself — see orders, update statuses, manage stock
  • Shipping and delivery setup matched to how you actually fulfil orders
  • Basic email notifications — order confirmation, shipping updates — set up and tested

Payment Methods Matched to Where You Sell

This is the detail most generic website builders get wrong, and it's the single biggest reason an e-commerce launch underperforms. We set up the right local payment rails, buy-now-pay-later options and cash-on-delivery where relevant, rather than defaulting to one generic international payment gateway and hoping it's close enough for your actual customers.

Which Platform We Build On

We choose the platform based on your actual scale and needs. A straightforward store with a manageable product count is usually faster and cheaper on a modern hosted platform where you handle inventory yourself. A store with complex product variants or unusual logic is sometimes better served by a custom build. We'll tell you honestly which fits.

WhatsApp Ordering, Where It Fits

For a lot of small businesses, customers genuinely prefer to inquire or order over WhatsApp before committing to an online checkout. We build a visible, working WhatsApp inquiry flow into every e-commerce project as standard, not as a custom add-on.

Trust Signals on the Store Itself

An unfamiliar online store asking for payment details triggers real hesitation in a first-time buyer. We build in the basics that reduce that friction: clear return and refund policy pages, visible contact information, and SSL/HTTPS set up correctly, not assumed.

Timeline

A straightforward store on a hosted platform typically takes 2–3 weeks from content handoff, depending on product catalogue size. A custom-built store with non-standard requirements takes longer — we'll scope and quote it properly once we understand what you actually need.

Once Your Store Is Live

We test the full order flow — a real test purchase through to a real test refund — before handing the store over. After launch, you manage day-to-day orders yourself through the platform dashboard, with us available for anything that needs a developer's hand.

Built for Mobile Shoppers First

In every one of our markets, the majority of online shopping now happens on a phone, not a desktop. We design and test the checkout flow on mobile first — large enough tap targets, a payment flow that doesn't require awkward zooming or horizontal scrolling, and page weights kept low enough that it loads quickly even on a patchy connection, which matters more than people expect for conversion.

Trust Signals on the Store Itself

An unfamiliar online store asking for payment details triggers real hesitation in a first-time buyer. We build in the basics that reduce that friction: clear return and refund policy pages, visible contact information, SSL/HTTPS set up correctly (not assumed), and, for markets where it applies, visible display of relevant business licensing or registration details. None of this is exotic — it's simply done properly and not skipped.

Once Your Store Is Live

We test the full order flow — a real test purchase through to a real test refund — before handing the store over, so the first real customer order isn't also the first time the flow has actually been run end to end. After launch, you manage day-to-day orders yourself through the platform dashboard, with us available for anything that needs a developer's hand.

Keeping Inventory Accurate

Nothing damages trust in a new online store faster than a customer ordering something that turns out to be out of stock. We set up stock-tracking from day one, sized to how you actually manage inventory — manual updates for a small catalogue, or a sync with your existing system where one already exists and a reasonable integration is possible.

Returns, Refunds and Local Expectations

What customers expect from a return policy varies by market, and we write the policy pages to match local norms and, where relevant, local consumer-protection requirements — rather than using one generic template across every market. Getting this specific detail right is a small thing that quietly affects how much a first-time customer trusts your store enough to actually buy.

Knowing What’s Actually Happening on Your Store

We set up basic analytics and conversion tracking as standard — not just so you can see visitor numbers, but so you can see where people are dropping off in the checkout, which is usually where the real, fixable problems in an online store live. A store you can't measure is a store you can't improve.

Selling Across More Than Just Your Own Site

For businesses that already sell or want to sell through other channels — Instagram, a marketplace, delivery apps — we'll talk through how your own site fits alongside those rather than competes with them. Often the right setup is a lean, fast website as your "home base" with clear links out to wherever else you sell, rather than trying to force every channel into one system.

Before We Call a Store “Done”

Every store goes through the same pre-launch checklist: a complete test purchase on both desktop and mobile, a check that order confirmation emails actually arrive, a check that stock levels update correctly after a sale, and a check that every payment method we've set up actually processes correctly. None of this is glamorous, and all of it is exactly what prevents an embarrassing problem in the store's first week live.

Growing the Store Later

We build the first version sized to what you actually need today, not an over-engineered system built for a scale you may never reach. If the business grows and the store needs to grow with it — more products, more complex shipping rules, a loyalty program — the platforms we build on are chosen specifically because they can grow with you, rather than needing to be rebuilt from scratch at the first sign of success.

Reducing Abandoned Carts

A meaningful share of online shoppers add something to a cart and leave without finishing — that's normal across every store, not a sign something's broken. We set up basic abandoned-cart follow-up (an automatic reminder email, where the platform supports it) as part of a standard build, since it's one of the highest-return, lowest-effort features a new store can have from day one.

Planning Around Busy Seasons

If your business has a predictable busy season — holiday shopping, a specific religious or cultural calendar, a seasonal product line — tell us when you first reach out. We'll plan the build and launch timeline to have your store live and fully tested well before that period starts, not scrambling to finish during it.

If Social Media Is Already Driving Sales

Plenty of small businesses are already selling informally through Instagram or Facebook DMs before they ever build a proper store. If that's you, we won't tell you to abandon what's working — we'll build a store that complements it, with clear links from your social profiles and a checkout that's faster than negotiating a sale over direct messages, so the two channels reinforce each other rather than compete.

Ready to Start Selling Online?

Tell us what you sell, roughly how many products, and which market your customers are in — we'll come back with a real plan and a real price.

A Quick Note on Product Photography

Good product photos sell far more effectively than good copy alone. If you don't have professional photography, basic phone photography shot in good natural light against a plain background is a reasonable starting point — we can advise on simple setup tips, or arrange professional photography as a separate quoted add-on if that's a better fit for your product range.

One More Thing Worth Saying

An online store is never really "finished" at launch — it's the starting point for a business that can now sell beyond whoever walks past the shop. We treat launch day as the beginning of that, not the end of our involvement, and we're glad to keep helping as the store grows.
