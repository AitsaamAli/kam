FBR SIM Block for Non-Filers in 2026: How Section 114B Works, Who Can Be Listed, and How to Get Your SIM Restored

20 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 20 November 2026
A SIM can be blocked only after a Section 114(4) notice, a missed deadline and no return. Filing is what reverses it.

Every few months a 'FBR SIM block list' story goes round, and people ask whether their number is next. The answer is in one section of the law, and it is narrower than the rumours suggest. Here is what Section 114B allows, the three conditions that must be met first, and the way out.

TL;DR

Section 114B of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 (inserted by the Finance Act 2022, expanded by the Finance Act 2024) lets the Board issue an 'income tax general order' against persons who are not on the Active Taxpayers List but are liable to file a return. The order may disable mobile phones or SIMs, discontinue electricity and gas connections, and restrict foreign travel for citizens (excluding NICOP holders, minors, students, Hajj and Umrah travellers and others the Board notifies). No one can be included unless a notice under Section 114(4) was issued, its compliance date has passed, and the return has still not been filed. The Board or the Commissioner may order restoration once the return is filed or the person is shown not to have been liable. The action does not prevent any other action under the Ordinance.

What Section 114B Says

Short answer: Section 114B lets the Board order SIM blocking, power and gas cuts and travel limits for people liable to file but not on the ATL.

Section 114B(1) says that, notwithstanding any other law, the Board has the power to issue an income tax general order in respect of persons who are not appearing on the Active Taxpayers List but are liable to file a return under the Ordinance.

Section 114B(2) lists the consequences the order may entail, any or all of them:

ClauseConsequence
(a)Disabling of mobile phones or mobile phone SIMs
(b)Discontinuance of electricity connection
(c)Discontinuance of gas connection
(d)Restriction on foreign travel for a citizen of Pakistan (added by the Finance Act 2024)

The travel restriction excludes persons holding a NICOP, minors, students, persons going abroad for Hajj or Umrah, and other classes the Board notifies.

Three Conditions Before Anyone Is Listed

Short answer: A Section 114(4) notice must have been issued, its date passed, and the return still unfiled.

Section 114B(4) says no person shall be included in the general order unless all three conditions are met:

  1. A notice under Section 114(4) has been issued, requiring the person to file a return.
  2. The compliance date in that notice has passed.
  3. The person has not filed the return.

So the order is not a surprise for someone who is watching their IRIS inbox and the address on their record. The notice comes first. If you have received a Section 114(4) notice, that is the moment to act, not after the SIM stops working.

Who Is Liable to File, and So at Risk

Short answer: Only people liable to file under Section 114 can be listed: income above the exempt amount, property, vehicles above 1000 cc or an NTN.

Section 114B reaches only people who are liable to file. Under Section 114(1), that includes every company, every other person whose taxable income exceeds the amount not chargeable to tax, and any person who: was charged to tax in either of the two preceding tax years; owns immovable property of 500 square yards or more, or a flat in the areas the section lists; owns a motor vehicle above 1000 cc; or has obtained an NTN. Under Section 115(3), a widow and an orphan below twenty-five, among others, are not required to file solely because they own such property or a vehicle.

If you were never liable, the restoration ground in Section 114B(3)(b) applies; you still need to show it.

How to Know If You Are Exposed

Short answer: Check your ATL status and your IRIS inbox for a Section 114(4) notice.

  1. Check your ATL status. If you appear on the list, the general order cannot cover you. Use the online or SMS ATL check.
  2. Check your IRIS inbox and post for a notice under Section 114(4).
  3. Look at your filing history. If your last return is missing and you have income or assets above the thresholds, file now.

We have not found an official FBR online tool that lists blocked SIMs by number, so we do not recommend third-party "SIM block check" sites asking for your CNIC.

How to Get a SIM, Electricity or Gas Restored

Short answer: File the return, then ask your Commissioner to order restoration under Section 114B(3).

Section 114B(3) lets the Board or the Commissioner who has jurisdiction over you order restoration of mobile phones, SIMs and electricity and gas connections where satisfied that:

  • (a) the return has been filed; or
  • (b) the person was not liable to file a return under the Ordinance.

In practice:

  1. File the return and wealth statement for the year in the notice.
  2. If it is late, the surcharge under Section 182A or Form ATL-U gets you onto the ATL; the ATL route calculator compares them.
  3. Submit a restoration request to your Commissioner with the acknowledgement of the filed return.
  4. Follow up until the order is withdrawn for your CNIC.

What Else Still Applies

Short answer: Restoration does not cancel other action; late filing penalties still apply (Section 114B(5)).

Section 114B(5) says the action does not preclude any other action under the Ordinance. Filing late still attracts the Section 182 late filing penalty (estimate it with our late filing penalty calculator), and the Commissioner may still make a best judgement assessment under Section 121 if the notice is ignored.

Common Misunderstandings

Short answer: Not every non-filer is blocked, and paying a fine alone does not restore a SIM; filing does.

  • "All non-filers will be blocked." Only persons liable to file, after a Section 114(4) notice and a missed deadline.
  • "Paying a fine unblocks the SIM." The law ties restoration to a filed return or proof of non-liability.
  • "Overseas Pakistanis cannot travel." NICOP holders are excluded from the travel restriction.
  • "Joining the ATL is enough without the return." You join the ATL by filing; there is no shortcut.

The Section 114(4) Notice Comes First

Short answer: A Section 114(4) notice gives 30 days (or the stated period) to file, and can cover up to five past tax years.

Under Section 114(4), the Commissioner may, by written notice, require a person who in the Commissioner's opinion is required to file a return for a tax year, but has not, to file it within thirty days of service, or a longer or shorter period stated in the notice or allowed by the Commissioner. Under Section 114(5), such a notice may cover one or more of the last five completed tax years. The notice usually arrives in the IRIS inbox and, for some people, by post or courier.

If You Were Never Liable

Short answer: Reply in writing with evidence; Section 114B(3)(b) allows restoration if you were not liable to file.

Some notices go to people who did not have to file, for example someone whose only link to a trigger was a property later sold, or a widow or orphan covered by Section 115(3). Section 114B(3)(b) allows restoration where the Commissioner is satisfied the person was not liable. Reply to the notice in writing, explain why you were not required to file for each year named, and attach evidence such as a sale deed, transfer letter or CNIC of the deceased spouse. A reply is still a reply; ignoring the notice is what leads to the order.

What a Late Return Costs Compared With a Block

Short answer: A late return's penalty is reduced if filed within three months of the due date; a block costs more.

People sometimes delay because they fear the penalty. The Section 182 late filing penalty is real, and is reduced to 75%, 50% or 25% if the return is filed within one, two or three months of the due date. But a blocked phone, a disconnected meter or a stopped trip costs more in a day than a filed return costs in fees. How to Make Sure It Never Happens

Short answer: Keep IRIS contact details current, file on time, answer every notice and check the ATL after filing.

  • Keep your IRIS contact details current. Notices go to the email, mobile and address on your record.
  • File by the due date every year. For tax year 2026 that was 31 October 2026; a timely return keeps you on the ATL.
  • Answer every notice, even when you believe you were not liable. A written reply with evidence is what Section 114B(3)(b) relies on.
  • Check the ATL after filing. FBR updates the list; your name should appear after the next update.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can FBR block my SIM for not filing a return?
Yes, under Section 114B, if you are liable to file, received a Section 114(4) notice, missed its deadline and still have not filed.
What else can a Section 114B order do?
Cut electricity and gas connections and, from the Finance Act 2024, restrict foreign travel for citizens (with exclusions such as NICOP holders, minors, students and Hajj or Umrah travellers).
How do I get my blocked SIM activated?
File the return, then ask the Commissioner to order restoration under Section 114B(3); restoration also applies if you show you were not liable to file.
Is there an official FBR SIM block check online?
We have not found one. Check your ATL status and your IRIS inbox for a Section 114(4) notice instead.
Can I be listed without a notice?
No. Section 114B(4) requires a Section 114(4) notice, its compliance date to have passed, and no return filed.
Does Section 114B apply to people on the ATL?
No. It covers persons not appearing on the Active Taxpayers List.
Does restoration cancel the late filing penalty?
No. Section 114B(5) says the action does not preclude any other action under the Ordinance.
Are overseas Pakistanis covered by the travel ban?
Persons holding a NICOP are excluded from the travel restriction in Section 114B(2)(d).
