Gulberg II

Lahore's startup scene has grown out of Gulberg's co-working spaces and offices: fintech, edtech, e-commerce and SaaS companies, often backed by angel investors, local VCs or foreign funds. Raising investment is mostly a legal and commercial exercise, but each round leaves a trail that the company's and the founders' tax records must follow.

Investment is not income

When an investor buys shares in your company, the money is share capital (and share premium, if issued above par). It's not the company's income and isn't taxed as revenue. It appears in the company's balance sheet as equity, and in the investor's records as an investment.

Getting the structure right first

Most investors require a private limited company — often with a holding company abroad for foreign funds. Before a round:

  1. The company should be incorporated with the SECP and registered with FBR.
  2. Founders' shareholdings should be properly allotted and documented.
  3. Any money founders put in should be recorded as share capital or a director's loan, not left vague.
  4. Past freelance or sole-proprietor income should be separated from the company's.

Valuation and share premium

Shares issued at a high valuation create share premium. Tax law contains provisions dealing with shares issued at a premium where the consideration may not be at fair value, and valuation support from the round documents helps. Keep the term sheet, subscription agreement and board approvals.

Founders' personal records

Founders often put their own savings into the company early on. Each founder's wealth statement should show the shares at cost and the funds used. If a founder later sells some shares to an investor (secondary sale), the gain is the founder's, reported in their personal return.

Investor due diligence will ask for the company's tax returns, withholding statements and PRA/FBR registrations. Startups that kept compliance current close rounds faster.

Burn, losses and later profits

Early-stage companies usually make losses. Business losses can generally be carried forward and set off against future profits for a limited number of years, under the rules. File every year even in loss, so the losses are on record. See our software house tax guide.

Gulberg II — questions we get asked

Is money raised from investors taxable income for our startup?
No. Money paid for shares is share capital and premium, not income. It's recorded as equity.
Do we need a private limited company to raise investment?
Most investors require one, and foreign funds often invest through a holding structure. We help set up and register the Pakistani entity.
Can we carry forward our startup's losses?
Business losses can generally be carried forward against future profits for a limited period. File every year so losses are on record.
I'm selling some of my founder shares to an investor. Is that taxed?
Yes, the gain on your shares is a capital gain reported in your personal return.
