Sole Proprietorship Registration in Pakistan 2026: What to Register, Where, and in What Order

10 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 10 November 2026
A sole proprietorship is the quickest way to start a business in Pakistan, but it is registered through the tax system, not through a separate business registry.

Most small businesses in Pakistan, from shops and workshops to agencies and online stores, start as sole proprietorships. The setup is cheap and fast, but owners often register in the wrong order or miss a registration that later blocks a bank account or a marketplace payout. This guide sets out what the law actually requires, in the order that avoids rework.

TL;DR

A sole proprietorship is a business owned and run by one individual with no separate legal identity. There is no SECP-style registry for it; in practice it is recognised through income tax registration under Section 181 of the Income Tax Ordinance, where the owner's CNIC serves as the NTN. The steps are: register (or use) the owner's IRIS profile, add the business name, address and branches through Form 181, open a bank account in the business name and add it to IRIS, then check any licence your trade needs. Online sellers must now be registered before marketplaces or couriers can serve them, and the first income tax return with business income follows at year end.

What a Sole Proprietorship Is in Law

A sole proprietorship is not a separate legal person. The business and its owner are one taxpayer: profits are the owner's business income, taxed at individual rates, and the owner is personally responsible for the business's debts. That is the key difference from a private limited company registered with SECP, which is a separate person, and from a partnership or association of persons (AOP), which has two or more members. Our comparison of sole proprietorship, partnership and private limited covers the choice in detail.

Because there is no separate legal person, there is no separate company-style register for a sole proprietorship. Partnerships can be registered with the provincial Registrar of Firms (see our guide); companies are incorporated with SECP; a sole proprietorship is recognised through the owner's tax registration and the registrations its activity requires.

Step-by-Step: Setting Up in the Right Order

  1. Get your IRIS profile active. If you have never registered, create an IRIS account with your CNIC; the CNIC becomes your NTN. Our NTN registration service does this for you.
  2. Add the business to your registration. Enter the business name, address and nature of business through Form 181 on IRIS, so FBR records match your signboard, invoices and bank account. Our step-by-step guide to adding a business to your NTN on IRIS explains the form and what to do if the online option is not working.
  3. Open the business bank account. Banks normally ask for proof of tax registration showing the business. Then add the bank account to your IRIS profile, since bank account details are one of the items Form 181 updates.
  4. Check trade-specific licences and provincial levies that apply to your activity, such as professional tax in Punjab.
  5. Set up records from day one. Section 174 requires records to be kept for six years. Our guide to records FBR expects lists what to keep.

Selling Online: The 2025 Rules

The Finance Act, 2025 added a rule that matters for online sole proprietors: Section 181(1) now expressly covers people selling digitally ordered goods or services from within Pakistan through an online marketplace or courier, and Section 181(1A) says a marketplace or courier must not let a vendor use its platform unless the vendor is registered.

In practice, a seller without an NTN can find a store or courier account blocked. Register before you launch.

Common Mistakes

  1. Using a business name that does not appear on the NTN. Banks, marketplaces and customers' withholding statements then do not match your records.
  2. Ignoring advance tax. Once income passes Rs 1 million, quarterly instalments are due, and missing them adds default surcharge.
  3. Mixing personal and business money. It makes the wealth statement and the profit figure hard to defend.
  4. Opening a second business without updating IRIS. Every business and branch should be on the registration.

How a Sole Proprietor's Profit Is Taxed

A sole proprietor's business profit is added to any other income and taxed at the rates for individuals whose income is not mainly salary. For tax year 2027 (1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027) the rates in the First Schedule are:

Taxable income (non-salaried individual, TY2027)Income tax
Up to Rs 600,000Nil
Rs 600,001 to Rs 1,200,00015% of the amount above Rs 600,000
Rs 1,200,001 to Rs 1,600,000Rs 90,000 + 20% of the amount above Rs 1,200,000
Rs 1,600,001 to Rs 3,200,000Rs 170,000 + 30% of the amount above Rs 1,600,000
Rs 3,200,001 to Rs 5,600,000Rs 650,000 + 40% of the amount above Rs 3,200,000
Above Rs 5,600,000Rs 1,610,000 + 45% of the amount above Rs 5,600,000

Worked example. A Lahore shop owner earns a business profit of Rs 2,400,000 in tax year 2027 and has no other income. Tax is Rs 170,000 plus 30% of Rs 800,000 (the amount above Rs 1,600,000), which is Rs 410,000. Adjustable tax deducted or collected during the year, for example tax on monthly electricity bills above Rs 30,000 (Section 235(4)(b)), is credited against it, and any balance is paid with the return. The figures are illustrative; the actual liability depends on the full return.

Because tax is charged on profit, not turnover, the records behind your expenses matter as much as the sales figures. Our records guide explains what FBR expects.

Choosing and Protecting a Business Name

There is no national register of sole proprietorship names, so registering the business name on IRIS does not stop someone else using it. If the name has value, consider a trademark application with IPO-Pakistan, the official intellectual property office. In any case:

  • Use exactly the same spelling on IRIS, the bank account, the signboard, invoices and online profiles.
  • Avoid words that suggest a company or a regulated business (for example "Limited", "Bank" or "Insurance") when you are not one.
  • Check that the name is not already widely used in your trade and city, to avoid disputes later.

Opening the Business Bank Account

Banks set their own account-opening rules, but for a sole proprietorship they commonly ask for the owner's CNIC, proof of tax registration showing the business, proof of the business address, and a letter on the business letterhead describing the activity. Once the account is open, add it to your IRIS profile through Form 181 so FBR's records and the bank's match. Use the account for all business receipts and payments; mixing personal and business money makes the wealth statement and the profit figure harder to defend.

Online Sellers: Getting It Right From Day One

Section 181(1A), added by the Finance Act, 2025, bars online marketplaces and couriers from letting an unregistered vendor use their platform for e-commerce. For a sole proprietor selling online, that means:

  1. Register on IRIS and add the online store as your business, with the store name you will use on the marketplace.
  2. Use the same name and CNIC when opening marketplace seller and courier cash-on-delivery accounts.
  3. Keep the marketplace settlement reports and courier statements every month; they are your sales records.
  4. Declare the profit in your return as business income, claiming the platform fees and delivery charges you can support with statements.

Leaving a Job to Run the Business Full Time

If you were salaried and now run the business full time, your registration stays the same; the change shows in your return. The year you leave will usually have both salary and business income. From the next year, only business income is declared, and because salary tax at source no longer covers you, check whether quarterly advance tax will apply once your business income has been assessed at Rs 1 million or more.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to register a sole proprietorship with SECP?
No. SECP incorporates companies. A sole proprietorship is not a separate legal person; it is recognised through the owner's income tax registration and any registrations its activity requires.
Is my CNIC my NTN as a sole proprietor?
Yes. Under Section 181(4) of the Income Tax Ordinance, from tax year 2015 an individual's CNIC is used as the National Tax Number.
How do I add my business name to my NTN?
Through Registration Form 181 (modification) on IRIS. FBR's page on changing details lists business address, business branches and bank account among the items it updates.
Can I sell on Daraz or other marketplaces without an NTN?
No. Since the Finance Act, 2025, marketplaces and couriers must not let unregistered vendors use their services for e-commerce transactions.
Is there a government fee for an NTN?
FBR charges no fee for NTN registration on IRIS.
How is a sole proprietor taxed?
Business profit is the owner's income, taxed at the rates for individuals with business income, through an annual return with a wealth statement.
When should a sole proprietor become a company?
Usually when liability protection, outside investment or larger contracts matter more than simplicity. Our SECP versus sole proprietorship guide compares the two.
How much tax does a sole proprietor pay?
For tax year 2027, non-salaried individuals pay nil up to Rs 600,000 of taxable income, then 15%, 20%, 30%, 40% and 45% in bands, reaching Rs 1,610,000 plus 45% above Rs 5,600,000.
When is a sole proprietor's tax return due?
Under Section 118, an individual's return is normally due by 30 September following the end of the tax year, unless FBR extends the date.
Can I register my business name as a trademark?
Yes. Registering the name on IRIS does not protect it; a trademark application with IPO-Pakistan does.
What does it cost to file late?
Under Section 182, 0.1% of the tax payable per day of default, up to 50%, with a minimum of Rs 40,000; and an individual pays a Rs 25,000 surcharge under Section 182A to rejoin the Active Taxpayers List.
Do I need a separate NTN for my online store?
No. A sole proprietor's store is added as a business on the owner's registration, where the CNIC is the NTN.
I left my job to run my business. Does my registration change?
No. The same registration continues; your return shows business income instead of, or alongside, salary for the year you switched.
