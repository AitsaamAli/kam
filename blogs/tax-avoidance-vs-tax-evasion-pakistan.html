Tax Avoidance vs Tax Evasion in Pakistan: What Is Legal, What Is a Crime, and What FBR Can Do (2026)

20 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 20 November 2026
Paying less tax is legal. Hiding income is not. Pakistan's law draws three lines, not two.

Most people use 'avoidance' and 'evasion' interchangeably. The law does not. One is planning, one is a scheme FBR can unwind, and one is an offence. Knowing where your arrangements sit is the cheapest tax advice you will ever get.

Short answer

Pakistan's Income Tax Ordinance 2001 (FBR text amended up to 30 June 2026) recognises three zones. Legal tax planning uses the allowances, credits and rates the law gives, such as zakat (Section 60), pension fund contributions (Section 63) and filing on time to stay on the Active Taxpayers List. A 'tax avoidance scheme', defined in Section 109(2) as any transaction where one of the main purposes is avoiding or reducing tax, can be disregarded or recharacterised by the Commissioner. Tax evasion, meaning concealing income or giving inaccurate particulars, attracts a penalty of Rs 100,000 or the tax sought to be evaded, whichever is higher (Section 182, entry 12), and can be prosecuted: up to two years' imprisonment where the revenue impact is Rs 500,000 or more (Section 192A), and up to three years or a fine of up to Rs 100,000 for a knowingly false verification (Section 192).

Three Zones, Not Two

Short answer: The law separates legal planning, tax avoidance schemes (Section 109) and evasion (Sections 182, 192, 192A).

Tax evasionConcealing income, claiming false deductions, false statementsPenalty and possible prosecution (Sections 182, 192, 192A)

Legal Tax Planning: What the Ordinance Itself Offers

Short answer: Using zakat, donations, pension credits and filer rates as the law intends is legal tax planning.

  • Zakat: a deductible allowance under Section 60. See our zakat guide.
  • Charitable donations: a tax credit under Section 61, explained in our donations guide.
  • Approved pension fund: a Section 63 tax credit on contributions up to 20% of taxable income.
  • Being on the ATL: the single biggest saving for most people. Bank profit is taxed at 20% instead of 40%, property sale advance tax at 2.75% instead of 11.5%, and there is no cash withdrawal tax.
  • Claiming every adjustable credit: see adjustable vs final tax.
  • Choosing the right structure for a business, sole proprietorship, AOP or company, for real commercial reasons.

Tax Avoidance Schemes: Section 109

Short answer: The Commissioner can disregard or recharacterise a transaction whose main purposes include reducing tax (Section 109).

Section 109(1) lets the Commissioner, in determining liability: recharacterise a transaction, or part of one, entered into as part of a tax avoidance scheme; disregard a transaction without substantial economic effect; recharacterise a transaction where the form does not reflect the substance; and, from tax year 2018, disregard an entity or corporate structure without economic or commercial substance or created as part of a tax avoidance scheme.

Section 109(2) defines a tax avoidance scheme as "any transaction where one of the main purposes of a person in entering into the transaction is the avoidance or reduction of any person's liability to tax". Section 109(3) adds that reduction includes avoidance or deferral of tax and an increase in a refund.

The test is not whether you saved tax, but whether the arrangement has real substance. Paper transfers to relatives, sham partnerships or loans that are never repaid are the typical targets.

Tax Evasion: Concealment and Its Penalty

Short answer: Concealment carries a penalty of Rs 100,000 or the tax evaded, whichever is higher (Section 182, entry 12).

Entry 12 of the Section 182 table covers a person who has concealed income or furnished inaccurate particulars of income, including suppressing income or claiming a deduction for expenditure not actually incurred. The penalty is Rs 100,000 or an amount equal to the tax the person sought to evade, whichever is higher. The entry also says no penalty is payable for the mere disallowance of a claim of exemption or of a declared expense, unless it is proved the person made the claim knowing it to be wrong. Honest mistakes are treated differently from deliberate ones.

When It Becomes a Crime

Short answer: Concealment with revenue impact of Rs 500,000 or more can mean up to two years' imprisonment (Section 192A).

Grey Areas We Are Often Asked About

Short answer: An arrangement is safe when it has real substance and documents; paper-only transfers are the usual target.

  • Putting property in a spouse's name: legal if the spouse genuinely owns it and paid for it or received a documented gift; risky if it is a paper arrangement.
  • Cash business sales not recorded: that is concealment, not planning.
  • Splitting income among family members who do not work in the business: likely to be recharacterised.
  • Using an approved pension fund to earn the Section 63 credit: planning, exactly as the law intends.

If You Have Already Crossed the Line

Short answer: Revising before an audit or Section 122(9) notice, with tax and surcharge paid, avoids penalty (Section 114(6A)).

Voluntary correction is far cheaper than waiting. Under Section 114(6A), a revised return filed before an audit or Section 122(9) notice, with tax and default surcharge paid, carries no penalty; after the Commissioner points out tax, 25% of the leviable penalties applies; after a show cause notice, 50%. Our guide to revising before a notice explains the steps.

How Hidden Income Usually Comes to Light

Short answer: Most cases turn on Section 111: unexplained investments, money or expenditure are added to income.

Section 111 is the provision most cases turn on. Where an amount is credited in a person's books, a person owns an investment, money or a valuable article, or has incurred expenditure, and offers no explanation of its nature and source, or an explanation the Commissioner does not find satisfactory, the amount is included in income under "Income from Other Sources" to the extent it is not adequately explained. The trigger is usually a mismatch: a property bought, a car registered or a foreign trip made that the declared income and wealth statement cannot support.

That is why a clean wealth statement is the best defence. When opening wealth, income, expenses and closing wealth reconcile, there is nothing unexplained. Our wealth reconciliation calculator runs that check.

The Cost of Getting It Wrong

Short answer: Concealment costs the tax, default surcharge and a penalty of at least Rs 100,000; voluntary revision avoids the penalty.

Take a trader who leaves Rs 2 million of sales out of a return and saves, say, Rs 300,000 of tax. If it is found, the tax is assessed, default surcharge runs on it under Section 205, and the concealment penalty under entry 12 of Section 182 is Rs 300,000 or Rs 100,000, whichever is higher. The revenue impact is under Rs 500,000, so Section 192A prosecution would not apply on these numbers; at a larger amount it would. Revising voluntarily before any audit or Section 122(9) notice, with the tax and surcharge paid, avoids the penalty entirely. Our default surcharge guide shows how the interest is worked out.

Advisers, Family Members and Abetment

Short answer: Knowingly helping someone evade tax is an offence under Section 199: a fine, up to three years' imprisonment, or both.

Section 199 makes it an offence to knowingly and wilfully aid, abet, assist, incite or induce another person to commit an offence under the Ordinance, punishable with a fine or imprisonment up to three years, or both. That covers anyone who knowingly helps build a false return or a sham arrangement, including an adviser, an accountant or a relative who lends their name. Section 200 also reaches the principal officers and directors of a company or AOP where the entity commits an offence. A good adviser protects you by refusing to do what would put both of you at risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between tax avoidance and tax evasion?
Avoidance uses arrangements to reduce tax; where its main purpose is reducing tax without substance, Section 109 lets the Commissioner disregard or recharacterise it. Evasion is concealing income or giving false particulars, which carries penalties and can be prosecuted.
Is tax avoidance legal in Pakistan?
Ordinary planning using reliefs the law offers is legal. A transaction that is a 'tax avoidance scheme' under Section 109(2) can be disregarded or recharacterised, though it is not by itself a crime.
What is the punishment for tax evasion in Pakistan?
A penalty of Rs 100,000 or the tax sought to be evaded, whichever is higher (Section 182 entry 12); prosecution with up to two years' imprisonment where the revenue impact is Rs 500,000 or more (Section 192A).
What is the penalty for a false statement in a return?
Section 192: a fine up to Rs 100,000 or imprisonment up to three years, or both, if the person knows the statement is false.
Is a disallowed expense the same as concealment?
No. Entry 12 says the mere disallowance of a claim does not attract the concealment penalty unless the claim was made knowing it to be wrong.
How can I legally pay less tax in Pakistan?
Claim zakat, donations and pension credits, stay on the ATL, claim every adjustable credit, and structure your business for genuine commercial reasons.
Can I fix a past understatement without a penalty?
Yes, by revising voluntarily before an audit or Section 122(9) notice and paying the tax and default surcharge (Section 114(6A)).
What is a tax avoidance scheme?
Section 109(2): any transaction where one of the main purposes of entering into it is avoiding or reducing any person's tax liability.
