The Simplified Turnover Tax Scheme — Right for Your Islamabad SME?

FBR's simplified turnover tax scheme lets many smaller businesses pay a flat percentage of turnover rather than computing tax on detailed profit-and-loss figures, which can genuinely simplify filing for the right business.

Common considerations: The scheme doesn't automatically benefit every SME — a business with strong margins might actually pay more under a flat turnover rate than it would under standard profit-based computation, so this needs evaluating rather than assumed as the simpler, cheaper default. Businesses growing quickly need to watch their turnover against the scheme's eligibility threshold each year, since exceeding it requires switching to standard computation. Kamboh Associates runs the actual numbers for each Islamabad SME client before recommending which approach to use.

Small IT Teams Between Freelance and Formal Company

A recurring Islamabad pattern involves a small IT or software team — often two to eight people — that's grown past individual freelance invoicing but hasn't yet incorporated as a formal Pvt Ltd company, sometimes operating as an AOP or informal partnership in the meantime.

Common considerations: Teams planning to eventually register with PSEB for export withholding benefits need to think through timing — some benefits are more naturally accessed post-incorporation, making the AOP-to-Pvt-Ltd decision worth planning ahead of rather than deferring indefinitely. Payroll withholding for the small team needs setting up correctly even at this scale, since there's no minimum-team-size exemption. Revenue split among founding members needs formal documentation rather than informal understanding, particularly before the team scales further and any ambiguity becomes more consequential.

Boutique Consultancies (F-7, F-11, Blue Area)

Small consultancy and advisory firms with a handful of staff, common across Islamabad's F-7, F-11, and Blue Area office spaces, often serve a client base that includes government or institutional contracts alongside private-sector work.

Common considerations: Consultancies with even occasional government-linked engagements need current ATL status and clean filing history ready ahead of any bid or tender, since compliance gaps discovered at bid time can disqualify a submission. Revenue recognition needs to match actual completed engagements rather than retainers received, affecting advance tax accuracy through the year even at SME scale.

From Freelance Collective to SME — Islamabad's Most Common Transition

A specific Islamabad path shows up often enough to be its own category: a handful of freelancers who found each other on shared projects, kept working together informally, and gradually became something that operates like a small agency without the formal structure to match.

Common considerations: The moment this group starts invoicing as a single entity rather than as separate freelancers, they've effectively created an informal partnership whether they've documented it or not — and formalizing that reality with proper NTN and, eventually, either an AOP or Pvt Ltd structure protects everyone involved better than leaving it undefined. International clients paying this group need clarity on who's actually receiving and declaring the payment, since inconsistent invoicing (sometimes one member's name, sometimes another's) creates a genuinely confusing tax picture that's hard to unwind later. Kamboh Associates has guided several Islamabad freelance groups through exactly this formalization, sorting out both the tax structure and the practical invoicing habits that need to change alongside it.
