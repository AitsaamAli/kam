Rang Mahal, Lahore — Cloth Merchants, Wholesale Credit and the Parchi System

Updated October 2026
Kamboh Associates
3 min read
Rang Mahal

Rang Mahal and the surrounding Walled City bazaars are among Lahore's oldest cloth markets — bridal fabrics, lawn, chiffon, karandi and suiting pass through dozens of hands from mills and weavers to retailers. The trade runs on credit chains settled through parchis (handwritten notes) and on trust built over generations. It works commercially; for tax, it needs to be translated into records.

The parchi chain

A wholesaler buys from a mill or an importer on credit, sells to a smaller wholesaler on credit, who sells to a retailer on credit. Each step is recorded on a parchi or in a khata. Payments flow back up the chain weeks or months later, often in cash.

For tax purposes, each trader needs:

  • purchases recorded with the supplier's name and amount;
  • sales recorded with the customer's name and amount;
  • outstanding balances (receivable and payable) at 30 June.

A khata kept honestly is a perfectly acceptable record. Parchis that are thrown away after settlement are not.

Sales tax on textiles

Textiles have had different sales tax regimes over the years, including special treatment for the textile sector and phases of zero-rating for export-oriented inputs. Wholesale cloth sold domestically is generally within sales tax at some stage of the chain. Wholesalers above the thresholds are generally required to register; mills and importers supplying them often insist on it. We check your position for your products.

Buying from unregistered weavers

Some fabrics come from small power-loom units that aren't registered for sales tax. Buying from them is legal, but:

  • you don't get input tax credit on those purchases if you're registered;
  • under some rules, purchases from unregistered persons carry extra tax consequences;
  • your records must still show the purchase.

Advance tax on wholesale sales

Provisions on advance tax for distributors, dealers and wholesalers may require collection of advance income tax on supplies in certain cases, depending on the year's law and the parties' filer status. These amounts appear on bills and are credits in returns.

Stock in the shop and godown

Cloth merchants hold large stocks by the than (bolt). Count closing stock at cost around 30 June. Out-of-fashion stock sold at a loss should be written down when sold.

If the business has been in the family for decades with no returns, start with the current year: register, record purchases and sales from today, and build from there. Most of the market's records are already in the khata — they just need to reach a return.

Rang Mahal — questions we get asked

Are parchis acceptable records for tax?
A khata or parchi record kept consistently is acceptable. The problem is when parchis are discarded after settlement.
Do cloth wholesalers need sales tax registration?
Wholesalers above the thresholds generally need to register. Textile sales tax regimes have varied, so we check your products.
I buy from unregistered weavers. Is that a problem?
It's legal, but you don't get input tax credit and there may be extra tax consequences under some rules. Record the purchases.
How do I value unsold cloth?
At cost, counted around 30 June. Write down stock when you sell it at a loss.
