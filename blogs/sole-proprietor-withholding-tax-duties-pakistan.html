Withholding Tax Duties of a Sole Proprietor: When You Must Deduct, Deposit and File Statements

10 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 10 November 2026
Withholding tax is not only something deducted from you. A growing sole proprietor can become legally responsible for deducting it from others.

Many business owners know withholding tax only as the amount banks or customers deduct from them. As a business grows, the law turns the owner into a withholding agent too. This guide sets out the exact thresholds in the Income Tax Ordinance, as amended up to 30 June 2026, that make a sole proprietor responsible for deducting tax. Current rates are in our withholding tax guide, linked below.

TL;DR

A sole proprietor must deduct tax when paying salary to employees (Section 149), when paying gross rent of Rs 1.5 million or more in a year (Section 155), and on payments for goods, services and contracts once turnover reaches Rs 100 million in any preceding tax year (Section 153). Tax deducted is reported in quarterly statements under Section 165, due by 20 April, 20 July, 20 October and 20 January. A person who fails to deduct or deposit is personally liable for the tax under Section 161, after being given an opportunity to be heard.

When a Sole Proprietor Becomes a Withholding Agent

PaymentDuty applies whenSection
Salary to employeesYou pay salary to any employee. The duty is on every person responsible for paying salary, with no size threshold.149
Rent of immovable propertyYou, as an individual or AOP, pay gross rent of Rs 1.5 million or more in a year155(3)(vib)
Payments for goods, services and contractsYour turnover was Rs 100 million or more in any preceding tax year153(7)(i)(i)
Same, for buildersYou derive income from construction and sale of buildings153(7)(i)(k)

Other withholding provisions can also apply depending on the payment, such as commission or brokerage. Check each type of payment you make, not only the ones above.

Salary: The Duty Most Owners Miss

Section 149 requires every person responsible for paying salary to deduct tax at the time of payment, at the employee's average rate on their estimated salary income for the year, adjusting for any tax already withheld under other heads and admissible credits. A shop with two salespeople earning below the taxable limit will compute nil, but the employer still needs the calculation and, where tax is due, must deduct and deposit it. Our withholding tax guide gives the current rates.

Rent: The Rs 1.5 Million Threshold

Section 155(3) makes individuals and associations of persons paying gross rent of Rs 1.5 million or more in a year prescribed persons who must deduct tax from rent. That is Rs 125,000 a month on average. A sole proprietor renting a shop, office or warehouse above that level deducts tax from the landlord's rent and deposits it.

Section 153: The Rs 100 Million Turnover Test

An individual with turnover of Rs 100 million or more in any of the preceding tax years is a "prescribed person" under Section 153(7). From then on, payments to suppliers for goods, to service providers and on contracts are subject to deduction at the rates in the Ordinance. The words "any of the preceding tax years" matter: one year above the threshold is enough, even if turnover falls later.

Depositing Tax and Filing Statements

Tax deducted is deposited through a payment slip (PSID) generated on IRIS, within the time set by the Income Tax Rules. Section 165 then requires a quarterly statement of tax deducted, with each payee's name, CNIC or NTN, address and amounts:

Quarter endingStatement due by
31 March20 April
30 June20 July
30 September20 October
31 December20 January

Salary withholding also appears in the annual statement of salaries paid. Give employees and suppliers the deduction certificates they need for their own returns.

What Happens If You Do Not Deduct

Under Section 161, a person who fails to deduct tax as required, or deducts it but does not pay it to FBR, is personally liable to pay the amount of tax. Section 161(1A) requires that the person first be given an opportunity of being heard. Default surcharge and penalties can follow, and expenses paid without required deduction can be challenged when your own return is assessed. Worked Example

A wholesaler in Faisalabad run as a sole proprietorship had turnover of Rs 115 million in tax year 2025, pays Rs 160,000 a month for a warehouse and employs six staff. From tax year 2026:

  • Section 153 applies to payments to suppliers and service providers, because turnover crossed Rs 100 million in a preceding year.
  • Section 155 applies to the warehouse rent: Rs 1.92 million a year is above Rs 1.5 million.
  • Section 149 applies to the six salaries.
  • Quarterly Section 165 statements are due by the 20th of the month after each quarter.

The figures are illustrative.

Compliance Checklist

Penalties for Late or Missing Statements

Section 182 sets the penalty for failing to file a withholding statement under Section 165 by the due date:

SituationPenalty
Tax was deducted and paid on time, and the statement is filed within 90 days of its due dateRs 50,000
All other late or missing statementsRs 2,500 for each day of default, minimum Rs 10,000
It is established that no tax had to be deducted in the periodMinimum Rs 10,000

These penalties are separate from the Section 161 liability for tax not deducted or not paid. A single missed quarter can therefore cost more than a year of professional help.

Setting Up Supplier Withholding After Crossing Rs 100 Million

  1. Build a supplier list with each supplier's name, CNIC or NTN and whether they supply goods, services or work under a contract.
  2. Check each supplier's status on the Active Taxpayers List before payment, because rates for persons not on the list are higher under the First Schedule.
  3. Apply the right rate at the time of payment and pay the supplier the net amount.
  4. Deposit the tax through a PSID on IRIS within the time set by the rules.
  5. Issue certificates so suppliers can claim the tax in their returns.
  6. File the quarterly statement listing each payee and amount.

Rates change through Finance Acts, so confirm them each year; our withholding rates guide is updated for the current year.

Salary Withholding: A Monthly Routine

  • At the start of the year, estimate each employee's annual salary.
  • Work out the tax for the year at the salary rates, adjust for tax already deducted under other heads and any credits supported by documents, and spread it over the remaining months.
  • Deduct each month's share when paying salary, and recalculate when pay changes.
  • Deposit the tax and include it in the Section 165 statement for the quarter.
  • Give each employee a certificate of tax deducted for their own return.

Rent Example

A sole proprietor rents a showroom for Rs 140,000 a month. Annual rent is Rs 1,680,000, which is above the Rs 1.5 million threshold in Section 155(3), so the tenant is a prescribed person and must deduct tax from each rent payment at the rate applicable to the landlord, deposit it and report it. A tenant paying Rs 100,000 a month (Rs 1.2 million a year) is below the threshold and has no Section 155 duty as an individual. The figures are illustrative.

Certificates Work Both Ways

As your business grows, you will both deduct tax from others and have tax deducted from you. Keep the two separate: tax you deducted belongs in your statements and must be deposited; tax deducted from you is a credit in your own return and, if adjustable, can reduce your advance tax instalments.

Just Realised You Should Have Been Deducting? A 30-Day Plan

  1. Days 1 to 5: list every payment of the last year by type: salaries, rent, suppliers, services, contracts.
  2. Days 6 to 10: mark which ones fell under Sections 149, 153 or 155 based on the thresholds above.
  3. Days 11 to 20: start deducting correctly from the next payment; do not wait for the past to be settled.
  4. Days 21 to 30: take advice on the past periods: what tax was missed, which statements are outstanding, and how to file them.

Correcting the practice quickly matters, because Section 161 liability and Section 182 penalties both grow with each missed period.

The Record Set an Officer Will Ask For

  • Payroll sheets and the tax calculation for each employee.
  • Rent agreements and proof of rent paid.
  • Supplier invoices with the deduction applied, and the supplier's NTN or CNIC.
  • Paid challans (CPRs) for every deposit.
  • Quarterly statements with their acknowledgements.
  • Copies of certificates issued to payees.

Keep this set for six years, as Section 174 requires. Our records guide shows how to organise it.

Why the Payee's Status Matters

The First Schedule usually sets two rates for the same payment: one for persons on the Active Taxpayers List and a higher one for persons who are not. If you apply the lower rate to a supplier who is not on the list, the difference is tax you failed to deduct, and under Section 161 you can be made personally liable for it. A two-minute check of each payee's status before payment, and a note of the date you checked, protects you. Re-check regularly, because the list changes, including after late filers rejoin it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a small shop owner have to deduct tax from staff salaries?
Section 149 puts the duty on every person paying salary. Where an employee's salary is below the taxable limit, the tax works out to nil, but the duty to calculate and, where due, deduct still applies.
At what turnover does a sole proprietor deduct tax from supplier payments?
When the individual's turnover was Rs 100 million or more in any preceding tax year, under Section 153(7).
When must a sole proprietor deduct tax from rent?
When the individual pays gross rent of Rs 1.5 million or more in a year, under Section 155(3).
When are withholding statements due?
Quarterly under Section 165: by 20 April, 20 July, 20 October and 20 January for the quarters ending March, June, September and December.
What if I forgot to deduct tax?
Under Section 161 you can be held personally liable for the tax you should have deducted, after an opportunity of being heard. Correct the practice now and take advice on the past payments.
If my turnover drops below Rs 100 million, does the Section 153 duty end?
The test is turnover of Rs 100 million or more in any of the preceding tax years, so a past year above the threshold can still count.
Where do I find the withholding rates?
In the First Schedule to the Income Tax Ordinance. Our withholding tax guide summarises the current rates.
What is the penalty for a late withholding statement?
Rs 50,000 if the tax was already paid and the statement is filed within 90 days of the due date; otherwise Rs 2,500 per day of default, with a minimum of Rs 10,000, under Section 182.
Do I check my supplier's filer status before deducting tax?
Yes. Rates for persons not on the Active Taxpayers List are higher, so check status at the time of payment.
Is withholding tax my own expense?
No. You deduct it from the payment and deposit it on the payee's behalf. It becomes your liability only if you fail to deduct or deposit it.
Is tax I deducted from my landlord a credit in my own return?
No. Tax you deduct belongs to the payee and is credited in their return. Only tax deducted from you is a credit in yours.
What should I do if I missed deductions in past months?
Start deducting correctly from the next payment, then work out the past periods, missed tax and outstanding statements with advice, because liability and penalties grow with each period.
How long must withholding records be kept?
Six years after the end of the tax year under Section 174, and longer if a proceeding is pending.
What if a supplier was not on the Active Taxpayers List but I deducted at the lower rate?
The difference is tax you failed to deduct, for which you can be held personally liable under Section 161. Check status before each payment and keep a record of the check.
Do I deduct tax when paying a small supplier in cash?
If you are a prescribed person under Section 153, the duty depends on the type of payment and the thresholds and exclusions in the law, not on whether you pay in cash or by bank. Record the deduction either way.
