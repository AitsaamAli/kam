Foreign Assets Left Out of Your Wealth Statement: Section 116, the 116A Statement and the Revision

24 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 24 November 2026
Foreign assets carry their own statement, their own penalty and their own timing rule. Disclosing them first is far cheaper than being found.

Many Pakistani families now hold something abroad: a savings account opened while working in the Gulf, a flat in Dubai bought with remittances, shares bought through a foreign broker, money kept abroad for a child's studies. A resident taxpayer is expected to show all of it. The common mistake is to treat the wealth statement as a Pakistan-only list. This guide sets out the rules for foreign assets in the Ordinance and the revision that brings a statement into line.

Short answer

Since the Finance Act 2024, section 116(1) expressly includes foreign assets and foreign liabilities in a wealth statement. A resident individual with foreign income of at least USD 10,000 or foreign assets worth at least USD 100,000 must also file a foreign income and assets statement under section 116A; the penalty for not doing so is 2% of the foreign income or of the value of the foreign assets for each year of default, and failing to comply with a 116A(2) notice is an offence under section 195A. Under section 111(2) unexplained foreign assets are taxed in the year before FBR discovers them. A resident who left a foreign account or property out can revise under section 116(3) before any 122(9) notice and within five years.

Resident or Non-Resident: Start Here

Short answer: Residents. Section 116(2) applies to resident individuals filing a return, and section 116A to resident individuals above its thresholds.

The wealth statement and the foreign assets statement are obligations of residents. Section 116(2) requires “every resident taxpayer being an individual filing a return of income” to furnish a wealth statement and reconciliation; section 116A applies to “every resident taxpayer being an individual” above its thresholds. Under section 82, an individual is resident if in Pakistan for 183 days or more in the tax year, or, for a citizen, if not present in any other country for more than 182 days in the year or not a resident taxpayer of another country. If you have lived abroad, establish your status for each year first; it decides which rules apply.

What Section 116 Requires

The Finance Act 2024 rewrote section 116(1) to refer to “assets including foreign assets and liabilities including foreign liabilities”, for you and for your spouse, minor children and other dependents, and added “including foreign assets” to the assets you transferred. A foreign savings account, a property abroad, foreign shares or a loan from a foreign bank all belong in the wealth statement of a resident, converted into rupees.

The Separate Foreign Income and Assets Statement

Short answer: Foreign income of USD 10,000 or more, or foreign assets of USD 100,000 or more, requires a separate foreign income and assets statement.

Section 116A, inserted by the Finance Act 2018, applies to a resident individual “having foreign income of not less than ten thousand United States dollars or having foreign assets with a value of not less than one hundred thousand United States dollars”. The statement gives:

ClauseParticulars
116A(1)(a)Total foreign assets and liabilities as on the last day of the tax year
116A(1)(b)Foreign assets transferred to any other person during the year, and the consideration
116A(1)(c)Complete particulars of foreign income and the expenditure incurred to earn it

Under section 116A(2), the Commissioner may serve a notice on a person he believes should have filed the statement but did not.

Penalties and Timing

Short answer: Section 182, row 1AAA: 2% of the foreign income or value of foreign assets for each year of default. Section 195A makes ignoring a 116A(2) notice an offence.

ProvisionWhat it says
Section 182, row 1AAAFailure to furnish the foreign assets and income statement by the due date: penalty of 2% of the foreign income or value of the foreign assets for each year of default
Section 182, row 1AAFailure to furnish a wealth statement or wealth reconciliation statement: higher of 0.1% of taxable income per week or Rs 100,000
Section 195ANon-compliance with a notice under section 116A(2) is an offence punishable on conviction
Section 111(2)(ii)Unexplained investment, money or valuable article situated outside Pakistan is taxed in the tax year immediately preceding the year of discovery

The Explanation to section 111(2) adds a protection: where a foreign asset acquired in an earlier year is discovered and you explain its acquisition from sources of the year it was acquired, that explanation is not to be rejected on the ground that the source does not relate to the year in which the amount is charged. Keeping records of how the asset was funded, years of salary abroad, remittances, a sale, is what makes that protection work.

How to Revise

Short answer: Revise the wealth statement under section 116(3) with a reconciliation explaining how the asset was funded, and file any 116A statement due.

  1. List and value. Every foreign account, property and investment, with the value on 30 June of each year and the rupee conversion you use.
  2. Trace the funding. For each asset, the source: foreign salary taxed abroad, remittances, a sale in Pakistan, a gift from a relative. See gifts and loans.
  3. Revise under section 116(3). Revised wealth statement, revised reconciliation and written reasons, under intimation to the Commissioner, before any 122(9) notice for that year and within five years of its return due date.
  4. File section 116A where due. If a year's foreign income or assets cross the thresholds, the separate statement is needed too.
  5. Revise income if needed. Foreign income taxable for a resident and left out of the return calls for a revised return under section 114(6).

Example: A Dubai Account and Flat

Kamran worked in Dubai until 2022 and has been resident in Lahore since. He holds a Dubai savings account of AED 90,000 and a studio flat bought in 2021 for AED 600,000, rented out. None of it is in his TY2025 or TY2026 statements.

Because the flat was bought from salary earned while Kamran was non-resident, the reconciliation explains it. Disclosing it himself, before any notice, avoids a later section 111(2) assessment and limits his exposure to the penalty rows that apply to the missed statements.

Foreign Assets and Visa Files

A foreign account sometimes surfaces in a visa application before FBR ever hears of it, for example a statement from a Gulf bank shown to a UK or Schengen consulate. If the Pakistani wealth statement attached to the same file does not include it, the two documents contradict each other. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to show my foreign bank account in my wealth statement?
Yes, if you are a resident. Section 116(1), as amended in 2024, expressly includes foreign assets and foreign liabilities.
When is the section 116A statement required?
For a resident individual with foreign income of at least USD 10,000 or foreign assets worth at least USD 100,000.
What is the penalty for not filing the 116A statement?
Section 182 row 1AAA: 2% of the foreign income or value of the foreign assets for each year of default. Not complying with a 116A(2) notice is an offence under section 195A.
In which year is an undisclosed foreign asset taxed?
Under section 111(2)(ii), in the tax year immediately before the year FBR discovers it, unless its source is adequately explained.
I bought the asset while living abroad. Is that a defence?
It is the explanation the law expects. The Explanation to section 111(2) says an explanation from sources of the acquisition year is not rejected only because it relates to an earlier year. Keep the records.
Do non-residents file a wealth statement?
Section 116(2) and section 116A apply to residents. Check your residence under section 82 for each year first.
