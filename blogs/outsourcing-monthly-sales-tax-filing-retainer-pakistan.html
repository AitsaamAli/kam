Outsourcing Monthly Sales Tax Filing — What a Retainer Actually Covers

September 2026 Kamboh Associates 10 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist

Twelve deadlines a year, each requiring invoice-level detail, input tax review, and careful reconciliation, is enough recurring work that most STRN holders eventually decide to outsource it rather than manage it internally month after month. This guide explains exactly what a proper monthly sales tax retainer covers, so you know what you are actually paying for.

TL;DR

A monthly sales tax filing retainer typically covers collecting and reviewing sales and purchase invoices each month, preparing the required annexures, computing input and output tax including any withholding credits, filing the return before the deadline, and keeping a running reconciliation against your annual income tax position. The value is in the consistency across all twelve months of the year, not just any single month's filing — a business that outsources this well should notice the monthly task essentially disappear from their own workload.

What the Service Actually Includes, Month by Month

  • Collecting sales and purchase invoices for the period, either through direct access to your records or a simple monthly submission process from your side.
  • Reviewing input tax claims for documentation adequacy and admissibility before they are included in the return.
  • Preparing the required annexures accurately from the underlying invoice-level detail.
  • Tracking any sales tax withheld by buyers and making sure the corresponding credit is properly claimed.
  • Computing the net position — payable, refundable, or carried forward — and filing before the statutory due date.
  • Maintaining a running reconciliation against your annual income tax figures, so year-end preparation is not a separate reconstruction exercise.

What You Actually Need to Provide Each Month

The retainer reduces your monthly burden, but it does not eliminate the need for basic, timely record-keeping on your side — sales invoices as they are issued, purchase invoices and receipts as they come in, and prompt notice of anything unusual (a large one-off transaction, a new type of supply, a new category of buyer). The quality and timeliness of what you provide directly affects how smoothly the monthly cycle runs on our end.

Why the Real Value Is Consistency, Not Any Single Month

Any individual month's sales tax return is not, on its own, especially complicated. The real value of a standing retainer shows up in the consistency across all twelve months — the same discipline applied every month, no month skipped or rushed because of a busy period elsewhere in the business, and a continuous, connected record that makes year-end reconciliation and any future review far more straightforward than twelve disconnected, ad-hoc filings would produce.

When Outsourcing This Makes the Most Sense

This tends to make the most sense for businesses without a dedicated, experienced internal accounts function, businesses with meaningful transaction volume where invoice-level review genuinely takes real time each month, and businesses that deal with more complex scenarios — mixed taxable/exempt supplies, withholding-category buyers, or import activity — where getting the calculation wrong has a real cost. A very small, simple-structure business with minimal transactions may find the internal effort manageable, but even then, a periodic professional review is worth considering.

Bundling With Other Recurring Compliance

Monthly sales tax filing is frequently bundled with other recurring obligations — quarterly advance tax, monthly withholding statement filing where applicable — into one combined retainer, since the same underlying financial records typically support all of these filings. Businesses managing several recurring compliance obligations at once generally find a single, coordinated retainer more efficient than separate, uncoordinated arrangements for each one.

Starting Outsourced Filing Partway Through a Year

You do not need to wait for a clean calendar break to start outsourcing this — most businesses that switch do so partway through a tax year, often right after a particularly stressful month made the case for change obvious. Onboarding partway through simply means the first task is reviewing what has already been filed for the current year to establish an accurate starting position, including any carried-forward input tax balance, before taking over the remaining months on a clean, connected basis going forward.

How to Tell Whether the Outsourcing Arrangement Is Actually Working Well

Beyond simply "the returns get filed," a well-functioning arrangement should show up in a few concrete ways: no missed deadlines across a full year, input tax claims that hold up cleanly on any cross-verification, a turnover figure that reconciles sensibly with your annual income tax return, and — perhaps most tellingly — the monthly task genuinely disappearing from your own list of things to worry about, rather than simply shifting from "doing it yourself" to "chasing someone else to do it."

Questions Worth Asking Before Committing to a Provider

Before signing up with any provider for this kind of ongoing retainer, it is reasonable to ask specifically how they handle a missed piece of information from your side, how quickly they flag a potential input tax issue before it becomes a filing problem, whether they proactively check for connections to your other recurring obligations like advance tax, and what happens if a mistake is later discovered in a return they filed. A provider who can answer these clearly and specifically, rather than only in general reassurances, is generally a better sign than pricing alone.

What a Good Handover Actually Looks Like

When outsourcing this for the first time, a proper handover should include reviewing at least the last several months of your filing history to catch any existing gap or inconsistency before it becomes the new provider's ongoing problem, establishing accurate carried-forward input tax and any other running balances, and agreeing on exactly what information you will provide each month and by when. A rushed handover that skips this review, jumping straight into "just send us next month's invoices," is more likely to inherit an existing problem unknowingly than one that starts with a proper look at where things actually stand.

Why This Tends to Be a Longer-Term Relationship Than a One-Off Service

Unlike a one-time service engaged for a specific task, a monthly sales tax retainer is inherently a longer-term working relationship, since it recurs every month indefinitely for as long as the STRN remains active. This has a practical benefit worth noting: a provider who has handled your filings for several years develops a genuinely deep familiarity with your specific business patterns, seasonal quirks, and typical transaction types, which makes them faster and more accurate at spotting when something looks unusual for your business specifically, compared to a provider seeing your records for the very first time each engagement.
