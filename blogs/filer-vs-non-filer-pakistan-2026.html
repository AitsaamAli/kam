TL;DR — Key Takeaways
  • Filers pay WHT at half the rate of non-filers on most transactions — property, vehicles, bank profits, services
  • Non-filers face higher WHT on 236A (property auctions), 236B (vehicle purchase), 236C (property disposal), and bank profit under Section 7B
  • ATL (Active Taxpayer List) appearance requires filing an income tax return — a simple return is sufficient
  • Becoming a filer takes 24–48 hours via IRIS after NTN registration and return submission
  • Non-filer WHT is final — filers can adjust WHT against actual tax liability and claim refunds

Pakistan's tax system creates a two-tier structure for taxpayers: those who file income tax returns (filers, also called active taxpayers) and those who do not (non-filers). The difference between these two statuses affects almost every significant financial transaction — property purchase, vehicle buying, bank profit, services payments, and dividend income. Non-filers pay significantly higher withholding tax rates on these transactions, and in many cases the WHT is a final tax with no ability to claim a refund.

Understanding the filer versus non-filer distinction is essential for every working adult in Pakistan in 2026. This guide covers every major WHT rate difference and explains how to transition from non-filer to filer status.

The Real Cost of Being a Non-Filer in 2026

The WHT rate differences add up significantly over a year. Consider a professional earning Rs. 3 million annually:

  • Bank profit on Rs. 2M savings at 20% return: Filer pays Rs. 60,000 WHT (15% of Rs. 400K profit); non-filer pays Rs. 120,000 (30%) — Rs. 60,000 extra
  • Buying a 1300cc car (Rs. 4 million): Filer pays Rs. 40,000 (1%); non-filer pays Rs. 120,000 (3%) — Rs. 80,000 extra
  • Selling property (Rs. 10 million value): Filer pays Rs. 100,000 (1% Section 236C); non-filer pays Rs. 200,000 (2%) — Rs. 100,000 extra
  • Cash withdrawals (above threshold): Filer pays zero; non-filer pays 0.6% on every large cash withdrawal

The total annual extra cost of being a non-filer can easily exceed Rs. 200,000–500,000 for a middle-class professional managing savings, making purchases, and receiving dividends. Against this, becoming a filer costs nothing but your time (or a small consultant fee of Rs. 2,500–5,000 for the first return).

Additional Restrictions on Non-Filers

Beyond higher WHT rates, non-filers face operational restrictions under Finance Act provisions:

  • Property purchase restriction: Non-filers cannot purchase property above Rs. 5 million (the threshold may be adjusted in Finance Act 2026 — verify current limit at FBR)
  • Vehicle registration: Non-filer vehicles registered above 1300cc may face additional advance tax obligations
  • Commercial import: Non-filers face higher WHT at import stage under Section 148
  • Franchises and government contracts: Many government tenders and contracts now require ATL verification — non-filers cannot participate
  • No tax refund eligibility: Non-filers cannot claim refunds for overpaid WHT — the WHT is a final tax for non-filers in most categories

How to Become a Filer in 2026 — Step by Step

  1. Register your NTN: Go to IRIS (iris.fbr.gov.pk), click "Registration for Unregistered Person," and complete the NTN application with your CNIC, mobile number, and email
  2. Create your IRIS account: After NTN registration confirmation, set up your IRIS login password
  3. File your income tax return: File your most recent tax year return (TY2026 = July 2025 to June 2026) declaring all income sources
  4. Verify ATL appearance: Check atl.fbr.gov.pk within 48 hours of filing. Enter your CNIC to confirm active filer status
  5. Inform your bank and employer: Once on ATL, inform your employer (for WHT at source correction under Section 149) and your bank (for reduced WHT on savings profit under Section 7B/151)
ATL Deadline: FBR updates the Active Taxpayer List annually. To appear on the ATL for 2026 (and get reduced WHT rates for the 2026–27 period), you must file your TY2025 return by the deadline. Returns filed late still appear on ATL but with a delay — late filers appear on the next update cycle, not immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

If I file my return today, when will I appear on the ATL?

FBR typically updates the ATL within 24–72 hours of return filing for returns filed through IRIS. Some cases may take up to one week during peak filing season (September). You can monitor your status at atl.fbr.gov.pk by entering your CNIC. Once you appear, your WHT rates are reduced for all future transactions for that tax year.

Can I get a refund of the extra WHT I paid as a non-filer?

No. WHT deducted at non-filer rates before you become a filer is treated as a final tax for that period. Once you become a filer for the next tax year, future transactions are subject to filer rates. You cannot retroactively reclaim the difference in WHT paid in prior periods when you were a non-filer. This is why becoming a filer as early as possible saves more money over time.

Do I have to file even if I have no taxable income?

Yes, if you want ATL benefits. Even if your income is below the taxable threshold (Rs. 600,000 for individuals under TY2026), filing a nil or low-income return is required to appear on the ATL. Filing a return with zero or minimal tax payable takes about 30 minutes on IRIS and costs nothing. The ATL benefits — reduced WHT on bank profits, vehicles, property — are worth far more than the time cost of filing.

Does being a filer reduce WHT on all bank transactions?

Filer status reduces WHT on bank profit (Section 7B / 151) from 30% to 15%. The Section 231A cash withdrawal tax (0.6%) applies only to non-filers. However, not all bank WHT is affected by filer status — for example, WHT on foreign remittances (Section 6) at 1% applies equally to filers and non-filers for amounts received through banking channels, though remittances declared in IRIS can be offset against income tax liability for filers.

What is the difference between ATL filer and active filer?

ATL filer and active filer mean the same thing — a person whose name appears on FBR's Active Taxpayer List (ATL) because they have filed their income tax return for the most recent tax year. "Filer" in WHT context specifically refers to ATL status. A person who has an NTN but has not filed a return is not an ATL filer and faces non-filer WHT rates.

My employer deducts WHT from my salary — am I automatically a filer?

No. Employer WHT deduction under Section 149 does not make you a filer for ATL purposes. Your employer deducts WHT on behalf of FBR, but you still need to file your own income tax return on IRIS to appear on the ATL. Many salaried employees do not realize this and miss out on ATL benefits for property, vehicle, and bank transactions. File your salaried return on IRIS — it typically takes under one hour if your employer provides a salary certificate or Form 12.
