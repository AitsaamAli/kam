Visa Refused Over Funds or Their Source? Correcting Your Wealth Statement Before You Reapply

24 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 24 November 2026
A second application with the same unexplained numbers is likely to meet the same doubt. Fix the record first, then explain the change.

Refusal letters vary by country, but a common thread in finance-related refusals is doubt: about whether the money is really yours, whether it was earned or received lawfully, or whether it was placed in the account only for the application. A revised wealth statement cannot argue with an embassy. What it can do is remove a genuine contradiction between the FBR record and the bank papers, so the next application tells one consistent story. This guide shows how to work through the refusal, line by line.

Short answer

If a visa was refused because the officer was not satisfied about your funds or where they came from, compare the refusal reasons with your FBR wealth statement before reapplying. Correct genuine errors under section 116(3), with a revised reconciliation and written reasons, as long as no section 122(9) notice has been received for that year and five years have not passed since its return due date. The embassy may still hold the earlier papers, so explain the revision in your cover letter. Never correct upwards to reach a number: section 111 taxes money that cannot be explained, and FBR can declare a revision void if it is not bona fide.

Step 1: Read the Refusal for Its Financial Reasons

Short answer: List each financial reason in the letter and find the document it refers to: a balance, a deposit, a salary, a property.

Separate the reasons in the letter into two groups. Some concern your purpose and ties: your job, your family, your travel history. A wealth statement does not change those. Others concern money: the balance was too low for the stay, a large deposit was unexplained, income on paper did not match the account, or the officer could not see where savings came from. Those are the reasons the FBR record can help with.

The official guidance explains why the source matters. The UK guide asks for “bank statements which detail the origin of the funds held” and says a decision maker may ask for additional information. Canada asks for at least six months of account history. The Schengen rules ask for sufficient means for the length of the stay. Each is a test of believability, not only of amount.

Short answer: For each reason, check what the wealth statement and return say about the same money on the same date.

If the FBR record already agrees with the bank papers, there may be nothing to revise. The fix then lies in better documents for the embassy, not in the wealth statement.

Step 3: Correct Only What Is Genuinely Wrong

Short answer: Correct only real errors under section 116(3), with documents. Leave true figures alone even if they are smaller than you would like.

Section 116(3) of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 allows a revised wealth statement when you discover “any omission or wrong statement”. It must be filed with a revised wealth reconciliation and the reasons, under intimation to the Commissioner, before you receive a notice under section 122(9) for that tax year, and never more than five years after the return due date for that year.

The proviso is the reason to be strict with yourself: the Commissioner may declare a revision void, after a hearing, if it “is not for the purpose of correcting a bona fide omission or wrong statement”. And section 111 adds to income the value of money, investments or valuable articles whose source is not adequately explained. A refusal is a strong temptation to show more. The law turns that into tax.

The most common genuine corrections after a refusal are covered in detail elsewhere in this series: omitted bank accounts, gifts from relatives, property sold or bought and guessed cash figures.

Check the Other Statements in the Family

A refusal sometimes turns on someone else's papers: a spouse whose assets were cited as ties, a parent named as sponsor, a brother who sent the money. Each has their own return and wealth statement, and each should tell the same story about the same money. A gift that appears in your reconciliation should also leave the giver's statement, under section 116(1)(c), as an asset transferred. Reviewing the whole family's records together, before reapplying, removes contradictions that no single revision can.

Step 4: Explain the Change

Short answer: The officer may compare old and new papers. A short cover note naming each revision and its evidence answers that.

An embassy that refused you may keep the papers from the first application. If the new wealth statement differs, the difference itself needs an explanation. A short cover note works best:

  • the tax year revised and the date of the revised IRIS acknowledgement;
  • each line changed, the old and new figure and the document behind it;
  • a sentence on why it was wrong, for example “the savings account opened in March 2026 was omitted in error”.

Keep the tone factual. The purpose is to show that the record was corrected because it was wrong, not because the first application failed.

Example: A Deposit Without a Recorded Source

Bilal, a Gujranwala shopkeeper, was refused a visit visa. The officer noted a Rs 1,800,000 credit two months before the application and a declared income too low to explain the balance. The credit was genuine: Bilal's mother had sold gold she inherited and transferred the money to him by bank. His wealth statement recorded neither the gift nor the higher balance.

When Not to Revise

  • When the figures are already true. A smaller, consistent statement is better than a larger, revised one without evidence.
  • When you have received a section 122(9) notice for that year. Revision is no longer open; respond to the notice instead, with our notice defence service.
  • When the money was borrowed only for the application. Show the loan as a liability or do not rely on the balance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I revise my wealth statement after a visa refusal?
Only if the refusal points to money that your FBR record shows wrongly or not at all. If your figures are already true, improve the evidence for the embassy instead.
Will the embassy notice that my wealth statement changed?
It may, if it kept your earlier papers. Explain each change in a short cover note with the document behind it.
Can I show a higher bank balance after a refusal?
Only a balance that is genuinely yours on the date shown. Money you cannot explain is taxed under section 111, and FBR can declare a non-bona fide revision void.
Is there a time limit on revising?
Yes. Before a section 122(9) notice for that year, and within five years of that year's return due date.
Does revising guarantee a visa?
No. A visa decision rests with the embassy. Revising removes contradictions in your FBR record; it cannot change ties, purpose or travel history.
What if my sponsor's papers caused the refusal?
Then the sponsor's return and wealth statement are the ones to review and, if wrong, revise.
