Cash Withdrawal Tax in Pakistan 2026: The 0.8% Charge on Non-Filers Under Section 231AB, and How to Stop It

20 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 10 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 20 November 2026
If you are on the Active Taxpayers List, there is no tax on cash withdrawals. If you are not, every day above Rs 50,000 costs 0.8%.

'Is there tax on cash withdrawal?' is one of the most searched tax questions in Pakistan, and the answer changed several times in the last few years. Here is the position as it stands in the Ordinance amended up to 30 June 2026, with the history that explains the confusion.

TL;DR

Section 231AB of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 (inserted by the Finance Act 2023; rate raised from 0.6% to 0.8% by the Finance Act 2025) requires every banking company to deduct advance adjustable tax at 0.8% of cash withdrawals by a person whose name is not on the Active Taxpayers List, where the total cash withdrawn in a day exceeds Rs 50,000. The limit is the aggregate of all cash withdrawals in a single day. People on the ATL pay no cash withdrawal tax. The 100% increase in the Tenth Schedule does not apply to this deduction (rule 10(ga)). The tax is adjustable: a non-filer recovers it only by filing and claiming it as a credit, and refunds are issued only once on the ATL. The older cash withdrawal and banking transaction taxes in Sections 231A, 231AA and 236P were omitted by the Finance Act 2021.

What Section 231AB Says

Short answer: Banks deduct 0.8% advance tax when a person off the ATL withdraws more than Rs 50,000 cash in a day (Section 231AB).

Every banking company shall deduct advance adjustable tax at the rate of 0.8% of the cash withdrawal from a person whose name is not appearing in the Active Taxpayers List, on the sum total of the payments for cash withdrawal in a day exceeding fifty thousand rupees. The Explanation adds that the Rs 50,000 is the aggregate of cash withdrawals in a single day.

PointRule
Who deductsThe bank
Who paysOnly a person not on the Active Taxpayers List
Rate0.8% of the cash withdrawn (Finance Act 2025; previously 0.6%)
WhenWhere total cash withdrawals in a day exceed Rs 50,000
NatureAdvance adjustable tax, credited in the return
Tenth Schedule doublingDoes not apply (Tenth Schedule rule 10(ga))

Do Filers Pay Cash Withdrawal Tax?

Short answer: No. People on the Active Taxpayers List pay no cash withdrawal tax.

No. Section 231AB applies only to persons whose names are not on the ATL. A person on the ATL can withdraw any amount of cash without this deduction. That is the whole difference, which is why getting on the list matters even for people with little or no taxable income.

Why People Are Confused: The History

Short answer: Sections 231A, 231AA and 236P were omitted in 2021; Section 231AB brought the tax back in 2023 for non-filers only.

ProvisionWhat it didStatus

Section 231AATax on banking instruments and transfers against cashOmitted by the Finance Act 2021

So for two years there was no cash withdrawal tax at all, then it returned for non-filers only. Articles written in that gap still circulate.

Worked Examples

Short answer: A non-filer withdrawing Rs 1,000,000 in a day pays Rs 8,000; a filer pays nothing.

SituationTax deducted
Filer withdraws Rs 500,000 in a dayNil
Non-filer withdraws Rs 40,000 in a dayNil (not above Rs 50,000)
Non-filer withdraws Rs 30,000 twice on the same day (Rs 60,000)0.8% × Rs 60,000 = Rs 480
Non-filer withdraws Rs 1,000,000 in a day0.8% × Rs 1,000,000 = Rs 8,000

A trader off the ATL who withdraws Rs 1 million in cash every week pays about Rs 8,000 a week, more than Rs 400,000 over a year, all for want of a filed return.

Recovering the Tax

Short answer: Claim it as a credit in your return; refunds are issued only once you are on the ATL (Section 182A).

Because it is adjustable, tax deducted under Section 231AB is credited against your tax for the year under Section 168 when you file. If your total tax is lower than the credits, the excess is refundable, but Section 182A(1)(c) says no refund is issued while you are not on the ATL. The practical order is: file the return and wealth statement, join the ATL, then claim. If your return is late, an individual can join without the Rs 25,000 surcharge through Form ATL-U.

Other Banking Taxes to Know

Short answer: Bank profit is taxed at 20% for filers and 40% for others; the Section 114C cash cap applies only once notified.

  • Bank profit: 20% for people on the ATL, 40% for others. See the bank profit tax calculator.
  • Online transfers and cheques: the older taxes on these were omitted in 2021; we found no current equivalent in the Ordinance.
  • Section 114C(1)(d), a separate cap: the Fifteenth Schedule sets an annual cash withdrawal limit of Rs 100 million across all bank accounts held by an individual. Unlike the rest of Section 114C it covers any person, filer or not, but it applies only from a date the Federal Government notifies in the official Gazette (Section 114C(5)). See our Section 114C guide.

How to Stop the Deduction

Short answer: Check your ATL status, file the return and wealth statement, and use the surcharge or Form ATL-U route if late.

  1. Check your ATL status with the online or SMS check.
  2. If you are not on it, file the latest return and wealth statement.
  3. If the return is late, use the surcharge or Form ATL-U route.
  4. After FBR's next ATL update, your bank applies the filer position automatically at the next withdrawal.

What It Means for Businesses and Traders

Short answer: A business off the ATL drawing Rs 250,000 a day pays about Rs 600,000 a year in cash withdrawal tax.

Section 231AB applies to any "person" off the ATL, so it reaches individuals, associations of persons and companies alike. For a shop or distributor that draws cash daily to pay suppliers and staff, the cost compounds quickly:

How the Bank Applies It

Short answer: The bank must deduct the tax and relies on FBR's Active Taxpayers List to decide who is a filer.

The deduction is the bank's legal duty, so a branch cannot waive it on request. Banks rely on FBR's Active Taxpayers List to decide who is a filer, and the list is updated by FBR, not by the bank. If you have just filed, keep the acknowledgement and check your status before a large withdrawal. Tax a bank deducts from you appears in your IRIS records and on the bank's tax certificate, which you use to claim the credit.

What Not to Do

Short answer: Do not route cash through relatives or file a false nil return to avoid the 0.8%.

  • Do not move business cash through relatives' accounts to avoid the deduction; it creates wealth statement gaps for everyone involved.
  • Do not assume an old article about "0.6%" is current; the Finance Act 2025 raised the rate to 0.8%.
  • Do not file a nil return just to get on the ATL if you have income; that trades a 0.8% deduction for a concealment problem. See avoidance vs evasion.

Overseas Pakistanis and Non-Residents

Short answer: Non-residents are not exempt: anyone off the ATL pays 0.8% above Rs 50,000 a day.

Section 231AB does not exempt non-residents. The test is only whether the person's name appears on the Active Taxpayers List. An overseas Pakistani who keeps a resident account in Pakistan, is not on the ATL, and withdraws more than Rs 50,000 in cash in a day will have 0.8% deducted. Many overseas Pakistanis file a return precisely to stay on the ATL; our published fee for an overseas Pakistani return is Rs 6,000. Note that Section 114C, if and when notified, treats non-residents differently: they are outside its vehicle, property and investment restrictions but not its cash withdrawal cap.

Work It Out Yourself

Short answer: Tax = the day's total cash withdrawn × 0.8%, once the total exceeds Rs 50,000 and you are off the ATL.

Tax = total cash withdrawn in the day × 0.8%, if the day's total exceeds Rs 50,000 and you are not on the ATL. The tax is on the whole day's total, not only the part above Rs 50,000: the section applies the rate to "the cash withdrawal" once the day's sum total crosses the limit. Add up withdrawals from the counter, cheques encashed and ATM withdrawals made in a day when you check.

Over a year, add up the deductions shown on your bank's tax certificate. That total is the credit you claim when you file.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a tax on cash withdrawal in Pakistan in 2026?
Only for people not on the Active Taxpayers List. Section 231AB requires banks to deduct 0.8% where their cash withdrawals in a day exceed Rs 50,000.
Do filers pay cash withdrawal tax?
No. Section 231AB applies only to persons whose names are not on the ATL.
What is the cash withdrawal tax rate for non-filers?
0.8% of the cash withdrawn, since the Finance Act 2025 (previously 0.6%).
Is the Rs 50,000 limit per transaction or per day?
Per day: the Explanation says it is the aggregate of cash withdrawals in a single day.
Does the Tenth Schedule double the cash withdrawal tax?
No. Rule 10(ga) of the Tenth Schedule excludes tax deducted under Section 231AB.
Can I get cash withdrawal tax back?
It is adjustable: claim it in your return. Refunds are issued only once you are on the ATL.
Is there tax on ATM withdrawals?
The same Section 231AB rule applies to cash withdrawals, whatever the channel, for persons off the ATL above Rs 50,000 a day.
Is there tax on online transfers?
The older banking transaction taxes (Sections 231AA and 236P) were omitted in 2021; we found no current equivalent in the Ordinance.
