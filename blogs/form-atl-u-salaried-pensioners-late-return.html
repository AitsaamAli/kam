Late Return for Salaried Employees and Pensioners in 2026: Using Form ATL-U Instead of the Rs 25,000 Surcharge

19 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 19 November 2026
Your tax was deducted from every salary slip, yet a late return still takes you off the ATL. Here is the quickest, cheapest way back.

Salaried people are the largest group of late filers, and usually for innocent reasons: the certificate arrived late, the deadline was extended twice and still slipped past, or they assumed the employer's deduction was enough. The law gives no exemption for good intentions, but it does give a cheaper route back.

Short answer

A salaried employee's tax is deducted by the employer under Section 149, but the return under Section 114 is still required, and a return filed after the due date keeps the person off the Active Taxpayers List under Section 182A(1). For Tax Year 2026 the extended due date was 31 October 2026. Off the ATL, bank profit, dividends, vehicle registration and property transactions attract higher withholding. To join the ATL after a late return, an individual pays a Rs 25,000 surcharge or furnishes Form ATL-U, promising not to obtain property for six months. The Section 182 penalty still applies, with a minimum of Rs 10,000 where at least 75% of income is salary, reduced by 75% if the return is filed by 30 November 2026.

Why You Must File Even Though Tax Was Deducted

Short answer: Employer deduction under Section 149 does not replace the Section 114 return, and only a filed return puts you on the ATL.

Section 149 makes your employer deduct tax from salary every month. That deduction settles most or all of your tax, but it does not file your return. Section 114 still requires a return from every individual whose taxable income is above the exemption, and from others the Ordinance names. Only a filed return puts you on the ATL, and only the ATL gives you the filer rates at the bank and the registrar.

What Happens When a Salaried Return Is Late

Short answer: A late salaried return means no ATL, blocked refunds and a Section 182 penalty with a Rs 10,000 minimum.

ConsequenceSectionEffect on a salaried person
Not on the ATL for that year182A(1)(a)Higher deductions on bank profit, dividends, vehicle tax, property
No refund while off the ATL182A(1)(c)Excess tax deducted by the employer cannot be refunded yet
Late-filing penalty182Minimum Rs 10,000 where 75% or more of income is salary

The minimum penalty for salaried individuals is lower than the Rs 50,000 that applies to others, but it is still real money. Section 182 penalty, as it stands in the Ordinance amended up to 30 June 2026: the higher of 0.1% of tax payable per day or Rs 1,000 per day; minimum Rs 10,000 for an individual with 75% or more of income from salary and Rs 50,000 in other cases; maximum 200% of tax payable; reduced by 75%, 50% or 25% if the return is filed within one, two or three months after the due date.

Pensioners

Short answer: Pensioners mostly need the ATL for the 20% bank-profit rate, and Form ATL-U usually fits them.

A pension is generally part of salary income, and many pensioners also earn profit on savings. For them, the ATL matters mostly at the bank: the 20% deduction on profit under Section 151 doubles to 40% when they are off the list. Our guide on late filer tax on bank profit shows the numbers. Few pensioners are buying property, so Form ATL-U usually fits.

Pensioners should check their wealth statement carefully. Commutation received on retirement, savings certificates and gifts to children all need to appear correctly, and the wealth statement must reconcile with the previous year's.

Documents for a Salaried Late Return

Short answer: Salary certificate, bank tax certificates, asset details at 30 June 2026 and last year's wealth statement.

  • Salary certificate or annual tax deduction certificate from the employer for July 2025 to June 2026.
  • Bank tax deduction certificates for profit, and statements showing year-end balances.
  • Details of any property, vehicle, savings certificates, gold and cash held at 30 June 2026.
  • Utility and mobile phone tax certificates if you want those deductions credited.
  • Last year's wealth statement, if one was filed.

The Steps, in Order

Short answer: Prepare and file the return and wealth statement, furnish Form ATL-U, then check the ATL and tell your bank.

  1. Prepare the Tax Year 2026 return from the certificates.
  2. Prepare the wealth statement and reconcile it with last year's.
  3. File both on IRIS and pay any balance tax.
  4. Furnish Form ATL-U on IRIS and save the acknowledgement. Our IRIS step-by-step guide shows the screens.
  5. Check the ATL after the next update and tell your bank once your name appears.

If Your Employer Deducted Too Much

Short answer: Excess salary tax can be refunded through the return once you are on the ATL.

A refund is possible where deductions exceed your tax, for example because a tax credit was not given through payroll. Section 182A(1)(c) holds refunds while you are off the ATL, so joining the ATL first is the practical step before any refund claim.

Does My Employer Need to Know?

Short answer: Your employer is not told; an ATL screenshot is enough if HR asks.

No. Form ATL-U is between you and FBR. Some employers ask staff for an ATL screenshot each year; once your name appears, a screenshot of the status check is enough.

Common Salaried Mistakes

Short answer: Skipping the wealth statement and declaring a different salary from the certificate are the common ones.

  • Filing the return but skipping the wealth statement.
  • Declaring a different salary from the certificate.
  • Forgetting the bank account where salary is not paid.
  • Assuming the employer's tax deduction makes the return optional.

Changed Jobs During the Year?

Short answer: Two employers mean two certificates; any shortfall in combined tax is paid with the return.

If you worked for two employers between July 2025 and June 2026, you need certificates from both. Each employer deducted tax on its own salary, often without knowing about the other, so the combined tax can be higher than what was deducted. The return brings both together; any shortfall is paid with the return. This is one of the commonest reasons salaried returns get delayed, and it is easy to fix.

Wealth Statement Items Salaried People Forget

Short answer: Second accounts, provident fund, a vehicle, wedding gold and loans to relatives belong in the wealth statement.

  • A second bank account opened for savings or a committee.
  • Provident fund balances, where your employer reports them.
  • A motorcycle or car in your name, even if a family member uses it.
  • Gold received at a wedding.
  • Money lent to a relative, or owed to one.

Each of these should be in the wealth statement at 30 June 2026. Leaving them out now and adding them later needs a revised statement, and our revision guide explains when that is possible.

How the Penalty Works for a Salaried Person

Short answer: Higher of 0.1% of tax payable or Rs 1,000 a day, minimum Rs 10,000, cut by 75% if filed within a month.

Section 182 sets the penalty as the higher of 0.1% of the tax payable for each day of default or Rs 1,000 for each day. For a salaried person with modest tax, the Rs 1,000 a day figure is usually the higher one. A minimum of Rs 10,000 applies where 75% or more of income is from salary, and the maximum is 200% of tax payable. The amount is then reduced by 75% if the return is filed within one month after the due date, 50% within two months and 25% within three.

"Tax payable" is defined in the Explanation to the entry as the higher of the tax on the assessed income for the year or the tax payable for the preceding year for which a return was duly filed. We work the actual figure out from your return and show it to you before filing.

If Your Employer Deducted Too Little

Short answer: If payroll under-deducted, pay the shortfall by PSID and file; delay only adds penalty.

Payroll sometimes under-deducts, for example when bonuses come late in the year or when you had two employers. The return calculates the full tax on the year's salary; any shortfall is paid with the return before filing. Pay it through the PSID, keep the challan, and the return is complete. Underpayment is not a reason to delay: the penalty grows each day the return is late.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the return required if my employer deducted tax?
Yes. Section 149 deduction does not replace the Section 114 return, and only a filed return puts you on the ATL.
What is the late-filing penalty for a salaried person?
The higher of 0.1% of tax payable or Rs 1,000 per day, with a minimum of Rs 10,000 where at least 75% of income is salary, reduced if filed within three months of the due date.
How much does a late salaried return with Form ATL-U cost with you?
Rs 3,500 for the return plus Rs 2,000 for Form ATL-U, Rs 5,500 in total. FBR's penalty, if any, is paid separately.
Can pensioners use Form ATL-U?
Yes. A pensioner is an individual, and the undertaking suits anyone not obtaining property for six months.
Will my employer be told about Form ATL-U?
No. It is furnished on IRIS between you and FBR.
Can I claim a refund of excess salary tax after a late return?
Yes, through the return, but Section 182A(1)(c) blocks refunds while you are off the ATL.
Do I need a wealth statement as a salaried person?
In most cases yes. Section 116 requires it with the return for individuals in the cases it specifies, and it is checked against your records.
When is next year's salaried return due?
30 September 2027 for Tax Year 2027, under Section 118, unless extended.
I changed jobs during the year. Do I need both certificates?
Yes. The return combines salary from both employers; any shortfall in tax is paid with the return.
I am booking a flat soon. Should I use Form ATL-U?
No. Booking is acquiring property. Pay the surcharge so you are on the ATL for the purchase.
Is the Rs 10,000 minimum penalty reduced if I file within a month?
The law reduces the amount of penalty by 75% for a return filed within one month after the due date. We work out the exact figure from your return before filing.
