The 31 October 2026 Tax Return Deadline Has Passed: Your Next Steps

1 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist · Facts checked 1 November 2026
The extended deadline for Tax Year 2026 returns closed at the end of 31 October 2026. You can still file, and the sooner you file, the less a late return costs.

For most individuals and associations of persons, the last date to file the Tax Year 2026 income tax return was 31 October 2026. FBR had already moved it twice, from 30 September to 15 October and then to 31 October. If your return is still not filed, this guide explains exactly where you stand from 1 November, what filing late will cost in rupees, and the order in which to fix it.

TL;DR

FBR's extended deadline for Tax Year 2026 income tax returns ended on 31 October 2026. As of 1 November 2026 FBR had not announced another extension. You can still file through IRIS, but the return now counts as late. That has three practical effects: a Section 182 penalty can be imposed, you stay off the Active Taxpayers List (ATL) until you pay the Section 182A surcharge (Rs 25,000 for individuals), and non-filer withholding rates apply to you in the meantime. Filing quickly limits all three.

What Closed on 31 October 2026

On 15 October 2026 FBR used its power under Section 214A of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001 to extend the filing date to 31 October 2026. The notification covered persons whose returns were due on 30 September 2026 and whose date had already been moved to 15 October. In practice, that means salaried individuals, business individuals, freelancers, landlords and AOPs filing for Tax Year 2026 (the year from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026).

FBR gave two reasons: requests from trade bodies and tax bar associations, and a filing season slowed by new return forms, extra disclosures, wealth statement reconciliation and IRIS problems. Interest was high. Dawn reported 6.71 million returns filed by 14 October, against 4.99 million at the same point a year earlier.

Companies with a 30 June year-end are not part of this deadline. Their return is normally due by 31 December, so this guide does not apply to them.

Can You Still File After 31 October?

Yes. IRIS keeps accepting Tax Year 2026 returns after the due date. A late return is still a valid return: it records your income, the tax already deducted from you, and your wealth statement. What changes is the status attached to it. Your return is now a late return, and three consequences follow.

Two common hopes no longer help. An extension application under Section 119 has to reach the Commissioner before the due date, so it cannot rescue a return that is already late. And waiting for a backdated general extension is a gamble. FBR has not announced one, and every day you wait adds to the penalty formula below.

How the Section 182 Penalty Is Worked Out

The penalty for not filing by the due date appears as entry 1 of the table in Section 182. As published on FBR's website (checked 1 November 2026), it works like this:

  • Daily rate: 0.1% of the tax payable for that tax year, for each day of default.
  • Ceiling: no more than 50% of the tax payable.
  • Floor: if the figure works out below Rs 40,000, or no tax is payable at all, the penalty is Rs 40,000.
  • Salaried relief: if at least 75% of your income is from salary and that salary income is below Rs 5 million, the minimum is Rs 5,000 instead of Rs 40,000.

"Tax payable" here means the tax chargeable on your taxable income for the year. It is not just the balance left after deductions. A salaried person whose employer deducted every rupee still has tax payable for this calculation.

Two points matter more than the arithmetic. First, the penalty is imposed by an order of the tax officer, normally after a notice that gives you a chance to explain. It is not a fee IRIS adds when you press submit. If you had a genuine, documented reason for the delay, that reply is where it belongs, and an order you disagree with can be appealed to the Commissioner (Appeals). Second, the business floor of Rs 40,000 means that for small businesses, a few days of delay already costs as much as several months. Do not let the penalty become an excuse to keep waiting.

Getting Back on the Active Taxpayers List

For most people, ATL status matters more than the penalty, because it sets the tax rate on bank cash withdrawals, property transfers and vehicle registration. Section 182A, as amended by the Finance Act 2026, keeps late filers off the ATL until they pay a surcharge:

For individuals there may be a second route. On 6 October 2026 FBR published draft rules for a Form ATL-U undertaking: you skip the surcharge and in return promise not to acquire any property for six months. At the time of writing, the reports we found describe this as a draft. Check IRIS when you file. We compare both routes in detail in our guide to the ATL surcharge and Form ATL-U.

The surcharge and the Section 182 penalty are separate. Paying one does not settle the other.

A 7-Day Plan to File Your Late Return Properly

  1. Day 1. Check your position. Log in to IRIS. Confirm whether a Tax Year 2026 return is saved as a draft or not started, and note any notices in your inbox. Check your current ATL status.
  2. Day 1–2. Collect documents. Salaried: salary certificate or the employer's annual tax deduction statement. Everyone: bank statements and withholding certificates for 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026. Business: sales, purchase and expense records. Freelancers: remittance records.
  3. Day 2–3. Rebuild the wealth statement. Start from last year's closing figures. Add assets bought and money received, subtract spending, and make sure the reconciliation balances. Most rushed returns go wrong here.
  4. Day 3–4. Pay any balance tax first. If your return shows tax still payable, pay it through a payment slip (PSID) before submitting, so the return is not filed with an unpaid demand.
  5. Day 4–5. Review and submit. Check every figure against the documents, submit the return and wealth statement together, and save the acknowledgement.
  6. Day 5–6. Decide the ATL route. Pay the Section 182A surcharge, or use the undertaking if it is available and fits your plans.
  7. Day 7. Confirm. Check that your ATL status has updated before you rely on it for a transaction. Keep copies of everything in case a penalty notice arrives later.

Mistakes That Make a Late Return Worse

  • Filing a "quick" return with a guessed wealth statement. A rushed reconciliation is one of the most common reasons for later notices. Revising it later is possible, but it is slower than doing it right the first time (see how revised returns work).
  • Leaving out income to keep the tax low. FBR matches returns against bank, property and withholding data. Understating income turns a late-filing problem into a concealment problem.
  • Ignoring a Section 114(4) notice. Once a notice arrives, the response has a deadline of its own. Our Section 114 notice guide explains how to answer one.
  • Assuming the surcharge cancels the penalty. It does not. They are two different provisions with two different purposes.
  • Waiting for a large transaction to force the issue. Discovering at the property registrar that you are off the ATL costs far more than the surcharge.

If You Also Missed Earlier Years

Some people find, while filing Tax Year 2026, that Tax Year 2025 or earlier returns were never filed either. Each missing year is a separate return with its own penalty position. The wealth statements also have to connect year to year. File the oldest missing year first, so each closing wealth figure becomes the next year's opening figure. Filing only the latest year with an opening position that FBR cannot trace is how notices start.

Frequently Asked Questions

Was the Tax Year 2026 return deadline extended beyond 31 October 2026?
As of 1 November 2026, FBR had not announced any extension beyond 31 October 2026. The date had already been moved twice: from 30 September to 15 October, and then to 31 October under Section 214A. Check FBR's official website or X account for any later notification.
Can I still file my income tax return in November 2026?
Yes. IRIS accepts Tax Year 2026 returns after the due date. The return is treated as late, so a Section 182 penalty can be imposed, and you join the Active Taxpayers List only after paying the Section 182A surcharge or using the undertaking route if available.
What is the minimum late filing penalty for a salaried person?
Under Section 182 as published by FBR, the minimum is Rs 5,000 if at least 75% of your income is from salary and that salary income is below Rs 5 million. Otherwise the minimum is Rs 40,000. Above the minimum, the penalty is 0.1% of tax payable per day of delay, capped at 50% of the tax payable.
Does the penalty apply if my employer already deducted all my tax?
It can. The formula uses tax payable, meaning the tax chargeable on your taxable income for the year, not the unpaid balance. The salaried minimum of Rs 5,000 applies to most salaried people below Rs 5 million in salary.
Is the Rs 25,000 ATL surcharge the same as the late filing penalty?
No. The Rs 25,000 surcharge for individuals is paid under Section 182A to be placed on the Active Taxpayers List after a late return. The Section 182 penalty is a separate penalty for the delay itself and is imposed by order.
Can I apply for an extension now that the deadline has passed?
An extension application under Section 119 must be made to the Commissioner before the due date, so it does not help once the return is already late. The practical step now is to file as soon as possible.
Do companies have to file by 31 October 2026?
No. The 31 October 2026 date covered persons whose returns were due on 30 September, mainly individuals and AOPs. Companies with a 30 June year-end normally file by 31 December.
