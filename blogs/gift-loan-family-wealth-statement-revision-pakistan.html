Gifts and Loans From Family Recorded Wrongly in Your Wealth Statement: The Rules and the Correction

24 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 24 November 2026
The same Rs 1,000,000 can be tax-free, taxable income or a liability, depending on who gave it, how, and on what terms. Record it the right way.

Pakistani families move money between each other constantly: a father helps with a house, a brother abroad sends money, a cousin lends for a wedding. On a wealth statement each of these has one correct place. Put a parent's gift into income and you overstate what you earned. Leave a loan out and your assets look unexplained. Accept cash from a friend and section 39(3) may make it income. This guide sets out the rules from the Ordinance and the revision that fixes a wrong entry.

Short answer

Section 39(1)(la) treats property or money received without consideration or as a gift as income from other sources, except a gift from a relative as defined in section 85(5): ancestors, descendants of grandparents, adopted children, and spouses, of you or your spouse. Section 39(3) separately treats a loan, advance, deposit or gift from a non-bank person as income unless it arrives by crossed cheque, banking channel or digital means from a person holding an NTN. So a parent's bank transfer is a gift in the reconciliation, a loan is a liability, and neither is your income. If yours were recorded wrongly, revise under section 116(3) before any 122(9) notice and within five years.

Who Counts as a Relative

Short answer: Section 85(5): ancestors, descendants of the grandparents, adopted children, of you or your spouse, and the spouses of all of them.

Section 85(5)(i) of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, as amended up to 30 June 2026, defines a relative of an individual as:

  • (a) an ancestor, a descendant of any of the grandparents, or an adopted child, of the individual or of the individual's spouse; or
  • (b) a spouse of the individual or of any person in (a).

In practice that covers parents and grandparents, children and grandchildren, brothers and sisters, uncles, aunts and cousins (all descendants of your grandparents), the same people on your spouse's side, and their spouses. A friend, a business partner or an employer is not a relative. Section 39(1)(la) was amended by the Finance Act 2021 to use this definition; it previously listed grandparents, parents, spouse, brother, sister, son or daughter.

When a Gift Is Income

Short answer: A gift from a relative is excluded from section 39(1)(la). A gift from anyone else is income from other sources.

Section 39(1)(la) includes in “Income from Other Sources” any amount or fair market value of property received without consideration or as a gift, other than a gift from a relative as defined in section 85(5). So:

The Banking Channel Rule in Section 39(3)

Short answer: Section 39(3): a non-bank loan, advance, deposit or gift not received by crossed cheque, banking channel or digital means from an NTN holder is income.

Section 39(3) applies to “any amount received as a loan, advance, deposit for issuance of shares or gift” from a person who is not a bank or financial institution. Unless it is received by a crossed cheque drawn on a bank, through a banking channel, or through digital means, from a person holding a National Tax Number, it is treated as income from other sources in the year received. Section 39(4) excludes advance payments for the sale of goods or supply of services.

Read together, the two provisions mean a gift from a relative is safest when it comes by bank transfer from a relative who holds an NTN, and that a loan from anyone should arrive through a bank and be recorded as a liability. Cash handed over at a family gathering is exactly what section 39(3) was written for.

The Giver's Side of the Same Gift

Every gift has two statements. Section 116(1)(c) asks the giver for “any assets including foreign assets transferred” to another person during the period and the consideration for the transfer. A father who transfers Rs 2,000,000 to his son should show it leaving his own statement, as a transfer with no consideration, in the same tax year in which the son records it as received. When only one side records the gift, the father's assets look higher than they are and the son's receipt has no matching source. If you are both giver and receiver in a family, revise both statements together.

Making the Correction

Short answer: Move the gift out of income into the reconciliation, add any loan as a liability, and file the revision with reasons.

Section 116(3) lets you revise a filed wealth statement on discovering an omission or wrong statement: a revised statement, a revised wealth reconciliation and the reasons, under intimation to the Commissioner, before receiving a section 122(9) notice for that year, and not after five years from its return due date. Where the correction changes income, for example a gift wrongly shown as income, the return is also revised under section 114(6), which has its own conditions; see our revised return guide.

Keep the evidence with the revision: bank transfer records, the relative's CNIC and NTN where available, and for a loan, a short written note of the terms and any repayments.

Example: Help With a House

Usman, a Karachi banker, bought a house in TY2026 for Rs 18,000,000. His father paid Rs 5,000,000 by bank transfer and his cousin lent Rs 2,000,000, repayable over three years. Usman's statement showed the house at Rs 11,000,000 (only his own contribution), no loan, and a balancing entry in cash.

Family Money in a Visa File

A recent gift from a parent is one of the most common reasons a Pakistani applicant's balance jumps. Recorded as a gift from a relative, with the transfer behind it, it is an ordinary, explainable source. Unrecorded, it is the “large unexplained deposit” that worries officers. Frequently Asked Questions

Is a gift from my father taxable?
No. Section 39(1)(la) excludes gifts from a relative as defined in section 85(5), which includes parents. Record it as a gift in the reconciliation, ideally received by bank transfer.
Is my cousin a relative for gift purposes?
Yes. Section 85(5) includes descendants of your grandparents, which covers cousins, and the same relatives on your spouse's side.
Is a gift from a friend taxable?
Yes. A gift from someone who is not a relative is income from other sources under section 39(1)(la).
How should a loan from a relative be shown?
As a liability in your wealth statement, received through a bank. Under section 39(3) a non-bank loan not received by crossed cheque, banking channel or digital means from an NTN holder is treated as income.
I showed a gift as income. Can I fix it?
Yes. Revise the wealth statement under section 116(3) and the return under section 114(6), subject to their conditions.
What proof should I keep?
The bank transfer record, the giver's CNIC and NTN where available, and for a loan, its written terms and repayments.
