Zakat Deduction in Pakistan's Income Tax: How Section 60 Reduces Your Taxable Income (2026)

20 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 20 November 2026
Zakat you have paid is not just an obligation met; under the tax law it also reduces the income you are taxed on.

Every Ramadan banks deduct zakat from eligible savings accounts, and many people also pay zakat directly. Few claim it in their tax return, and those who do sometimes claim it in the wrong place. Here is exactly what the law allows, and how much it is worth.

TL;DR

Section 60 of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 (FBR text amended up to 30 June 2026) entitles a person to a deductible allowance for any zakat paid in the tax year under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance, 1980. A deductible allowance reduces taxable income, so the saving equals the zakat multiplied by your marginal tax rate. It does not apply to zakat already taken into account under Section 40(2), it cannot reduce income taxed under the final tax regime (Section 169), and any part that cannot be used in the year is lost: it is not refunded, carried forward or carried back (Section 60(3)). Keep the certificate of zakat deducted or paid.

What Section 60 Says

Short answer: Section 60 allows zakat paid under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980 as a deductible allowance against taxable income.

Section 60(1): a person is entitled to a deductible allowance for the amount of any zakat paid by the person in a tax year under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance, 1980. Section 60(2): this does not apply to zakat taken into account under Section 40(2). Section 60(3): any allowance, or part of an allowance, that cannot be deducted under Section 9 for the year is not refunded, carried forward to a later year or carried back to an earlier one.

Three points follow. The zakat must be paid in the tax year (1 July to 30 June). It must be zakat under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance. And the allowance is use-it-or-lose-it.

An Allowance, Not a Credit

Short answer: The saving is zakat multiplied by your marginal tax rate, because an allowance reduces income, not tax.

A deductible allowance is subtracted from your total income to arrive at taxable income (Section 9). It is different from a tax credit, which is subtracted from the tax itself. The difference matters for how much you save:

Example: if your income falls in a band taxed at 11% at the margin, Rs 100,000 of zakat reduces your taxable income by Rs 100,000 and your tax by about Rs 11,000. In a 25% band, the same zakat saves about Rs 25,000.

The Final-Tax Trap

Short answer: Zakat cannot reduce income taxed under the final tax regime, and unused zakat is lost (Section 60(3)).

Section 169(2)(c)(i) says income taxed under the final tax regime is not reduced by any deductible allowance under Part IX of Chapter III, which is where Section 60 sits. So zakat cannot be claimed against, for example, prize bond winnings or export proceeds taxed finally under Section 154A. If most of your income is final-tax income, much of the zakat allowance may simply have nothing to reduce, and Section 60(3) means the unused part is lost.

Who Benefits Most

Short answer: Salaried people and business owners with normal taxable income gain most; people with only final-tax income gain nothing.

  • Salaried people whose tax is deducted at normal slab rates: zakat lowers taxable income and can produce a refund of salary tax already deducted.
  • Business individuals and sole proprietors taxed at normal rates.
  • Landlords and others with income taxed at normal rates.

It helps least where income is mainly taxed separately or finally, such as profit on debt under Section 7B or export proceeds under Section 154A.

Zakat Deducted by Your Bank

Short answer: Zakat deducted by your bank under the Zakat Ordinance counts; keep the bank's zakat deduction certificate.

Banks deduct zakat from eligible accounts under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance and issue a certificate of the amount. That certificate is the simplest evidence for your claim. If you pay zakat directly, keep a receipt or record that shows it was paid under the Zakat law; payments that do not meet that description are better treated as donations and checked under Section 61.

How to Claim It in IRIS

Short answer: Enter the zakat paid in the deductible allowances part of the IRIS return and keep the certificate.

  1. Get the zakat certificate from each bank, and receipts for any other zakat paid in the tax year.
  2. In the return, enter the amount under the zakat deductible allowance.
  3. Check that the income it reduces is normal taxable income, not final-tax income.
  4. Show the zakat as an outflow in your wealth reconciliation, so the statement balances.

Worked Example

Short answer: Rs 100,000 zakat at an 11% marginal rate saves about Rs 11,000 of income tax.

A salaried teacher has taxable salary of Rs 1.8 million. Her bank deducted Rs 40,000 zakat in Ramadan, and she paid Rs 60,000 directly under the Zakat law with receipts. Total zakat: Rs 100,000. Her taxable income becomes Rs 1.7 million. If her marginal rate is 11%, her tax falls by about Rs 11,000, and because her employer deducted tax on the full salary, the difference becomes a refund once she files and is on the ATL.

Common Mistakes

Short answer: Common mistakes are claiming zakat as a tax credit, claiming it against final-tax income, and not keeping certificates.

  • Claiming zakat paid outside the tax year.
  • Claiming it against final-tax income.
  • Treating general charity as zakat; it may qualify as a donation instead.
  • Not keeping the bank certificate or receipts.

Zakat or Donation: Section 60 or Section 61?

Short answer: Zakat under the Zakat law is a Section 60 allowance; gifts to approved bodies are a Section 61 tax credit. One amount cannot be claimed under both.

Nature of reliefDeductible allowance: reduces taxable incomeTax credit: reduces tax at your average rate
What qualifiesZakat paid under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980Donations to approved bodies and institutions named in the law
EvidenceBank zakat certificate or receipt under the Zakat lawReceipt from the approved recipient
Unused amountLost (Section 60(3))Limited by the Section 61 ceilings

Giving to a mosque, madrasa or relative as zakat is a religious duty, but for the income tax claim the question is whether it was paid under the Zakat law. If it was not, check whether the recipient is an approved body under Section 61; our donations guide explains the credit. Claiming the same amount under both sections is not allowed.

Zakat in Your Wealth Statement

Short answer: Show zakat paid as an outflow in the wealth statement so it reconciles.

Zakat paid is money that leaves your wealth during the year. In the wealth statement it belongs among personal expenses and outflows, so that the opening wealth plus income minus outflows equals the closing wealth. If you leave it out, the statement shows wealth that has gone missing, which is exactly the kind of gap that leads to a Section 111 question about unexplained assets. Our wealth statement guide covers the reconciliation.

Timing Matters

Short answer: Zakat counts in the tax year (1 July to 30 June) in which it was actually paid.

Section 60 allows zakat "paid by the person in the tax year". Banks usually deduct zakat on the first day of Ramadan, so the deduction falls in whichever tax year (1 July to 30 June) contains that date. Zakat paid directly counts in the year you actually paid it. A payment made after 30 June belongs to the next year's return, not the one you are filing.

Business Owners, AOPs and Companies

Short answer: Any person, including an AOP or company, can claim zakat it paid itself under the Zakat law.

Section 60(1) gives the allowance to "a person" for zakat paid "by the person". Under the Ordinance a person includes an individual, an association of persons and a company, so the allowance is not limited to salaried individuals. Two conditions follow from the wording. The zakat must be paid by the same person who claims it: zakat a business owner pays personally is claimed in the owner's return, not the firm's, and zakat paid by a company is claimed by the company. And it must be paid under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980.

Section 60(2) adds one exclusion: zakat already taken into account under Section 40(2), the provision on deductions against income from other sources, cannot be claimed again under Section 60. The same amount is never deducted twice.

Short answer: Confirm it was paid under the Zakat law, in the tax year, by you, against normal taxable income, with a certificate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is zakat deductible from income tax in Pakistan?
Yes. Section 60 gives a deductible allowance for zakat paid in the tax year under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance, 1980.
Does zakat reduce tax or taxable income?
Taxable income. It is a deductible allowance, so the saving equals the zakat times your marginal rate.
Can unused zakat be carried forward?
No. Section 60(3) says any part that cannot be deducted is not refunded, carried forward or carried back.
Can zakat reduce final-tax income?
No. Section 169(2)(c) bars deductible allowances against final-tax income.
What document do I need?
The bank's zakat deduction certificate, or receipts showing zakat paid under the Zakat law.
Is zakat the same as a charitable donation for tax?
No. Zakat is an allowance under Section 60; donations may give a tax credit under Section 61.
Can a salaried person get a refund from zakat?
Yes, if salary tax was deducted on the full salary, the lower taxable income can produce a refund after filing, once you are on the ATL.
Which tax year does bank zakat belong to?
The tax year in which it was deducted; Ramadan deductions fall in the July-June year in which they occur.
