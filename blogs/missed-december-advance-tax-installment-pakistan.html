What Happens If You Miss a December Advance Tax Installment

September 2026 Kamboh Associates 9 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist

December is a busy month for most businesses — year-end closing, holiday-season sales, staff bonuses — and the second advance tax installment of the year can easily slip past unnoticed until it is already overdue. If that has happened to you, the first thing to know is that missing one installment is a fixable, common situation, not a crisis. What matters is understanding exactly what happens next and acting on it quickly.

TL;DR

Missing a December advance tax installment does not cancel the obligation — the amount is still due, and a default surcharge typically starts accruing on the unpaid amount from the point it became overdue. The surcharge builds the longer the payment is delayed, so the single most effective response is to compute and pay the outstanding installment as soon as possible, rather than waiting for the next quarterly date to "catch up together."

What Actually Happens When a Quarter Is Missed

Nothing about the underlying tax liability changes just because a deadline passed. The installment amount was owed based on your estimated income for that quarter, and it remains owed. What changes is that FBR's system now treats the amount as overdue, and a default surcharge typically begins to accrue on it from the date it should have been paid.

This is different from a criminal or prosecution matter — a single missed installment is, in the vast majority of cases, a financial and procedural issue, resolved by paying the amount plus the accrued surcharge.

How the Default Surcharge Builds Over Time

The surcharge on late advance tax payments is calculated with reference to the period the amount remains unpaid — the longer it sits outstanding, the larger the surcharge grows. This is precisely why waiting until the next quarterly installment date to deal with a missed one is usually the wrong instinct: every additional week the December amount sits unpaid adds to the surcharge, and by the time March arrives you may be facing a combined bill that is considerably larger than either installment alone.

The exact surcharge rate and computation method are set by FBR and can change with each Finance Act — we always compute the current, applicable rate for your case rather than relying on a rate quoted in an older article.

Paying a Late Installment — What the Process Looks Like

  • Recalculate the December installment amount based on your actual estimate at that time (not adjusted retroactively for hindsight).
  • Add the applicable default surcharge for the period the amount has been outstanding.
  • Generate a fresh PSID reflecting the combined amount and make the payment.
  • Confirm the payment reflects correctly against the relevant quarter in your IRIS profile.

Does a Missed Installment Affect Your Filer Status

A missed advance tax installment, on its own, is a separate matter from your Active Taxpayer List (ATL) status, which is tied to filing your annual return on time. However, unresolved advance tax issues can complicate your overall compliance picture, and a pattern of repeated missed installments is more likely to draw FBR's attention than a single, promptly corrected one. Clearing the missed December installment before it compounds keeps your overall record clean heading into the March and June installments.

Preventing a Repeat Next December

The businesses that miss the December installment most often are the ones without a standing reminder system — the quarter genuinely gets lost in year-end workload. A simple fix is to set the reminder two to three weeks before the deadline rather than on the deadline itself, giving enough buffer to compute the estimate calmly rather than in a rush at year-end.

Why December Is the Installment Most Often Missed

Of the four quarterly dates, December has a particular pattern working against it. Many businesses are simultaneously closing half-year books, managing holiday-season demand swings, processing year-end staff bonuses, and — for businesses on a calendar financial year separate from the tax year — dealing with their own internal December close as well. Advance tax competes for attention against all of this at once, in a way that the September, March, and June installments generally do not face to the same degree. Recognising this pattern in advance is useful precisely because it means the risk is predictable, not a one-off accident.

A Simple Illustration of How Surcharge Accumulates

To make the "act quickly" advice concrete: imagine an installment that should have been paid in mid-December is instead paid two months later, in mid-February. The surcharge for that period is calculated with reference to the full length of the delay — not just a flat one-time penalty regardless of timing. Waiting a further month, to pay it alongside the March installment instead, extends that same calculation window further still. The number itself depends on the current, applicable rate, which we always confirm at the time of payment rather than quoting a fixed figure that may be out of date by the time you read this — but the direction is always the same: shorter delay, smaller surcharge.

Do You Need to Proactively Notify FBR

In most straightforward cases of a single missed installment that is then paid with the correct surcharge attached, no separate explanatory correspondence to FBR is required — the payment itself, correctly computed, resolves the matter. Proactive communication becomes more relevant if the delay is unusually long, if multiple consecutive installments have been missed, or if FBR has already issued a specific notice or query about the outstanding amount. If any of those apply to your situation, that is the point at which a more formal, documented response becomes worth preparing.

What If More Than One Quarter Has Been Missed

If December was missed and, looking back, an earlier quarter was too, resist the urge to treat this as one combined problem to solve later. Each missed installment has its own surcharge clock running from its own original due date, and the earliest missed quarter is accumulating the largest surcharge of all of them by the time you address it. The right sequence is to compute and pay the oldest outstanding installment first, then work forward chronologically, rather than waiting to address everything at once in a single year-end reconciliation. Businesses that have fallen behind on more than one quarter benefit noticeably from professional help at this point, simply because untangling several overlapping surcharge calculations by hand is easy to get wrong.

Planning the Cash Flow to Clear a Missed Installment

Businesses that missed December sometimes did so partly because the cash for the installment was not readily available at the time, not purely because the date was forgotten. If that is closer to your actual situation, it is worth being honest about it and planning the catch-up payment around your real cash position — perhaps a specific week once receivables come in — rather than repeatedly deferring because the amount, plus its growing surcharge, is still not comfortably available. A clear, committed date to clear the balance, even if it is a few weeks out rather than immediate, is a better position than open-ended postponement.

A Quick Clarification on Terminology

The term "default surcharge" is sometimes used loosely alongside words like "penalty" or "fine" in everyday conversation, but it is worth being precise: a default surcharge is specifically a charge calculated on the delay in payment, distinct from a penalty that might apply to a different kind of compliance failure, such as not filing a required document at all. Understanding which specific charge applies to your situation — a surcharge for late payment of an installment that was otherwise correctly computed, versus a penalty for a more fundamental filing failure — helps in discussing your case accurately with FBR or with a consultant, rather than the two concepts blurring together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is missing one advance tax installment a serious legal problem?
No, in the vast majority of cases it is a financial matter resolved by paying the outstanding amount plus the applicable default surcharge — not a prosecution or criminal issue.
Does the surcharge keep growing the longer I wait?
Yes. The surcharge is generally calculated with reference to how long the amount has remained unpaid, so it increases the longer the payment is delayed.
Should I wait and pay the missed December amount together with the March installment?
No — this is usually the wrong approach, since the surcharge on the December amount continues to build in the meantime. Pay the outstanding installment as soon as possible instead.
Does a missed installment affect my Active Taxpayer List status?
Not directly — ATL status is primarily tied to annual return filing. But unresolved advance tax issues add to your overall compliance picture, so it is best addressed promptly.
Can I set up reminders so this does not happen again?
Yes — a standing quarterly advance tax service tracks all four dates and reminds you well before each one, which is the most reliable way to avoid repeat misses.
Why does December get missed more often than other quarters?
December installments compete with half-year book closing, holiday-season demand swings, and year-end bonus processing all at once, which pulls attention away from the deadline more than the other three quarters typically do.
Do I need to write to FBR explaining why I missed the installment?
Usually not, if a single missed installment is simply paid with the correct surcharge attached. Proactive written explanation becomes more relevant for unusually long delays, multiple missed quarters, or if a specific notice has already been issued.
Is there a flat penalty for a missed installment, or does it scale with time?
It scales with time — the surcharge is calculated with reference to the length of the delay, so a two-month delay costs less in surcharge than a four-month delay on the same amount.
What if the cash for a missed installment genuinely was not available at the time?
Be honest about your real cash position and set a clear, committed date to clear the balance — even a few weeks out — rather than leaving it open-ended, since the surcharge keeps building the longer it is postponed.
Does a missed installment ever get escalated beyond a standard surcharge?
It can, in cases involving a very long delay or a repeated pattern across several quarters, but a single missed installment addressed promptly rarely reaches that point.
