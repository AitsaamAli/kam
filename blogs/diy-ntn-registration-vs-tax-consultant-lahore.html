DIY Registration Is Genuinely Possible — Here's the Honest Truth

FBR's IRIS portal at iris.fbr.gov.pk is designed as a self-service system. Anyone with a CNIC, an active mobile number, and basic comfort navigating a government web portal can create an account, select a taxpayer category, enter their details, and submit a registration — entirely without paying anyone. There's no hidden requirement forcing you to use a consultant, and no legitimate reason to claim otherwise.

What DIY Registration Actually Requires From You

  • Comfort with a government web portal that isn't always the most intuitively designed system
  • Correctly identifying your own taxpayer category — salaried, business individual, AOP — without guidance
  • Time to work through the process, including any error messages or unclear steps that come up
  • Patience for occasional portal downtime or technical glitches, which do happen on IRIS from time to time

Where a Consultant Actually Adds Value

The value a consultant adds isn't access to some hidden process — it's correct category selection (getting this wrong is genuinely common and creates future correction work), speed (a consultant doing this constantly moves faster than a first-timer working through it alone), and having someone to ask if anything looks confusing partway through, rather than being stuck alone with a confusing error message.

When DIY Genuinely Makes Sense

If you're comfortable with web forms generally, have a straightforward salaried situation with no ambiguity about your category, and don't mind spending some time working through an unfamiliar portal, DIY registration is a perfectly reasonable choice — there's no need to pay for something you're genuinely capable of doing yourself for free.

When Hiring a Consultant Makes More Sense

A consultant is more clearly worth it if your situation isn't a simple, obvious case — you're unsure whether you're salaried or business-category, you're registering a business or partnership rather than an individual, you've tried IRIS before and hit a confusing error, or you simply value your time enough that same-day, guaranteed-correct registration is worth Rs. 2,000 to you.

What Can Go Wrong With DIY Registration

The most common DIY mistake is selecting the wrong taxpayer category — registering as salaried when you're actually a business individual, or vice versa — which creates a correction requirement later rather than a clean registration from the start. This isn't catastrophic, but it does mean extra steps down the line that a correctly-guided registration would have avoided from day one.

A Worked Example: A DIY Attempt That Hit a Category Error

A freelancer decided to register her own NTN on IRIS, comfortable with web forms generally and wanting to save the Rs. 2,000 fee. She selected "salaried individual" as her category, since this was the first option that seemed to apply, not realizing that freelance/business income requires the separate "business individual" category. The registration went through, but when she later tried to file her freelance income under this incorrectly-categorized NTN, the mismatch caused confusion that took a correction step to resolve — ultimately costing more time than the DIY attempt was meant to save.

This isn't a criticism of DIY registration generally — for a genuinely simple salaried case, the same process would have worked cleanly. It's specifically an illustration of where category ambiguity (freelance income, in this case) makes guided registration worth the modest fee.
