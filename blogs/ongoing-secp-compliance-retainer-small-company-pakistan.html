SECP and Corporate Statutory Compliance

Ongoing SECP Compliance Retainer for a Small Private Limited Company

September 2026 Kamboh Associates 10 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist

Between tracking the AGM and Form A deadlines, filing Form 29 whenever a director changes, maintaining statutory registers, and monitoring whether the audit threshold has been crossed, SECP compliance for even a small private limited company involves enough recurring, interconnected obligations that most companies eventually move it to a standing retainer rather than managing it informally.

TL;DR

A proper ongoing SECP compliance retainer for a small private limited company covers tracking the company's specific compliance calendar (anchored to its financial year-end and incorporation date), filing the annual return and financial statements on schedule, handling event-triggered filings like Form 29 whenever a director or secretary change happens, maintaining the statutory registers, and monitoring the company's position against the audit threshold as it grows. The value is in one party tracking the full, interconnected picture rather than each obligation being remembered separately and inconsistently.

What the Retainer Covers, End to End

  • Building and maintaining a compliance calendar anchored to the company's specific financial year-end and incorporation date.
  • Preparing and filing the annual return (Form A) following each AGM.
  • Filing Form 29 promptly whenever a director, chief executive, or company secretary change happens.
  • Maintaining the company's statutory registers (members, directors, charges) alongside these filings.
  • Monitoring the company's position against the audit threshold each year as it grows.
  • Handling any specific ad hoc filings — a registered address change, a capital increase — as they arise.

Why One Connected Process Matters More for SECP Than for Some Other Compliance

SECP obligations are unusually interconnected compared to some other recurring compliance — a director change affects both the Form 29 filing and the statutory register, a capital increase can affect the audit threshold calculation, and every annual filing depends on the AGM having been properly held and documented first. A retainer where one party sees this whole interconnected picture catches dependencies that a fragmented, ad hoc approach handled piecemeal by different people at different times is likely to miss.

Who Benefits Most From This Kind of Retainer

This is particularly valuable for a growing company anticipating changes — a new investor coming in, a director being added, approaching the audit threshold — since these are exactly the events that a standing retainer is built to handle smoothly as they happen, rather than each one becoming its own separate, unfamiliar scramble. It is also valuable for any company that has already experienced a missed filing, a penalty, or worse, a strike-off, and wants a structural fix going forward.

How This Connects to a Company's Other Recurring Compliance

A private limited company typically carries tax obligations alongside its SECP compliance — its own income tax filing, sales tax if registered, withholding statements if it is a registered agent — and these often draw on overlapping underlying financial records. Coordinating SECP compliance with these other recurring obligations under one retainer, rather than through entirely separate, uncoordinated relationships, tends to be more efficient and catches cross-cutting issues (like a director change that also affects a bank mandate or tax authorization) more reliably.

Signs a SECP Compliance Retainer Is Actually Working

A well-functioning retainer should show up as: no missed annual return or AGM deadlines, Form 29 filed promptly whenever a director change happens rather than discovered months later, statutory registers that are genuinely current rather than a formality nobody has touched since incorporation, and the audit threshold monitored proactively rather than discovered as a surprise at year-end.

Starting This Retainer Partway Through a Compliance Year

As with other recurring compliance retainers, a company does not need to wait for a clean starting point — most companies move to a standing SECP retainer partway through their own compliance cycle, often prompted by a specific missed deadline or an upcoming director change that made the gap in their existing approach obvious. Onboarding involves reviewing what has already been filed, confirming the current compliance status, and building the calendar from that accurate starting point.

Switching From Self-Managed Compliance or Another Provider

A proper handover — whether from a company handling this internally or from a previous consultant — should include a full review of the company's filing history against SECP's own record, not just a review of what the company believes it has filed. This catches any gap between what was assumed to be filed and what SECP actually shows as filed, which is exactly the kind of quiet mismatch that can surface as an unpleasant surprise later if it is not caught during the transition.

Questions Worth Asking Before Committing to a Provider

Before committing to an SECP compliance retainer, it is reasonable to ask how quickly the provider responds to an urgent event-triggered filing like a director change, whether statutory register maintenance is included as standard or a separate add-on, and how they handle the transition if your company is already carrying an existing filing gap. A provider who treats these as vague, open-ended questions rather than giving specific, confident answers is worth being cautious about entrusting with an ongoing compliance function.

What the Retainer Does Not Remove From the Founder's Own Responsibility

Even with a full compliance retainer in place, the founders and directors still need to promptly inform the retainer provider whenever a relevant event happens — a new investor coming in, a director stepping down — since the provider can only track and file what it actually knows about. The retainer removes the burden of tracking deadlines and handling the filing mechanics, but it does not remove the need for timely internal communication about the events that trigger those filings in the first place.
