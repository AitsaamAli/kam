PRA Registration for a Sole Proprietor: Individual Category, Documents and Steps (2026)

21 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 10 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 21 November 2026
A sole proprietor registers with PRA as an individual, under his own CNIC and NTN. Here is what that means in practice.

Most PRA registrations we file are for one person running a service business: a salon, a consultancy, an agency, a workshop, a caterer. The Act and rules do not have a separate sole proprietor form, so the question is how a one-person business fits the individual route. This guide answers that, with the details that trip sole proprietors up.

Short answer

A sole proprietorship has no separate legal identity, so on Form PST-01 it registers in the Individual category under the owner's CNIC, with the owner's FBR NTN supplying the basic details on PRA IRIS. The trade name goes against each business place. Every service can go on one application and one certificate (rule 2(3)), and several shops with central billing in Punjab can share one registration (rule 2(2)). PRA charges no fee (rule 15); our fee is Rs 5,000, plus Rs 2,000 if an NTN is needed first.

Which Category: Individual

Short answer: Individual: the proprietor's CNIC and FBR NTN are the identity; the trade name goes against the business place.

Field 4 of Form PST-01 offers three categories: company, individual and AOP. A sole proprietorship is not a company and not an association, so it registers as an individual. The identity is the owner's: field 7 asks for the CNIC of an individual, and the PRA IRIS Basic Information screen fills from the owner's FBR record and cannot be edited there.

The business name is not lost. Fields 20 to 22 record, for each business place, a business or branch name and a trade name. Your shop or brand name sits there, under your own registration.

Steps for a Sole Proprietor

Short answer: Update the FBR NTN, collect premises, utility and bank details, then file Form PST-01 on PRA IRIS as an individual.

  1. Check or get your FBR NTN. PRA takes your basic details from FBR. If you have no NTN, start with NTN registration.
  2. Collect the business details: address of each place, owned or rented (with the landlord's CNIC or NTN), the electricity reference number, gas connection, start date, and each business bank account. Our documents checklist lists them.
  3. Decide your principal service. One activity must be marked principal on IRIS.
  4. File on PRA IRIS under New Registration, Individual, with the codes sent to your phone and email. See the step-by-step guide.
  5. Keep the provisional certificate issued on applying (rule 3(1)) until the final PST-03 certificate arrives.

One Person, Several Services or Shops

Short answer: Form PST-01 records a business or branch name and a trade name for each place, under the owner's registration.

Rule 2(5) then requires your registration number on every invoice, bill and voucher. Put it on your invoice template the day the certificate arrives.

Small Businesses and Voluntary Registration

Some sole proprietors register because a corporate client or tender asks for a PRA number. If section 25 does not require you to register, section 26 allows voluntary registration where PRA is satisfied you provide or will provide a service that would be taxable, have a place of business, will keep proper records and file returns, and, if trading already, have kept records and complied with other tax laws. Your FBR income tax compliance matters for that last condition. Read who must register to see which side of the line you are on.

Changes, Selling and Closing

Short answer: Selling a registered business needs prior permission from the Commissioner (rule 14) and the buyer needs its own registration.

  • New address or name: report it on Form PST-01 under rule 11; PRA decides, preferably within thirty days.
  • Selling the business: a registered business "shall not be sold or disposed off without prior permission from the Commissioner" (rule 14(1)); the buyer needs a new registration or approved changes (rule 14(2)). The Act also requires the former owner to tell the Commissioner within one month of a sale or transfer (section 20(1)).
  • Closing down: apply for de-registration on Form PST-02 (rule 5). See changes and de-registration.

Sole proprietors also have income tax work alongside PRA. Our sole proprietor services cover the FBR side.

Example: A Lahore Salon Owner

Sana runs a salon in her own name from a rented shop, with a second small outlet. Both use one booking and billing system. Her route:

  1. She already has an NTN, so we check her FBR profile and correct the address.
  2. Category: individual, under her CNIC. Trade name: the salon's name, entered against both outlets.
  3. Rule 2(2): one registration, because billing is centralised in Punjab; both outlets are listed.
  4. Each outlet's rent agreement, landlord's CNIC and electricity reference number go on the form.
  5. Her business bank account goes in the bank section.
  6. Provisional certificate on filing; PST-03 after verification.

Her cost: Rs 5,000 professional fee. PRA charges nothing.

Your First Invoice After Registration

Section 18(2) of the Act sets when a service counts as provided for a tax period: when it is provided to the recipient, when an invoice is issued or due to be issued, or when payment is received, whichever is earlier. Section 18(1) links payment of the tax to the return for that period. For a new registrant that means two things: date your invoices accurately, and put your registration number on them from the first one (rule 2(5)).

If the Owner Dies or the Business Passes On

A sole proprietorship is tied to one person, so the Act covers what happens next. Section 21 makes the tax liability of a deceased registered person "the first charge on his estate in the hands of his successors". A family member who carries the business on should apply for a registration in their own name rather than use the late owner's number, which rule 4 does not allow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which category does a sole proprietor choose on PRA?
Individual. Form PST-01 offers company, individual and AOP; a sole proprietorship registers under the owner's CNIC and NTN, with the trade name against the business place.
Can my shop name appear on the PRA registration?
Yes. Form PST-01 records a business or branch name and a trade name for each place.
I have two shops. Do I need two PRA registrations?
Not if billing or accounting is centralised in Punjab; rule 2(2) allows one registration, with both places listed.
How much does PRA registration cost a sole proprietor?
PRA charges nothing (rule 15). Our fee is Rs 5,000, plus Rs 2,000 if you need an FBR NTN first.
Can I sell my PRA-registered business?
Only with prior permission from the Commissioner (rule 14), and the buyer needs a new registration or approved changes.
Do I need a separate bank account for PRA?
Form PST-01 asks for the bank accounts used in the business. It does not require a new account, but each business account must be listed.
Can a freelancer register with PRA as a sole proprietor?
Yes, as an individual, if they provide a taxable service from a place of business in Punjab or register voluntarily under section 26.
Will my PRA registration show my own name or the shop name?
The registered person is you, under your CNIC; Form PST-01 records the trade name against each business place.
What if I move my shop?
Report the new address on Form PST-01 under rule 11; if the move takes you to another PRA office's area, rule 12 covers transfer of the registration.
Do I need PRA registration if I only work from home?
Section 25(1)(a) asks whether you provide a taxable service from an office or place of business in Punjab. A home used for the business is listed as the business place on Form PST-01.
Can my wife or son run the business under my PRA number?
Rule 4 says only the registered person may use the registration or its number. If someone else will own and run the business, they need their own registration.
