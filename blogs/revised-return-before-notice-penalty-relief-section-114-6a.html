Revising Your Return Before FBR Sends a Notice: How Section 114(6A) Removes or Reduces Penalties

11 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 11 November 2026
Under Section 114(6A), the timing of a revised return decides the penalty: none if you act first, a quarter after audit findings, half after a show cause notice.

Many people know their return has a problem, an undeclared property, rent or bank profit, but wait to see whether FBR notices. The law makes waiting expensive. This guide explains the three stages in Section 114(6A), what you pay at each, and how to use the voluntary stage while it is still open.

TL;DR

Section 114(6A) of the Income Tax Ordinance gives a strong reason to correct a return early. If a taxpayer voluntarily files a revised return with the tax short paid and default surcharge before receiving a notice under Section 177 (audit) or Section 122(9), no penalty is recovered. If the tax is pointed out by the Commissioner during audit or before a 122(9) notice, the taxpayer pays the tax, default surcharge and 25% of the leviable penalties. After a show cause notice under Section 122(9), it is the tax, default surcharge and 50% of the penalties, and the notice then abates. Separately, Section 114(6B) lets a taxpayer who accepts a settlement from FBR's algorithmic settlement mechanism revise without approval and without separate penalty or surcharge.

The Three Stages and What Each Costs

When you reviseWhat you pay with the revised returnSection
Voluntarily, before any notice under Section 177 (audit) or 122(9)Tax short paid + default surcharge; no penalty is recovered114(6A)
After the Commissioner points out tax during audit, or before a 122(9) notice is issuedTax + default surcharge + 25% of the leviable penalties114(6A), first proviso
After a show cause notice under Section 122(9)Tax + default surcharge + 50% of the leviable penalties; the notice then abates114(6A), second proviso

"Leviable penalties" means the penalties that could be imposed under the Ordinance for the default, for example for concealment or for incorrect particulars. The relief is applied to those amounts, which is why the stage you are at changes the total so much.

Stage One: Voluntary Revision

To qualify for full penalty relief you must:

  1. File the revised return voluntarily, whenever the error comes to your notice.
  2. Deposit the tax short paid, or the tax sought to be evaded, with the revised return.
  3. Deposit the default surcharge on that tax.
  4. Do it before you receive a notice under Section 177 or Section 122(9).

The revised return must still meet the conditions in Section 114(6): written reasons, revised accounts where relevant, and Commissioner approval unless you are within 60 days of filing, approval is deemed granted, or your declared income is increasing. Because you are increasing income, the approval is deemed granted under the proviso for income above the Section 120 assessment.

Working Out Default Surcharge

Default surcharge under Section 205 runs on tax not paid by its due date, at the rate set in that section (for most taxes, the higher of 12% a year or KIBOR plus 3%), for the period of default. Calculate it to the day you pay, and pay it with the tax, because Section 114(6A) relief depends on both being deposited.

Section 114(6B): FBR's Algorithmic Settlement Offers

If FBR's algorithmic settlement mechanism offers you a settlement and you accept it, Section 114(6B) lets you file a revised return without the Commissioner's approval. You pay the tax determined by the mechanism, no separate penalty or default surcharge is payable, and the return must be accompanied by the documents required under Section 114(6). Check any such offer carefully against your records before accepting.

Wealth Statements in the Same Exercise

A revision that adds income almost always changes the wealth statement. Section 116(3) allows a revised wealth statement with a revised reconciliation and reasons before a 122(9) notice for that year. File both together so they tell the same story.

Why Waiting Is Riskier for Assets

Undeclared property, vehicles or bank balances are not only an income problem. Under Section 111, money, investments or valuable articles with no satisfactory explanation of their source can be included in income as income from other sources. Declaring them voluntarily, with an explanation of where the money came from, keeps control of the explanation with you.

A Safe Sequence

  1. List what was left out, by year, with documents.
  2. Recompute the tax for each affected year and the default surcharge to the planned payment date.
  3. Prepare revised returns, revised wealth statements and written reasons.
  4. Pay the tax and surcharge.
  5. File the revisions on IRIS and keep the acknowledgements and payment proof.

For the 60-day window on the latest return, see our Tax Year 2026 revision guide; for older years, see revising the last five years.

What Counts as "Voluntary"?

Section 114(6A) gives full relief when the revised return is filed voluntarily, before a notice under Section 177 or 122(9). In practice, a revision filed on your own initiative, before any audit selection notice or show cause notice for that year, is the voluntary stage. Other letters, such as a notice asking you to file a return or a general information request, are not the notices named in the section; but once FBR has started asking about the same issue, act quickly, because the next step may be one of the named notices.

Documents to Assemble

  • The original return and wealth statement for each year affected.
  • Evidence of the omitted income or asset: bank statements, deeds, tenancy agreements, invoices.
  • Computation of the tax short paid and the default surcharge to the payment date.
  • Paid challans for the tax and surcharge.
  • Signed reasons for each revision.

Mistakes That Lose the Relief

  • Filing the revised return but not paying the tax and surcharge with it.
  • Paying the tax but leaving out the default surcharge.
  • Waiting until an audit or show cause notice has been received.
  • Revising one year but leaving the same omission in later years.
  • Filing a revised return that misses a Section 114(6) condition, which makes it invalid.

Is a Voluntary Revision Treated Confidentially?

A revised return is filed through IRIS like any return and is part of your tax record. It is not published. What it does is put the correct position on record, with the tax paid, before FBR raises the question. From that point, the revised return is treated as your amended assessment under Section 122(3).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a penalty if I revise my return voluntarily?
No, if you file the revised return with the tax short paid and default surcharge before receiving a notice under Section 177 or 122(9), under Section 114(6A).
What if the error is found during an audit?
If you deposit the tax pointed out during audit, or before a 122(9) notice, you pay the tax, default surcharge and 25% of the leviable penalties.
What if I already received a 122(9) show cause notice?
You can still revise, paying the tax, default surcharge and 50% of the leviable penalties; the show cause notice then abates.
Do I need the Commissioner's approval to revise and pay more tax?
Approval is deemed granted when the revised taxable income is more than the amount assessed under Section 120, and is not needed within 60 days of the original filing.
What is the algorithmic settlement mechanism?
Under Section 114(6B), if you accept a settlement offered by FBR's algorithmic mechanism, you can revise without approval and pay the tax determined, with no separate penalty or default surcharge.
Does default surcharge still apply on a voluntary revision?
Yes. Section 114(6A) relief requires the tax and the default surcharge to be deposited with the revised return.
Should I revise my wealth statement as well?
Usually yes. Section 116(3) allows a revised wealth statement before a 122(9) notice for that year.
Does a notice asking me to file a return stop the no-penalty relief?
Section 114(6A) names notices under Section 177 and Section 122(9). Other letters are not those notices, but act quickly once FBR is asking about the same issue.
Do I have to revise all affected years?
Yes. Relief applies to each revised return; leaving the same omission in later years leaves those years exposed.
What makes a revised return invalid?
Missing any condition in Section 114(6), such as written reasons or required approval, makes it an invalid return.
Will a voluntary revision make FBR look at me more closely?
A revision becomes part of your tax record and is treated as an amended assessment. It does not remove FBR's power to amend within the time limits, but it puts the correct position on record with the tax paid, which is the strongest position to be in.
