Schengen Visa From Pakistan: Revising Your FBR Wealth Statement So It Matches Your Means of Subsistence

24 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 24 November 2026
Schengen states judge your funds against the length of your stay. A wealth statement that contradicts your bank papers weakens the whole file.

Pakistani applicants for Germany, Italy, Spain, France and the other Schengen states usually build a financial file of bank statements, a salary letter or business papers, and FBR documents. The EU rules do not tell you which Pakistani document to use; they tell the consulate what it must be satisfied about. Knowing that test explains which wealth statement errors matter and which do not, and how to correct the ones that do without creating a tax problem at home.

Short answer

Article 14(1)(c) of the EU Visa Code asks Schengen applicants for documents showing sufficient means of subsistence for the stay, and Article 6(4) of the Schengen Borders Code assesses those means by the length and purpose of the stay against average budget board and lodging costs in the state you visit. The FBR wealth statement is not a named requirement, but it is often in the file as proof that your assets are lawful and declared. If it disagrees with your bank statements or property papers, revise it under section 116(3) before you apply: real figures, a revised reconciliation and written reasons, before any section 122(9) notice for that year.

What the EU Rules Actually Ask For

Short answer: Visa Code Article 14(1)(c): documents showing sufficient means of subsistence for the stay and the return, or the ability to acquire them lawfully.

Two EU regulations set the financial test for a short-stay Schengen visa. The Visa Code, Regulation (EC) No 810/2009, says in Article 14(1)(c) that an applicant must present “documents indicating that the applicant possesses sufficient means of subsistence both for the duration of the intended stay” and for the return or transit, or that he is in a position to acquire such means lawfully. Article 14(3) points to a non-exhaustive list of supporting documents in Annex II, and Article 14(5a), added in 2019, lets the Commission adopt a harmonised list for each country.

The Visa Code does not name an FBR return or wealth statement. Pakistani applicants include them because they show that the money in the bank was earned or received lawfully and declared at home. Our country guides for Germany and Spain cover the wider FBR paperwork; this guide deals with correcting the wealth statement inside that file.

How Means of Subsistence Is Judged

Short answer: Borders Code Article 6(4): means are judged by duration and purpose of stay, against average budget board and lodging prices, times the number of days.

Article 6(4) of the Schengen Borders Code, Regulation (EU) 2016/399, explains the test: “Means of subsistence shall be assessed in accordance with the duration and the purpose of the stay and by reference to average prices in the Member State(s) concerned for board and lodging in budget accommodation, multiplied by the number of days stayed.” Reference amounts are set by each Member State and notified to the Commission.

Three practical consequences follow for a Pakistani file:

  • Length of stay drives the figure. A 30-day trip needs more than a 10-day one at the same daily reference amount, so the balance you show should fit the itinerary.
  • Several forms of evidence count. Article 6(4) says cash, travellers' cheques and credit cards may be evidence, and that sponsorship declarations or a host's guarantee letter may also constitute evidence of sufficient means.
  • Consistency is the real test. A balance that fits the stay but appears from nowhere a fortnight before the application is weaker than a smaller one with a history.

Where the Wealth Statement Helps, and Where It Hurts

Short answer: The wealth statement shows the means are lawful and declared; it must agree with the bank and property papers in the same file.

Situation in the fileWhat a consistent wealth statement showsWhat an inconsistent one suggests
Rs 2,500,000 balance in a savings accountThe account and its 30 June balance, with salary and savings building it upAn account missing from FBR records, so the funds look new or undeclared
Recent large credit from a parentA gift from a relative recorded in the reconciliationIncome or a loan nobody can explain
Property papers to show ties to PakistanThe plot at its cost, held for yearsA plot that FBR has never been told about
Business owner with irregular incomeBusiness capital and drawings that match the bank flowCash figures that do not agree with deposits

The wealth statement shows assets on 30 June of the tax year, while the bank statements you attach usually cover the last few months. They will not be identical, and that is normal. What matters is that the 30 June position is true and that later movements in the bank statement have an obvious source.

Correcting the Statement Under Section 116(3)

Short answer: Correct omitted accounts, wrong balances, unrecorded gifts and stale property entries, with a revised reconciliation and reasons.

If you find an error, section 116(3) of the Income Tax Ordinance lets you file a revised wealth statement with a revised wealth reconciliation and the reasons for revision, under intimation to the Commissioner, before receiving a notice under section 122(9) for that year, and within five years of that year's return due date. For a Schengen file the usual corrections are:

  1. A missing account. Add it at the bank's certified 30 June balance; see our guide to bank balance corrections.
  2. A parent's or sibling's transfer. Record it as a gift from a relative in the reconciliation, not as your income; see gifts and loans.
  3. A guessed cash figure. Replace it with the real amount; see cash in hand.
  4. A plot or car sold or bought. Update it at actual cost or sale price; see property revisions.

The proviso to section 116(3) allows the Commissioner to declare a revision void, after a hearing, if it does not correct a bona fide error. A correction backed by a bank certificate passes that test easily. A revision that adds funds to reach a consulate's reference amount does not.

Example: A Twelve-Day Trip to Italy

Ayesha, a Lahore teacher, applies for a twelve-day tourist visa. Her bank statement shows Rs 1,600,000 at the end of October, built from salary and a Rs 700,000 transfer from her brother in May. Her TY2026 wealth statement shows Rs 300,000 in the bank and no gift.

When a Relative Sponsors the Trip

Short answer: If a host or relative sponsors you, the Borders Code accepts sponsorship declarations as possible evidence; the sponsor's own papers then matter too.

Many Pakistani applicants travel on a parent's or spouse's funds. The Borders Code accepts sponsorship declarations as possible evidence of means. In that case it is the sponsor's papers that carry weight, and the sponsor's wealth statement must support the money they are giving. Our guide for sponsor parents explains how a sponsoring parent checks and corrects their own statement.

How Much Balance, and for How Many Months?

“How much bank statement for Schengen visa from Pakistan” is one of the most searched visa questions in the country, and the honest answer is that the EU rules do not give one figure. Article 6(4) of the Schengen Borders Code makes the amount depend on the length and purpose of the stay and on average budget board and lodging prices in the state you visit, multiplied by the days, and each Member State notifies its own reference amount. Neither Article 14 of the Visa Code nor Article 6 of the Borders Code fixes how many months of statements to submit; that comes from the checklist of the consulate or visa centre you apply through. Use that checklist for the format and period, and use the wealth statement to show that the money in those months is lawfully yours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an FBR wealth statement required for a Schengen visa?
The Visa Code does not name it. Article 14(1)(c) asks for documents showing sufficient means of subsistence. Pakistani applicants often add the FBR return and wealth statement to show those means are lawful and declared.
How much money do I need to show for Schengen?
Each Member State sets its own reference amount. Under Borders Code Article 6(4) means are judged by the duration and purpose of the stay, against average budget board and lodging prices multiplied by the number of days.
My bank balance today is higher than in my wealth statement. Is that a problem?
Not by itself. The wealth statement shows 30 June; the bank statement shows recent months. It becomes a problem only if the 30 June figure is wrong or the later rise has no source.
Can I add my brother's gift to my wealth statement?
Yes, as a gift from a relative in the reconciliation, if it was genuinely received. A brother is a relative under section 85(5). Receiving it by bank transfer keeps the record clean.
When is it too late to revise?
After you receive a section 122(9) notice for that tax year, or five years after that year's return due date.
Can you check my whole Schengen file?
We check the FBR side: the return, wealth statement and how they match your bank papers. Visa decisions rest with the consulate.
How much bank balance is required for a Schengen visa from Pakistan?
There is no single EU figure. Under Borders Code Article 6(4) it depends on the length and purpose of the stay and the reference amount the Member State you visit has notified.
How many months of bank statements for a Schengen visa?
The Visa Code and Borders Code articles we checked do not fix a number of months. Follow the checklist of the consulate or visa centre handling your application.
