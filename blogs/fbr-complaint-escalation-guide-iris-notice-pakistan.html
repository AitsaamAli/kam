FBR Complaint and Escalation Guide — What to Do When IRIS or a Notice Goes Wrong

August 2026 Kamboh Associates 8 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN • SECP • Sales Tax Specialist

IRIS freezing mid-filing, a notice that seems to reference the wrong tax year, a refund that never arrives — these are genuinely practical, day-to-day frustrations distinct from a formal legal dispute, and Pakistan actually has a specific, structured escalation path for exactly this kind of problem that many taxpayers don't realize exists.

TL;DR

When IRIS malfunctions, a notice seems genuinely incorrect, or FBR staff behavior itself is the problem, the first step is FBR's own helpline (051-111-772-772) or its online complaint portal, not a formal legal appeal. Where a complaint genuinely goes unresolved, the Federal Tax Ombudsman (FTO) exists specifically to investigate maladministration by FBR — a genuinely distinct, faster track from the formal three-tier appeals process covered elsewhere on this site for actually disputing a tax assessment's substance. A Genuinely Important Distinction — Complaint vs Formal Appeal

This guide addresses a genuinely different problem from the formal legal appeals process covered in detail elsewhere on this site — a complaint or escalation is the right path for procedural, administrative, or technical problems (IRIS malfunctioning, a notice with apparently incorrect details, unresponsive staff, a delayed refund), while a formal appeal through the Commissioner (Appeals) and Appellate Tribunal is the right path for genuinely disputing the substance of a tax assessment or demand itself. A taxpayer should identify which category their specific problem actually falls into before choosing which path to pursue, since these are genuinely separate mechanisms with different processes and different appropriate uses.

FBR Helpline — The First, Fastest Contact Point

FBR maintains a dedicated helpline, reachable at 051-111-772-772, alongside an email contact at helpline@fbr.gov.pk, specifically to help taxpayers with genuine procedural and technical issues — IRIS access problems, portal errors, and similar day-to-day difficulties. A taxpayer encountering a genuine technical or procedural problem should generally start here first, since many issues (a portal glitch, a straightforward misunderstanding about a notice, a password or access problem) can often be resolved through this direct helpline contact without needing to escalate further.

Key point: Start with the FBR helpline (051-111-772-772) or online complaint portal for genuine procedural and technical problems — reserve the Federal Tax Ombudsman for situations where this first level of contact genuinely hasn't resolved the issue.

The Online Complaint Portal Within IRIS

Beyond the phone helpline, FBR also provides an online complaint mechanism accessible through the IRIS system itself, letting a taxpayer log a specific complaint and track its status digitally rather than relying purely on phone contact. A taxpayer preferring a documented, trackable complaint trail — genuinely useful if an issue takes time to resolve or might need to be referenced later — should use this online complaint channel specifically, keeping a record of the complaint reference for their own files.

What Genuinely Counts as Complaint Material

Genuine complaint material includes things like: IRIS being down or malfunctioning during a filing deadline, a notice that appears to reference incorrect personal details or an apparently wrong tax year, unreasonable delay in processing a straightforward refund, or unprofessional conduct by FBR staff during an interaction. A taxpayer experiencing any of these kinds of genuinely procedural or administrative problems should recognize this as complaint-appropriate territory, distinct from a situation where the actual substance of a tax assessment is being disputed, which instead calls for the formal appeals process.

The Federal Tax Ombudsman — A Genuinely Distinct Escalation Layer

Where a complaint to FBR directly genuinely hasn't resolved the underlying issue, the Federal Tax Ombudsman (FTO) — an entirely independent office, not part of FBR itself — exists specifically to investigate complaints of tax maladministration and work to rectify injustice caused by FBR's own actions or inaction. A taxpayer whose genuine complaint has gone unresolved through FBR's own helpline and portal channels should understand the FTO as the next, genuinely appropriate escalation layer, accessible through the FTO's own website (fto.gov.pk) or its Islamabad secretariat, rather than assuming a formal legal appeal is the only remaining option.

Why the FTO Is Genuinely Different From the Formal Appeals Path

The Federal Tax Ombudsman specifically investigates maladministration — how FBR handled a matter procedurally, whether staff acted properly, whether reasonable timelines were followed — rather than re-litigating the actual correctness of a tax assessment's substance, which remains the formal appeals process's specific domain covered in detail in this site's dedicated appeals guides. A taxpayer with a genuine grievance about how FBR handled their situation, separate from disagreeing with the actual tax amount assessed, should recognize the FTO as specifically suited to this kind of complaint, while a taxpayer genuinely disputing the assessed amount itself should pursue the formal appeal route instead.

Documentation That Genuinely Strengthens a Complaint

A taxpayer filing any complaint, whether through FBR's own helpline or eventually the FTO, should maintain clear documentation supporting their specific complaint — screenshots of IRIS errors with timestamps, copies of the notice in question, records of prior contact attempts and their outcomes, and a clear timeline of what happened. A taxpayer with well-organized documentation genuinely strengthens their complaint's credibility and speeds up resolution, compared to a vague complaint lacking specific, verifiable details supporting what actually went wrong.

Setting Realistic Expectations for Resolution Time

A taxpayer filing a genuine complaint should understand that resolution isn't necessarily instant — a technical IRIS issue might resolve within days, while a more involved complaint requiring actual investigation, particularly one escalated to the FTO, can genuinely take longer given the investigation process involved. A taxpayer with a genuinely time-sensitive issue, such as a deadline approaching while IRIS itself is malfunctioning, should document the technical problem clearly and promptly, since this documentation can itself become relevant if a deadline-related penalty is later disputed on the basis that the portal itself was genuinely unavailable.

When Professional Guidance Genuinely Helps With Escalation

A taxpayer facing a genuinely complex or high-stakes complaint situation — particularly one that might eventually need to move toward the FTO or even intersect with a formal appeal — benefits from professional guidance in correctly framing the complaint, ensuring the right escalation path is chosen, and maintaining the kind of documentation that genuinely supports resolution. A tax professional familiar with both the complaint and formal appeal mechanisms can help a taxpayer avoid pursuing the wrong track entirely, which can genuinely waste time compared to correctly identifying and following the right process from the outset.

A Notice You Disagree With vs a Notice That's Genuinely Wrong

It's worth distinguishing a notice a taxpayer simply disagrees with on substance — believing the assessed amount is incorrect, for instance — from a notice that's genuinely, factually wrong in some administrative sense, such as referencing the wrong taxpayer's details, citing an incorrect tax year, or containing an obvious clerical error. The first situation genuinely calls for the formal response and appeal process covered elsewhere on this site for responding to FBR notices, while the second — a genuine factual or clerical error in the notice itself — is legitimately complaint territory, since the problem here is administrative accuracy rather than a substantive disagreement about the underlying tax position.

Delayed Refunds — A Particularly Common Complaint Category

A genuinely delayed tax refund — where a taxpayer is clearly owed money back and a reasonable processing period has passed without resolution — represents one of the more common specific situations that genuinely warrants a formal complaint, and this is an area where the Federal Tax Ombudsman has specifically and actively intervened in cases involving systemic refund processing issues. A taxpayer facing a genuinely significant refund delay should document the original claim, the expected processing timeline, and the actual elapsed time clearly, since this kind of concrete, well-documented delay is precisely the sort of maladministration issue the FTO exists to address.

Why Social Media Complaints Aren't a Genuine Substitute

Some taxpayers, frustrated with a genuine issue, turn to posting complaints on social media platforms hoping for visibility-driven resolution, but this genuinely isn't a substitute for the formal helpline, complaint portal, and FTO channels covered throughout this guide — social media attention might occasionally prompt informal follow-up, but it doesn't create the same documented, trackable complaint record that the proper formal channels provide. A taxpayer with a genuine grievance should prioritize the formal complaint channels as their primary path, treating any social media visibility as, at most, a supplementary and genuinely unreliable addition rather than a real substitute for proper escalation.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing a procedural complaint with a formal tax appeal: these are genuinely separate mechanisms — complaints address administrative/technical problems, appeals dispute the assessment's substance.
  • Escalating directly to the FTO without first trying FBR's own helpline and complaint portal: the FTO is specifically for situations where the first level of contact hasn't resolved the issue.
  • Not documenting IRIS errors or notice problems with specific evidence: screenshots, timestamps, and clear records genuinely strengthen a complaint's credibility.
  • Assuming complaint resolution is always instant: some issues resolve quickly, others genuinely require more time, particularly once formal investigation is involved.
  • Not seeking professional guidance for a genuinely complex escalation: the wrong track (complaint vs appeal) can waste meaningful time compared to correctly identifying the right process upfront.

A Worked Example

A taxpayer experiences IRIS repeatedly timing out during an attempt to file close to the annual deadline, taking clear screenshots with timestamps documenting each failed attempt. After contacting FBR's helpline directly without a satisfactory resolution, the taxpayer logs a formal complaint through IRIS's own complaint portal, referencing the documented technical failures and the approaching deadline specifically. When the complaint remains unresolved after a reasonable period, the taxpayer escalates to the Federal Tax Ombudsman, presenting the same organized documentation, recognizing this specific situation — a genuine technical failure preventing timely filing — falls squarely within the FTO's maladministration mandate rather than requiring the entirely separate formal appeals process that would apply if the taxpayer were instead disputing the actual substance of an assessed tax amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between filing a complaint and filing a formal tax appeal?
A complaint addresses procedural or administrative problems like IRIS malfunctions or notice errors, while a formal appeal disputes the actual substance of a tax assessment — genuinely separate mechanisms.
What is FBR's helpline number?
051-111-772-772, alongside email contact at helpline@fbr.gov.pk — this is generally the first, fastest contact point for genuine procedural and technical issues.
What is the Federal Tax Ombudsman?
An entirely independent office, separate from FBR, that investigates complaints of tax maladministration and works to rectify injustice caused by FBR's own actions or inaction, reachable through fto.gov.pk.
When should I escalate to the Federal Tax Ombudsman?
When a genuine complaint through FBR's own helpline and complaint portal hasn't resolved the underlying issue — the FTO is the next appropriate escalation layer, not the first step.
What documentation should I keep when filing a complaint?
Screenshots of IRIS errors with timestamps, copies of the notice in question, records of prior contact attempts, and a clear timeline — this genuinely strengthens the complaint's credibility.
Does the Federal Tax Ombudsman handle disputes about how much tax I owe?
No — the FTO investigates maladministration and procedural issues, not the actual correctness of a tax assessment's substance, which remains the formal appeals process's domain.
My notice has an obvious clerical error — is that a complaint or an appeal?
A complaint — a genuine factual or clerical error (wrong taxpayer details, wrong tax year) is an administrative accuracy issue, distinct from disagreeing with the substance of an assessment.
Can posting on social media get my FBR issue resolved faster?
It's not a genuine substitute for the formal helpline, complaint portal, and FTO channels — prioritize those as your primary path rather than relying on social media visibility.
