Default Surcharge Under Section 205: The Rate, the Formula, and How to Calculate It (2026)

20 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 20 November 2026
Default surcharge is interest on late tax. It runs per day, and it is calculated per amount.

Default surcharge is the interest FBR charges on money paid late. It is not a penalty, and it is not optional. People search for 'the default surcharge formula' because notices rarely show the working. Here it is, section by section, with examples you can check against your own notice.

Short answer

Section 205 of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 (FBR text amended up to 30 June 2026) charges default surcharge on tax, penalties and amounts under Sections 140 and 141 not paid by the due date, at the higher of 12% or KIBOR plus 3% a year, from the due date to the date of payment (Section 205(1), rate set by the Finance Act 2024). Advance tax under Section 147 not paid carries 12% a year until paid or the return due date, whichever is earlier (Section 205(1A)); an underestimate below 90% carries 12% a year from 1 April (Section 205(1B)). A withholding agent who fails to collect, deduct or deposit tax pays 12% a year from the date it should have been collected or deducted (Section 205(3)). Surcharge is refunded to the extent the underlying amount is held not payable (Section 205(2)), and it is assessed and recovered as if it were tax. Formula: amount × rate × days ÷ 365.

What Default Surcharge Is

Short answer: Default surcharge is a charge for paying tax, penalties or other amounts late; it is not a penalty.

Part XII of Chapter X of the Ordinance is headed "Default Surcharge". Until 2010 it was called "additional tax"; the Finance Act 2010 renamed it. It compensates the Government for the time value of money paid late. It is separate from penalties under Section 182, so a late payer can owe both.

The Four Default Surcharge Rules

Short answer: Unpaid tax carries the higher of 12% or KIBOR plus 3% a year; advance tax and withholding defaults carry 12%.

Sub-sectionWhat was unpaidRatePeriod
205(1)Any tax (other than s.147 advance tax and the surcharge itself), any penalty, or amounts under s.140 or s.141Higher of 12% or KIBOR + 3% a yearDue date to date of payment
205(1A)Advance tax under s.147 not paid12% a yearDue date to date paid or the return due date, whichever is earlier
205(1B)Estimate under s.147(4A) or (6) not paid, or paid tax below 90% of the tax chargeable12% a year1 April to the assessment date or 30 June following, whichever is earlier
205(3)Withholding or collection agent failing to collect, deduct or deposit tax12% a yearDate tax should have been collected or deducted to date paid to the Commissioner

When Is Tax 'Due'?

Short answer: Tax with a return is due on the return due date (Section 137(1)); order demands are due on the notice date.

Under Section 137(1), tax payable on taxable income for a tax year, including minimum tax under Section 113 and 113A, is due on the due date for filing the return for that year. For tax year 2026, that is 31 October 2026. Tax payable under an assessment or other order is due on the date in the notice under Section 137(2). Default surcharge under Section 205(1) is counted from these dates.

The Formula

Short answer: Unpaid amount × annual rate × days late ÷ 365.

Default surcharge = unpaid amount × annual rate × number of days late ÷ 365

Count days from the due date to the date of payment. Calculate each unpaid amount separately if they fell due on different dates. Under Section 205(1), compare 12% with KIBOR plus 3% for the period and use the higher. We do not quote a KIBOR figure here because it changes; use the rate published for the relevant period.

Worked Examples (at 12%)

Short answer: Rs 100,000 paid 73 days late at 12% costs Rs 2,400 in default surcharge.

If KIBOR plus 3% exceeds 12% for a Section 205(1) amount, recompute with the higher rate. Sections 205(1A), (1B) and (3) use a flat 12%.

Advance Tax Instalments

Short answer: Missed advance tax carries 12% a year until paid or the return due date, whichever is earlier (Section 205(1A)).

Under Section 147, certain taxpayers pay advance tax in quarterly instalments. Missing an instalment triggers Section 205(1A), but only until the instalment is paid or the return falls due, whichever comes first; after that, any tax still unpaid falls due with the return under Section 137(1). Underestimating the year's tax to pay less than 90% triggers Section 205(1B). Our advance tax instalment calculator helps you plan the instalments.

Withholding Agents and Section 161

Short answer: Agents who fail to deduct or deposit tax owe the tax (Section 161) plus 12% a year surcharge (Section 205(3)).

Employers, banks and businesses that must deduct or collect tax carry two separate exposures. Under Section 161, a person who fails to collect or deduct tax, or collects it and fails to pay it, is personally liable to pay the tax. Under Section 205(3), default surcharge at 12% a year runs from the date the tax should have been collected or deducted until it is paid to the Commissioner. The proviso to Section 205(3) relieves surcharge for the period after a Section 161 order if the person pays by the date in the Section 137(2) notice and does not appeal.

Exceptions and Refunds

Short answer: Surcharge is refunded to the extent the underlying amount is held not payable (Section 205(2)).

  • Appeal effect relief: under the proviso to Section 205(1), a person who pays tax due under an order of the Commissioner (Appeals) under Section 129 by the date in the Section 137(2) notice, and does not appeal further under Section 131, does not pay surcharge for the period from the due date under the order appealed against to that payment.
  • Refund: Section 205(2) refunds surcharge to the extent the tax, penalty or other amount is held not payable.
  • Revised returns: voluntary revision before an audit or Section 122(9) notice avoids penalties under Section 114(6A), but tax and default surcharge must still be paid. See revising before a notice.

How FBR Recovers It

Short answer: Default surcharge is assessed, recovered and appealed as if it were tax (Section 205(5) and (6)).

Section 205(5) says the Commissioner assesses default surcharge as if it were tax, and Section 205(6) applies the appeal and recovery provisions to it in the same way. An unpaid surcharge demand can therefore be recovered by the same means as unpaid tax, and it can be appealed in the same way.

Practical Tips

Short answer: Pay the tax first; the surcharge stops the day you pay.

  • Pay the tax first; surcharge stops the day you pay.
  • Keep the payment challan (CPR) dates; they decide the number of days.
  • Check that a notice uses the right sub-section; advance tax defaults are capped at the return due date.
  • If the underlying tax is reduced on appeal, claim the surcharge back under Section 205(2).

Default Surcharge vs Penalty vs ATL Surcharge

Short answer: Default surcharge is for paying late; Section 182 penalties are for filing late or concealing; Section 182A is the ATL surcharge.

Counting the Days: A Dated Example

Short answer: Rs 240,000 paid 76 days after 31 October at 12% costs Rs 5,996 in default surcharge.

A sole proprietor's tax year 2026 return shows Rs 240,000 payable. The due date was 31 October 2026. The return is filed on time, but the tax is paid on 15 January 2027.

  • Days late: 1 November to 15 January = 76 days.
  • Default surcharge at 12%: 240,000 × 12% × 76 ÷ 365 = Rs 5,996.
  • If KIBOR plus 3% for the period was higher than 12%, Section 205(1) uses that rate instead.

Paying in two parts? Work each part separately: the part paid earlier stops accruing on the day it is paid.

An Advance Tax Example

Short answer: A Rs 1,000,000 instalment paid 60 days late costs Rs 19,726 at 12% under Section 205(1A).

A company due to pay a Rs 1,000,000 quarterly instalment under Section 147 pays it 60 days late. Under Section 205(1A): 1,000,000 × 12% × 60 ÷ 365 = Rs 19,726. The surcharge stops at the date of payment or the return due date, whichever is earlier; it does not keep running into the next year on the same instalment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the default surcharge rate in Pakistan in 2026?
Under Section 205(1), the higher of 12% or KIBOR plus 3% a year on unpaid tax, penalties and Section 140/141 amounts. Advance tax and withholding defaults carry 12% a year.
How is default surcharge calculated?
Unpaid amount × annual rate × days late ÷ 365, counted from the due date to the date of payment.
Is default surcharge a penalty?
No. It is a separate charge for late payment. A late payer can owe both a Section 182 penalty and default surcharge.
Does default surcharge apply to advance tax?
Yes, at 12% a year under Section 205(1A), until paid or the return due date, whichever is earlier.
What is the default surcharge for withholding agents?
12% a year under Section 205(3), from the date the tax should have been collected or deducted to the date paid to the Commissioner.
Can default surcharge be refunded?
Yes, to the extent the underlying tax, penalty or amount is held not payable (Section 205(2)).
What was default surcharge called earlier?
Additional tax. The Finance Act 2010 renamed it default surcharge.
Can I appeal against default surcharge?
Yes. Section 205(6) applies the appeal provisions to a default surcharge assessment as if it were tax.
