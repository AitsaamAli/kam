Pension Tax Pakistan 2026 — Is Pension Income Taxable?

June 2026 Kamboh Associates 6 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website

Is pension income taxable in Pakistan? Government pension is exempt, but "pension" covers several genuinely different arrangements — Voluntary Pension System funds, employer gratuity, and private annuities — and each is taxed differently. Here's exactly which is which.

Short answer

Since the Finance Act 2025, pension from a former employer (Government or private) is taxed only where it exceeds Rs 10 million in a tax year, and not at all from age 70 (Section 12(2A)). VPS contributions earn a Section 63 tax credit, and up to 50% of the balance is tax-free at retirement. Gratuity is exempt in full from an approved fund or under Government service rules, Rs 300,000 under a Board-approved scheme, and otherwise the lesser of 50% or Rs 75,000. Overview — Is Pension Income Taxable in Pakistan?

Short answer: pension is taxed only above Rs 10 million a year since the Finance Act 2025; VPS, gratuity and commutation each follow their own rules.

"Pension" gets used loosely to describe several genuinely different arrangements, and the tax answer depends entirely on which one you actually have. A retired government employee's monthly pension is treated differently from a private-sector employee's payout from a Voluntary Pension System (VPS) fund, which is again different from a lump-sum gratuity paid at the end of employment. The common thread is that Pakistan's tax system is generally favourable toward genuine retirement income — but "generally favourable" is not the same as "always fully exempt," and the exemptions that do exist come with specific conditions worth understanding before you assume a payout is tax-free.

Government and Employer Pension — Taxed Only Above Rs 10 Million

Short answer: under Section 12(2A), pension from a former employer is taxed only above Rs 10 million a year, and not at all from age 70.

Until 30 June 2025, pension from a former employer was exempt under clause (8) of Part I of the Second Schedule. The Finance Act 2025 omitted that clause. Under Section 12(2A) of the Income Tax Ordinance (FBR, amended up to 30.06.2026), checked 20 November 2026: pension received from a former employer is charged to tax only where it exceeds Rs 10 million in a tax year, as a final tax at the rate in the proviso to clause (2) of Division I of the First Schedule; an individual who has attained the age of 70 is not charged to tax on pension income at all; and a pensioner who continues to work for the former employer or its associate is taxed at the normal rates. For most retired government and private-sector employees, whose pension is well below Rs 10 million a year, no tax arises on the pension itself, but it should still be declared in the return.

Voluntary Pension System (VPS) — Private Retirement Savings

Short answer: VPS contributions earn a Section 63 tax credit on up to 20% of taxable income, and up to half the balance is tax-free at retirement.

The Voluntary Pension System, regulated by SECP and offered through licensed pension fund managers, is Pakistan's main private-sector retirement savings vehicle for individuals without a government pension. It works in two tax-relevant stages. During the contribution years, contributions to an approved pension fund qualify for a tax credit under Section 63 — the credit is based on the lower of actual contribution or a percentage of taxable income, capped at 20% of taxable income for everyone (the extra allowance for people over 40 applied only up to 30 June 2019). At retirement (from age 60, or earlier under specific conditions), the account holder can typically withdraw a portion as a tax-exempt lump sum, with the remainder used to purchase an approved income stream (annuity) or withdrawn under a structured payment plan — the ongoing tax treatment of that remainder depends on how it's drawn, not treated as a single blanket exemption the way government pension is.

Early Withdrawal From a VPS Fund — A Different Tax Story

Short answer: early withdrawals and amounts above 50% are taxed at the Section 12(6) rate, deducted by the fund manager.

Clause (23A) of the Second Schedule exempts up to 50% of the accumulated VPS balance received at retirement, on disability or, by survivors, on death. Withdrawals before retirement age (other than on disability or death) and amounts above 50% are taxed at the rate in Section 12(6), which the pension fund manager deducts at the time of payment. That can mean a materially larger tax bill, so check the scheme's rules before withdrawing early.

Employer Pension Funds and Gratuity

Short answer: approved-fund gratuity is exempt in full; otherwise Rs 300,000 under a Board-approved scheme or the lesser of 50% or Rs 75,000.

Many private-sector employers run their own approved gratuity or pension funds rather than relying solely on VPS. The tax treatment here hinges heavily on whether the fund is "approved" by the Commissioner Inland Revenue under the Sixth Schedule:

Whether the employer's fund is approved by the Commissioner makes a large difference: approved-fund gratuity is exempt in full, while gratuity paid directly with no approved fund or scheme is exempt only up to the lesser of 50% or Rs. 75,000. Ask HR for the approval order well before retirement.

Family Pension for Widows and Dependents

Short answer: family pension for dependants of those who die in service is exempt; other family pension follows the Rs 10 million rule.

Family pension of the families and dependants of public servants or members of the Armed Forces who die during service is exempt under clause (9)(ii) of the Second Schedule. Other family pension, for example a widow's pension after the death of a retired pensioner, falls under the general rule in Section 12(2A): taxed only where the pension exceeds Rs 10 million in a tax year, and not taxed where the recipient is 70 or older. Confirm the type of family pension from the pension order.

Pension vs Provident Fund vs Gratuity — Don't Confuse Them

Short answer: pension is recurring, provident fund is a saved balance, gratuity is a one-time payment, and each has its own tax rule.

These three terms get used almost interchangeably in casual conversation but are legally distinct benefits with separate tax rules. Pension is a recurring periodic payment (or its commuted lump sum) tied to years of service, taxed as described above. A provident fund is a savings account built from employee and employer contributions during employment, paid out as a lump sum at separation, with its own exemption rules depending on whether it's a government, recognised, or unrecognised provident fund. Gratuity is a separate lump-sum benefit paid at the end of employment based on length of service, following the approved/unapproved exemption thresholds covered above. An employee's total retirement payout often includes all three at once, and each needs to be classified and taxed separately rather than lumped together as one undifferentiated "retirement money" figure — for a deeper look specifically at provident fund and gratuity mechanics, see our dedicated guide on provident fund and gratuity tax treatment.

Commutation of Pension

Short answer: commutation from Government or a Board-approved scheme is exempt under clause 12.

Commutation of pension received from Government, or under a pension scheme approved by the Board, is exempt under clause (12) of the Second Schedule. Commutation under a scheme that is not approved does not get that exemption, so check the scheme's approval before relying on it.

A Worked Example — VPS Tax Credit

Short answer: on Rs 30 lakh income with Rs 4 lakh tax, a Rs 5 lakh VPS contribution gives a tax credit of about Rs 66,667.

Consider a salaried professional with taxable income of Rs. 3,000,000 and tax of Rs. 400,000 before credits, who contributes Rs. 500,000 to an approved VPS fund. The ceiling is 20% of taxable income, Rs. 600,000, so the full Rs. 500,000 counts. Section 63 credit = (A/B) × C = (400,000 / 3,000,000) × 500,000 = Rs. 66,667, which reduces the tax payable directly. The same 20% ceiling applies at every age.

How Pensioners Should File Their Return

Short answer: declare the pension, bank profit, VPS withdrawals and gratuity correctly, and file the wealth statement.

  • Declare pension income — pension below Rs 10 million a year is not charged to tax under Section 12(2A), but declare it in the return so the wealth statement reconciles
  • Report VPS withdrawals according to how they were structured — lump sum, annuity, or phased withdrawal each has different reporting
  • Include gratuity in the year received, under salary income, applying the correct approved/unapproved exemption threshold
  • File the wealth statement alongside the return — a lump-sum gratuity or commutation payment will visibly increase bank balances, and FBR's reconciliation expects this to match declared exempt/taxable income
  • Keep pension fund statements and gratuity payment letters as supporting documentation in case of a notice

Key point: A large, one-time gratuity or commutation deposit that isn't matched by a corresponding declaration in your wealth statement is one of the more common triggers for a Section 111 unexplained-income notice among newly retired taxpayers — declare it even where it's exempt.

Common Mistakes With Pension Tax

Short answer: relying on the old blanket pension exemption and assuming all gratuity is exempt are the costly ones.

The most frequent errors are relying on the old blanket pension exemption, which the Finance Act 2025 replaced with the Rs 10 million rule in Section 12(2A), and assuming all gratuity is exempt. Gratuity from an approved fund is exempt in full, but gratuity paid directly by an employer without an approved fund or scheme is exempt only up to the lesser of 50% or Rs 75,000. Confirm the approval with HR before retirement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is pension income taxable in Pakistan 2026?
Since the Finance Act 2025, pension from a former employer is taxed under Section 12(2A) only where it exceeds Rs 10 million in a tax year, as a final tax, and not at all for individuals aged 70 or over. A pensioner still working for the former employer is taxed at normal rates.
What is the tax credit for pension fund contributions in Pakistan?
Contributions to approved pension funds under the Voluntary Pension System qualify for an income tax credit based on the lower of actual contribution or a percentage of taxable income, with age-based limits. The maximum permitted contribution percentage increases for contributors after age 41.
How do pensioners file their income tax return in Pakistan?
File on IRIS: declare pension (taxed only above Rs 10 million a year), bank profit with its tax certificates, other income, and every asset in the wealth statement. Filing keeps you on the ATL for lower withholding on bank profit.
Are gratuity payments taxable in Pakistan?
Under clause 13 of the Second Schedule: exempt in full from a fund approved by the Commissioner or under Government service rules; up to Rs 300,000 under a Board-approved scheme; otherwise the lesser of 50% or Rs 75,000. The rest is taxed as salary in the year received.
