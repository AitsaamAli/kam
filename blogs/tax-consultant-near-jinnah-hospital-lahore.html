Near Jinnah Hospital, Lahore — Private Ambulance and Patient Transport Services

Updated October 2026
Kamboh Associates
3 min read
Near Jinnah Hospital

Large public hospitals like Jinnah Hospital, near Allama Iqbal Town and the Canal, attract a ring of private services: ambulances, patient transport vans, dead-body carriers, medical equipment rental and attendant services. Private ambulance operators charge families or work under contracts with hospitals and insurers. It's a small transport-and-service business with specific costs.

Income sources

SourceNotes
Families paying per tripMostly cash; record each trip
Contracts with private hospitalsMonthly bills; withholding likely
Insurance / corporate panelsInvoices; withholding likely
Inter-city patient transfersHigher fares; record route and payment

Keep a trip log: date, pick-up and drop, patient/family name, fare, payment method.

Vehicles and equipment

Ambulances are expensive: the vehicle plus conversion, oxygen, stretchers, monitors. Record each vehicle and its fitted equipment at cost and depreciate them. Vehicles financed by banks carry a liability.

Running costs

  • Fuel, maintenance, tyres.
  • Drivers' and paramedics' wages.
  • Oxygen and consumables.
  • Registration, token, permits and insurance.
  • Parking or base rent near hospitals.

Sales tax on services

Health-related services are often favourably treated under provincial sales tax on services, but patient transport's exact classification under PRA should be checked for your operation. Most small operators are below thresholds.

Charitable and commercial

Several charities run free or subsidised ambulances. If you run a commercial service but also do some free trips, the free trips are simply costs without income. If you operate under a charity's name or through a trust, see family trusts for that side.

Advance tax on vehicles

Advance income tax collected with registration and token is a credit in your return if vehicles are in your name and you file.

Ambulance operators often keep vehicles in relatives' names. Keep them in the name of the person or business that runs the service, so costs, advance tax and income stay together.

Equipment rental

Renting out oxygen cylinders, hospital beds and wheelchairs to patients' families is a separate rental business: rental income, deposits held as liabilities, equipment as assets.

Medical attendants and home-care services

Some operators also supply attendants or nurses to care for patients at home after discharge. That's a separate service business: fees from families, wages to attendants, and a register of assignments. If attendants are paid a share of the fee, write the arrangement down; it affects whether they're employees or contractors.

Dead-body transport and funeral vans

Funeral transport — often to other cities — is part of many operators' work. Fares are income like any other trip. Charitable discounts or free trips are simply costs without income.

Near Jinnah Hospital — questions we get asked

Is my ambulance service's income taxable?
Yes. Fares and contract payments are business income, with fuel, wages, maintenance and depreciation as costs.
Hospitals pay me monthly with tax deducted. How do I claim it?
Collect their withholding certificates and claim them in your return.
Does an ambulance service need PRA registration?
Health-related services are often favourably treated, but patient transport's classification should be checked. Most small operators are below thresholds.
I rent out oxygen cylinders and beds. Is that the same business?
It's a separate rental activity. Deposits held are liabilities; equipment is an asset.
