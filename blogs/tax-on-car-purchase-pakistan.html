Advance Tax on Car Purchase Pakistan 2026 — Filer vs Non-Filer

June 2026 Kamboh Associates 6 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN • SECP • Sales Tax Specialist

Every car registration in Pakistan involves an advance tax collection under Section 231B — separate from sales tax, separate from the price on the tag. Here's exactly who collects it, how the rate scales with engine capacity, and what changes if you're a non-filer or leasing instead of buying outright.

TL;DR

Section 231B advance tax is collected at vehicle registration or transfer, scaling with engine capacity and roughly doubling for non-filers. New cars: the manufacturer/dealer collects it. Used car transfers: the excise office collects it. Leased vehicles: the leasing company collects it. It's adjustable against your annual tax liability. What Is Section 231B Advance Tax?

Section 231B of the Income Tax Ordinance requires advance income tax to be collected whenever a private motor vehicle is registered, whenever registration is transferred from one owner to another, and whenever a vehicle is leased. This is entirely separate from sales tax charged on the vehicle's price and from the annual token tax collected by the provincial excise department — it is a federal income tax collection mechanism, treating vehicle ownership as a proxy for taxable capacity the same way property and banking transactions are used elsewhere in the Ordinance. The tax is collected once at the triggering event (registration, transfer, or lease), not annually, and — like most withholding taxes — it is adjustable against the buyer's actual income tax liability for the year.

Who Collects It — Manufacturer, Excise Office, or Leasing Company

Who actually collects the tax depends on the transaction type. For a brand-new, locally manufactured or assembled vehicle, the manufacturer or authorized dealer collects advance tax at the time of sale and initial registration, folding it into the total amount paid at booking or delivery. For a transfer of an already-registered vehicle between two owners — the typical used-car sale — the provincial excise and taxation department collects it at the time registration is transferred into the new owner's name. For a vehicle acquired through a leasing arrangement rather than outright purchase, the leasing company itself collects advance tax at the time the lease is executed, applying the same rate structure. In every case, the tax lands on the buyer or lessee — not the seller.

The Ordinance sets these as fixed rupee amounts per engine-capacity bracket rather than a percentage of price, and every bracket's exact amount is revised through Finance Act notifications most years — sometimes upward, occasionally restructured entirely. Treat any specific rupee figure quoted online, including in past versions of this guide, as indicative rather than current; the excise office or your dealer will apply whatever the notified rate is at the time of your actual registration, and it's worth confirming that figure directly before budgeting a purchase.

New Purchase vs Used Transfer vs Lease

The engine-capacity rate structure is broadly similar across all three trigger events, but the collection point and paperwork differ meaningfully. A new car buyer sees the advance tax already built into the dealer's invoice — there's no separate visit to the excise office required for this specific tax. A used car buyer completing a transfer, by contrast, deals with the excise office directly as part of the transfer process, and the tax is calculated and collected there based on the vehicle's engine capacity and the buyer's filer status at that moment. A lessee taking a vehicle on lease has the leasing company collect the tax up front as part of the lease documentation — worth factoring into total lease cost comparisons, since it isn't always itemized as clearly as it would be on a cash purchase invoice.

Non-Filer Restrictions on Vehicle Registration

Beyond simply paying a higher rate, non-filers have at various points faced outright restrictions on registering vehicles above certain engine capacities — a policy that has been introduced, challenged in court, and revised more than once across recent Finance Acts. Because this specific restriction has changed materially between tax years and is more legally fluid than the underlying rate table, don't rely on a general understanding of "non-filers can't buy big cars" without confirming the current-year rule for the specific engine capacity you're considering. If registering as a non-filer is genuinely blocked for your intended vehicle, becoming a filer first — a same-day process for most individuals — resolves the restriction and also cuts the advance tax rate roughly in half.

Claiming Section 231B as a Credit in Your Return

Advance tax paid under Section 231B is adjustable, not a separate final cost sitting outside your tax return. When you file your annual income tax return, the amount collected at registration is entered as a tax credit and reduces your computed tax liability for the year — if your actual liability is lower than the advance tax already collected, the difference is refundable. This is one of the more commonly missed refund opportunities: buyers pay the advance tax at the dealer or excise office, then never file a return to claim it back, effectively leaving money with FBR that they were entitled to recover. Even someone whose total income is otherwise below the taxable threshold, and who wouldn't normally have a reason to file, can still benefit from filing a return purely to reclaim advance tax withheld on a vehicle purchase — the credit exists independently of whether the underlying income itself was taxable.

Imported and Used-Import Vehicles

Vehicles imported into Pakistan — whether brought in under a personal baggage scheme, gift scheme, or as a used import through a commercial importer — still go through local registration once they clear customs, and Section 231B applies at that registration exactly as it would to a locally manufactured car of the same engine capacity. Import duty and customs valuation are calculated separately at the port under an entirely different set of rules, so a buyer of an imported vehicle should budget for both: customs duty and taxes at import, and then Section 231B advance tax again at the point of first local registration. Overseas Pakistanis bringing a vehicle home under one of the import schemes sometimes assume the scheme's duty concessions also cover registration-stage advance tax — they don't; the two remain entirely independent obligations at two separate stages of getting the car legally on the road in Pakistan.

A Worked Example

Consider two buyers each registering a 1800cc sedan in the same month. Buyer A is an active filer — the excise office applies the filer-bracket advance tax for the 1601-1800cc range, and Buyer A pays that amount at transfer, which they later claim back in full as a credit against their annual tax liability since their actual tax due for the year comfortably exceeds it. Buyer B let their ATL status lapse two months earlier by missing a return deadline and is currently a non-filer — the same transaction triggers the non-filer rate for the same bracket, roughly double what Buyer A paid, and unless Buyer B refiles and gets back onto the ATL before other transactions that year, they face the same elevated rate on every subsequent withholding-taxed transaction too, not just this one vehicle purchase. The difference between the two buyers isn't the car, the dealer, or the province — it's entirely the two months of lapsed filer status, which is why tax consultants routinely advise clients to check their own ATL status before, not after, a major purchase.

Advance Tax vs Other Vehicle-Related Taxes

Section 231B is easy to confuse with other vehicle taxes because they're often paid around the same time, but each is a genuinely separate levy. Provincial token tax (motor vehicle tax) is an annual, recurring provincial tax paid to keep a vehicle legally on the road, unrelated to income tax and not adjustable against your FBR return. Sales tax on a new vehicle's price is charged by the manufacturer as part of the purchase price, following ordinary sales tax rules rather than Section 231B's withholding mechanism. Professional tax, where applicable, is a small provincial levy tied to income-earning activity, not vehicle ownership specifically. Only Section 231B advance tax is the one collected specifically because of registration/transfer/lease and creditable against your federal income tax return — the others are either recurring provincial obligations or ordinary purchase-price taxes with no refund mechanism through IRIS.

Common Mistakes With Vehicle Advance Tax

The most frequent error is assuming filer status is checked once and stays fixed — ATL status is verified at the moment of registration or transfer, so a buyer who let their filer status lapse (by missing a return deadline) can find themselves charged the higher non-filer rate even though they were a filer the previous year. Another common mistake is treating the advance tax as a sunk cost rather than an adjustable credit, and simply never filing a return to recover any excess. Used-car buyers sometimes also assume the seller is responsible for any tax due at transfer — in fact, the obligation and the higher non-filer exposure sit entirely with the buyer, so a buyer who is a non-filer pays the higher rate regardless of the seller's own status. Finally, some buyers negotiate a purchase price assuming the advance tax is a dealer or excise-office fee that can be waived or discounted — it can't; it's a statutory federal tax collected on FBR's behalf, and no dealer or excise official has discretion to reduce it below the notified rate for the applicable bracket.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is advance tax on car purchase under Section 231B in Pakistan?
Section 231B requires advance tax collection at vehicle registration, transfer, or lease. For a new vehicle, the manufacturer/dealer collects it at sale; for a transfer, the excise authority collects it. The rate depends on engine capacity and filer status.
How much is advance tax on car purchase by engine capacity in 2026?
Rates scale up with engine capacity and roughly double for non-filers compared to filers. Because these fixed rupee amounts are revised through Finance Act notifications, confirm the current-year slab for your specific engine capacity before finalizing a purchase.
Can a non-filer buy a car in Pakistan?
Non-filers can generally still register vehicles but pay a substantially higher rate, and restrictions above certain engine capacities have been introduced and revised across different Finance Acts and court rulings. Confirm the current-year rule for your engine capacity before assuming either outcome.
Is advance tax on car purchase different for a leased vehicle?
Yes. Where a vehicle is leased, the leasing company collects advance tax at the time of leasing using the same filer/non-filer rate structure, separate from any tax collected later when the vehicle is registered in the lessee's own name.
Is Section 231B advance tax adjustable against my annual tax liability?
Yes. It's credited against your total annual income tax liability when you file your return. If it exceeds your actual liability, the excess is refundable, provided you file a return and claim it.
Does selling my personal car trigger any tax for me as the seller?
No. Section 231B applies to the buyer at registration/transfer, not the seller of a personal-use vehicle. Only someone who buys and sells vehicles as a business activity has income tax implications on the profit from those transactions.
