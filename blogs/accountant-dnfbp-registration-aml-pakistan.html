Accountants and Company Service Providers (Non-ICAP/ICMAP): DNFBP Registration and AML Rules in Pakistan

10 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 10 November 2026
Accountants who are not ICAP or ICMAP members become DNFBPs through the services they provide, not through their title or turnover. There is no minimum threshold.

For accountants and company service providers, the hard question is not how to comply but whether the rules apply to them at all. FBR's February 2023 FAQs answer that in unusual detail, with service-by-service examples. This guide brings those answers together for practitioners, consultancy firms and corporate service providers who are not regulated by ICAP or ICMAP.

TL;DR

Under FBR's AML/CFT regime, sole practitioners, partners and employees of firms not regulated by ICAP or ICMAP are DNFBPs when they carry out any of 14 listed services: for example managing client money or accounts, forming companies or firms on a client's behalf, acting as or arranging a director, partner, trustee or nominee shareholder, or providing a registered or correspondence address. Tax advisory, statutory audit, internal audit and pure bookkeeping are not covered. There is no financial threshold, and free services count too. Covered firms must register with FBR, carry out CDD on the clients who receive those services, report STRs to FMU on goAML and keep records.

Who Counts as an "Accountant" for AML Purposes

The AML Act, 2010 lists accountants as DNFBPs in Section 2(xii). FBR supervises those other than members regulated by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Pakistan (ICAP) and the Institute of Cost and Management Accountants of Pakistan (ICMAP); those institutes supervise their own members. FBR's definition covers sole practitioners, partners and professionals employed in firms when they carry out the listed activities. FBR's FAQs group these activities as "Accountant" or "Trust and Company Service Provider" (TCSP) services, together called specified services.

Two rules from the FAQs surprise many firms:

  • No threshold. Coverage depends only on the services provided. There is no turnover, fee or transaction limit (FAQ 19).
  • Free work counts. A specified service provided without a fee still makes the firm a DNFBP (FAQ 20).

Covered or Not? FBR's Own Answers

FBR's FAQs answer many borderline questions directly. The most useful, in one place:

Registering a company with SECP for a client, or forming a partnership, firm or societyYesActing as a formation agent; filing incorporation forms is a TCSP activity (FAQs 3 and 10).
Filing annual returns and statutory forms (Form A, B, 29, 45) after incorporationNoA routine regulatory requirement under the Companies Act, 2017 (FAQ 11).

Certifying the SBP Foreign Royalty Payment Form, or receipt of subscription money for a rights issueNoNot within the definitions (FAQs 13 and 22).

"Arranging" has a narrow meaning. It applies, for example, where you supply a director yourself, choose them without going back to the client, and complete the appointment formalities. Normal headhunting or advertising for a candidate does not count, and neither does sharing a list of candidates when you have no say in the selection (FAQs 3 and 4).

"Ordinary course of business" is a judgment call. FBR suggests asking whether the activity is normal, frequent, regular, involves significant money or resources, earns income, or is offered as a service to customers (FAQ 5).

The Accountant Population Determination Survey

FBR's FAQs state that all practising accountants are required to submit the Accountant Population Determination Survey (APDS). FBR uses it to identify which practices provide specified services. Submitting the survey is separate from DNFBP registration. If your answers show you provide specified services, the full DNFBP duties apply.

What Changes for a Covered Firm

  • Register with FBR as a DNFBP and on goAML with FMU.
  • Appoint a compliance officer from senior management. The role does not have to be standalone; the person can carry out other duties too (FAQ 29).
  • CDD on the right clients. CDD is mandatory for existing and new clients who receive Accountant or TCSP services. For other services such as audit it is not mandatory, though FBR encourages it (FAQ 17).
  • Enhanced due diligence for high-value engagements. Where you will manage significant assets or funds above a threshold set in your own AML policy, obtain more information and verification of source of funds or wealth, and senior management approval before onboarding (FAQ 8).
  • Beneficial ownership. For companies, identify the natural persons who ultimately own or control the client, using the company's register of ultimate beneficial owners.
  • Report STRs promptly to FMU on goAML as soon as suspicion arises, not at year end (FAQ 15).
  • Keep records: CDD for at least five years, STR and CTR records for at least ten.

Red Flags FMU Lists for Accountants

  • A corporate structure more complex than the business needs, or links to nominee shareholders, bearer shares or foreign shell companies.
  • A client who is secretive about the beneficial owner or the source of funds, or who provides fabricated records.
  • Assets abroad that are not declared in the client's tax return, or loans from unidentified parties.
  • A company dormant after incorporation that suddenly shows large activity, or high turnover with no physical presence or visible business.
  • An address shared by many other companies, suggesting a mailbox service, or the same directors and owners across many companies.
  • Payments to related companies with the same directors or owners, without a plausible reason.
  • Unusually high fees offered for routine work, or sudden last-minute changes in how payment is made.
  • Trusts or waqf set up in a way that hides ownership, or that hold the shares of a company.

A Practical Approach for Small Practices

  1. List your services and mark each one against the table above.
  2. If none is a specified service, keep a short written note of that assessment and review it when you add services.
  3. If any is, register, appoint the compliance officer, and apply CDD to the clients who receive those services.
  4. Adopt engagement terms that ask for CDD documents at onboarding and allow you to decline work when information is refused.
  5. Train staff on red flags and tipping off.

Company formation is the service that most often brings small practices into scope. If you register companies with SECP for clients, treat it as a specified service.

Onboarding a Client for a Specified Service

  1. Identify the service: confirm which of the 14 activities you are being engaged for.
  2. Identify the client: CNIC for individuals; for companies, incorporation documents and the register of ultimate beneficial owners.
  3. Identify the beneficial owners and anyone acting on the client's behalf.
  4. Screen the client, beneficial owners and agents against the sanctions lists.
  5. Understand the purpose: why the company is being formed, why client money is being managed, what the address service is for.
  6. Rate the risk and, for higher risk or high-value engagements above your policy threshold, obtain source of funds or wealth and senior approval.
  7. Engage in writing, with terms that require CDD documents and allow you to withdraw if information is refused.

Where TCSP Services Are Most Exposed

FMU's red flags for accountants highlight the situations most open to misuse:

  • Nominee arrangements: forming a company through a nominee director, or acting as or arranging a nominee shareholder, is a specified service, and nominee structures can hide the real owner.
  • Registered address services: an address shared by many companies suggests a mailbox service, which FMU flags.
  • Dormant then active companies: a company inactive after incorporation that suddenly shows large financial activity.
  • Trusts and waqf: arrangements that obscure ownership or hold shares of a company.

None of these services is prohibited; they require closer due diligence and a clear record of why the client needs them.

Sample Note: Practice Not in Scope

If your assessment shows you provide no specified services, keep a short dated note on file. For example:

Assessment of AML/CFT applicability, [date]. Services provided: tax returns, tax advisory, bookkeeping and statutory filings after incorporation. None of these is a specified service under FBR's AML/CFT Regulations (FBR FAQs, February 2023, questions 6, 11 and 24). The practice does not form companies, manage client funds, provide registered addresses or act as nominee or trustee. To be reviewed if services change. Signed: [partner].

Review the note whenever you add a service. Company formation is the addition that most often changes the answer.

Practices With Mixed Services

Many practices do both: mostly tax and bookkeeping, plus a few company formations a year. FBR's FAQs say CDD is mandatory for clients receiving Accountant or TCSP services and encouraged for others. In practice, apply full CDD to every formation or client-money client, keep a lighter identification file for the rest, and register as a DNFBP because the specified services are part of your ordinary business.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are all accountants in Pakistan DNFBPs?
No. FBR supervises accountants not regulated by ICAP or ICMAP, and only when they provide one of the 14 listed services, such as managing client money, forming companies or providing a registered address.
Does tax return filing or tax advice make me a DNFBP?
No. FBR's 2023 FAQs say tax advisory services do not fall within the Trust and Company Service Provider definition. You may still be covered if you also provide a listed service.
Is registering a company with SECP for a client covered?
Yes. FBR's FAQs treat registering a company or forming a firm on a client's behalf as acting as a formation agent, which is a specified service.
Are annual SECP returns covered?
No. Filing annual returns and statutory forms after incorporation is a routine requirement under the Companies Act, 2017 and is not a reporting activity, according to FBR's FAQs.
Is there a turnover threshold for accountants?
No. FBR's FAQs say coverage is based on the services provided and there is no financial limit, threshold or benchmark.
If I do the service for free, am I still covered?
Yes. FBR's FAQs say an entity providing these services without any fee is still classified as a DNFBP.
Is statutory audit or bookkeeping covered?
No. Statutory audit, internal audit and pure bookkeeping are not specified services under FBR's FAQs.
What is the APDS?
The Accountant Population Determination Survey. FBR's FAQs say all practising accountants are required to submit it.
Must I do CDD on all my clients?
CDD is mandatory for clients who receive Accountant or Trust and Company Service Provider services. For other services, such as audit, it is not mandatory but FBR encourages it.
Is acting as a nominee shareholder allowed?
It is not prohibited, but acting as or arranging a nominee shareholder is a specified service under FBR's list, and nominee structures require close due diligence.
Should I keep a record if my practice is not in scope?
Yes. A short, dated assessment note explaining why your services are outside the 14 activities is good practice and should be reviewed when services change.
Do I need full CDD for every client if I do a few company formations?
CDD is mandatory for clients receiving specified services; for other clients FBR encourages it. Applying full CDD to formation clients and lighter identification to others is a practical approach.
Does a single company formation make my practice a DNFBP?
FBR's test looks at specified services carried out in the ordinary course of business, considering factors such as frequency, regularity and whether it is a service you offer. A regular formation service brings you into scope.
