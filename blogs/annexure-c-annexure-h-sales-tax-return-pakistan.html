Annexure-C and Annexure-H — What These Monthly Sales Tax Attachments Actually Are

September 2026 Kamboh Associates 9 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist

Anyone who has looked at a sales tax return form in Pakistan has run into a set of annexures with letter names — C, H, and others — that can feel like unexplained jargon at first. These are not optional extras; they are where most of the actual substance of a monthly return lives. This guide gives a plain-language explanation of two of the most commonly referenced ones.

TL;DR

The main sales tax return form is a summary; the annexures are where the detailed, invoice-level data actually lives. Annexure-C generally captures sales/output-side invoice detail, while Annexure-H (relevant to specific categories, including certain stock/closing position reporting) captures a different dimension of the business's position. Getting the underlying invoice-level data right in the correct annexure is what makes the summary figures on the main return accurate — errors usually originate at the annexure level, not the summary level.

Why the Return Is Split Into a Summary and Annexures

The main sales tax return form presents a compact summary — total output tax, total input tax, net payable. But FBR's system needs the underlying, invoice-by-invoice detail behind those summary numbers, both to allow proper cross-verification between buyers and sellers and to support any future review. The annexures are where that detail is submitted — each one structured for a specific category of information, feeding into the summary figures on the main form.

Annexure-C — Sales-Side Invoice Detail

Annexure-C is generally where the detailed record of sales invoices for the period is reported — each individual sale, the buyer's registration details where applicable, the value, and the tax charged. This is the data that ultimately builds up to the output tax figure on the summary return, and it is also the data that a buyer's own input tax claim gets cross-checked against on the other side of the transaction. Errors or omissions here do not just affect your own return — they can create a mismatch for whichever buyer is trying to claim input tax based on your invoice.

Annexure-H — Stock and Position Reporting

Annexure-H is associated with reporting on stock or closing position for relevant categories of registered persons — essentially a periodic snapshot that supports the overall picture of goods moving through the business, beyond just the transaction-level sales and purchase data. Not every registered business is required to complete every annexure; which ones apply depends on the nature and category of the business's registration, which is worth confirming rather than assuming a one-size-fits-all set of requirements.

Why Accuracy Has to Start at the Annexure Level

A common misunderstanding is treating the summary figures on the main return as the "real" numbers and the annexures as supporting paperwork filled in afterward to match. In practice it works the other way — the annexure-level, invoice-by-invoice data is the actual source of truth, and the summary figures should be a direct output of that detailed data, not a separately estimated number that the annexures are then made to fit. Building the process annexure-first, summary-second, produces far fewer reconciliation problems.

A Practical Monthly Workflow Around the Annexures

  • Compile the month's sales invoices in the format the relevant annexure requires, as invoices are issued during the month rather than all at once at month-end.
  • Compile purchase invoices with the same discipline, checking each one meets input tax documentation standards as it comes in.
  • Reconcile any stock or position reporting required for your specific registration category.
  • Let the summary return figures be generated from this underlying detail, and review the summary as a check on the detail rather than the primary data entry point.

Annexure-C and Annexure-H Are Not the Only Ones

Beyond these two, the sales tax return system includes several other annexures covering different categories of information — depending on the nature of the business, additional schedules may apply for specific transaction types, particular industries, or specific reporting requirements tied to a given registration category. Rather than trying to memorize every letter-named annexure in the abstract, the more useful approach is confirming, for your specific business, exactly which annexures apply and building a monthly checklist around that confirmed, specific list.

What Happens When an Annexure Contains an Error

Because the annexures are the actual source data behind the summary figures, an error at the annexure level does not stay contained to a cosmetic detail — it flows directly into the output or input tax totals on the main return, and depending on the nature of the error, it can also create a mismatch when cross-verified against a counterparty's own filing. This is why annexure-level accuracy deserves the same seriousness as the summary figures themselves, rather than being treated as secondary paperwork that simply needs to be attached.

Making Annexure Preparation Less Time-Consuming Each Month

For businesses with meaningful transaction volume, manually compiling annexure data invoice-by-invoice each month is genuinely time-consuming if done from scratch every time. Maintaining a consistent, organized invoicing system throughout the month — rather than a folder of loosely collected paper or scattered digital receipts assembled only at month-end — makes the difference between annexure preparation being a quick, largely mechanical export and it being a stressful, error-prone scramble against the deadline.

Software-Assisted Versus Fully Manual Annexure Preparation

Some businesses use accounting or invoicing software capable of exporting data in formats close to what the annexures require, which meaningfully reduces the manual re-entry burden each month, while others — particularly smaller businesses with simpler operations — manage perfectly well with a well-organized manual spreadsheet approach. Neither is inherently wrong; the right choice depends on transaction volume and the complexity of the business, and a system that feels adequate at a smaller scale is worth revisiting as transaction volume grows, since manual approaches that worked fine at low volume can become the actual bottleneck once volume increases.

A Final Review Step Before Submitting

Even with a well-organized process, a brief final review of the compiled annexure data against a simple sanity check — does the total roughly match what you expected for the month, are there any invoices with unusually large or unusual values worth double-checking, are there any obvious duplicates — catches a meaningful share of errors before submission, at a fraction of the effort of correcting them afterward through a formal revision. This final check should be a deliberate, distinct step, not just an assumption that the compiled data is automatically correct because it was compiled carefully.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the main sales tax return and the annexures?
The main return is a compact summary of totals. The annexures contain the detailed, invoice-by-invoice data that those summary totals are actually built from.
What does Annexure-C generally cover?
It generally captures detailed sales-side invoice information for the period — individual sales, buyer details where applicable, values, and tax charged — which feeds into the output tax figure.
What is Annexure-H used for?
It is associated with stock or closing position reporting for relevant categories of registered persons, supporting a broader picture of goods movement beyond just transaction-level sales and purchase data.
Do all registered businesses need to complete every annexure?
No — which annexures apply depends on the nature and category of the specific registration. Confirm which ones are relevant to your business rather than assuming a uniform requirement.
Should I fill in the summary return first or the annexures first?
The annexures first. The detailed invoice-level data in the annexures is the actual source of truth, and the summary figures should be generated from that detail, not filled in separately and then matched.
Are Annexure-C and Annexure-H the only ones I might need to worry about?
No — several other annexures exist for different categories of information, and which ones apply depends on your specific business and registration category. Confirm the exact list relevant to you rather than assuming only these two matter.
Does an error in an annexure matter as much as an error in the summary figures?
Yes, arguably more — annexure errors flow directly into the summary totals and can create cross-verification mismatches with a counterparty's own filing, so they deserve the same seriousness as the headline numbers.
How can I make preparing the annexures less time-consuming each month?
Keep a consistent, organized invoicing system throughout the month rather than assembling scattered receipts at month-end — this turns annexure preparation into a quick, largely mechanical export instead of a stressful scramble.
Do I need accounting software to prepare annexures properly?
Not necessarily — smaller, simpler businesses often manage fine with a well-organized manual spreadsheet. Software becomes more valuable as transaction volume grows and manual re-entry starts to become the actual bottleneck.
Is a final review before submitting the annexures worth the extra time?
Yes — a brief sanity check for unexpected totals, unusually large values, or obvious duplicates catches many errors before submission, at far less effort than correcting them afterward through a formal revision.
Who should perform this final review — the same person who compiled the data?
A second set of eyes, even briefly, tends to catch more than the person who compiled the data reviewing their own work, since they are more likely to overlook their own assumptions.
Does the format required for annexures ever change?
It can, as FBR updates its systems and requirements over time, so it is worth confirming the current expected format each time rather than assuming last year's template is still exactly correct.
Is it normal for annexure preparation to take longer in some months than others?
Yes — months with higher transaction volume, more suppliers, or more unusual transactions naturally take longer to compile properly, and budgeting extra time in those specific months rather than assuming every month takes the same effort helps avoid a rushed filing.
Are the annexure requirements the same for every industry?
Not entirely — some categories of business have additional or slightly different annexure requirements specific to their sector, so confirm what applies to your particular industry rather than assuming a generic list covers everyone.
Where can I find the current, official annexure requirements for my category?
FBR's own portal and notified rules are the authoritative source, and it is worth reconfirming there periodically rather than relying solely on what a previous filing looked like.
