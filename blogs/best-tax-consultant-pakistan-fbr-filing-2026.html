TL;DR — Key Takeaways
  • A qualified tax consultant in Pakistan must be an FBR-active filer and familiar with current Finance Act provisions
  • Red flags: consultants who guarantee "no tax payable" or ask for cash payments with no receipts
  • For salaried employees, look for consultants who understand Section 149 employer WHT reconciliation
  • For businesses, prioritise consultants with WHT agent and sales tax return filing experience
  • Always confirm your return was actually submitted — FBR IRIS has the "Filed Returns" view for verification

Pakistan has thousands of tax consultants offering FBR filing services. Some are experienced professionals with deep knowledge of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001 and Sales Tax Act 1990. Others are one-man operations using outdated software with no understanding of Finance Act 2026 changes. Choosing the wrong consultant can result in returns filed with incorrect income declarations, wrong WHT credits, or missing wealth statement entries — all of which attract FBR notices and penalties.

This guide provides a practical framework for identifying the best tax consultant in Pakistan for your specific situation — whether you are a salaried employee, freelancer, business owner, or overseas Pakistani.

Qualifications and Credentials to Look For

Pakistan does not yet have a mandatory licensing system for individual tax consultants (unlike chartered accountants and cost accountants who are licensed through ICAP and ICMAP). However, certain credentials indicate a serious practitioner:

  • FBR Active Filer: The consultant must themselves be an active taxpayer on the FBR ATL. Check the consultant's NTN on the FBR ATL portal — if they are not filing their own taxes, they should not be filing yours
  • ICAP/ICMAP membership: Chartered accountants (CA) and certified management accountants (CMA) have structured training in tax law. For complex corporate returns, these qualifications matter
  • Tax Law Familiarity: A good consultant should be able to cite sections — Section 149 (salary WHT), Section 153 (contractor WHT), Section 147 (advance tax), Section 182 (late filing penalty) — without looking them up
  • Finance Act 2026 awareness: Tax law changes every July. Ask the consultant about key changes in Finance Act 2026 — if they cannot answer, their knowledge is outdated

How to Evaluate a Tax Consultant — 7 Questions to Ask

  1. "Do you have your own NTN? Can I check your ATL status?" — The answer should be yes, and they should welcome the verification
  2. "How do you handle the wealth statement?" — The wealth statement is the most common source of FBR notices. A good consultant knows that every asset, liability, and income item must reconcile
  3. "What version of IRIS do you use for filing?" — Serious consultants use IRIS directly, not third-party software that may not update for Finance Act changes in real time
  4. "Have you worked with clients in my industry?" — Freelancers, property investors, overseas Pakistanis, and corporate entities each have different tax treatment. Industry-specific experience matters
  5. "What happens if FBR sends me a notice after you file?" — The answer should include support for at least 12 months post-filing, not an additional charge for every interaction
  6. "Can I see my draft return before you submit it?" — Never allow a consultant to submit without showing you the return first. You are legally responsible for the accuracy of your own return
  7. "What is your refund policy if there is an error?" — Errors happen. A professional will acknowledge this and offer to fix at no charge

Red Flags — Warning Signs of Unqualified Consultants

Avoid consultants who:
  • Guarantee "zero tax" without reviewing your actual income and asset position — this usually means underdeclaring income, which creates Section 111 audit risk
  • Ask for cash payments with no invoice or receipt — professional consultants issue receipts
  • Cannot tell you which IRIS section your wealth statement entries go into
  • Promise ATL appearance without filing a return — ATL appearance requires an actually-filed return
  • Offer to "fix" past returns by amending them with lower income — this is tax fraud under Section 192 and creates criminal liability
  • Cannot provide references or show you previous client returns (appropriately redacted)

How to Verify Your Return Was Actually Filed

A common complaint: clients pay a consultant and assume their return was filed, only to receive an FBR non-filer notice months later. After your return is filed:

  1. Go to IRIS (iris.fbr.gov.pk) and log in with your NTN/CNIC and password
  2. Click "Filed Returns" in the left panel
  3. Verify the return for the relevant income tax return filing appears with status "Submitted"
  4. Download the filed return and acknowledgment slip for your records
  5. Confirm your ATL status at atl.fbr.gov.pk within 48 hours of filing
Best practice: Never hand over your IRIS login credentials to a consultant. Instead, ask the consultant to prepare the return draft and share it for your review. Then either you submit it yourself, or the consultant submits through a tax agent profile linked to their own NTN — not your personal IRIS credentials.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a chartered accountant or a tax consultant for my income tax return?

For individuals — salaried employees, freelancers, overseas Pakistanis — a qualified tax consultant is sufficient. Chartered accountants (CAs) are recommended for private limited companies that require audited financial statements, complex corporate transactions, or SECP compliance. A good tax consultant can handle the majority of individual and small business cases effectively at lower cost than CA firms.

What should a tax consultant charge for a simple salaried return?

Simple salaried employee returns typically range from Rs. 2,500 to Rs. 8,000 depending on complexity (number of employers, bonus income, investment declarations, rental income). Returns with wealth statements and business income are priced higher — Rs. 8,000 to Rs. 25,000+ depending on turnover and number of transactions. Be cautious of very low prices below Rs. 1,500 which often indicate inexperienced preparers using template returns.

How do I check if a tax consultant is an FBR active filer themselves?

Ask the consultant for their NTN or CNIC number and check it on the FBR Active Taxpayer List at atl.fbr.gov.pk. Enter the NTN or CNIC on the portal and it shows whether the person appears on the current ATL for TY2026. A consultant not on the ATL is not filing their own taxes — a major credibility issue.

Can a tax consultant represent me before FBR in case of a notice?

Yes. Tax consultants can be authorized representatives before tax authorities at the assessment stage (responding to notices, attending hearings before the Commissioner). However, for appeals before the Commissioner of Inland Revenue Appeals (CIR-A) or ATIR, it is advisable to engage a consultant familiar with formal appeal procedures, including the 10% mandatory payment requirement under Section 127 for CIR-A appeals.

Is it safe to share my CNIC and IRIS password with a tax consultant?

Sharing your CNIC for NTN registration is necessary and standard practice. However, sharing your IRIS login password carries risk — a consultant with access to your IRIS can file or amend returns without your approval. Better practice: let the consultant prepare the return, review the draft yourself, then either file it yourself or use a tax agent authorization within IRIS that gives limited access rather than full password access.

What happens if my tax consultant makes an error in my return?

You as the taxpayer remain legally responsible for your own return — errors by a consultant do not transfer legal liability to the consultant. However, errors can be corrected by filing a revised return under Section 114(6) within 5 years of the original due date. If the error leads to an FBR notice or penalty, a professional consultant should assist with the correction and response at no additional charge. This is why it is important to work with consultants who stand behind their work.
