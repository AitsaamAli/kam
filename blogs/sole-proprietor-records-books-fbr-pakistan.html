Sole Proprietor Records and Books in Pakistan: What FBR Expects You to Keep, and for How Long

10 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 10 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 10 November 2026
Records are what turn a return into a defensible return. The law requires six years of them, and longer if a case is pending.

Most disputes between small businesses and FBR are not about the law but about evidence. A sole proprietor who can show where each rupee came from and went usually wins; one who cannot often loses deductions even for genuine expenses. This guide explains what the law requires and how to keep records in a way that is simple enough to actually do.

TL;DR

Section 174 of the Income Tax Ordinance requires every taxpayer to keep the accounts, documents and records prescribed, in Pakistan, for six years after the end of the tax year they relate to, and until any pending proceeding is finally decided. The Commissioner can disallow or reduce a deduction if you cannot produce a receipt or other evidence without reasonable cause. For income, assets or expenses covered by Section 111(2)(ii), the time limit does not apply.

What the Law Requires

A Simple System That Works

  1. Separate bank account for the business, used for every business receipt and payment where possible.
  2. One folder per month, physical or digital, with sales, purchases, expenses and bank statement.
  3. Scan or photograph receipts the day you get them. Paper fades.
  4. Monthly summary of sales, purchases, expenses and drawings. It takes an hour and makes the annual return straightforward.
  5. Annual archive labelled by tax year, kept for at least six years after the year ends.

Cash Payments and Drawings

Cash is where small businesses lose most arguments. Pay suppliers and staff through the bank where you can, record cash expenses on the day with the payee's name, and record the owner's drawings separately from business expenses. Money you take out for household spending is not a business expense, and it must line up with your wealth statement.

When Records Are Asked For

Officers usually ask for records through a notice: to explain income, to support a deduction, or as part of an audit. Reply on time, provide what is asked in an organised form, and keep a copy of what you submitted. Our FBR notice defence service prepares replies where the records are complex.

Common Record-Keeping Mistakes

  • Throwing records away after five years. The income tax period is six years, and longer while a case is pending.
  • Keeping only bank statements, not the invoices behind them.
  • Running business payments through a family member's account.
  • Claiming round-figure expenses with no receipts.

What Poor Records Can Cost

FailurePenalty under Section 182

On top of penalties, Section 174(2) lets the Commissioner disallow or reduce expenses you cannot support, which increases taxable income and the tax on it. Records are the cheapest protection a small business has.

A Month-End Routine in Ten Steps

  1. Download the business bank statement.
  2. Match each deposit to a sale or other receipt.
  3. Match each payment to a bill, salary sheet or receipt.
  4. Record cash sales from the daily summaries.
  5. Record cash expenses with the payee's name and purpose.
  6. Separate the owner's drawings from business expenses.
  7. File withholding certificates received from customers.
  8. File proof of tax you deducted and deposited, if any.
  9. Note stock purchases and any large stock movements.
  10. Store the month's folder, physical or digital, under the tax year.

Keeping Records Digitally

Photographs and scans are practical, but they must be legible and retrievable for the whole retention period. Use one folder per tax year and month, name files consistently (date, party, amount), and keep a backup in a second place. Remember that Section 174(1) requires records to be maintained in Pakistan, and Section 174(5) allows FBR to require specified electronic systems for some persons or classes; if your business is notified, the prescribed system takes priority over your own method.

Records Behind the Wealth Statement

The wealth statement compares what you owned at the start and end of the year with your income and spending. For a sole proprietor, the business records feed it directly: closing stock, business bank balances, receivables, payables and business assets all appear in it. If business records are incomplete, the reconciliation does not balance, which is one of the most common triggers for notices.

Making Cash Sales Credible

Cash businesses are usually questioned on whether all sales were declared. The answer is a consistent trail: a daily sales summary or POS report, regular bank deposits that match it, and stock records showing purchases moving into sales. A shop that deposits takings irregularly, or keeps no daily figure, has little to show when an officer estimates sales from other information.

If Records Are Lost or Damaged

  1. Record what happened and when, with any police report or insurance claim for theft or fire.
  2. Rebuild what you can from bank statements, supplier ledgers, marketplace reports and copies held by your accountant.
  3. Ask suppliers and customers for copies of invoices.
  4. Keep a note of what could not be recovered and why; Section 174(2) allows deductions to be disallowed only where you cannot provide evidence without reasonable cause.

Records for Advance Tax and Withholding

Keep advance tax PSIDs and paid challans with the tax year they relate to, so they can be claimed in the return. If you deduct tax from others, keep the payroll calculations, rent agreements, supplier details, deposit challans and quarterly statements as a separate set; see our withholding guide for the full list.

What the Annual File Should Contain

  • Twelve monthly folders and summaries.
  • Stock count and valuation at the year end.
  • List of business assets with purchase documents.
  • Bank statements for the full year for every business account.
  • Withholding certificates received and, if applicable, tax you deducted and deposited.
  • Advance tax challans.
  • The filed return, wealth statement and acknowledgement.
  • Any notices received and the replies sent.

Being Audit-Ready Without Extra Work

An audit under Section 177 asks for the same documents you should already be keeping. If your monthly folders, summaries and annual file are in order, responding is mostly a matter of copying and indexing. Number the documents you submit, keep a list of what was given and when, and keep copies. That list is your evidence if an officer later says something was not produced.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long must a sole proprietor keep records?
Six years after the end of the tax year under Section 174(3), and until any pending proceeding is finally decided.
What happens if I cannot produce a receipt for an expense?
Under Section 174(2), the Commissioner may disallow or reduce the deduction if you cannot provide a receipt or other evidence without reasonable cause.
Can I keep records digitally?
Digital copies are practical, but the law requires records to be maintained in Pakistan and producible when asked. FBR can also require specific electronic systems under Section 174(5).
Are bank statements enough?
Usually not. Bank statements show money moving; invoices, receipts and contracts show what it was for.
Does the six-year limit always apply?
No. It does not apply to records of income, assets, expenses or transactions covered by Section 111(2)(ii), and records must be kept while proceedings are pending.
What is the penalty for not keeping records?
Under Section 182, Rs 10,000 or 5% of the tax on income, whichever is higher, for failing to maintain records required under the Ordinance or rules.
What happens if I do not produce records in an audit?
Under Section 182, penalties of Rs 100,000, Rs 200,000 and Rs 300,000 apply for failing to produce records on the first, second and third notices under Section 177.
Until when must I keep tax year 2026 records?
At least until 30 June 2032, six years after the tax year ended on 30 June 2026, and longer if a proceeding is pending.
Do business records affect my wealth statement?
Yes. Stock, business bank balances, receivables, payables and assets from the business all feed into the wealth statement reconciliation.
What evidence works best for rent?
A rent agreement, bank transfers to the landlord and, where you deducted tax, the deposit challan.
What if my records were destroyed?
Document the event, rebuild what you can from bank statements and third-party copies, and keep a note of what was lost; Section 174(2) allows disallowance only where evidence is missing without reasonable cause.
How do I support cash sales?
Keep daily sales summaries or POS reports, deposit takings regularly so bank deposits match, and keep stock records.
Is a monthly summary enough, or do I need full books?
For many sole proprietors, a monthly summary built from organised invoices, receipts and bank statements is enough to prepare a defensible return, but the law requires the underlying records to be kept for six years.
What should I keep at the year end?
A stock count and valuation at 30 June, a list of business assets, full-year bank statements, tax certificates, advance tax challans and the filed return.
What should I do when an audit notice asks for records?
Reply by the date given, submit the documents in an organised, numbered set, and keep a list and copies of what you provided.
