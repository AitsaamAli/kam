How to Respond to FBR Tax Notice in Pakistan 2026

June 2026 Kamboh Associates 8 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN • SECP • Sales Tax Specialist — FBR Active Filer | NTN • SECP • Sales Tax Specialist

FBR sends various types of notices to taxpayers in Pakistan — for verification, audit, unpaid tax demands, or Section 111 unexplained income. Ignoring any FBR notice is dangerous. This guide explains every type of FBR notice and exactly how to respond to each one correctly.

TL;DR

FBR IRIS is the online portal for all Pakistani tax compliance: NTN registration, income tax returns, wealth statements, WHT payments, and FBR notice responses. Register free at iris.fbr.gov.pk.

General Rules for Responding to Any FBR Notice

  • Never ignore it: Ignoring a notice allows FBR to pass an ex-parte order (against you without your input)
  • Note the deadline: Mark it in your calendar immediately. Request an extension in writing if you need more time
  • Respond in writing: All responses must be in writing with supporting documents. Oral responses have no legal weight
  • Keep copies: Keep a copy of your response with the receiving stamp or registered mail receipt
  • Engage a consultant early: The earlier a qualified tax consultant reviews the notice, the better the outcome

How to Respond to an FBR Section 111 Notice (Unexplained Income)

This is the most common serious notice. Respond by:

  1. Identifying which amount FBR is questioning
  2. Gathering documentary evidence for the source: salary certificate, loan agreement, gift deed, property sale proceeds
  3. Filing any missing returns for the years in question
  4. Drafting a point-by-point reply addressing each questioned amount with attached evidence
  5. Submitting to the relevant Commissioner before the deadline

How to Respond to an FBR Audit Notice (Section 177)

  1. Identify the scope: which tax years are included and what specifically is being examined
  2. Compile all documents: returns, wealth statements, bank statements, business records
  3. Do not submit anything before your consultant reviews it — once submitted, it becomes the official record
  4. Attend hearings with complete documentation and your consultant
  5. Respond to the draft order within the deadline given

How to Respond to a Non-Filing Notice (Section 114)

The simplest response: file your income tax return immediately. Once the return is filed, the non-filing notice is resolved automatically. File late returns with payment of late filing penalty (Rs. 1,000/day, max Rs. 50,000 for individuals).

What if FBR Issues a Tax Demand You Disagree With?

You have the right to appeal within 30 days:

  • First appeal: Commissioner Inland Revenue (Appeals) — Section 127
  • Second appeal: Appellate Tribunal Inland Revenue — Section 131
  • High Court: Reference on points of law only — Section 133
  • ADR: Alternative Dispute Resolution under Section 134A — faster settlement

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if I receive an FBR notice but I have already paid all my taxes?
Respond to the notice by submitting your filed returns, payment receipts, and any other evidence showing compliance. Even correctly filed taxpayers get selected for audit. Your clean records will resolve the matter. Never ignore a notice even if you are fully compliant.
Can I hire a lawyer or consultant to respond to an FBR notice on my behalf?
Yes. You can authorize a tax consultant or lawyer through a signed Power of Attorney to represent you before FBR. They can appear at hearings, submit documents, and file appeals on your behalf. This is highly recommended for Section 111 notices and audit proceedings.
How long does FBR take to close a notice after I respond?
It depends on the notice type. Simple non-filing notices close within days of filing the return. Audit proceedings can take 3–6 months. Section 111 cases vary — with a strong response and proper documentation, many are resolved within 60–90 days. Complex cases may go to appeal and take longer.

Documents to Prepare Before Responding to Any FBR Notice

The quality of your response depends entirely on the documents you provide. Gather the following before drafting your reply:

  • Copy of the FBR notice — note the IRIS notice number, issuance date, and response deadline
  • Filed income tax returns — for the tax year(s) mentioned in the notice
  • Wealth statements — all years referenced, showing asset and liability positions
  • Bank statements — 12 months for the relevant tax year, all accounts
  • Salary certificates / payslips — if your income is employment-based
  • Property purchase/sale documents — if the notice relates to real estate transactions
  • Business records — profit and loss, invoices, GST returns, if self-employed
  • Loan agreements / gift deeds — for amounts received from family or third parties
  • Foreign remittance records — bank advice slips if income came from abroad

Important: Never submit original documents to FBR — always submit attested photocopies and retain originals. Keep a copy of your complete response package with proof of submission.

Common Mistakes That Make FBR Notices Worse

Taxpayers frequently turn manageable notices into serious legal proceedings by making avoidable errors:

  • Ignoring the notice entirely — FBR passes an ex-parte assessment (without your input) and raises the maximum possible demand. This becomes much harder and more expensive to fight on appeal.
  • Responding verbally or by phone — Phone calls have no legal standing. All responses must be in writing with supporting documents.
  • Submitting incomplete documents — A partial response is often treated as no response. FBR uses whatever gaps exist against you.
  • Responding without reconciling your wealth statement — If the assets in your response don't match your previously filed wealth statements, FBR will flag further inconsistencies.
  • Missing the deadline without requesting extension — You can request a time extension in writing before the deadline expires. Requesting late is rarely granted.
  • Self-representing in complex Section 111 or audit cases — These proceedings have legal and procedural rules that require professional knowledge. One wrong statement can harm your case.

What Happens After You Submit Your Response?

Understanding the post-response timeline helps you manage expectations:

  1. FBR reviews your response — The tax officer examines your documents and explanation. For simple notices (non-filing, demand), this often closes within 15–30 days if evidence is complete.
  2. Hearing notice may be issued — For audit and Section 111 notices, FBR typically issues a hearing notice to discuss your response in person. Attend with your documents and consultant.
  3. Draft order — After hearings, FBR issues a draft assessment order. You have a right to object to this draft within the time specified.
  4. Final order — If you do not object or FBR rejects your objections, a final assessment order is issued. This creates a tax demand you must pay or appeal.
  5. Appeal — You have 30 days from the final order to appeal before the Commissioner Inland Revenue (Appeals) under Section 127. This is your strongest protection against incorrect assessments.
