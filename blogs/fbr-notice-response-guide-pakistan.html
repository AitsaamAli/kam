How to Respond to FBR Tax Notice in Pakistan 2026

June 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali — Tax Consultant | NTN • SECP • Sales Tax Specialist — FBR Active Filer
TL;DR — Key Takeaways
  • FBR issues notices under multiple sections — each has a different response deadline and format
  • Section 114 notices require filing a return; Section 176 requires submitting information; Section 122 requires challenging or accepting an amended assessment
  • Never ignore an FBR notice — default responses lead to ex-parte assessments under Section 122(5)
  • Most notices can be resolved by filing the return, submitting requested documents, or filing a formal written reply through IRIS
  • Complex notices (Section 111 unexplained income, Section 122 assessment) should be handled by a qualified tax consultant

Receiving a notice from FBR (Federal Board of Revenue) is stressful but manageable if you understand what type of notice you have received and what action is required. The biggest mistake taxpayers make is ignoring FBR notices — an ignored notice leads to ex-parte assessments, demands, and escalation to recovery proceedings under Section 138. Acting correctly and on time avoids almost all of these consequences.

This guide covers every major type of FBR notice issued in Pakistan, the response deadline for each, and the correct action to take.

Section 114 — Return Not Filed Notice

A Section 114 notice is issued when FBR identifies you as a person who is required to file an income tax return but has not done so. Persons required to file include those with any source of taxable income, holders of NTN, registered businesses, holders of commercial utility connections, and persons appearing in third-party data (bank data, property purchase records, car registration).

Response: Simply file your outstanding return(s) on IRIS. Once filed, the notice is considered addressed. If you disagree that you are required to file, file a reply explaining why — for example, if your only income is exempt agricultural income or you have been incorrectly identified.

Section 176 — Information Request Notice

FBR can request information, documents, or books of accounts from any person under Section 176. This includes bank statements, purchase records, property documents, and financial statements. Failure to respond to a Section 176 notice without reasonable cause attracts a penalty of Rs. 25,000 under Section 182.

Response: Compile the requested documents and submit through IRIS (Correspondence section) or by registered post to the issuing tax officer. Include a cover letter listing all documents submitted and request acknowledgment. If you need more time to compile records, file a written extension request before the deadline.

Section 111 — Unexplained Income Notice

Section 111 is FBR's tool for addressing income or assets whose source a taxpayer cannot adequately explain. If your bank account received large deposits that don't reconcile with your declared income, or your wealth statement shows assets that exceed your declared income accumulation, FBR may issue a Section 111 notice.

Unexplained income under Section 111 is taxed at 35% flat rate plus 100% penalty — effectively making the total tax liability 70% of the unexplained amount. This is the harshest provision in the income tax system.

How to respond to Section 111:
  • Identify what FBR is questioning (specific bank credits, property purchase, vehicle registration)
  • Gather documentary proof of income source (salary slips, freelance contracts, foreign remittance advice, sale deed for previous asset, loan agreement)
  • For gifts or family remittances from abroad: prepare a written gift declaration with sender details
  • Submit a written reply with all supporting documents within the notice deadline
  • If the source is genuinely explainable, a professional response nearly always resolves Section 111 notices without additional tax

Section 122 — Amendment and Best Judgment Assessment

Under Section 122, the Commissioner can amend your filed return if they have grounds to believe income was understated or tax was miscalculated. Before issuing a final amended order, FBR must issue a draft amendment notice giving you an opportunity to respond (Section 122(9)).

Response process: You have 30 days from the draft order to submit your objections in writing. If you have evidence that the Commissioner's proposed amendment is wrong — receipts, bank statements, contracts — submit these with your objections. The Commissioner must consider your objections before passing the final amended assessment order. If you disagree with the final order, you have 30 days to file an appeal before the Commissioner of Inland Revenue (Appeals) under Section 127.

How to Submit Your FBR Notice Reply

  1. Log in to IRIS (iris.fbr.gov.pk) with your NTN/CNIC and password
  2. Navigate to Correspondence → Inbox — your notices appear here
  3. Click the relevant notice to open it and review the details (notice section, deadline, what is requested)
  4. Use the Reply function in IRIS to submit your response and attach supporting documents (PDF, JPEG)
  5. Keep a screenshot or PDF of your submitted reply as proof of response
  6. For critical notices (Section 111, 122), simultaneously send a hard copy by registered post to the issuing officer's address shown on the notice

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if I ignore an FBR notice?

Ignoring an FBR notice leads to an ex-parte assessment — FBR determines your tax liability based on available information without your input, resulting in an inflated assessment. This is followed by a demand notice and, if not paid, recovery proceedings under Section 138 which can include bank account attachment and property seizure. Always respond within the deadline, even if just to request an extension.

How do I know which FBR officer issued my notice?

FBR notices identify the issuing officer by name, designation, and address on the notice document. Your IRIS inbox shows the notice with the assigned tax circle. If your tax circle is unknown, log in to IRIS and check your taxpayer profile — it shows your assigned RTO (Regional Tax Office) and tax circle. The circle number determines which officer handles your case.

Can I get an extension to respond to an FBR notice?

Yes. Write a formal extension request letter to the issuing officer before the deadline, explaining the reason (need to compile documents, consultant unavailability, medical emergency). Extensions are granted at officer discretion — they are more readily granted for document-heavy requests (Section 176, Section 177 audits) than for return-filing notices (Section 114). Always submit the extension request before the original deadline expires.

What documents should I keep to defend against FBR notices?

Keep bank statements for at least 6 years (FBR can go back 6 years under Section 122). Also preserve: salary slips or employer certificates, foreign remittance advice letters, property purchase and sale deeds, vehicle registration documents, loan agreements, business purchase/sale invoices, and WHT certificates from clients. These documents are the foundation of any FBR notice response.

What is the penalty for not responding to a Section 176 notice?

Failing to provide information required under Section 176 without reasonable cause attracts a penalty of Rs. 25,000 under Section 182(1). If the failure continues after the penalty order, additional daily penalties may apply. The Commissioner can also proceed to make a best judgment assessment under Section 121 based on available information, which may significantly overstate your actual tax liability.

My FBR notice mentions "best judgment assessment" — what does this mean?

A best judgment assessment (Section 121 or Section 122) is when the Commissioner estimates your income and tax liability based on available information — without relying on your filed return. This typically results in an inflated assessment since FBR uses conservative assumptions in your absence. You can challenge it: respond to the draft order (Section 122(9)) with actual income documentation, or appeal the final order under Section 127 within 30 days.
