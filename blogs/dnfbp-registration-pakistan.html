DNFBP Registration in Pakistan 2026: Who Must Register With FBR and What Comes After

9 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 14 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 9 November 2026
Real estate agents, builders, jewellers and non-ICAP/ICMAP accountants are supervised by FBR for anti-money laundering. Registration is the first step, not the last.

Most property dealers and jewellers in Pakistan already know about NTN, income tax returns and the Active Taxpayers List. DNFBP registration is a separate regime with a different purpose: it is about preventing money laundering and terrorist financing, not collecting tax. This guide explains, from FBR's own regulations, FAQs and sector guidance, who is covered, how registration works, the duties that start the day you register, and how FBR enforces them.

TL;DR

DNFBP means Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions. Under the Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2010 and FBR's AML/CFT Regulations (S.R.O. 924(I)/2020), FBR supervises three groups: real estate agents (including builders and developers when they deal in property), dealers in precious metals and stones who take cash of Rs 2 million or more in a transaction, and accountants not regulated by ICAP or ICMAP when they carry out listed activities. These businesses must register with FBR, appoint a compliance officer, check customers (CDD), screen them against sanctions lists, report suspicious and large cash transactions to the Financial Monitoring Unit on goAML, and keep records for 5 to 10 years. The Sanctions Rules allow penalties of up to Rs 100 million per violation.

What a DNFBP Is, in Plain Terms

Banks have long been required to know their customers and report suspicious transactions. Money launderers responded by moving value through businesses that handle large sums outside the banking system: property deals, gold and jewellery, and professionals who set up companies or manage client money. The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) calls these businesses Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions, and Pakistan brought them into its anti-money laundering law to meet FATF standards.

The legal base is Section 2(xii)(a), (b) and (c) of the Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2010 (AMLA). FBR is the AML/CFT regulatory authority for these three categories, and it issued the FBR AML/CFT Regulations for DNFBPs through S.R.O. 924(I)/2020 on 29 September 2020. A Directorate General of DNFBPs inside FBR handles registration, supervision, inspections and sanctions. Other DNFBPs, such as lawyers and chartered accountants, are supervised by their own bodies, not by FBR.

Who Must Register With FBR

The test is the activity, not the label on your shop. FBR's February 2023 FAQs and its sector guidance define each group as follows:

CategoryYou are a DNFBP when you…Not covered (examples)
Real estate agents (REAs)Execute the purchase or sale of real property, take part in a real estate transaction in that capacity, or carry on professional activity for a property transfer. FBR's short guidance says this includes builders, real estate developers, brokers and property dealers.Someone selling their own house once, with no business of dealing in property.
Dealers in precious metals and stones (DPMS)Deal in bullion, jewellery, gold, platinum, diamonds, precious or semi-precious stones or pearls and engage in a cash transaction of Rs 2 million or more with a customer.A jeweller whose cash dealings with every customer always stay below Rs 2 million (but see the warning on split payments below).
Accountants (non-ICAP / ICMAP)Sole practitioners, partners or employees of firms who buy or sell real estate for clients, manage client money, securities or accounts, organise contributions for companies, create or manage companies, trusts or waqf, or act as formation agents, among 14 activities listed by FBR.Members regulated by ICAP or ICMAP (supervised by their institutes). Ordinary tax return and tax advisory work is not one of the listed activities.

A warning on thresholds. The Rs 2 million cash test for jewellers is per transaction with a customer, but FMU's red flags treat a series of smaller payments that add up to a large amount as suspicious in itself. Splitting a sale to stay under the limit does not take a business out of the regime; it creates a reporting problem.

How DNFBP Registration Works

The FBR AML/CFT Regulations (S.R.O. 924(I)/2020) require every DNFBP to be registered with FBR and to give FBR whatever information it needs to register the business and keep the registration up to date, including the criminal record of senior management and beneficial owners. Registration is done online on FBR's IRIS portal, where the business uses its existing taxpayer login. A practical sequence:

  1. Get your tax registration in order. You need an NTN and IRIS login for the individual, firm (AOP) or company that runs the business. If you do not have one, start with NTN registration.
  2. Identify who must be declared. The owner or partners, directors, and any natural person who ultimately owns or controls the business (the beneficial owner), plus the person you will appoint as compliance officer.
  3. Collect documents. CNICs of owners, partners or directors; business address and contact details; the registration documents of the firm or company where applicable; and character or criminal-record certificates for senior management and beneficial owners, since the Regulations require that information.
  4. Complete the DNFBP registration in IRIS for your category (real estate agent, dealer in precious metals and stones, or accountant) and submit it with the attachments. Menu names in IRIS change from time to time; if the option is hard to find, the DNFBP helpline below can guide you.
  5. Register on goAML separately. Reports go to the Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU), not FBR, through goAML. FBR's FAQs say every reporting entity must register on goAML to file STRs and CTRs. Do this straight after FBR registration, before you need to file anything.
  6. Keep the registration current. Inform FBR when owners, partners, directors, the compliance officer or the business address change.

Which FBR office supervises you depends on where the business operates. Under S.R.O. 1497(I)/2026 dated 2 September 2026, the Directorate General has all-Pakistan jurisdiction, with Directors at Islamabad, Karachi, Lahore and Quetta. The Lahore Directorate, for example, covers businesses within the jurisdiction of RTO-I and RTO-II Lahore, CTO Lahore, LTO Lahore and Multan, and the RTOs of Sialkot, Gujranwala, Faisalabad, Sargodha, Multan, Sahiwal and Bahawalpur.

Customer Due Diligence: What to Collect

FBR's sector guidance sets out a CDD form and lists the information for each type of customer:

  • Individuals: full name, date of birth, residential address, CNIC, NICOP, POC or passport number, whether they act for themselves or someone else, purpose of the relationship, beneficial ownership and, for higher-risk customers and PEPs, the source of wealth or funds. Verification can use the CNIC, NADRA verification services, utility bills, bank statements, tax documents or salary slips.
  • Companies and other legal persons: name, registration number, taxpayer number, business address, nature of business, ownership and control structure, and the natural persons who ultimately own or control it, verified from the incorporation certificate, memorandum and articles, FBR documents and the company's register of ultimate beneficial owners.

How often to review a customer depends on risk. FBR's FAQs give a guide: at least once a year for high-risk customers, every two years for medium risk and every three years for low risk, or sooner when something changes. You may rely on another regulated party's CDD in limited cases, but you stay responsible for it.

Higher-Risk Customers and Enhanced Due Diligence

Enhanced due diligence (EDD) means more questions, better evidence and senior management approval before you start or continue the relationship. FBR's guidance points to these as higher risk:

  • Politically exposed persons (PEPs), meaning people who hold or held a prominent public function in Pakistan, abroad or in an international organisation, and their family members and close associates.
  • Customers whose beneficial owner is unclear because of a complex ownership chain.
  • Links to countries that FATF has listed for countermeasures or enhanced due diligence.
  • Unknown NGOs, or NGOs exposed to terrorist financing abuse.
  • Customers in high-risk businesses, or with adverse media reports.

Source-of-wealth evidence can include a bank statement showing where funds came from, a sale deed for a property sold earlier, a wealth statement, inheritance papers, or audited accounts of a business.

ReportWhen to filePoints to remember
STR (Suspicious Transaction Report)Promptly, when you know, suspect or have reason to suspect that funds come from crime, are linked to terrorism, are meant to evade reporting, or have no apparent lawful purpose.Covers attempted transactions too. Tipping off is an offence. Owners, officers and staff must not tell the customer, directly or indirectly, that a report has been or will be filed. Once you file an STR, your role is complete; any investigation is for FMU or the investigating agency, and FMU must keep the reporter's identity confidential under AMLA.

Inspections and Penalties

FBR's DNFBP teams inspect registered businesses for compliance. The AML/CFT Sanctions Rules, 2020 (S.R.O. 950(I)/2020) set out what FBR may do when it finds a breach:

  • A monetary penalty of up to Rs 100 million per violation, worked out on a risk-based penalty scale.
  • Conditions, limits or restrictions on the business, or de-registration.
  • A warning, suspension or removal of a person in management.
  • A censure or reprimand, or directions such as a remedial plan with a deadline.

A sanction must be given by written notice that states the violation and your right of appeal. Significant sanctions are published quarterly, with the name of the business, which can hurt a property or jewellery business more than the fine itself. Unpaid penalties can lead to further sanctions.

A 30-Day Compliance Checklist After Registration

  1. Week 1: appoint the compliance officer and share their details with FBR. Register on goAML.
  2. Week 1–2: write a short risk assessment for your business: who your customers are, where they come from, how they pay, and which deals carry more risk.
  3. Week 2: adopt an AML/CFT policy covering CDD, EDD, screening, reporting, record keeping and training. Keep it practical and specific to your business.
  4. Week 3: start using a CDD form for every customer, and set up sanctions screening using the lists FBR points to, including its DNFBP mobile app.
  5. Week 4: train your staff, set up a file or folder system that keeps records for the required period, and diary the next review.

How DNFBP Rules Sit Alongside Your Tax Duties

DNFBP registration does not replace your income tax return, and a tax return does not register you as a DNFBP. The two systems do meet, though: the CDD you collect, the source of funds you record and the cash you receive should match what appears in your own return and wealth statement. Property dealers should also understand the advance tax on property under Sections 236C and 236K, and jewellers the rules in our guide to tax on gold and jewellery.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does DNFBP stand for?
Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions. In Pakistan, FBR supervises three of them for anti-money laundering: real estate agents (including builders and developers when dealing in property), dealers in precious metals and stones, and accountants not regulated by ICAP or ICMAP.
Is DNFBP registration the same as NTN or sales tax registration?
No. NTN and sales tax registration are for tax. DNFBP registration is under the Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2010 and FBR's AML/CFT Regulations, and brings duties such as customer due diligence and reporting to FMU. You normally need an NTN and IRIS login first.
Does a jeweller have to register if most sales are below Rs 2 million?
A jeweller is a DNFBP when it engages in a cash transaction of Rs 2 million or more with a customer. If that ever happens, or could happen, register. Breaking one sale into smaller cash payments is itself a red flag for FMU.
Are builders and developers DNFBPs?
Yes, when they deal in property. FBR's short guidance for real estate agents states that the category includes builders, real estate developers, brokers and property dealers when they execute the purchase or sale of real property.
Do tax consultants have to register as DNFBPs?
Not for tax work alone. Non-ICAP/ICMAP accountants are DNFBPs only when they carry out listed activities such as managing client money, forming or managing companies or trusts, or buying and selling property for clients. Ordinary tax returns and tax advice are not on the list.
Who can be the AML/CFT compliance officer?
A person in senior management, such as the sole proprietor, CEO, managing partner, or a partner or employee with enough knowledge and authority. A sole proprietor with no employees is deemed to be the compliance officer.
Where are STRs and CTRs filed?
With the Financial Monitoring Unit on its goAML portal (goamlweb.fmu.gov.pk), not with FBR. Each DNFBP must register on goAML first.
How long must DNFBP records be kept?
Customer due diligence and relationship records for at least five years, and records of STRs and CTRs for at least ten years from the date of filing, according to FBR's 2023 FAQs.
What is the penalty for not complying?
Under the AML/CFT Sanctions Rules, 2020, FBR may impose a monetary penalty of up to Rs 100 million per violation on a risk-based scale, along with restrictions, de-registration or action against management. Significant sanctions are published every quarter.
