Canada Visitor Visa From Pakistan: Making Your Wealth Statement Agree With Six Months of Bank Records

24 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 24 November 2026
IRCC reads six months of balances. Large credits without a recorded source are the first thing a wealth statement should explain.

A Canadian visitor visa file from Pakistan is usually decided on two questions: can you pay for the trip, and will you come back. The money question is answered mostly by your bank statement. When the bank statement shows a large deposit or a balance that does not fit your salary, an FBR wealth statement that records the source, a plot sold, a parent's gift, business profit, can help. One that contradicts the bank statement does the opposite. Here is how to line them up lawfully.

Short answer

IRCC's visitor visa page asks for a bank account statement with “at least 6 months of account details, including balances”, in your name and address, and an employer letter with your current salary dated no earlier than three months before you apply. Your FBR wealth statement is not on that list, but applicants often add it, and it should explain where the money in those six months came from. If an account, a gift or a property sale is missing or wrong, revise the statement under section 116(3) before applying. IRCC lists misrepresentation, including false or withheld information, as a reason to be refused a visa.

What IRCC Asks For

Short answer: A bank account statement with at least six months of account details and balances, proof it is your account, and an employer letter with current salary.

The IRCC page “Apply for a visitor visa” (modified 16 November 2026) lists a bank account statement among the documents you might need, because it “can help us understand if you have enough money to support yourself during your stay in Canada.” It should show:

Item on the IRCC pageWhat it means for a Pakistani applicant
Bank name and contactA statement on bank letterhead or a stamped printout
Proof it is your accountYour name and address on the statement
At least 6 months of account details, including balancesEvery credit and debit in that period is visible to the officer
Employer letter with current salary, dated no earlier than 3 months before you applySalary credits in the statement should match this letter

The eligibility page (modified 28 August 2026) adds that you must “have enough money for your stay”, and that the amount depends on how long you stay and whether you stay in a hotel or with friends or relatives. Tax returns and wealth statements are not named on these pages. If you are applying for permanent residence rather than a visit, our Canada PR guide covers that route.

Why Six Months of History Changes the Picture

Short answer: IRCC's page does not mention large deposits; the six-month history is what shows them. A recorded source in the wealth statement is the clean answer.

A single balance certificate hides how the money arrived. Six months of transactions do not. A teacher earning Rs 120,000 a month whose balance rises from Rs 400,000 to Rs 2,900,000 in May will be asked, in effect, where Rs 2,500,000 came from. IRCC's page says nothing about large deposits as such, but the six-month history makes them visible.

The FBR wealth statement is where that answer is recorded in Pakistan. If the money came from a plot sold in May, the TY2026 statement (30 June 2026) should show the plot gone and the sale proceeds in the bank. If it came from a father, the reconciliation should show a gift from a relative. If it is a loan from a friend, it is a liability, not savings, and should be shown as one.

The Corrections That Usually Come Up

Short answer: Revise omitted accounts, unrecorded gifts, sold or bought property and guessed cash, with a revised reconciliation and reasons.

Bank statement showsWealth statement showsCorrection under s.116(3)
A second account with savingsOnly the salary accountAdd the account at its 30 June balance
Rs 2,500,000 credit from a property buyerThe plot still heldRemove the plot, record the sale proceeds and any gain; see property revisions
Monthly transfers from a parent abroadNothing in the reconciliationRecord gifts from a relative; check foreign assets if the parent's money sits abroad in your name
A friend's Rs 800,000 credited for a few weeksNothingShow the loan as a liability, or do not rely on that balance at all

Section 116(3) of the Income Tax Ordinance requires the revised statement, a revised wealth reconciliation and written reasons, filed under intimation to the Commissioner before any section 122(9) notice for that year and within five years of that year's return due date. Our IRIS revision guide walks through the screens.

Salary Credits, the Employer Letter and the Return

IRCC asks for an employer letter that includes your current salary and is dated no earlier than three months before you apply. The officer can then compare three figures: the salary in the letter, the monthly salary credits in your six-month bank statement, and the income in your FBR return. They will rarely match to the rupee, because the letter shows gross pay and the bank shows pay after tax and deductions. They should still be plainly consistent. A return declaring Rs 900,000 a year next to a letter showing Rs 250,000 a month is a gap the wealth statement cannot explain; it points to an income figure in the return that needs a revised return under section 114(6), not just a revised wealth statement. Our revised return guide explains those conditions.

Borrowed Balances: The Mistake to Avoid

A common Pakistani practice is to park a relative's or friend's money in the applicant's account before a visa application and return it afterwards. Section 39(3) of the Ordinance already treats a loan, advance, deposit or gift received from a non-bank person otherwise than by crossed cheque, banking channel or digital means from a person holding an NTN as income from other sources, so cash parked this way can become taxable income at home. On the visa side, IRCC's inadmissibility page lists misrepresentation, which “includes providing false information or withholding information”, and says a person found inadmissible is denied a visa or refused entry. Presenting a loan as your savings fits that description.

The honest version is usually stronger: your own balance, plus a declared sponsor or host where one exists. IRCC's page itself notes that staying with friends or relatives changes how much money you need.

Example: A Retired Couple Visiting Their Son

Mr and Mrs Qureshi, retired in Rawalpindi, plan a six-week visit to their son in Toronto. Their joint bank statement shows a Rs 3,200,000 credit in February 2026 from the sale of a shop, and pension credits each month. Mr Qureshi's TY2026 wealth statement still lists the shop at its old cost and shows only Rs 450,000 in the bank.

LineFiledRevisedReason

Frequently Asked Questions

Does IRCC ask for my FBR tax return or wealth statement?
The visitor visa page we checked lists a bank account statement and an employer letter, not FBR documents. Applicants often add FBR papers to show where funds came from; they should agree with the bank statement.
How many months of bank statements does Canada want?
IRCC's page says at least six months of account details, including balances.
A large deposit appears in my bank statement. What should I do?
Make sure its source is recorded: a property sale, a gift from a relative, or business income. If your wealth statement omits it, revise under section 116(3) before applying.
Can a relative put money in my account for the visa?
Presenting someone else's money as your savings is risky on both sides: IRCC treats false information as misrepresentation, and section 39(3) can tax some unrecorded loans and gifts as income.
Is there a deadline for revising?
Yes. Before you receive a section 122(9) notice for that tax year, and never more than five years after that year's return due date.
What if the property sale also needs to be in my return?
Then the return is revised as well, under section 114(6) and its conditions. We check both and tell you before filing.
