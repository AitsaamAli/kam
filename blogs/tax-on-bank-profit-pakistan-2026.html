Tax on Bank Profit in Pakistan 2026 - Savings Account Interest Tax

June 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website
Short answer

Bank profit (savings, fixed deposits, National Savings) is profit on debt. Banks deduct 20% for people on the Active Taxpayers List and 40% for others (Section 151 and the Tenth Schedule). For individuals, Section 7B taxes profit up to Rs 5 million separately at that 20% rate, so the bank deduction is usually the full tax. Declare the profit and every account balance in your return and wealth statement.

Bank profit — whether from a savings account, a fixed deposit, National Savings Certificates, or government securities — is taxable income in Pakistan under Section 7B of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001. Banks deduct withholding tax compliance at source, but many account holders do not realise that: (a) the WHT rate doubles if you are a non-filer, and (b) active filers can claim a refund if the WHT deducted exceeds their actual tax liability. This guide explains every rate, exemption, and filing step for income tax return filing 2026.

Update, 27 November 2026: FBR extended the Tax Year 2026 deadline from 30 September to 31 October 2026, and it has passed. You can still file late: next steps, section 182 penalty.

What Counts as “Profit on Debt” Under Section 7B

Short answer: any return paid to you for lending or depositing money, including bank and Islamic deposits, National Savings certificates and government securities.

FBR uses the term profit on debt to cover all returns earned from lending money to banks or the government. Under Section 7B of ITO 2001, the following income is taxable:

  • Profit on savings accounts and current accounts (conventional and Islamic banking)
  • Interest / profit on fixed deposits and term deposits
  • Profit on National Savings Certificates: Defence Savings Certificates (DSC), Special Savings Certificates (SSC), Regular Income Certificates (RIC)
  • Yield on Treasury Bills (T-Bills) and Pakistan Investment Bonds (PIBs)
  • Profit on Kisan Vikas Certificates and Behbood Savings Certificates
  • Returns on Roshan Digital Accounts (with important exceptions for non-residents — see below)
  • Profit distributed by cooperative societies and microfinance banks

In short: if any institution pays you a return for depositing or lending money, it falls under “profit on debt” and is subject to withholding tax at source.

Withholding Tax Rates on Bank Profit — 2026

Short answer: banks deduct 20% of the profit for people on the Active Taxpayers List and 40% for people who are not.

The rate depends on whether your name appears on FBR’s Active Taxpayers List (ATL) on the day the profit is paid:

Account holderDeduction on bank profitLaw
On the ATL20%Section 151; First Schedule, Part III, Division IA(a)
Not on the ATL20% increased by 100% = 40%Tenth Schedule, rule 1

How the Tax Is Settled: Section 7B

For an individual (and any person other than a company), Section 7B taxes profit on debt separately from other income, at the rate in Division IIIA of the First Schedule (20% for profit from a bank account or deposit), on the gross amount. It does not apply where the profit is exempt or exceeds Rs 5 million; above that, the profit is taxed with your other income at the normal rates. In practice, for most savers the 20% deducted by the bank is the tax, and the profit is reported in the return under Section 7B.

If you were off the ATL and the bank deducted 40%, the deduction above the 20% Section 7B tax can be claimed in your return, but Section 182A(1)(c) blocks refunds while you are not on the ATL. Getting back on the ATL is therefore the first step.

Worked Example: How Much Tax on Rs. 2 Lakh Bank Profit

Short answer: on Rs 200,000 of bank profit, a filer pays Rs 40,000 and a person off the ATL has Rs 80,000 deducted.

Scenario: You earned Rs. 200,000 profit from savings and fixed deposits during Tax Year 2026.

  • On the ATL: the bank deducts 20% = Rs. 40,000, which is normally the full Section 7B tax on that profit.
  • Not on the ATL: the bank deducts 40% = Rs. 80,000. The extra Rs. 40,000 can be claimed in the return, but no refund is issued until you are on the ATL.

For larger balances the gap grows: on Rs. 10 lakh of profit, Rs. 200,000 is deducted on the ATL and Rs. 400,000 off it. Check your own figure with the bank profit tax calculator.

National Savings Instruments — Tax Treatment

Short answer: National Savings and other government securities paid to individuals carry a 15% deduction on the ATL (Division IA(c)), doubled for people off it.

Pakistan’s National Savings Centre (NSC) manages some of the most popular savings products in the country. All carry the same profit-on-debt WHT:

National Savings collects WHT from each profit payment and remits it to FBR. The annual profit certificate from NSC shows the gross profit and WHT deducted — use this when filing your return.

Roshan Digital Account — Tax Position for Non-Residents

Short answer: residents with an RDA face the normal 20% or 40% deduction; non-residents should confirm their status and any exemption before relying on it.

The State Bank of Pakistan’s Roshan Digital Account (RDA) allows overseas Pakistanis to hold PKR or foreign-currency accounts. Tax treatment:

  • Non-resident account holders: Profit on RDA is exempt from Pakistani income tax provided the account holder is genuinely non-resident and funds were remitted from abroad. WHT is not deducted on the profit portion for qualifying non-residents.
  • Resident account holders: If you are a Pakistani resident who opened an RDA, standard Section 151 deduction applies at 20% (on the ATL) or 40% (non-filer).
  • Profit must still be declared in the wealth statement if you hold an RDA balance.

Is There a Threshold? The Rs 5 Million Line in Section 7B

Short answer: there is no general tax-free limit for bank profit; Section 7B taxes it separately at 20% up to Rs 5 million, and above that it joins your other income.

Some older guides mention a declaration that stops the bank deducting tax on small profits. We could not find such a general exemption in the Income Tax Ordinance as amended up to 30 June 2026, so we do not rely on it. What the law does set is a Rs 5 million line: Section 7B(3)(b) says the separate Section 7B tax does not apply to profit on debt that exceeds Rs 5 million. Below that line the profit is taxed separately at the Division IIIA rate; above it, it is taxed with your other income at the normal rates, and the bank deduction is adjusted against that tax.

If you believe a specific exemption applies to you (for example under the Second Schedule), ask us to check it against the current text before you rely on it.

Islamic Banking — Is Mudaraba Profit Also Taxable?

Short answer: yes, Islamic deposit profit is taxed exactly like conventional bank profit: 20% on the ATL, 40% off it.

Yes. FBR treats profit from Islamic banking products — whether from a Mudaraba current account, Murabaha arrangement, or Diminishing Musharaka — identically to conventional interest under Section 7B. Banks offering Sharia-compliant products deduct the same WHT rates.

This is a common misconception among Islamic bank account holders who assume their profits are tax-free. All profit on deposits — regardless of the banking model — is subject to withholding under Pakistani tax law.

How to Get Your Bank Profit Certificate

Short answer: download the annual withholding tax certificate from your bank's app or ask the branch; you need one from every bank.

To file your return, you need a profit certificate showing gross profit earned and WHT deducted during the tax year (July 2025 to June 2026). Here is how to obtain it:

  • Online banking apps: Most major banks (HBL, MCB, UBL, Meezan, Askari, Allied, Bank Alfalah) now allow you to download annual profit certificates or WHT certificates directly from the app under “Statements” or “Documents.”
  • Bank branch: Visit your home branch with your CNIC and request the Annual Tax Deduction Statement for Tax Year 2026.
  • IRIS pre-filled data: Log into iris.fbr.gov.pk and check Pre-filled Tax Information — Withholding Tax Credits. Many large banks submit data directly to FBR, so it may already appear.
  • National Savings: NSC branches issue profit certificates on request. Online portal also shows deductions.

If you have accounts at multiple banks, collect a certificate from each. The total gross profit from all sources is what you declare in your return.

Can Filers Get a Refund on Bank Profit WHT?

Short answer: only where more was deducted than the Section 7B tax, for example 40% deducted while you were off the ATL; refunds are issued only once you are on the ATL.

Because Section 7B taxes profit on debt up to Rs 5 million separately at 20%, the 20% a bank deducts from a filer is normally the final figure, whatever your other income. A refund arises mainly where:

  • the bank deducted at 40% because you were off the ATL, and you then file and rejoin the ATL; or
  • your profit exceeded Rs 5 million and is taxed with your other income at a lower overall figure than was deducted.

Section 182A(1)(c) blocks refunds while a person is not on the ATL, so a late filer should rejoin first, through the surcharge or Form ATL-U.

Bank Balances and the Wealth Statement

Short answer: declare the 30 June balance of every account in the wealth statement, including joint and foreign currency accounts.

Beyond declaring profit income, your bank accounts must appear in your annual wealth statement:

  • Declare the closing balance of every bank account (savings, current, Islamic) as of June 30, 2026
  • Include National Savings balances separately under “Government Securities” in the wealth statement
  • Joint accounts: declare your share of the balance
  • Foreign currency accounts: declare in both PKR (at the June 30 exchange rate) and original currency

FBR Section 111 risk: If your bank balance increased significantly during the year but your declared income does not explain the increase, FBR can issue a notice under Section 111. For example: Opening balance Rs. 5 lakh + Salary Rs. 12 lakh − Expenditure Rs. 10 lakh = Expected closing balance Rs. 7 lakh. If your actual closing balance is Rs. 15 lakh, the Rs. 8 lakh gap needs a documented explanation (gift, inheritance, loan, etc.).

Frequently Asked Questions

My bank already deducted tax on my profit. Do I still need to file a return?
Yes. The bank deduction does not replace the return. Declare the gross profit and the tax deducted under Section 7B, and the bank balance in the wealth statement. If you do not file, you drop off the ATL and the deduction rises from 20% to 40%.
Is profit from Islamic savings accounts (Mudaraba) also taxable?
Yes. Profit from Islamic deposits is profit on debt for tax purposes. Banks deduct 20% for account holders on the ATL and 40% for others, and the profit is reported in the return.
I have accounts in three different banks. Do I need a certificate from each?
Yes. Each bank issues its own Annual WHT Certificate or Profit Certificate. Collect a certificate from every bank where you earned profit during the tax year. Add up all the gross profits and all the WHT deductions from each certificate to get your totals for the return. You can also check IRIS Pre-filled Tax Information — major banks submit data directly to FBR, and some amounts may be pre-filled already.
Is there a tax-free limit on bank profit?
We could not find a general tax-free limit for bank profit in the Income Tax Ordinance as amended up to 30 June 2026. Section 7B taxes profit on debt separately at 20% up to Rs 5 million; above that it is taxed with your other income.
Can I get a refund on bank profit WHT that was already deducted?
Only where more was deducted than the Section 7B tax, typically 40% deducted while you were off the ATL, or where profit above Rs 5 million is taxed at a lower overall figure. Refunds are not issued while you are off the ATL (Section 182A(1)(c)).
I am a senior citizen (over 60). What rate do I pay on bank profit?
We could not find a separate senior-citizen rate for bank profit in the Income Tax Ordinance as amended up to 30 June 2026. Banks deduct 20% for people on the ATL and 40% for others. Filing on time keeps you at 20%.
