Pakistan's National Risk Assessment 2023: Real Estate Rated Very High, DPMS High, and What It Means for Your Business

26 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 26 November 2026
FBR expects every DNFBP's own risk assessment to reflect the national one. Real estate starts at the top of the scale.

When FBR asks a property dealer or jeweller for a risk assessment, it is not asking for an essay. It is asking whether the business understands where it is exposed, starting from the national picture. Pakistan's third National Risk Assessment, completed in 2023 after the first in 2017 and an update in 2019, sets that picture. This guide summarises what the NRA 2023 summary says about DNFBPs and shows how a small business turns it into the documented assessment regulation 4 requires.

Short answer

FBR's summary of Pakistan's National Risk Assessment 2023 rates the inherent money laundering and terrorist financing vulnerability of real estate agents (including builders and developers) as Very High and of dealers in precious metals and stones as High, both supervised by FBR. Accountants are rated Low, and lawyers, TCSPs and notaries Low. The NRA names real estate, the construction industry, cash-intensive businesses and DPMS among channels used to launder proceeds. FBR's Regulations require each DNFBP to assess its own risks and consider risk assessments provided by FBR, and the NRA summary asks reporting entities to update their internal risk assessments and procedures to reflect it.

What the NRA 2023 Is

FBR publishes a “Summarized National Risk Assessment 2023 for DNFBPs, Legal Persons & Limited Liability Partnerships (LLPs) and Waqf, Trusts & Cooperatives” on its DNFBP risk assessment page. It explains that Pakistan “completed its first comprehensive ML/TF National Risk Assessment in 2017, updated it in 2019 and has conducted its third National Risk Assessment in 2023.” Its stated purpose is to help stakeholders understand their risks, and it says those subject to Pakistan's AML/CFT laws “are expected to use the findings of this document to update their internal risk assessment documents / Standard Operating Procedures”.

The NRA used four rating levels: Very High, High, Medium and Low, and adds that “low risk doesn’t mean that no action is required”.

The DNFBP Ratings

Short answer: Real estate agents (including builders/developers) Very High; DPMS High; accountants Low; lawyers, TCSPs and notaries Low.

The NRA assessed each sector on “their unique characteristics, clientele, the offered products and services, their geographic reach, and the channels they operate through.” The two sectors FBR supervises most heavily, real estate and DPMS, sit at the top. That is the context for FBR's registration drive, for Condition No. 2 on housing societies (see developers and societies) and for the 2026 amendment that added title transferring authorities to the real estate definition (see S.R.O. 1439).

How the NRA Says Money Is Laundered

Short answer: The NRA names real estate, the construction industry, cash-intensive businesses and DPMS among channels used to launder proceeds.

In its money laundering threat section, the NRA summary lists the channels used to launder proceeds of predicate offences as including “illegal MVTS/ hundi/ hawala, cash smuggling, bank accounts, real estate, construction industry, cash-intensive businesses, DPMS, and front & shell companies”, and names “investment in real estate/ precious metals & stones/ other high-value assets” among the ways proceeds are used. It assigned ML threat ratings to 21 predicate offences and rated corruption and bribery, illegal MVTS/hundi/hawala, tax crimes, smuggling and cash smuggling “Very High”.

For a property dealer or jeweller, the message is practical: cash, high-value assets and third-party payments are the features that make a deal risky, and those are the features your CDD and reporting should focus on.

What Regulation 4 Requires of You

Short answer: Regulation 4 requires a documented risk assessment that considers FBR's risk assessments; the NRA summary asks entities to update theirs.

Regulation 4Requirement
4(1)Identify, assess and understand risks for customers, countries or geographic areas, and products, services, transactions or delivery channels
4(1)(a)Document the risk assessment
4(1)(b)–(d)Consider all relevant risk factors; keep it up to date; be able to provide it to FBR
4(2)Policies, controls and procedures approved by senior management, managing risks in your own assessment “and any other risk assessment publicly available or provided by FBR”
4(2)Enhanced measures where higher risks are identified
4(3)Simplified measures allowed for lower risks, never where there is suspicion

FBR's FAQs (Q28) tell DNFBPs to consider the nature, size and complexity of the business, products and delivery methods, types of customers, countries dealt with and FBR guidance, and to consider whether any product involves new technologies favouring anonymity.

Example: A Small DPMS in Gujranwala

A family jewellery shop sells mostly to local wedding buyers, about a quarter of sales above Rs 2 million in cash. Its assessment starts from the NRA's High rating for DPMS, then records its own picture: local, known customers (lower), heavy cash (higher), occasional buyers paying for others (higher). It sets controls to match: CDD and screening for every cash sale of Rs 2 million or more, a CTR to FMU for each, questions on who is paying, and senior approval for any PEP. The owner signs the policy, as regulation 4(2) requires, and reviews it each year. That two-page document is what FBR's inspector asks for first.

Keeping the Assessment Current: New Products and Technology

A risk assessment written once and filed away does not meet regulation 4(1), which requires DNFBPs to keep the assessment up to date and to have mechanisms to provide it to FBR. Three events should prompt a review:

  1. A new national or FBR assessment. The NRA summary itself asks entities to update their internal assessments and procedures in line with its findings.
  2. A change in your own business. A dealer who starts handling overseas buyers, a jeweller who starts taking large online orders, or a society opening file trading.
  3. A new product, practice or technology. Regulation 5 requires a DNFBP to identify and assess the ML and TF risks of new products, business practices and delivery mechanisms, and the use of new or existing technology, and to do so before launching them. FBR's FAQs (Q28) ask DNFBPs to consider whether any of their products involve new or developing technologies that may favour customer anonymity.

Selling plots through an app or social media, accepting payment through a new digital channel, or offering instalment files to buyers you never meet are all examples where regulation 5 applies. The review need not be long, a dated page recording the change, its risks and the controls added, but it must exist before the new practice starts.

Where Lower Risk Allows Simplified Measures

The NRA's ratings do not mean every customer is high risk. Regulation 10 allows simplified due diligence where lower risks are identified through an adequate analysis, for example verifying identity after the relationship begins or reducing the frequency of monitoring, and FBR's FAQs (Q36) suggest review cycles of up to three years for low-risk customers. Regulation 10(3) adds the limit: no simplified measures whenever there is a suspicion of ML or TF. For a real estate agent starting from a Very High sector rating, simplified measures should be the documented exception, not the default.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does Pakistan's NRA 2023 rate real estate agents?
FBR's summary of the NRA 2023 rates real estate agents, including builders and developers, at Very High inherent ML/TF vulnerability.
How are jewellers rated in the NRA 2023?
Dealers in precious metals and stones (DPMS) are rated High.
Are accountants high risk under the NRA 2023?
No. The summary rates accountants Low, and lawyers, TCSPs and notaries Low.
Do I have to use the NRA in my own risk assessment?
Regulation 4(2) requires your controls to manage risks in your own assessment and any risk assessment provided by FBR, and the NRA summary asks entities to update their internal assessments with its findings.
How often was the national risk assessment done?
First in 2017, updated in 2019, and the third in 2023, according to FBR's summary.
Does a Low rating mean no action is needed?
No. The NRA summary says low risk does not mean that no action is required; each stakeholder must still adopt controls for low-risk items.
Do I need a new risk assessment when I start selling online?
Regulation 5 requires a DNFBP to assess the ML/TF risks of new products, practices, delivery mechanisms and technologies before launching them.
Can a real estate agent use simplified due diligence?
Regulation 10 allows it only where lower risks are identified through adequate analysis, and never where there is suspicion; for a sector rated Very High it should be the documented exception.
