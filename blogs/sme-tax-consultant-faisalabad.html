Does the Simplified Turnover Scheme Make Sense for Your Unit?

Many small Faisalabad businesses qualify for FBR's simplified turnover tax scheme, paying a flat percentage of turnover instead of computing tax against detailed profit-and-loss figures.

Common considerations: A unit with genuinely thin power-loom or trading margins often benefits from turnover-based computation, but a business with stronger margins might pay less under standard profit-based filing — this needs checking against actual numbers, not assumed. Growth needs monitoring against the scheme's turnover threshold each year, since exceeding it requires switching approaches going forward. Kamboh Associates runs the comparison for each Faisalabad SME client before recommending which scheme to use.

Small Power Loom & Weaving Units

The smallest tier of Faisalabad's power loom sector — often family-run, a handful of machines, supplying local buyers rather than export markets directly — represents one of the largest genuinely underserved SME populations in the city's tax base.

Common considerations: First-time NTN registration is usually the actual starting point, since many units have operated for years without any formal tax registration at all. Once registered, turnover needs to track credibly against yarn and raw material purchases, even at small scale, since FBR's benchmarking doesn't only target larger mills. Units supplying larger exporters as subcontractors still need their own registration, since that income is the unit's own, regardless of the final buyer.

Small Retail & Trading Businesses

Single-shop traders and small wholesale operations across Faisalabad's bazaar network make up another significant SME segment, generally registering once a bank or supplier first requires it rather than proactively.

Common considerations: High-volume, thin-margin trading businesses cross the sales tax threshold earlier than owners typically expect, since it's easy to underestimate cumulative annual turnover spread across many small daily transactions. Family businesses with two or three relatives informally sharing management need clarity on how income and any eventual formal structure will reflect each person's actual involvement.

Bank Financing for Faisalabad's Smaller Textile Units

Faisalabad's small power loom and trading units frequently need bank financing for a new machine, seasonal raw material purchases, or working capital between order cycles — and this is often the exact moment a unit's lack of formal tax history becomes a real, immediate problem rather than a background compliance gap.

Common considerations: Even SME-scale financing schemes generally expect at least a year or two of filed returns and current ATL status before extending credit, which catches out units that have operated informally for years without issue until the moment they actually need a loan. Building this filing history takes time, so units anticipating a financing need in the next year or two benefit from formalizing now rather than waiting until the application is urgent. Kamboh Associates helps Faisalabad SME clients build exactly this kind of financing-ready filing history.
