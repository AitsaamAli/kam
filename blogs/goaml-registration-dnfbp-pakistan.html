goAML Registration for DNFBPs in Pakistan: Step-by-Step Guide to FMU's Reporting Portal

10 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 10 November 2026
FBR registration does not register you on goAML. Every DNFBP needs a separate goAML account with the Financial Monitoring Unit before it can file an STR or CTR.

Many property dealers and jewellers register with FBR as a DNFBP and assume the job is done. It is not. Reports go to a different body, the Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU), through its own portal, goAML, and a business that has not registered there cannot report when it needs to. This guide follows FMU's own goAML registration guideline for DNFBPs field by field, then explains what happens after approval.

TL;DR

goAML is the Financial Monitoring Unit's online portal for suspicious transaction reports (STRs) and currency transaction reports (CTRs). FBR's FAQs say every DNFBP must register on it, and that STRs go only to FMU, not to FBR. Registration is done at goamlweb.fmu.gov.pk using 'Register New Organization': you enter the business details, then the details of a senior person who will be the goAML admin, attach a signed authority letter on your letterhead as a PDF, and send the hard copy of that letter to the Additional Director, FMU. FMU approves the request, after which the admin logs in with the email and password chosen at registration.

What goAML Is and Why DNFBPs Need It

goAML is the reporting system FMU uses to receive reports from banks and other reporting entities. For DNFBPs it has two uses: filing a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) when you know or suspect that a transaction involves the proceeds of crime or terrorist financing, and filing a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) when a cash transaction with a customer is Rs 2 million or more.

FBR's February 2023 FAQs make three points clear. Each reporting entity must register on goAML to file STRs and CTRs. STRs are filed with FMU only, not with FBR. And an STR is filed promptly, as soon as the suspicion arises, not at the end of the year.

Before You Start: What to Prepare

  • Business NTN, or the owner's CNIC for a sole proprietorship.
  • Business details: registered name, type of entity (sole proprietorship, partnership or AOP, company), full address and phone number.
  • A contact person for the business, normally the owner, director or chief executive, with an email address.
  • An admin user: FMU's guideline says the business administrator should be a senior official, preferably the head of compliance. For a small business this is usually the owner, who is also the AML/CFT compliance officer.
  • The admin's details: name, date of birth, 13-digit CNIC, phone and address.
  • An authority letter on your letterhead, signed by the head of compliance, scanned as a PDF. FMU publishes a specimen on its DNFBP facilitation page.

Step-by-Step: Registering Your Business on goAML

The steps below follow FMU's Guidelines for Registration on goAML for DNFBPs. Screen labels can change, so compare with FMU's own help page if something looks different.

  1. Open the registration page. Go to goAML through FMU's website (fmu.gov.pk/goaml) and choose Register New Organization.
  2. Register as a reporting entity. Choose to register as a reporting entity.
  3. Choose your business type. For example, "Dealer of Precious Metals & Stones (Jeweler)" or "Real Estate".
  4. Answer "Is financial?" with No. DNFBPs are not financial institutions.
  5. Enter the business name, for example "ABC Real Estate & Builders".
  6. Enter the NTN of the business, or the owner's CNIC.
  7. Choose the entity type: partnership, proprietorship, association of persons or company.
  8. Add the contact person and their email.
  9. Add the business phone number with country code 92 and without the leading zero, for example 321xxxxxxx for a mobile.
  10. Add the business address.
  11. Fill in the admin user section (the "registering person"): username, which must be an email address, entered twice; password; title; first and last name; date of birth (DD/MM/YYYY); CNIC (13 digits, no dashes); phone number (92 code, no leading zero); and address, which may be the same as the business address.
  12. Attach the authority letter as a PDF and submit the form.
  13. Post the hard copy of the signed authority letter to the Additional Director, FMU.
  14. Wait for approval. FMU processes the request and notifies you. Once approved, the admin logs into the goAML production site with the username and password set at registration.

Treat the admin login like a bank login. Do not share it with staff who should not see reports. Tipping off a customer that a report has been made is an offence, and a shared password makes leaks more likely.

Common Reasons a goAML Request Is Delayed

  • No hard copy sent. The guideline requires both the PDF upload and a posted original of the authority letter.
  • Letter not on letterhead, or unsigned. The letter must be on the business's letterhead and signed by the head of compliance.
  • Mismatched details. The NTN, business name and admin CNIC should match your FBR and NADRA records exactly.
  • Phone numbers in the wrong format, with a leading zero or a + sign.
  • A junior employee named as admin. FMU expects a senior official.

After Approval: Filing STRs and CTRs

Once logged in, reports are submitted through goAML's web forms. FMU's goAML Web Users Guide explains the screens. Some practical rules apply to every report:

  • STR: file promptly once you know, suspect or have reason to suspect that funds come from crime, are linked to terrorism, are meant to dodge reporting, or have no apparent lawful purpose. This includes attempted transactions that did not go ahead.
  • CTR: file for any cash transaction with a customer of Rs 2 million or more. No suspicion is needed; it is a threshold report.
  • Write a clear narrative in an STR: who, what, when, how much, and why it looked suspicious, with the red flags you saw. FMU's sector red flag lists help you describe the concern.
  • Keep copies. FBR's FAQs require records of STRs and CTRs to be kept for at least ten years from filing.
  • Do not tell the customer. Once the report is filed, your duty is done; any follow-up is for FMU or an investigating agency. FMU must keep the reporter's identity confidential under the AML Act.

What a Good STR Narrative Contains

An STR is only as useful as its description. FMU does not ask for proof of a crime, only for the facts and the reasons for your suspicion. A practical checklist for the narrative:

  1. Who: the customer, any person acting for them, and any third party who paid or received value, with identity details from your CDD file.
  2. What and when: the property, jewellery or service involved, dates, amounts and how each payment was made.
  3. Why it is suspicious: the specific red flags, in plain words, for example "buyer's stated salary does not support a Rs 4 crore cash purchase" or "payments split into several cash amounts below Rs 2 million over three days".
  4. What you did: questions you asked, documents requested or refused, and whether you declined the transaction.
  5. Supporting documents: attach copies from your CDD file where goAML allows.

Avoid accusations and opinions about guilt. Describe what you saw and why it did not fit the customer's profile.

From Concern to Report: An Internal Workflow

  1. Staff notice a red flag and record it on an internal concern form: who, what, when, and why it looked wrong.
  2. The compliance officer reviews the customer file, asks for any missing information, and decides whether there are grounds for suspicion.
  3. The decision is recorded, whether or not an STR is filed, with the reasons.
  4. If suspicion remains, the transaction is declined and the STR is filed promptly on goAML.
  5. Nothing is said to the customer about the concern or the report.

This workflow is short enough for a two-person business and shows an inspector that suspicion is handled consistently.

CTRs in Practice

A CTR is a threshold report: no suspicion is needed. To file one accurately you need, at the time of the transaction, the customer's identity details, the date, the amount of cash, and what it paid for. Capture these on the CDD form and in the cash register, then file the CTR on goAML and note the reference against the register entry. Keep the CTR record for at least ten years from filing.

Keeping goAML Access Working

  • When the admin leaves, arrange a new admin with FMU before the old login is lost; a business without a working login cannot report.
  • Use an email the business controls, so password resets do not depend on a former employee.
  • Limit who can see reports; confidentiality protects both the business and the customer process.
  • Test the login every few months, not only when a report is due.

The First Month After goAML Approval

Approval is the start, not the end. In the first month, log in once to confirm the admin account works, note where the STR and CTR forms are, and write a one-page procedure that names who files, how the decision is recorded, and where the acknowledgement is kept. Tell staff who to go to with a concern, and remind them never to discuss a report with a customer. If you have filed nothing by the end of the month, that is normal; what matters is that you are ready when a reportable transaction happens.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is goAML registration the same as DNFBP registration with FBR?
No. DNFBP registration is with FBR on IRIS. goAML registration is with the Financial Monitoring Unit and is needed to file STRs and CTRs. A DNFBP needs both.
Who should be the goAML admin for a small business?
FMU's guideline says the admin should be a senior official, preferably the head of compliance. In a sole proprietorship that is usually the owner, who is also the AML/CFT compliance officer.
Can I use a Gmail address as the goAML username?
Yes. FMU recommends an official business email, but its DNFBP registration guideline shows a Gmail address as an accepted example.
What is the goAML authority letter?
A letter on the business's letterhead, signed by the head of compliance, authorising the admin user. It is uploaded as a PDF with the form, and the hard copy is posted to the Additional Director, FMU. FMU publishes a specimen.
What are the password rules for goAML?
At least eight characters, including at least one small letter, one capital letter, one number and one special character.
Do I file STRs with FBR or FMU?
With FMU through goAML only. FBR's FAQs say reporting entities are only required to file STRs with FMU.
When must a CTR be filed?
When the business engages in a cash transaction with a customer worth Rs 2 million or more. It is a threshold report and no suspicion is needed.
Can I file an STR for a deal that did not happen?
Yes. FBR's FAQs say an STR can be filed for an attempted transaction that raises suspicion.
How long should goAML reports be kept?
Records of STRs and CTRs should be kept for at least ten years from the date of filing, according to FBR's 2023 FAQs.
What details do I need to file a CTR?
The customer's identity details, the date, the cash amount and what it paid for, captured on the CDD form and in the cash register at the time of the transaction.
What if our goAML admin leaves the business?
Arrange a new admin with FMU before the old login is lost, and use an email address the business controls.
Should we record decisions not to file an STR?
Yes. Recording the reasons for each decision shows an inspector that concerns are handled consistently.
Do I have to file a report every month?
No. Reports are filed when a reportable transaction or suspicion arises. There is no monthly filing requirement for a DNFBP with nothing to report.
Where do I get help with goAML technical problems?
FMU's DNFBP registration guideline lists goamlhelpdesk@fmu.gov.pk for goAML queries, and FMU's goAML help page explains the screens.
