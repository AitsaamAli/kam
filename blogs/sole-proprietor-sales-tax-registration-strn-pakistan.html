Sales Tax Registration for Sole Proprietors: When an STRN Is Compulsory Under Section 14

10 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 10 November 2026
Sales tax registration is compulsory only for the categories in Section 14 of the Sales Tax Act. The Finance Act, 2026 changed the Tier-1 retailer test.

Sole proprietors are often told either that every business needs a sales tax number or that small businesses never do. Neither is right. The answer depends on what you do and, for manufacturers and retailers, on a few precise tests in the law. This guide applies them to common sole proprietor businesses.

TL;DR

Under Section 14 of the Sales Tax Act, 1990 (as amended up to 30 June 2026), a person making taxable supplies must register if they are a manufacturer other than a cottage industry, a retailer liable to pay sales tax (other than retailers paying through electricity bills), an importer, an exporter seeking refunds, a wholesaler, dealer or distributor, or a person required by another law. Online sellers of goods must also register under Section 14(1A). A cottage industry must meet all four tests, including annual turnover of no more than Rs 8 million. The Tier-1 retailer definition now includes retailers with turnover above Rs 200 million. Persons who should be registered but are not face bank account and property restrictions and, ultimately, sealing of premises.

The Section 14 Categories

Section 14(1) requires every person making taxable supplies in Pakistan, including zero-rated supplies, in the course of a taxable activity, to register if they fall in any of these categories:

The Cottage Industry Test

Section 2(5AB) defines a cottage industry as a manufacturing concern that meets all of these conditions:

  1. No industrial gas or electricity connection.
  2. Located in a residential area.
  3. A total labour force of no more than ten workers.
  4. Annual turnover from all supplies of no more than Rs 8 million.

Fail any one, for example by taking an industrial electricity connection or moving to a commercial area, and the manufacturer must register.

The Tier-1 Retailer Test (Updated by the Finance Act, 2026)

Section 2(43A), as amended up to 30 June 2026, defines a Tier-1 retailer as a retailer in any of these categories:

  • A unit of a national or international chain of stores.
  • A retailer in an air-conditioned shopping mall, plaza or centre, excluding kiosks.
  • A retailer whose cumulative electricity bill for the previous twelve consecutive months exceeds Rs 1.2 million.
  • A wholesaler-cum-retailer with turnover above Rs 200 million engaged in bulk import and supply of consumer goods to retailers and consumers.
  • A retailer with turnover above Rs 200 million in the previous twelve months, by declaration or as worked back from tax deducted under Sections 236G or 236H of the Income Tax Ordinance (added in 2026).
  • Any other person or class prescribed by FBR.

The 2026 changes removed some earlier categories and added the Rs 200 million turnover test. If you registered as Tier-1 under an older category, check whether you still fall within the definition before acting either way.

Online Sellers

Section 14(1A), added by the Finance Act, 2025, requires every person selling digitally ordered goods from within Pakistan through an online marketplace, website or app to register, except cottage industry and retailers paying through electricity bills. Section 14(1B) stops marketplaces and couriers from serving them unless they hold an NTN and, where Section 14(1A) applies, a sales tax registration.

Services Are Usually Provincial

Federal sales tax under the Sales Tax Act applies to goods. Most services are taxed by the provinces through the Punjab Revenue Authority, Sindh Revenue Board, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Revenue Authority or Balochistan Revenue Authority. A consultant, salon, restaurant or agency run as a sole proprietorship checks its provincial registration, not an FBR STRN, for its services.

What Happens If You Should Register but Do Not

Sections 14AC to 14AE give FBR escalating powers against persons who make taxable supplies without the registration they need: restrictions on operating bank accounts, restrictions on transferring immovable property after a committee process that includes a chamber or trade association member, and finally sealing the business premises, seizing movable property or appointing a receiver.

After Registration

  • File monthly sales tax returns, even for a nil month.
  • Issue tax invoices that meet Section 23.
  • Keep sales tax records for six years after the tax period (Section 24).
  • If the business later falls out of every category, de-registration under Section 21 is possible after a final return under Section 28. See our guide to closing a sole proprietorship.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is sales tax registration compulsory for every sole proprietor?
No. It is compulsory only for the categories in Section 14 of the Sales Tax Act, such as manufacturers that are not cottage industries, liable retailers, importers, wholesalers, dealers, distributors and online sellers of goods.
What is the turnover limit for a cottage industry?
Annual turnover from all supplies must not exceed Rs 8 million, and the unit must also have no industrial gas or electricity connection, be in a residential area and have no more than ten workers.
What changed in the Tier-1 retailer definition in 2026?
The Finance Act, 2026 added retailers with turnover above Rs 200 million in the previous twelve months, set a Rs 200 million turnover condition for wholesaler-cum-retailers, and removed some earlier categories.
Is a shop in an air-conditioned plaza a Tier-1 retailer?
Yes, a retailer operating in an air-conditioned shopping mall, plaza or centre is Tier-1, except kiosks.
Do online sellers need an STRN?
Sellers of digitally ordered goods through a marketplace, website or app must register under Section 14(1A), except cottage industry and retailers paying through electricity bills.
Do service providers register with FBR for sales tax?
Most services are taxed by provincial authorities such as PRA, SRB, KPRA and BRA, so service businesses usually register with the province.
What can FBR do if I should be registered but am not?
Sections 14AC to 14AE allow restrictions on bank accounts and on transferring immovable property, and ultimately sealing premises, seizing movable property or appointing a receiver.
Can I cancel my STRN later?
De-registration under Section 21 is possible for persons not required to be registered, after filing a final return under Section 28.
