Bank Balances That Do Not Match Your Wealth Statement: Omitted Accounts, Wrong Dates and the Fix

24 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 24 November 2026
FBR can see bank data. A balance your statement does not show is the easiest mismatch for anyone, FBR or an embassy, to spot.

Bank balances look like the simplest line in a wealth statement, and they cause a surprising share of the corrections we make. Filers copy a balance from a mobile app on the day they file, forget the account their employer opened for them, leave out a savings certificate account, or enter a joint account twice. Because banks report to FBR and embassies read bank statements, a wrong balance is the mismatch most likely to be noticed. This guide covers the common causes and the revision.

Short answer

A wealth statement should show the balance of every account you and your dependents hold, in Pakistan and abroad, on the last day of the tax year, 30 June. Mismatches come from accounts forgotten or opened mid-year, balances taken on the wrong date, joint accounts entered in full or not at all, and profit credited after the balance was copied. Each is a wrong statement under section 116(3) and can be revised with a revised reconciliation and reasons, before any section 122(9) notice and within five years of the return due date. Use the bank's certificate of balance as on 30 June; it is the document that settles the figure.

The Right Date: 30 June

Short answer: The balance on 30 June, the last day of the tax year, taken from a bank certificate, not the balance on the day you file.

The wealth statement filed with a return under section 116(2) describes your position at the end of the tax year, which in Pakistan runs from 1 July to 30 June. Section 116A uses the same anchor for foreign assets, “as on the last day of the tax year”. The figure for each account is therefore its balance on 30 June. Banks issue a certificate of balance for a stated date on request, and that certificate is the cleanest evidence.

The most common error is using the balance on the day of filing, which may be months later. That one mistake makes two years wrong: the closing balance of one year and the opening balance of the next.

Which Accounts Belong in the Statement

Short answer: Every account of yours and of dependents under section 116(1)(b), including foreign-currency and foreign accounts.

Why Balances Go Wrong

  1. Wrong date. Balance taken on the filing day, not on 30 June.
  2. Forgotten account. A second bank, a digital bank account, or an account opened in the middle of the year.
  3. Joint account entered twice. Husband and wife both enter the full balance, or neither does.
  4. Profit missing. Profit credited on 30 June itself left out, which also means the income in the return is incomplete.
  5. Business and personal mixed. A proprietor's business account entered as a personal account and again inside capital.

Profit on bank deposits is taxed at source at 20% for persons on the Active Taxpayers List and 40% for others, so a missing account often also means profit and tax deducted that the return does not show.

Bank Loans, Financing and Closed Accounts

The bank side of a wealth statement has liabilities too. Section 116(1)(a) covers your “total assets including foreign assets and liabilities including foreign liabilities”, so the amount outstanding on 30 June on a car financing facility, a house finance, a personal loan or a running finance line belongs in the statement, from the bank's statement of outstanding balance for that date. Leaving a loan out makes the asset it paid for look unexplained: a car worth Rs 6,000,000 bought with Rs 4,500,000 of bank financing needs that liability recorded, or the reconciliation must find Rs 4,500,000 from somewhere else.

Credit cards are a smaller case of the same rule: an unpaid card balance on 30 June is a liability, although the UK guide notes that credit card statements are not evidence of funds for a visa.

Accounts closed during the year still matter. An account open on 30 June of the previous year was in last year's closing assets; if it was closed in March, its balance moved somewhere, to another account, a purchase or spending, and the reconciliation should show where. A closed account that simply disappears from one year to the next is a gap.

Revising the Statement

Short answer: Section 116(3): revised statement, revised reconciliation and reasons, before a 122(9) notice and within five years.

Section 116(3) allows a revised wealth statement when you discover any omission or wrong statement. File it with the revised wealth reconciliation and the reasons, under intimation to the Commissioner, before receiving a notice under section 122(9) for that tax year, and within five years of the return due date. Because a balance at 30 June is both a closing and an opening figure, check the next year too.

A higher balance must be explained in the reconciliation: income, a gift from a relative, a sale. A lower one may reveal that an earlier balancing entry, usually cash in hand, was covering the gap. Our guide to a reconciliation that will not match helps when the figures still do not agree.

Bank Balances and Visa Files

An embassy sees your bank statements directly. The UK guide asks for bank statements that “detail the origin of the funds held” and says statements issued more than a year before the application should not be used; Canada asks for six months of account details. An account on your bank statement that is missing from your FBR wealth statement is a contradiction in your own file. Our guide to revising before a visa covers the whole file.

How to Get the Right Balance: The Bank Certificate

The figure that settles a bank balance is the bank's own certificate of balance for 30 June. Ask your bank for one that states the account title, number and the balance as on 30 June of the year you are revising. For a term deposit or savings certificate account, ask for the principal and any profit accrued and credited up to that date. Keep the certificates with the revision: they are the documents that make the correction bona fide under the proviso to section 116(3), and they answer any later question from FBR in one page.

The same certificates help with withholding tax. The bank's annual withholding tax certificate shows tax deducted on profit, which you claim in the return; if an account was missing from the statement, its profit and tax were probably missing from the return too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which date's bank balance goes in the wealth statement?
The balance on 30 June, the last day of the tax year, ideally from a bank certificate of balance for that date.
Do I need to show a dormant account?
Yes. Every account is an asset, whatever its balance.
How do I show a joint account?
Show the part that is genuinely yours and explain the rest. Entering the full balance in two people's statements counts it twice.
Is my foreign-currency account part of the statement?
Yes, converted to rupees at 30 June. An account abroad is also included for a resident; it may trigger section 116A.
I left out an account. Is it too late?
You can revise under section 116(3) until you receive a 122(9) notice for that year, and within five years of its return due date.
What tax applies to profit on my bank account?
Profit on bank deposits is taxed at source at 20% for ATL filers and 40% for others.
How do I get my bank balance as on 30 June?
Ask your bank for a certificate of balance as on 30 June of that year, stating the account title and number.
Do I need to revise my return if I add a missing bank account?
If the account earned profit that the return left out, yes: the profit and the tax deducted on it belong in the return, revised under section 114(6) and its conditions.
