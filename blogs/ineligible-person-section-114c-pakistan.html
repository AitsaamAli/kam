Ineligible Person Under Section 114C: Who Can Buy Cars, Property and Shares Above the Thresholds, and What 'Sufficient Resources' Means

20 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 20 November 2026
Section 114C is law, but its restrictions start only from a date the Federal Government notifies. Get eligible before that happens.

Section 114C is the most talked-about change in the Finance Act 2025, and the most misunderstood. It is not a ban on non-filers buying anything; it is a test of whether your declared resources support a large purchase. Here is the full text, explained.

Short answer

Section 114C of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, with the Fifteenth Schedule (both inserted by the Finance Act 2025), stops an 'ineligible person' from booking, buying or registering a motor vehicle above Rs 7 million, registering or attesting transfer of immovable property above Rs 100 million fair market value, and holding securities or mutual fund investments above Rs 50 million. Separately, it sets an annual cash withdrawal limit of Rs 100 million across all bank accounts of an individual. An eligible person has filed a return for the tax year immediately before the transaction and has 'sufficient resources' (130% of declared cash and equivalent assets) for it, or has filed a sources of investment and expenditure statement. For an individual, eligibility includes immediate family members: parents, spouse and dependent children. Non-residents and public companies are outside the first three restrictions. Under Section 114C(5), the restrictions come into force only from a date the Federal Government notifies in the official Gazette.

Is Section 114C in Force?

Short answer: Section 114C applies only from a date the Federal Government notifies in the official Gazette (Section 114C(5)).

Section 114C(5) says all or any of the restrictions "shall come into force on such date as the Federal Government may, by notification in official Gazette, appoint", with any reductions or enhancements in the thresholds it considers appropriate. The section is part of the law, but the restrictions bite only from the notified date, and the notification may change the thresholds. Check whether a notification has been issued before relying on either position. The preparation below is worth doing either way, because it is the same work that keeps your wealth statement sound.

The Four Restrictions and Their Thresholds

Short answer: Cars above Rs 7 million, property above Rs 100 million, investments above Rs 50 million, and cash withdrawals of Rs 100 million a year.

Clauses (a) to (c) apply to ineligible persons. Clause (d) is worded differently: a bank shall not allow cash withdrawal from any account of any person above the threshold. Section 114C(2) excludes non-residents and public companies from the restrictions, except clause (d).

Who Is an Eligible Person

Short answer: An eligible person filed last year's return with sufficient resources, or a sources of investment and expenditure statement.

Section 114C(4)(a) defines an eligible person as one who has filed either:

  1. a return of income for the tax year immediately preceding the year of the transaction, and has sufficient resources in the wealth statement (individuals) or financial statements (companies and AOPs) for the transaction; or
  2. a sources of investment and expenditure statement declaring sufficient resources and explaining them for that particular purchase or investment.

For an individual, the proviso says the eligible person includes immediate family members, defined in Section 114C(4)(b) as parents, spouse and dependent children. An ineligible person is anyone who is not an eligible person.

What 'Sufficient Resources' Means: the 130% Rule

Short answer: Sufficient resources means 130% of declared cash and equivalent assets.

Section 114C(4)(e) defines sufficient resources as 130% of the cash and equivalent assets declared in the sources of investment and expenditure statement or the latest wealth statement. Cash and equivalent assets include cash in local or foreign currency, the fair market value of gold, the net realisable value of stocks, bonds and receivables, and other cash-equivalent assets as prescribed. For a company or AOP, it is the cash and equivalent assets in the financial statements attached to the latest return.

The proviso adds that where the asset is bought by exchanging capital assets already declared, the disposal of those assets counts as cash equivalent to the extent of the value in the agreement.

The Sources of Investment and Expenditure Statement

Short answer: The sources of investment statement allows the deal but is not proof of the source of income under Section 111.

Section 114C(4)(d) describes it as a declaration filed on the Board's web portal specifying the sources of funds for the transaction. Section 114C(3) says this statement, and the sufficient resources in it, shall not be construed as the nature and source of income for the purposes of Section 111. In other words, it lets the transaction go ahead, but it does not by itself answer a later question about where the money came from.

How to Prepare

Short answer: File on time, declare liquid assets fully, reconcile the wealth statement, and revise before buying if needed.

  1. File the return for the tax year before the purchase, on time, with a complete wealth statement.
  2. Declare bank balances, gold and investments at the values the law uses.
  3. Reconcile the wealth statement with income and expenses using the wealth reconciliation calculator.
  4. If something was left out, revise before a notice.
  5. For a property deal, estimate the advance taxes with the property transfer tax calculator.

Myths About Section 114C

Short answer: Filing alone is not enough, only cars above Rs 7 million are covered, and non-residents are outside clauses (a) to (c).

  • "Filers can buy anything." A return alone is not enough: 130% of your declared cash and equivalent assets must cover the transaction.
  • "Non-filers cannot buy any car." Only cars above Rs 7 million are covered, and only once in force.
  • "Overseas Pakistanis are blocked." Non-residents are outside clauses (a) to (c).
  • "The cash cap is only for non-filers." Clause (d) applies to any person.

Short answer: A filer with Rs 7 million declared liquid assets can buy a car up to Rs 9.1 million; a car below Rs 7 million is outside 114C.

Section 114C is different from the others. It looks not only at whether you filed, but at whether your declared resources support the purchase. A filer with an understated wealth statement can be ineligible; a careful filer is not. See our guides to cash withdrawal tax and Section 114B.

When to Start

Short answer: Eligibility rests on the previous tax year's return, so prepare a year before the purchase.

Because eligibility depends on the return for the tax year immediately before the transaction, preparation has to start a year ahead. Someone planning to buy a house in the year to 30 June 2027 relies on the tax year 2026 return, due 31 October 2026. If that return is filed and the wealth statement shows the cash and liquid assets that will fund the purchase, the test is met when the notification comes.

Companies and AOPs

Short answer: Companies and AOPs are tested on cash and equivalents in the financial statements filed with the latest return.

For a company or an association of persons, the eligibility test uses the financial statements attached to the return for the latest tax year instead of a wealth statement: sufficient resources are 130% of the cash and equivalent assets shown there. Public companies are outside the vehicle, property and investment restrictions altogether under Section 114C(2). A private company planning a large purchase should therefore make sure its last audited accounts, filed with the return, show the liquid resources the purchase will draw on.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Section 114C of the Income Tax Ordinance?
A Finance Act 2025 provision restricting ineligible persons from vehicle purchases above Rs 7 million, property transfers above Rs 100 million and investments above Rs 50 million, plus an annual cash withdrawal cap of Rs 100 million for an individual.
Is Section 114C in force?
Its restrictions apply from a date the Federal Government notifies in the official Gazette (Section 114C(5)). Check whether a notification has been issued.
Who is an eligible person under Section 114C?
A person who filed a return for the preceding tax year with sufficient resources for the transaction, or filed a sources of investment and expenditure statement declaring sufficient resources.
What does sufficient resources mean?
130% of cash and equivalent assets declared in the latest wealth statement or the sources of investment and expenditure statement.
Can my family's resources count?
For an individual, the eligible person includes immediate family members: parents, spouse and dependent children.
Does Section 114C apply to overseas Pakistanis?
Non-residents are excluded from the vehicle, property and investment restrictions, but not from the cash withdrawal cap.
Is there a cash withdrawal limit under Section 114C?
Yes: Rs 100 million a year across all bank accounts of an individual, once notified. It applies to any person.
Does the sources of investment statement prove where the money came from?
No. Section 114C(3) says it is not the nature and source of income for Section 111.
