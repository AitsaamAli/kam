Multan's Agricultural-Trade and Handicraft Economy

Multan sits at the center of southern Punjab's agricultural trade, particularly mango and citrus exports, alongside a long-standing handicraft tradition in blue pottery, camel-skin lamps, and embroidered textiles sold both domestically and for export. This gives Multan a taxpayer base weighted more toward seasonal trading income and small export-oriented workshops than most other Punjab cities of comparable size.

Kamboh Associates files income tax returns, registers NTN, and handles sales tax registration for Multan clients 100% online via WhatsApp — from mango commission agents to Cantt-based salaried professionals.

Mango & Citrus Export and Commission Trade

Multan's mango and citrus trade runs on a mix of orchard owners, commission agents (arhtis) who broker sales between growers and buyers, and exporters who consolidate produce for shipment abroad during the harvest season.

Common tax situations: Commission agents earn income that is highly seasonal, concentrated in just a few months of the year — this needs to be declared as business income for the full tax year, not treated as a one-off windfall. Orchard owners selling agricultural produce directly benefit from agricultural income tax treatment (a provincial matter), but any non-agricultural income — trading, transport, or processing margins — must still be declared federally. Exporters need NTN and, where they operate at meaningful scale, sales tax registration with correct zero-rating documentation for export sales.

Handicraft, Camel-Skin & Ceramics Workshops

Multan's traditional handicraft sector — blue pottery (kashi), camel-skin lamps, and embroidered textiles — is largely made up of small family workshops, many supplying both local retail and export buyers through Karachi and Lahore-based trading houses.

Common tax situations: Most handicraft workshops operate below the formal tax net entirely, which becomes a problem the moment an export order requires an NTN and STRN for customs and buyer compliance documentation. Workshops selling through a Lahore or Karachi export house still need their own NTN even if the export house handles the actual shipping and foreign buyer relationship, since the income is earned by the workshop, not the intermediary.

Cantt & Gulgasht Colony — Professional Residential Areas

Multan Cantt and Gulgasht Colony are the city's established upper and middle-income residential areas, home to doctors, engineers, government officers, and business owners, with Nishtar Medical University and several private hospitals drawing a significant medical-professional population.

Common tax situations: Doctors with a private clinic alongside a hospital position need salary and practice income combined correctly on one return. Government officers in Cantt need advance tax reconciliation and correct GP Fund declaration. Property owners in Gulgasht Colony and Cantt must declare every plot or house in their wealth statement — this is where FBR notices most often originate in these areas, when a declared income doesn't match visible property holdings.

Bosan Road, Vehari Road & New Multan

Bosan Road, Vehari Road, and New Multan form Multan's newer commercial and residential expansion corridor, with retail showrooms, private schools, and a growing number of housing societies attracting professionals relocating from the older city core.

Common tax situations: Retailers and showroom owners along Bosan Road who cross the sales tax threshold need STRN registration and monthly filing — a step many delay until a supplier or bank requires proof of registration. Buyers of plots in New Multan's newer societies typically need NTN registration completed before their booking is finalized, and every purchased plot must appear in the following year's wealth statement.

Overseas Multan Workers & Family Remittances

Multan and the surrounding southern Punjab districts send a large number of workers to the Gulf, particularly Saudi Arabia and the UAE, and remittances sent home to family in Multan fund a significant share of local property purchases and household spending.

Common tax situations: Remittances received through official banking channels from a family member working abroad are exempt from income tax, but the receiving family member's wealth statement still needs to correctly record the source as foreign remittance rather than leaving the corresponding property or bank balance unexplained. Families who used remittance money to buy a plot in Gulgasht Colony or New Multan on behalf of the overseas worker need the asset declared under the correct owner's name, matching the property documents — a mismatch here is a frequent, avoidable cause of FBR scrutiny. Overseas workers who retain Pakistani income sources, such as a rented-out family property, still need to file a Pakistan return for that income even while living and working abroad.

Nishtar Medical University & the Private Healthcare Cluster

Multan's status as southern Punjab's main medical hub, anchored by Nishtar Medical University and a large cluster of private hospitals and diagnostic labs, means the city has an unusually high concentration of doctors, consultants, and healthcare entrepreneurs relative to its size — many drawing patients from Dera Ghazi Khan, Bahawalpur, and across the southern Punjab belt.

Common tax situations: Consultants who see patients across multiple private hospitals need each hospital's withholding tax deduction (typically under Section 153) correctly reconciled against total practice income on their annual return. Diagnostic lab and small clinic owners crossing the sales tax threshold on lab services need to confirm whether their specific services fall under sales tax on services (a provincial matter in Punjab, administered separately from federal FBR sales tax) — a distinction Kamboh Associates clarifies case by case, since misapplying the wrong regime is a common and costly error.

Active Filer Status and Why It Matters More for Multan Traders

Multan's economy runs on a higher proportion of cash and trade-based transactions than a typical salaried city, which means the withholding-tax gap between filers and non-filers is felt more directly here — on bank transactions, on vehicle registration through the Excise and Taxation office, and on property transfers across Cantt, Gulgasht Colony, and New Multan. A commission agent or workshop owner who has never filed pays a materially higher effective tax rate on these routine transactions than a formally registered active filer, even before considering the audit risk of remaining outside the tax net.

Frequently Asked Questions

I'm a mango commission agent in Multan — how is my seasonal income taxed?
Your commission income for the entire harvest season is declared as business income for the full tax year, not treated separately as a seasonal windfall. Kamboh Associates prepares returns for Multan commission agents with this treatment applied correctly.

Yes. The income belongs to your workshop even if an export house in Lahore or Karachi handles the shipping and foreign buyer relationship. Most export houses now require your own NTN for compliance documentation before they will place repeat orders.

My family in Multan bought property using money I sent from Saudi Arabia — how should this be declared?

I'm a doctor practicing at more than one private hospital in Multan — how is my tax handled?
