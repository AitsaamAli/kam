Overseas Pakistanis: What the October 15 Extension Means for You

October 2026 Kamboh Associates 10 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist

Living abroad doesn't put you outside FBR's filing system, and FBR's decision to push the Tax Year 2026 deadline from 30 September to 15 October applies to overseas Pakistanis in exactly the same way it applies to residents — with one added advantage: filing from abroad has never required being physically present in Pakistan, so the extension is simply extra time to handle something that was always going to be a remote process anyway.

TL;DR

The extension to 15 October 2026 applies to overseas Pakistanis the same way it applies to residents. NICOP holders, RDA account holders, and anyone with property or Pakistan-source income back home should use the extra time to gather documents and file remotely — Kamboh Associates prepares overseas Pakistani returns from Rs. 6,000, entirely over WhatsApp, with no need to be physically present.

Why This Extension Matters Even If You Don't Live in Pakistan

A meaningful share of overseas Pakistanis delay filing not out of avoidance but out of friction — time zones, uncertainty about what's actually required from abroad, and the assumption that it somehow needs an in-person step that living overseas makes difficult. None of that has ever been true, and the extension to 15 October is simply more time to get past that friction and file a return that was always fully doable remotely.

Do Overseas Pakistanis Even Need to File

It depends on your specific situation, but it's more common than many assume: if you hold an NTN, own property in Pakistan, have Pakistan-source income — rent, a business interest, dividends — or maintain accounts that require filer status to operate efficiently, you likely have a filing obligation even while living and working abroad. Residency status affects how certain income is treated, but it doesn't automatically remove you from the system.

What Counts as Pakistan-Source Income for Someone Abroad

Rent from a property you still own in Pakistan, profit on an investment held there, income from a business interest or partnership back home, and gains from selling property are the most common categories overseas Pakistanis need to declare, regardless of where they personally live and work. Salary earned entirely abroad, from a foreign employer, is generally treated differently — but the Pakistan-sourced pieces still need proper disclosure.

The NICOP and RDA Account Angle

Roshan Digital Account holders and NICOP-based filers have specific disclosure requirements around foreign remittances and account activity, and getting that disclosure right matters more for this group than most, since RDA transactions are closely tracked by design. If you hold an RDA or file using NICOP details rather than a standard CNIC, make sure whoever prepares your return understands exactly how that account activity should be reported.

Why Filing From Abroad Is Easier Than Most Overseas Pakistanis Assume

The entire process — document submission, review, and the actual IRIS filing — happens without needing to set foot in Pakistan. The assumption that overseas filing requires a visit, a power of attorney, or some in-person step is simply outdated; it's a remote process by default, handled the same way whether the client is sitting in Lahore or Los Angeles.

What Documents Actually Travel Well Over WhatsApp

  • A clear photo or scan of your CNIC or NICOP
  • Bank statements — both Pakistani accounts and, where relevant, the foreign account receiving your income
  • Property documents, if you own property in Pakistan generating rent or held as an investment
  • RDA statements, if applicable
  • Any foreign tax documents relevant to income that also needs reporting in Pakistan

None of this requires originals or in-person verification — clear photographs sent over WhatsApp are sufficient for preparing the return.

Property Back Home and Why This Deadline Matters to It

If you own property in Pakistan, your filer status directly affects the withholding rate applied whenever that property is sold, and your wealth statement needs to reflect it accurately regardless of where you live. Overseas Pakistanis planning to sell property in the near future have a direct, practical reason to make sure their filing is current well before any such transaction, not scrambled together once a buyer is already in the picture.

Time Zones and the Practical Side of a Remote Deadline

15 October in Pakistan Standard Time is the date that matters, regardless of what time it is where you're based. WhatsApp communication works across time zones without requiring both sides to be online simultaneously — send your documents whenever it's convenient for you, and the preparation happens on our end without needing a live call scheduled around your local hours.

What Happens If You Simply Never Register

Staying off FBR's system entirely doesn't remove the obligation if one genuinely exists — it simply means any Pakistan-source income or property you hold accumulates risk quietly, surfacing later as a larger, more complicated problem (often when a property sale, an inheritance matter, or a bank requirement forces the question) rather than a straightforward annual filing handled consistently each year.

Double Taxation and Tax Treaties

Many overseas Pakistanis live in countries with a double-taxation treaty with Pakistan, which can affect how certain income is treated to avoid being taxed twice on the same earnings. This is a genuinely technical area, and it’s worth getting specific advice on your situation rather than assuming either that nothing from your country of residence is relevant, or that a treaty automatically exempts everything. The treaty’s terms, and how your specific income is classified, both matter.

Inheritance, Gifts and Money Sent to Family

Money you send to family in Pakistan for their support, or that you receive as a gift or inheritance, generally isn’t treated as your taxable income in the way earned income is — but it often needs to be properly documented and, where relevant, reflected in the wealth statement so that assets purchased with those funds have a clear, traceable source. Overseas Pakistanis who’ve been sending money home for years sometimes find this is the part of their filing history that needs the most careful reconstruction.

If You Don’t Have an NTN Yet

Some overseas Pakistanis reach the point of needing to file — because of a property sale, a bank requirement, or simply deciding it’s time — without ever having registered for an NTN in the first place. That’s a straightforward first step, handled the same way remotely: CNIC or NICOP details, proof of address, and the registration itself typically processes quickly once submitted, after which the return can follow in the same sitting.

If You’re Planning to Return to Pakistan Soon

Overseas Pakistanis planning a permanent or long-term return to Pakistan have a particular reason to get their filing current before the move — residency status, how foreign assets and income are treated, and your Active Taxpayer List status all become more immediately relevant once you’re living and transacting in Pakistan day to day. Sorting this out from abroad, while it’s still a remote, lower-pressure process, is considerably easier than doing it after you’ve already relocated.

When a Family Member Manages Your Property in Pakistan

It’s common for an overseas Pakistani’s property to be managed day-to-day by a relative back home — collecting rent, handling maintenance, dealing with tenants. That arrangement doesn’t change whose income it is for tax purposes; the income still belongs to you as the owner and needs to be declared on your own return, even though someone else is handling it practically. Keep a clear record of what’s collected on your behalf, even informally, so it can be reflected accurately.

When Both Spouses Are Living Abroad

If you and your spouse are both filing from abroad, each of you files individually — income and assets aren’t automatically combined into a single joint return the way some other countries’ tax systems work. Keep each person’s documents and income sources separate and clear, even when finances are shared practically as a household, since the filing itself needs to reflect each individual’s own position.

The Bottom Line for Overseas Filers

Distance from Pakistan has never been the obstacle it’s sometimes assumed to be — the process, the documentation, and now the extended 15 October deadline all work the same way whether you’re filing from Lahore or from another continent. The only real requirement is starting the conversation with enough lead time to gather documents across whatever time zone you’re working from.

The Bottom Line

Living abroad was never the obstacle to filing that it’s sometimes assumed to be. The process is remote by design, the deadline is the same 15 October regardless of your location, and the only thing distance genuinely affects is how far in advance you should start — which, with the extension in place, you still have time to do comfortably.

NTN registration and income tax filing are purely FBR processes, handled online, with no embassy or consular step required at any stage regardless of which country you’re filing from.
