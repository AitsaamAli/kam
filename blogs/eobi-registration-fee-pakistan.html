Which Employers Are Legally Required to Register for EOBI

EOBI registration is mandatory for employers meeting the threshold set under the Employees' Old-Age Benefits Act — broadly, businesses employing a minimum number of eligible workers. Many small business owners assume EOBI only applies to large factories or corporations, but a shop, a small service business, or an office with enough eligible employees can fall within scope. Skipping registration when required is a compliance gap that can surface during an EOBI inspection or an employee complaint, well after the fact.

If you're unsure whether your business meets the threshold, this is worth confirming before assuming either way — WhatsApp your employee count and business type to 0328-4675162.

What the Rs. 8,000 Registration Fee Covers

The one-time Rs. 8,000 employer EOBI registration fee, published on our pricing page, includes:

  • Employer registration on the EOBI portal with your business/company details
  • Initial enrollment of your existing eligible employees under the employer's EOBI code
  • Confirming correct employee categorization and contribution base for each enrolled employee
  • Delivery of your EOBI registration confirmation and employer code

This is a one-time setup fee. It does not include the ongoing monthly contribution filing required from the point of registration onward — that's a separate recurring service, explained next.

The Ongoing Cost: Monthly EOBI Contribution Filing

Once registered, an employer must file EOBI contributions every month for as long as eligible employees remain on payroll — this is a continuous obligation, not a one-time event, much like sales tax return filing. Kamboh Associates charges Rs. 2,000 per month for ongoing EOBI contribution filing, which includes calculating the correct employer and employee contribution amounts, submitting the monthly filing, and confirming receipt.

As with sales tax, the real first-year cost of EOBI compliance is the Rs. 8,000 registration plus 12 months of Rs. 2,000 filing — roughly Rs. 32,000 for the first year, not just the headline registration number.

What EOBI Actually Provides Employees

Beyond the compliance obligation, EOBI registration genuinely benefits registered employees — it builds toward an old-age pension, and provides survivor and invalidity benefits under the scheme. Framing EOBI purely as a compliance cost misses that it's also a real, tangible benefit an employer is providing, which matters when explaining the deduction to employees who may not be familiar with what it actually funds.

What Happens During an EOBI Inspection

EOBI can conduct compliance inspections of registered employers, checking whether declared employee counts and contribution amounts match actual payroll records. An employer with accurate, consistently filed monthly contributions has little to worry about in this scenario — the inspection simply confirms what's already correctly on record. The real risk is for employers who registered but let filings lapse, or who under-declared employee counts to reduce contribution costs — both of which surface clearly during an inspection and can result in backdated contribution demands plus penalties, a far larger cost than simply filing correctly and continuously from the start.

How EOBI Fits Alongside a Business's Other Employer Obligations

EOBI is one of several distinct employer obligations that often get confused with each other, so it's worth being clear about how it differs from related requirements. Withholding tax on employee salaries (Section 149) is a separate filing, covering income tax deducted at source from each employee's pay — this continues monthly regardless of EOBI status and is priced separately (Rs. 2,500/month, see our monthly retainer cost guide). Provincial social security contributions (where applicable, depending on province and sector) are a further, separate obligation from EOBI, which is a federal scheme. An employer with eligible staff may therefore be juggling EOBI, withholding tax, and potentially provincial social security simultaneously — three distinct filings with three distinct deadlines, easy to lose track of without a coordinated view of all three.

This is also why EOBI registration shouldn't be evaluated in isolation when budgeting employer costs — a realistic monthly employer compliance cost for a business with staff typically bundles EOBI's Rs. 2,000/month together with the WHT filing, not as two unrelated line items handled by different processes.

Payment Terms for EOBI Registration and Ongoing Filing

The Rs. 8,000 registration fee is paid once, upon successful employer code issuance — there's no advance payment required before the registration application is filed. Ongoing monthly contribution filing at Rs. 2,000/month is billed per month as the filing is completed, not as an annual lump sum upfront, so there's no large advance commitment required to start. If your employee count changes significantly during the year — a round of hiring, or unfortunately a round of layoffs — let us know as it happens rather than at the next filing deadline, since contribution calculations need to reflect the actual current roster accurately each month, not a stale headcount from months earlier.

Common EOBI Compliance Mistakes

  • Assuming EOBI only applies to large factories — the threshold can capture smaller offices and shops with enough eligible employees, and this assumption is one of the most common reasons businesses discover a compliance gap only during an inspection.
  • Not updating employee records when staff join or leave — an outdated EOBI roster creates a mismatch between actual payroll and what's being filed monthly.
  • Miscalculating the contribution base — under- or over-stating an employee's salary for contribution purposes creates a discrepancy that surfaces during any future EOBI review.
  • Treating EOBI purely as a cost rather than explaining its benefit to employees — this can create unnecessary friction with staff who don't understand what the deduction actually funds (old-age pension and survivor benefits).
  • Letting monthly filings lapse after initial registration — registering once and then treating EOBI as "done" misses that the monthly filing obligation continues indefinitely while eligible employees remain on payroll.
