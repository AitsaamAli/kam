Liberty Chowk Lahore — Jewellers and Gold Dealers: Stock, Old Gold and Sales Tax

Updated October 2026
Kamboh Associates
3 min read
Liberty Chowk

Jewellery shops around Liberty Chowk and Gulberg's markets serve Lahore's bridal and investment demand. A jeweller's business is unusual: high-value stock that changes in price daily, a constant flow of old gold bought back from customers, and making charges that are really the business's profit. Tax records have to cope with all three.

Stock: weight and value

A jeweller's stock is gold, silver, stones and finished jewellery. For tax and accounts, stock is generally valued at cost, but because gold prices move constantly, keep records by weight and purity as well as value:

Stock itemRecord
Raw gold / barsWeight, purity, cost
Finished jewelleryWeight, purity, cost (gold + making)
Old gold bought from customersWeight, purity, price paid, seller's CNIC
StonesDescription, cost

A weight-based register is what makes a jeweller's stock credible.

Buying old gold from customers

Families sell old jewellery to fund weddings or exchange it for new pieces. For each purchase, record the seller's CNIC, the weight and purity, and the price paid. This protects you against stolen goods, satisfies due-diligence obligations, and supports your cost of stock.

Making charges and margins

Jewellers typically charge the gold value plus making charges (and sometimes a margin on gold). Your income includes all of it. A jeweller who records only making charges as income is understating turnover.

Sales tax on jewellery

Gold and jewellery have specific sales tax treatment, with special rates and arrangements for jewellers in recent years. Larger jewellers and those in qualifying premises may also fall under tier-1 retailer requirements. We check your status and the current schedules.

Due diligence obligations

Dealers in precious metals and stones are treated as designated non-financial businesses for anti-money-laundering purposes, with customer due diligence and record-keeping obligations, especially for large cash transactions. This isn't tax law, but it affects what records you must keep.

Customers often ask jewellers for a receipt when they sell gold, to explain the cash in their own records. Issue proper receipts — they help your customers and document your purchases.

The jeweller's own gold

A jeweller's personal or family gold should be kept separate from business stock. If family jewellery is in the shop's safe, it's still personal. Record it in your wealth statement separately.

Gold savings schemes and advance bookings

Some jewellers run savings schemes where customers pay monthly towards future jewellery, or take advance payments for bridal sets to be delivered months later. Money received before delivery is a liability to the customer, not income. Record each customer's balance, and recognise the sale when the jewellery is handed over. If gold prices move in between, the scheme's terms decide who bears the change.

Liberty Chowk — questions we get asked

How should a jeweller value gold stock?
Generally at cost, with a register by weight and purity as well as value.
What records do I need when buying old gold from customers?
The seller's CNIC, weight, purity and price paid, for every purchase.
Are making charges my only income?
No. Your income includes the full sale value — gold plus making charges and any margin.
Do jewellers have anti-money-laundering duties?
Yes. Dealers in precious metals and stones have customer due diligence and record-keeping obligations, especially for large cash transactions.
