Punjab Sales Tax on Services (PRA) Pakistan 2026 — Complete Guide

June 2026 Kamboh Associates 6 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN • SECP • Sales Tax Specialist

PRA is not a Punjab branch of FBR — it's an entirely separate tax authority, with its own registration, its own return, and its own rules, and a business that only ever dealt with FBR can find PRA's system genuinely unfamiliar the first time it applies.

TL;DR

The Punjab Revenue Authority (PRA) administers sales tax on services rendered in Punjab, entirely separate from FBR's federal sales tax on goods and from other provinces' revenue authorities. Registration is required once a service provider crosses PRA's threshold or falls within a category PRA has specifically brought into the tax net, and PRA-registered businesses file their own monthly returns distinct from any FBR sales tax filing. Confusing PRA registration with FBR STRN registration, or assuming a service is automatically exempt, are the most common compliance failures. Overview — PRA Is a Separate Authority, Not an FBR Branch

Since the 18th Constitutional Amendment shifted the power to tax services to the provinces, each province created its own revenue authority to administer sales tax on services within its territory — in Punjab, that's the Punjab Revenue Authority (PRA). PRA is not a regional office of FBR and does not share FBR's registration database, return-filing system, or enforcement process. A business dealing with both federal sales tax on goods and Punjab's sales tax on services is, in practical terms, dealing with two separate tax administrations that happen to both call their charge "sales tax," which is precisely where a lot of the confusion around this topic comes from.

Which Services PRA Actually Taxes

PRA's list of taxable services has expanded significantly since the authority's creation, now covering a wide range of professional, technical, franchise, advertising, hospitality, and consumer services rendered in Punjab, alongside categories added or adjusted through successive Punjab Finance Acts. Because this list is periodically revised — services can be added, rates changed, or exemptions narrowed from one year to the next — a business needs to check its specific service category against PRA's current notified list rather than relying on what was or wasn't taxable in a previous filing year.

Registration Threshold and Process

A service provider whose business falls within a PRA-taxable category, and whose turnover crosses the applicable registration threshold, is required to register with PRA and obtain a registration number distinct from any FBR NTN or STRN the business may already hold. Registration is generally handled through PRA's own online portal, requiring business documentation, CNIC/NTN details of the proprietor or company, and bank account information — a separate application process from FBR's own registration, even though some of the underlying documents overlap.

Key point: An FBR NTN or federal sales tax STRN does not substitute for PRA registration. A Punjab-based service business can be fully compliant with FBR and still be in default with PRA if it never separately registered for provincial sales tax on services.

Standard PRA Rate vs Sector-Specific Rates

PRA applies a standard rate to most taxable services, but a number of specific service categories carry reduced or sector-specific rates set out separately in PRA's notifications, reflecting policy choices about which sectors should carry a lighter or heavier tax burden. Because these sector-specific rates are adjusted periodically and don't move in lockstep with the standard rate, a business shouldn't assume its applicable rate is fixed permanently at whatever applied when it first registered — the current rate for its specific service category needs to be confirmed at each filing.

Filing Returns with PRA

PRA-registered businesses generally file monthly returns declaring output tax on services rendered during the period and any allowable input tax adjustment against tax paid on qualifying business inputs, broadly mirroring the structure of FBR's own sales tax return cycle but submitted through PRA's separate portal, on PRA's own deadline, with its own late-filing penalty structure. A business already used to FBR's monthly sales tax rhythm can find PRA's cycle procedurally familiar, but the two filings are not linked — filing on time with FBR has no bearing on PRA compliance status, and vice versa.

Input Tax Adjustment — Services vs Goods Sales Tax

A registered service business can generally adjust input tax paid on qualifying purchases against its PRA output tax liability, similar in principle to how goods-based sales tax registration works with FBR — but the specific rules on what qualifies as an admissible input, and how cross-adjustment between a PRA services liability and an FBR goods-related input tax is or isn't allowed, differ from the federal goods regime and shouldn't be assumed to work identically. A business operating in both goods and services needs to keep these two input tax pools properly separated in its accounting rather than netting them together informally.

PRA's Withholding Regime — When the Client Deducts Instead

Separate from standard registration and self-filing, PRA also operates a withholding mechanism under which certain categories of service recipients — often government departments, larger companies, or other designated withholding agents — are required to withhold PRA sales tax when paying a service provider, particularly where that provider isn't itself properly registered with PRA. This shifts part of the collection responsibility onto the payer rather than relying solely on the service provider to self-charge and deposit the tax, and a business receiving services from an unregistered provider needs to check whether it falls into a category obligated to withhold, since failing to do so when required creates its own separate default distinct from the service provider's own compliance failure.

Exemptions and Zero-Rating Under PRA

Not every service rendered in Punjab is taxable — PRA notifies specific exemptions and, in some cases, zero-rated treatment for particular service categories, often reflecting policy priorities such as supporting export-oriented services or specific social-sector activities. As with the taxable services list itself, exemptions and zero-rating categories are subject to periodic revision, and a service provider relying on an exemption should periodically reconfirm that the exemption still applies to their specific category rather than assuming a status obtained years earlier remains unchanged indefinitely.

Enforcement and Penalties for Non-Compliance

PRA maintains its own penalty structure for late registration, late filing, and non-payment, separate from and not coordinated with FBR's penalty regime for federal tax defaults — a business can be in good standing with FBR while simultaneously accumulating PRA penalties for a service tax obligation it never addressed, and the two compliance histories don't offset each other in any way. PRA also conducts its own audits and enforcement actions independent of FBR, meaning a business's federal tax audit history offers no protection or immunity from a separate PRA inquiry into its provincial service tax compliance.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming FBR registration covers PRA obligations: operating a Punjab-based service business without ever registering separately with PRA, believing an existing NTN or STRN was sufficient.
  • Missing an added service category: continuing to treat a service as untaxed after PRA has brought that category into the tax net through an updated notification.
  • Confusing cross-provincial service delivery rules: assuming registration in one province automatically covers services rendered to clients physically located in Punjab.
  • Filing FBR sales tax but skipping PRA, or vice versa: treating the two filing obligations as interchangeable when a business genuinely owes both.
  • Applying the wrong rate for a sector-specific service category: defaulting to the standard rate, or an outdated reduced rate, without checking the currently notified rate for that specific service.

A Worked Example

A Lahore-based marketing and advertising agency provides services exclusively to clients within Punjab, and has an existing FBR NTN and income tax filing history but has never registered with PRA, on the assumption that "sales tax" already meant dealing with FBR. Once the agency's turnover crosses PRA's registration threshold for its service category, it needs a separate PRA registration, monthly PRA returns charging the applicable rate on its advertising services, and its own input tax reconciliation — entirely independent of its existing FBR income tax filing, which continues unchanged and unaffected by the new PRA obligation sitting alongside it. If one of the agency's clients happens to be a government department designated as a PRA withholding agent, that client may also need to withhold PRA tax directly from its payments to the agency, adding a third moving part to a relationship that, from the agency's point of view, used to be a simple invoice-and-get-paid arrangement before PRA registration became necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PRA the same as FBR?
No. PRA (Punjab Revenue Authority) is Punjab's own provincial tax authority, separate from FBR, responsible specifically for sales tax on services rendered within Punjab. FBR handles federal sales tax on goods and income tax; PRA has its own registration, its own return, and its own enforcement, entirely independent of FBR's systems even though a business may deal with both simultaneously.
Which services need to register with PRA?
PRA taxes a defined list of services rendered in Punjab, which has expanded over successive Punjab Finance Acts to cover an increasingly broad range of professional, technical, and consumer services. Whether a specific service is currently within PRA's tax net, and at what threshold registration becomes mandatory, needs to be checked against PRA's current notified list rather than assumed based on what was taxable in a prior year.
Do I need both an FBR sales tax registration and a PRA registration?
It depends on what the business does. A business selling goods generally deals with FBR's sales tax system; a business providing taxable services in Punjab generally deals with PRA; and a business doing both — selling goods and providing services — can genuinely need both registrations simultaneously, filing separate returns to each authority for the respective activity.
What happens if I provide services in Punjab but I'm registered elsewhere?
Where a service is treated as rendered in Punjab, PRA can expect registration and tax collection regardless of where the service provider's head office or primary registration sits, since the taxing point generally follows where the service is actually rendered or consumed rather than the provider's home base. Cross-provincial service businesses need to assess this carefully rather than assuming registration in one province covers services delivered in another.
How often are PRA returns filed?
PRA-registered businesses generally file returns on a monthly cycle, declaring output tax collected on taxable services and any allowable input tax adjustment, similar in structure to FBR's own sales tax return cycle but submitted separately to PRA rather than to FBR.
