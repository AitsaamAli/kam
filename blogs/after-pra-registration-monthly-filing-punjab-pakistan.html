After PRA Registration — What a Punjab Services Business Must File Every Month

September 2026 Kamboh Associates 9 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist

Registering with the Punjab Revenue Authority is a necessary first step for a Punjab-based services business, but registration alone does not satisfy the ongoing obligation — a monthly return needs to be filed with PRA for as long as the business remains registered and providing taxable services, regardless of how the registration process itself went.

TL;DR

A services business registered with PRA must file a monthly sales tax return reflecting its taxable services provided during that period, the tax collected, and any input tax adjustments — following its own recurring cycle independent of any other tax obligation the business carries. This ongoing monthly filing is where most of the actual compliance effort lies, well beyond the one-time registration step.

Registration Is Just the Starting Point

Many businesses treat PRA registration as the finish line, when in practice it is the starting point for a recurring monthly obligation that continues for as long as the business remains registered and actively provides taxable services. The registration itself is typically the quicker, more mechanical step; the ongoing monthly filing discipline is where the real, sustained compliance effort actually lives.

What the Monthly PRA Return Actually Covers

  • Total taxable services provided during the month, with the corresponding sales tax collected from clients.
  • Any input tax adjustments the business is entitled to claim against its output tax liability.
  • The net tax payable (or, in some cases, refundable) for that specific month.
  • Any specific annexures or supporting schedules PRA's current return format requires.

When the Monthly Return Is Due

PRA sets its own specific monthly deadline for the return, and this should be confirmed against PRA's current, published schedule rather than assumed to mirror FBR's federal sales tax deadline, since provincial and federal filing calendars are not necessarily identical. Building this specific deadline into a business's own recurring compliance calendar, alongside any FBR obligations, prevents it from being overlooked.

Does a Month With No Taxable Services Still Need a Filing

Generally yes — a registered business is expected to file its monthly return even in a period with no taxable activity, similar in principle to how FBR sales tax registration requires a nil return rather than simply skipping a quiet month. Confirming this specific expectation for your registration category avoids assuming a quiet month means no filing obligation.

Keeping Records Organized for Each Month's Filing

A running record of invoices issued, tax collected, and any input tax documentation throughout the month — rather than trying to reconstruct the full picture only at filing time — makes each month's PRA return considerably more straightforward to prepare accurately and on time. This is the same underlying discipline that makes FBR monthly sales tax filing manageable, applied to the provincial obligation as well.

Our related guide on the general mechanics of the monthly provincial sales tax return covers the input tax and output tax calculation logic in more depth.

Common Mistakes Businesses Make in the Months After Registering

The most common issue is simply losing momentum after the registration process concludes — the first month or two get filed carefully, and then the recurring discipline slips as other business priorities take over. Treating the monthly PRA filing with the same standing, recurring priority as payroll or rent — a fixed, non-negotiable monthly task — prevents this common drift.

What Happens If a Monthly Filing Is Missed

A missed PRA monthly filing generally carries its own penalty exposure, and the sooner a missed month is identified and filed, the more contained the resulting cost — this mirrors the same escalating-cost logic that applies to FBR filings, and the same urgency around prompt correction applies here as well.

Coordinating PRA Filing With a Business's Other Tax Obligations

A Punjab services business often carries FBR income tax obligations, and possibly FBR sales tax registration too, alongside its PRA registration — treating these as one coordinated compliance calendar, rather than several separately-tracked obligations that happen to belong to the same business, reduces the chance any one of them gets overlooked while attention is on another.

Filing Through PRA's Own Online Portal

PRA maintains its own dedicated online portal, separate from FBR's IRIS system, for registration, monthly return filing, and payment — a business new to provincial compliance should expect a genuinely different system with its own login, its own interface conventions, and its own specific quirks compared to what they may already be familiar with from FBR filing. Budgeting a little extra time to get comfortable with this separate system in the first few months is a reasonable expectation rather than assuming it will feel immediately identical to FBR's process.

PRA's Specific Late Filing Penalty Structure

PRA applies its own specific penalty framework for late monthly filings, distinct from FBR's federal structure and from other provincial authorities' frameworks — our related guide on provincial sales tax late filing penalties covers the general escalating-cost principle that applies here, though the exact PRA-specific figures should be confirmed against PRA's own current published schedule.
