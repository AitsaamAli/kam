Assets Commonly Missing From Wealth Statements in Pakistan, and How to Add Them Correctly

11 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 11 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 11 November 2026
Section 116 requires all assets and liabilities, including foreign ones, and those of dependents. Most problems come from a few assets people simply forget.

A wealth statement does not need to be complicated, but it must be complete. FBR increasingly receives information from banks, property registrars, vehicle registration authorities and other sources. This guide goes asset by asset through what people commonly leave out, how to value it, and how to add it properly.

TL;DR

Section 116 requires a wealth statement to show the person's total assets and liabilities, including foreign assets and liabilities, the assets of a dependent spouse, minor children and other dependents, assets transferred to others during the year with the consideration, and personal expenditure. The assets most often left out are plots and files, vehicles, gold, cash, bank and savings accounts, prize bonds and certificates, loans given, business capital and foreign accounts. Missing assets can be added by a revised wealth statement under Section 116(3) before a 122(9) notice; unexplained assets can otherwise be taxed under Section 111.

What Section 116 Requires

  • Your total assets and liabilities, including foreign assets and liabilities, as on the date specified (30 June for the annual statement).
  • Assets and liabilities of your spouse, minor children and other dependents; a spouse's assets only if the spouse is dependent.
  • Assets transferred by you to any other person during the period, and the consideration received.
  • Your personal expenditure and that of your dependents.
  • The wealth reconciliation statement.

Liabilities People Forget

  • Bank loans, car financing and credit card balances.
  • Loans from relatives, which should be documented.
  • Advances received against a sale not yet completed.

Forgetting a liability overstates your wealth and can create an apparent gap that you then have to explain.

Adding an Asset Means Explaining Its Source

Every asset added must be explained by money that was already declared or by a new, documented source: savings in an earlier statement, a gift (with the giver's details), inheritance (with succession documents), a loan (with the lender's details), or income. If the source is income that was not declared, the return for that year must also be revised and the tax paid. Under Section 111, an asset with no satisfactory explanation of its source can be included in income as income from other sources.

When Was It Acquired?

If the asset was acquired in an earlier year, the correction usually starts in that year and carries forward, because each closing statement is the next opening statement. FBR can amend assessments for up to five years under Section 122(2), so the years from about Tax Year 2021 onwards are the ones to review. See our guide to revising the last five years.

How to Add a Missing Asset

  1. Identify the year the asset was acquired and its cost.
  2. Collect evidence: purchase deed, transfer letter, payment records, gift or inheritance papers.
  3. Revise the wealth statement for that year under Section 116(3), with a revised reconciliation and written reasons, before any 122(9) notice for that year.
  4. Revise later years so the asset appears each year until disposed of.
  5. If income arose from it (rent, profit), revise the return for those years too.

For the most recent year, our guide to the Tax Year 2026 revision window explains the 60-day rule for returns.

How to Value Assets Consistently

Use the same basis every year so the statement can be followed from one year to the next. For most assets, that means the cost you actually paid, adjusted for additions such as construction or improvements, and for disposals. Do not inflate or deflate values to make a reconciliation balance; a value that jumps without a transaction is itself a question.

Inherited Assets

An inherited share in a house, land or gold is your asset from the date of inheritance. Show your share, keep the succession certificate or mutation, and explain the inflow in the reconciliation as inheritance. If the property earns rent, your share of the rent is your income.

Gifts Within the Family

Gifts are a common explanation for new assets, and a common source of questions. Record the giver's name and CNIC, the date and amount, and how it was given, ideally by bank transfer. The giver's own wealth statement should show the gift going out, as Section 116(1)(c) requires assets transferred to others, and the consideration, to be shown.

Jointly Owned Assets

Where an asset is owned jointly, for example a plot bought by two brothers, each shows his own share at his share of the cost, and each explains the source of his own contribution. Showing the full value in one statement and nothing in the other is a frequent error.

Assets Sold or Given Away During the Year

An asset disposed of during the year disappears from the closing statement, but the disposal must be visible: the sale proceeds appear as an inflow in the reconciliation, and any gain may be taxable. Assets transferred to another person must be shown with the consideration received.

Why Missing Assets Get Noticed

FBR's powers include gathering information from third parties, and much of what people own leaves a record: property registrations, vehicle registrations, bank accounts, and foreign remittances. When those records show an asset that is not in your wealth statement, the gap is easy to spot. Declaring the asset yourself, with its source explained, is always easier than responding after the question is asked.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to show my wife's assets in my wealth statement?
Only if she is dependent on you. Assets of a dependent spouse, minor children and other dependents are included.
Do foreign bank accounts go in the wealth statement?
Yes. Section 116 expressly includes foreign assets and foreign liabilities.
How do I show a plot I am still paying instalments on?
Show it at the amount paid to date, and keep the allotment and payment records.
Should gold received at marriage be shown?
Yes, with its weight, value and source, such as a gift or inheritance.
Do loans I gave to relatives count?
Yes. Money lent is an asset (a receivable) and should be shown with the borrower's name.
What happens if I add an asset I cannot explain?
Under Section 111, an asset whose source is not satisfactorily explained can be included in income as income from other sources.
Can I add a missing asset without revising the return?
Yes, if it was bought from already declared money. If its source was undeclared income, the return must also be revised.
How should I value assets in the wealth statement?
Use a consistent basis each year, usually the cost actually paid plus improvements, and do not adjust values to force a reconciliation.
How do I show an inherited property?
Show your share from the date of inheritance, keep the succession or mutation papers, and record the inheritance in the reconciliation.
How do I show a plot owned jointly with my brother?
Each owner shows his own share at his share of the cost and explains the source of his own contribution.
What if I sold an asset during the year?
Remove it from the closing statement and show the sale proceeds as an inflow in the reconciliation; any gain may be taxable.
Do I show a car bought on lease or bank financing?
Show the car as an asset and the outstanding financing as a liability, so the net position is correct.
Should prize bonds be in the wealth statement?
Yes. Prize bonds are assets and should be shown at cost with their details.
