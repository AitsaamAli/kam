Closing or Changing a Sole Proprietorship: Removing a Business From Your NTN the Right Way

10 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 9 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 10 November 2026
Stopping trading does not end your tax obligations. A business has to be formally discontinued with FBR and its last year reported properly.

Owners who stop trading often simply close the shop and stop filing. Returns go unfiled, notices follow, and the NTN still shows a business that no longer exists. Closing properly takes a few steps and avoids years of follow-up.

TL;DR

FBR's guidance says discontinuance of business and deregistration cannot be done through the online Form 181; you visit your Regional Tax Office with relevant documents. Your income tax return for the year the business closes must still be filed, with the closing stock, assets and bank balances reflected in the wealth statement. Records must be kept for six years after the relevant tax year, under Section 174.

Before You Close: Decide What Is Changing

SituationWhat to do
The business has stopped for goodDiscontinue it with FBR at your RTO, and file the final income tax return.
You are moving to a new addressUpdate the business address through Form 181 on IRIS; no closure needed.
One branch closes, others continueUpdate the branches on your registration; the business continues.
You are converting to a companyThe new company registers separately; the sole proprietorship is then discontinued. See our SECP versus sole proprietorship guide.
You are changing the line of businessUpdate the nature of business on IRIS so your registration matches what you do.

Step 1: Discontinue the Business for Income Tax

FBR's page on changing registration details lists discontinuance of business and deregistration among changes that require a visit to your Regional Tax Office with the relevant documents, rather than the online Form 181. Prepare:

  • A written application stating the business name, NTN (CNIC) and date trading stopped.
  • Evidence of closure, such as the end of the rent agreement, disconnection of commercial utilities, or closure of the business bank account.
  • Copies of the last filed returns.

Your personal NTN continues. You remain an individual taxpayer; only the business is removed.

Step 2: The Last Income Tax Return

The business's income up to the date of closure is declared in your normal annual return for that tax year. Points to get right:

  • Closing stock sold, taken for personal use or written off.
  • Business assets sold or kept, with any gain declared.
  • Money withdrawn from the business shown correctly in the wealth statement reconciliation.
  • Withholding certificates for tax deducted from you up to closure, claimed as credits.
  • If you were a withholding agent, final quarterly statements filed (see our withholding guide).

Step 3: Keep the Records

Closing the business does not shorten record-keeping duties. Income tax records are kept for six years after the end of the tax year under Section 174, longer if any proceeding is pending. Store the closure documents with them.

Example Timeline

WhenStep
Week before closingCount and value stock; decide what is sold, kept or written off. Settle supplier and staff dues.
Closing dateStop trading; issue final invoices; note the date in writing.
Next few weeksVisit the RTO with the discontinuance application and closure evidence.
After tax is settledClose the business bank account.
Return seasonFile the income tax return for the year, with the closing position in the wealth statement.

The order matters: closing the bank account before final returns are filed and tax paid makes the last steps harder.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Stopping filing altogether, so FBR still expects returns for a business that no longer exists.
  • Closing the bank account before final returns are filed and tax paid.
  • Leaving the business on the NTN, so FBR still expects business income.
  • Discarding records once the shop is shut.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I remove my business from my NTN online?
No. FBR's guidance says discontinuance of business and deregistration require a visit to your Regional Tax Office with relevant documents.
Does closing my business cancel my NTN?
No. Your CNIC remains your NTN as an individual. Only the business is discontinued.
Do I need to file an income tax return for the year I closed?
Yes. Income up to the closure date is declared in your annual return, and the wealth statement must reflect the closing position.
How long should I keep the closed business's records?
Six years after the relevant tax year, under Section 174 of the Income Tax Ordinance, and longer if proceedings are pending.
