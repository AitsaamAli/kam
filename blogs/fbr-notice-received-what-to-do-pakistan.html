FBR Notice Received — What to Do?
Step-by-Step Response Guide Pakistan 2026

TL;DR

FBR IRIS is the online portal for all Pakistani tax compliance: NTN registration, income tax returns, wealth statements, WHT payments, and FBR notice responses. Register free at iris.fbr.gov.pk.

Emergency Checklist — First 24 Hours

  • Photograph or scan the notice immediately, including the envelope/delivery date if it arrived by post — the delivery date can matter for calculating your actual deadline
  • Note the exact deadline date somewhere visible — a missed deadline is the single biggest factor that turns a manageable notice into a serious problem
  • Do not respond verbally or informally to any FBR official before you have a documented strategy — informal conversations are not a substitute for a formal written response and can sometimes complicate the record if they're inconsistent with what you submit later
  • Locate your last 2-3 years of filed returns and wealth statements — these are the baseline documents almost every response starts from, and having them ready saves a full day of back-and-forth once you do engage a consultant
  • Contact a tax consultant the same day if the notice involves Section 111, 122, or 177 — these categories benefit most from an early start rather than a rushed response near the deadline, since gathering the right supporting documents can itself take a week or more

Don't Panic — But Don't Ignore It Either

Receiving an FBR notice can be alarming. But with the right response, most FBR notices can be resolved without any major penalty or assessment. The worst thing you can do is ignore the notice — that almost always makes the situation much worse than the original query ever was.

This guide covers exactly what to do within the first 24-48 hours of receiving an FBR FBR notice defense in Pakistan — from the immediate first steps through what actually happens if the matter reaches a hearing.

Time is Critical: Most FBR notices require a response within 15–30 days. Missing the deadline can result in an ex-parte order (FBR decides against you without your input), additional penalties, and bank account freeze.

Step 1 — Identify Which Type of FBR Notice You Received

Notice TypeSectionWhat It MeansResponse Time
Non-filing Notice114(4)You have not filed tax return(s)30 days
Audit / Assessment Notice177 / 121FBR wants to audit your return15-30 days
Unexplained Income Notice111FBR detected income not on your return30 days

Withholding Tax Notice161Failure to deduct/deposit WHT30 days

Step 2 — Gather Your Documents Immediately

  • The original FBR notice (check date, section number, and deadline clearly)
  • All previously filed tax returns (if any)
  • Bank statements for the relevant income tax return filing
  • Business accounts / income records
  • Property documents, vehicle registration if mentioned in the notice
  • CNIC and NTN details

Step 3 — Contact a Tax Consultant Immediately

Do not attempt to respond to an FBR notice without professional help. A wrongly worded or incomplete response can escalate a simple notice into a full audit or demand order. An experienced tax consultant will:

  • Identify the exact legal basis for the notice
  • Determine if the notice is valid or has procedural flaws
  • Draft a legally sound written response within the deadline
  • Attend any hearings at the FBR office on your behalf (as authorized representative)
  • Negotiate the best outcome and avoid unnecessary tax demands

Fees for notice response work vary with complexity — a straightforward Section 114 non-filing notice is typically resolved for a few thousand rupees (essentially the cost of filing the missing return), while a Section 111 or Section 177 audit response with a hearing and multiple document rounds is priced according to the scope of work. Ask for a clear quote before work begins, and confirm what happens (in terms of cost) if a follow-up round or hearing is needed.

Is This Actually Serious? Routine vs Serious Notices

Not every notice carries the same weight, and knowing where yours falls helps set the right level of urgency:

Notice CharacterExamplesTypical Outcome
Routine / AdministrativeNon-filing reminder (114), minor document request (176)Usually resolved by filing the missing return or submitting the requested document
Requires ExplanationUnexplained income/assets (111), WHT default (161)Resolved with proper documentary proof of source of funds
Serious — Needs Professional HandlingAudit (177), amended assessment (122), demand notice (137)Requires a structured written response and possibly a hearing; DIY response risks a worse outcome

The common thread across all three categories: none of them improve by being ignored, and the "routine" ones can escalate into the "serious" category if the deadline passes without a response. Knowing which bucket your notice falls into also helps you calibrate how much professional help you actually need — a missed-deadline reminder rarely justifies the same level of engagement as an audit notice.

Documents Needed by Scenario

The exact documents you need depend on what the notice is actually asking you to explain:

  • Inheritance: Succession certificate or registered will, and the deceased's property/asset records showing the asset existed before transfer
  • Gift from a family member: A written, dated gift deed signed by the donor, plus the donor's own tax returns/bank records showing they had the capacity to give that amount
  • Foreign remittance: SWIFT transfer receipts or bank remittance advice showing the sender, amount, and banking channel used
  • Agricultural income: Land ownership records (fard/intiqal) and crop sale receipts from the local market committee or buyer
  • Business income discrepancy: Sales ledgers, purchase invoices, and bank statements reconciling declared turnover with actual deposits
  • Property purchase: Registry/transfer deed, proof of payment (bank transfer, not cash), and the wealth statement entry for the relevant year
  • Prize bond or lottery winnings: The prize claim voucher/certificate and the bank credit advice showing the winning amount was received through proper banking channels

Undocumented explanations rarely work: Saying "it was a gift" or "it was agricultural income" without the matching paperwork carries very little weight with FBR. The strength of your response is almost entirely a function of the documents behind it, not how the explanation is worded — this is why gathering documents (Step 2 above) matters more than how eloquently the covering letter is drafted.

Consequences of Ignoring an FBR Notice

  • Ex-parte order: FBR makes a tax assessment without your input — almost always higher than actual liability
  • Additional penalties: On top of the tax demand, penalties of 25% or more of tax amount
  • Bank account attachment: FBR can issue garnishment orders to your bank
  • Property seizure: Immovable property can be attached for tax recovery
  • Criminal prosecution: In cases of tax fraud, criminal proceedings under Section 192

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do immediately after receiving an FBR notice?
Read the notice carefully, identify the section and deadline, gather your financial records for the relevant period, and contact a tax consultant immediately. Do not respond without professional guidance.
Can I ignore an FBR notice if I disagree with it?
Never. Even if you believe the notice is incorrect, you must respond formally within the deadline. If the notice is wrong, a consultant can challenge it legally through proper channels.
How long do I have to respond to an FBR tax notice?
Typically 15 to 30 days depending on the section. Always check the specific deadline written in the notice. Extensions can sometimes be requested before the deadline passes.
Can a tax consultant attend FBR hearings on my behalf?
Yes. You can authorize a registered tax consultant or lawyer to represent you at FBR through a written power of attorney. You do not need to appear yourself in most cases.
I can't afford to pay a demand raised by FBR right now — what are my options?
You still must respond to the notice or appeal the assessment within the deadline — inability to pay is not a reason to stay silent. If the assessment itself is contested, filing an appeal (Commissioner Appeals within 30 days) and requesting a stay of recovery is the correct route. If the tax is genuinely owed but you need time, FBR can, in some cases, agree to an installment arrangement — a consultant can help structure this request.
Will responding to a notice myself (without a consultant) make things worse?
For a simple non-filing notice, filing the missing return yourself is fine. For anything involving unexplained income, an audit, or an amended assessment, a poorly worded or incomplete self-response is one of the most common reasons a routine notice escalates — not because the underlying facts were bad, but because the explanation wasn't presented in the way the law requires.
Does receiving a notice affect my filer/ATL status?
Receiving a notice by itself does not remove you from the Active Taxpayer List. What does affect ATL status is failing to file the underlying return the notice may be about, or a resulting assessment that isn't paid or appealed. Responding correctly and on time keeps your filer status intact throughout the process.

What Actually Happens at an FBR Hearing

If your notice requires an in-person or video hearing rather than just a written reply, knowing the format in advance removes most of the anxiety:

  • You (or your authorized representative) are called at a scheduled time — hearings can run late, so build in wait time
  • The officer reviews your written response and asks clarifying questions about specific line items or documents
  • You (or your representative) answer directly — vague or evasive answers extend the process; specific, document-backed answers close it faster
  • A follow-up may be scheduled if additional documents are requested — this is normal and not a sign the case is going badly, and often just reflects the officer's own workload and caseload rather than anything about your specific response
  • An order is eventually passed — either accepting your explanation, partially accepting it, or raising a demand, which then carries its own appeal rights and deadline

You do not have to attend personally: A registered tax consultant can attend on your behalf as an authorized representative via power of attorney, which is especially useful for overseas Pakistanis or anyone unable to travel to the relevant tax office.

Common Mistakes People Make When Responding

  • Answering only part of the query: If the notice asks about three specific transactions, address all three — leaving one unaddressed often triggers a follow-up notice on just that item
  • Submitting documents without a cover explanation: A pile of bank statements without a written explanation connecting them to the officer's specific question is far less effective than a short letter that walks through the logic
  • Waiting until the last day: Submitting on the deadline day leaves no room for a technical IRIS upload issue, a slow bank turnaround on a requested statement, or a document you realize you're missing at the last minute
  • Volunteering unrelated information: Stick to what was asked — providing extra unrelated financial details can open new lines of inquiry you weren't prepared for
  • Assuming a small notice will resolve itself: Even a routine Section 114 non-filing notice needs a formal response; assuming it will be forgotten is how simple notices turn into ex-parte orders

A Realistic Timeline — Section 111 Notice to Resolution

Typical case flow: Notice received (Day 0) → documents gathered and consultant engaged (Day 1-5) → written response with supporting evidence submitted (Day 10-15, well before the 30-day deadline) → hearing scheduled and attended by authorized representative (Week 4-6) → possible follow-up document request (Week 6-8) → final order issued accepting the explanation or raising a reduced demand (Week 8-12). Cases with clean documentation (proper gift deeds, bank trails, succession certificates) typically resolve faster and with no additional tax; cases with weak or missing documentation take longer and carry more risk of an adverse order that then needs its own appeal.
