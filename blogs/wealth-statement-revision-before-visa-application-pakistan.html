Revising Your Wealth Statement Before a Visa Application: What to Fix, What Never to Touch (2026)

24 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 24 November 2026
A wealth statement that does not match your bank statements raises more questions than one that shows less money. Fix it the lawful way, before you apply.

Visa officers do not read Pakistani tax law, but they read numbers. The UK guide asks for bank statements that “detail the origin of the funds held”; Canada asks for six months of account details; the Schengen rules ask for proof of sufficient means of subsistence. When the wealth statement you attach tells a different story from those bank papers, the officer has a reason to doubt both. This guide explains what the law lets you correct before you apply, how to do it so it stands up later with FBR, and the revisions people make that do lasting damage.

Short answer

Under section 116(3) of the Income Tax Ordinance you can revise a filed wealth statement when you discover an omission or wrong statement, with a revised wealth reconciliation and written reasons, at any time before you receive a notice under section 122(9) for that year and never later than five years from that year's return due date. Before a visa application, revise only to make the statement true: a missing bank account, a car or plot recorded at the wrong cost, a gift booked as income. Never revise to show more money than you have. FBR can declare a revision void if it is not a bona fide correction, and unexplained assets are taxed under section 111.

Why the Wealth Statement Matters to a Visa Officer

Short answer: Because the wealth statement is the one official record that explains where your money came from, and officers compare it with your bank papers.

None of the official visa pages we checked lists an FBR wealth statement as a mandatory document. What they ask for is proof that you can pay for the trip and that the money is genuinely yours:

CountryWhat the official guidance asks forWhere the wealth statement fits
United KingdomFinancial documents showing funds, such as “bank statements which detail the origin of the funds held”, and proof of earnings (GOV.UK guide, updated 8 October 2026)It is the document that explains the origin of a large balance: a plot sold, a gift from a parent, business profit
Canada“At least 6 months of account details, including balances”, plus an employer letter with current salary (IRCC, modified 16 November 2026)Balances that jump without a recorded source are what officers notice; the statement records that source
Schengen statesDocuments showing “sufficient means of subsistence” for the stay (Visa Code, Article 14(1)(c))Property, savings and income declared to FBR support the claim that the means are lawful

So the wealth statement is supporting evidence. Its value depends on one thing: that it agrees with every other paper in the file. A Rs 4,200,000 balance on the bank statement and Rs 900,000 of declared cash and bank on the wealth statement is a gap the officer can see in a minute. Our country guides for the UK visitor visa, the USA B1/B2 and Australia explain which FBR papers each embassy is usually given. This guide is about correcting the statement before you hand it over.

What the Law Lets You Revise, and When

Short answer: Section 116(3): revised statement plus revised reconciliation plus written reasons, before a 122(9) notice and within five years of the return due date.

Section 116(3) of the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, as amended up to 30 June 2026, is the only route to correct a filed wealth statement. Read in full, it sets five conditions:

ConditionWhat section 116(3) says
TriggerYou “discover any omission or wrong statement” in a statement you have filed
What you fileA revised wealth statement “along with the revised wealth reconciliation and the reasons for filing” it
HowUnder intimation to the Commissioner, in the prescribed form and manner (on IRIS)
DeadlineBefore you receive a notice under section 122(9) for that tax year
Outer limitNot after five years from the due date of the return for that tax year (Explanation added in 2020)

The proviso matters most for visa applicants. Where the Commissioner thinks a revision “is not for the purpose of correcting a bona fide omission or wrong statement”, he may declare it void by a written order, after giving you a hearing. A revision filed a week before a visa appointment that suddenly adds Rs 3,000,000 of cash at home is exactly the kind that invites that question.

A wealth statement revision does not change the income in your return. If the error is in income too, for example rent you never declared, the return must be revised under section 114(6), which has its own conditions; our guide to the revised return window covers those. The IRIS steps for the wealth statement itself are in our revision how-to.

Errors Worth Fixing Before You Apply

Short answer: Fix genuine errors: a missing account, wrong cost of an asset, a gift recorded as income, a dependent's asset left out.

These are the corrections that make the statement true and the visa file consistent. Each has its own guide in this series.

What went wrongWhy it hurts the visa fileThe correction
A savings or foreign-currency account left outBank statement shows an account FBR has never seenAdd it at the 30 June balance
Cash in hand typed as a round guessA large cash figure with no bank trail looks inventedCorrect to the real figure with its source
A plot sold but still shown, or bought but missingProperty papers and the statement disagreeRecord the sale or purchase at actual cost and price
A parent's transfer booked as your incomeInflates income you cannot show tax onReclassify as a gift from a relative
Money or property abroad not declaredThe officer may see the foreign account; FBR has no recordDeclare it, and check section 116A

Two smaller corrections also come up often: a dependent spouse's assets missing (section 116(1)(b) requires the assets of a spouse, minor children and other dependents, and the Explanation added in 2024 limits the spouse's assets to a dependent spouse), and a car still shown after it was sold. Both are covered in our guide to assets missing from a wealth statement.

Revisions That Do Lasting Damage

Short answer: Never add cash or assets you do not hold. The revision can be declared void and the extra amount taxed as unexplained income.

The pressure before a visa appointment is to make the statement look richer. The Ordinance makes that expensive in three ways.

  • Section 111. If you own money, an investment or a valuable article and offer no explanation of its source, or an explanation the Commissioner finds unsatisfactory, its value is added to your income under “Income from Other Sources” to the extent it is not adequately explained. Cash added to impress an embassy is cash you must later explain to FBR.
  • The void proviso. A revision that does not correct a bona fide error can be declared void, so the original statement stands and the revision itself becomes part of the record.
  • The visa side. Canada's inadmissibility page lists misrepresentation, which “includes providing false information or withholding information”, and says an inadmissible person is “denied a visa”, refused entry or removed. The UK guide warns that a decision maker may ask for additional information, so a figure you cannot support can be questioned.

The safe rule is short: revise to match the documents you have, never revise to create documents you do not have.

A Worked Example

Hamza, a salaried engineer in Lahore, plans a UK family visit. His TY2026 wealth statement shows Rs 650,000 in one bank account and Rs 300,000 cash. His real position on 30 June 2026 was Rs 650,000 in his salary account, Rs 1,150,000 in a savings account opened in March, and about Rs 40,000 cash. In April his father transferred Rs 1,000,000 to the savings account by bank transfer.

ItemFiled statementRevised statementReason given
Salary accountRs 650,000Rs 650,000No change
Savings accountNot shownRs 1,150,000Account omitted; balance per bank certificate
Cash in handRs 300,000Rs 40,000Estimate replaced by actual
Gift from fatherNot shownRs 1,000,000 (reconciliation)Gift from a relative received by bank transfer

Net assets rise by Rs 890,000. The revised reconciliation shows the Rs 1,000,000 gift as an inflow and restates personal expenses to the real figure, so it balances without any invented income. Every figure now matches a bank document, and the gift is from a relative as defined in section 85(5), received through a banking channel. Hamza revises in November, before any notice, and only then books his appointment.

Timing and the Papers to Keep

Short answer: Revise first, apply after, and keep the revised IRIS acknowledgement and your reasons letter together with the visa file.

  1. Collect first. Bank certificates of balance as on 30 June, property and vehicle papers, and transfer receipts for gifts.
  2. Revise next. File the revised statement and reconciliation on IRIS with the reasons, while no 122(9) notice exists for that year.
  3. Keep the trail. Save the revised acknowledgement and the reasons note. If an officer or FBR asks why two versions exist, the answer is on one page.
  4. Apply last. Bank statements you attach should be recent; the UK guide says not to use bank statements or letters issued more than a year before the application.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I revise my wealth statement just before a visa interview?
Yes, if you are correcting a genuine omission or wrong statement and no section 122(9) notice has been received for that year. Do it before you apply, and keep the revised acknowledgement and reasons together.
Does the UK embassy require an FBR wealth statement?
The GOV.UK supporting documents guide does not list it. It asks for financial documents such as bank statements that detail the origin of funds. A wealth statement can explain that origin, so it should agree with the bank statements.
Will a revised wealth statement look suspicious?
A correction backed by bank certificates and a clear reasons note is normal. A last-minute revision that adds large cash with no source is what draws questions, from FBR and from an officer.
Do I also need to revise my income tax return?
Only if the error affects income. A wealth statement correction alone is filed under section 116(3); a change in income needs a revised return under section 114(6), which has separate conditions.
How far back can I revise?
The Explanation to section 116(3) bars revision after five years from the due date of the return for that tax year.
