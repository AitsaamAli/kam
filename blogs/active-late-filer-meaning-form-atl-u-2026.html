Active Late Filer, Inactive Filer and ATL Status in 2026: What They Mean and How Form ATL-U Changes the Picture

19 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 19 November 2026
Checked your status and saw a word you did not expect? Here is what the ATL status terms mean in 2026 and what to do about each one.

Searches for 'active late filer meaning' jump every year after the deadline, because people see the phrase on a bank form or a status check and assume it is a penalty. The phrase has a history, and in 2026 its meaning changed. Here is the plain explanation, with the law behind it.

TL;DR

The Active Taxpayers List (ATL) shows whether you have filed the return that the list relates to. 'Active' means you are on it; 'inactive' generally means FBR does not have a return from you for that year, so you are not on the list. The term 'active late filer' came from rule 1A of the Tenth Schedule, which from 1 July 2024 charged higher withholding rates to people on the ATL who had filed after the due date. The Finance Act 2026 omitted rule 1A, so from 1 July 2026 there is no separate rate for late filers who are on the ATL. A late filer reaches the ATL either by paying the Section 182A surcharge (Rs 25,000 for an individual) or, as an individual, by furnishing Form ATL-U and not obtaining property for six months.

The Status Terms in Plain Words

Short answer: Active means you are on the current ATL; inactive means you are not, usually because the return is missing or late without an ATL route.

Status you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
ActiveYour name is on the current ATLNothing, except file next year's return on time
Inactive / not foundFBR does not have a return from you for the year the list relates to, or you filed late and have not completed an ATL routeFile the missing return, then use the surcharge or Form ATL-U
Active late filer (bank or registrar wording)On the ATL, but the return was filed after the due dateSince 1 July 2026, no separate rate applies; check the bank applied ordinary filer rates

Different banks and registrars use slightly different words on their own screens. The legal question is always the same: is the person "appearing in the active taxpayers' list"? That is the phrase the Tenth Schedule uses.

Where "Active Late Filer" Came From

Short answer: 'Active late filer' came from rule 1A of the Tenth Schedule, which the Finance Act 2026 omitted, so late filers on the ATL now pay ordinary filer rates.

From 1 July 2024, the Finance Act 2024 inserted rule 1A into the Tenth Schedule. It applied to "persons appearing on the active taxpayers' list who have not filed the return by the due date" and gave them their own rates for certain transactions, most visibly advance tax under Section 236C on selling property, which was higher than the ordinary filer rate. Banks and property dealers started calling these people "active late filers", and the term stuck.

The Finance Act 2026 omitted rule 1A. In the Ordinance amended up to 30 June 2026, the rule appears only in a footnote recording what was omitted. The practical result from 1 July 2026:

  • If you are on the ATL, you are charged the ordinary filer rates, whether you filed on time or late.
  • If you are not on the ATL, rule 1 of the Tenth Schedule applies: rates increased by 100%, and higher specific rates on some transactions.

So in 2026 the question that matters is no longer "on time or late", it is "on the ATL or not".

Late Filer Not Yet on the ATL

Short answer: A late filer is off the ATL until the Rs 25,000 surcharge is paid or, for an individual, Form ATL-U is furnished.

Section 182A(1)(a) keeps a late filer off the ATL for the year of the late return unless the proviso is satisfied: payment of Rs 25,000 for an individual, Rs 50,000 for an AOP, or Rs 100,000 for a company. Section 182A(3) adds a second door for individuals only: the undertaking in Form ATL-U not to obtain ownership or beneficial interest in any property for six months.

Until one of those is done, three more consequences apply under Section 182A(1): no refund is issued while you are not on the ATL, the period does not count for additional payment on delayed refunds, and a loss for the late year cannot be carried forward.

What Staying Inactive Costs

Short answer: Off the ATL, rule 1 of the Tenth Schedule raises withholding by 100%, and property sale tax is 11.5% instead of 2.75%.

For someone with Rs 2 million in a savings account earning 10% a year, the profit is Rs 200,000. At the filer rate the bank deducts Rs 40,000; at the doubled rate it deducts Rs 80,000. The Rs 40,000 difference alone is more than the surcharge, and Form ATL-U removes it without the surcharge.

How to Become Active From Inactive

Short answer: File the missing return and wealth statement, then use Form ATL-U or the surcharge, and check after the next ATL update.

  1. Find out which year is missing. The ATL relates to a specific tax year; for the list issued in 2027 that is Tax Year 2026.
  2. File that return and the wealth statement. If earlier years are also missing, discuss them before filing so that the wealth statements reconcile.
  3. Choose the ATL route. No property purchase planned for six months: Form ATL-U. A purchase planned: the surcharge.
  4. Wait for the next ATL update and check. Use the online or SMS check.

The Late-Filing Penalty Is a Separate Question

Short answer: Becoming active does not remove the Section 182 penalty, but filing by 30 November 2026 cuts it by 75%.

Becoming active does not settle the Section 182 penalty. Section 182 penalty, as it stands in the Ordinance amended up to 30 June 2026: the higher of 0.1% of tax payable per day or Rs 1,000 per day; minimum Rs 10,000 for an individual with 75% or more of income from salary and Rs 50,000 in other cases; maximum 200% of tax payable; reduced by 75%, 50% or 25% if the return is filed within one, two or three months after the due date. The one-month reduction is the reason to file now: a Tax Year 2026 return filed by 30 November 2026 qualifies.

Staying Active Next Year

Short answer: File the Tax Year 2027 return by 30 September 2027 and none of this applies.

The Tax Year 2027 return is due on 30 September 2027 under Section 118, unless extended. File on time and none of this applies: no surcharge, no undertaking, no penalty. Salaried people can usually file in July or August as soon as the employer's certificate is ready.

Three Cases, Worked Through

Short answer: Late with no route: inactive. Late with surcharge or Form ATL-U: active at ordinary filer rates.

Case 1: Filed on 5 November 2026, nothing else done

The return is late, so under Section 182A(1)(a) the person is not on the ATL for Tax Year 2026. Any status check shows them as not active. Their bank deducts profit tax at the increased rate. Fix: Form ATL-U (if no property plans) or the surcharge.

Case 2: Filed late, paid the Rs 25,000 surcharge

The person is included in the ATL once FBR's list reflects the payment. Before 1 July 2026, rule 1A would have charged them separate rates as a late filer on the ATL; with rule 1A omitted, they now pay ordinary filer rates. The surcharge was the price of not having to keep a six-month promise.

Case 3: Filed late, furnished Form ATL-U

Same result as Case 2, without the Rs 25,000, as long as no property is obtained in the six months. If they breach, the benefit is withdrawn from the breach date and they are back to needing the surcharge.

Because each check happens at the time of the transaction, two people with identical returns can pay different amounts depending only on when their names reached the list. That is why we file the return and the ATL route on the same day.

If Older Years Are Also Missing

Short answer: File missing earlier years together so each wealth statement reconciles with the one before.

The ATL looks at the return for one tax year, but your wealth statement links every year to the one before. If Tax Years 2024 and 2025 were never filed, the 2026 wealth statement has nothing to reconcile from, and assets may look unexplained. It is usually better to file the missing years at the same time, carefully, rather than leave a gap that a later notice will find.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does active late filer mean?
It describes a person on the ATL who filed the return after the due date. Rule 1A of the Tenth Schedule used to charge such people higher rates; the Finance Act 2026 omitted it, so from 1 July 2026 they pay ordinary filer rates.
What does inactive filer mean?
Your name is not on the current ATL, usually because the return for the relevant year is missing or was filed late without the surcharge or Form ATL-U.
How do I become active from inactive?
File the missing return and wealth statement, then either pay the surcharge or, as an individual not obtaining property for six months, furnish Form ATL-U.
Do active late filers still pay higher tax on property?
Not from 1 July 2026. The separate rates in rule 1A were omitted by the Finance Act 2026; a person on the ATL pays 2.75% under Section 236C when selling.
Is the late-filing penalty removed once I am active?
No. The Section 182 penalty is separate from ATL status.
Can a bank still deduct the higher rate after I become active?
Banks apply the rate according to the ATL on the date of the transaction. After your name appears, ask the bank to apply the filer rate going forward.
How long does Form ATL-U keep me active?
Your ATL inclusion relates to the tax year. The six-month promise is the condition; breaking it brings back the surcharge rule.
Which return decides my status in 2027?
The ATL issued in 2027 relates to Tax Year 2026, the return that was due on 31 October 2026.
Is 'active late filer' an official FBR status?
It came from rule 1A of the Tenth Schedule, which referred to persons on the ATL who had not filed by the due date. That rule was omitted by the Finance Act 2026.
Can I be active for one year and inactive for another?
Yes. The ATL relates to a tax year. Filing one year late does not change your status for a year you filed on time, and vice versa.
