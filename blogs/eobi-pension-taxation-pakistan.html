EOBI Pension Taxation — Is Your Retirement Pension Taxable?

August 2026 Kamboh Associates 8 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website

A retiree drawing an EOBI old-age pension after years of covered employment generally wants a simple confirmation that this money isn't going to be taxed away — and the good news is that pension income is broadly exempt in Pakistan, though the picture gets genuinely more specific once a retiree has more than one pension source to consider.

TL;DR

EOBI old-age pension is part of salary income and, on its own, is normally below the level at which salaried tax starts. The old rule that only the higher of several pensions is exempt ended with the Finance Act 2025: pension from a former employer is now taxed only above Rs 10 million a year (5% of the excess) and not at all from age 70 (Section 12(2A)).

EOBI Pension and Income Tax: The Current Rule

Short answer: EOBI pension is part of salary income, and an EOBI pension on its own is normally far below the taxable threshold, so no tax arises on it.

Under Section 12(2)(f) of the Income Tax Ordinance (FBR, amended up to 30.06.2026), "salary" includes any pension. We could not find a specific exemption for EOBI pension in the Ordinance (checked 20 November 2026). In practice, an EOBI old-age pension is small enough that, on its own, the pensioner's taxable income stays below the Rs 600,000 level at which salaried tax starts, so no tax is payable. If you have other income, the pension is added to it.

Multiple Pension Sources After the Finance Act 2025

Short answer: the old "only the higher pension is exempt" rule ended with the Finance Act 2025.

Until 30 June 2025, clause (8) of Part I of the Second Schedule exempted pension from a former employer, and a proviso exempted only the higher pension where a person received more than one. The Finance Act 2025 omitted clause (8), proviso included. Section 12(2A) now decides how pension from a former employer is taxed: only where it exceeds Rs 10 million in a tax year (as a final tax), not at all for an individual aged 70 or over, and at normal rates for a pensioner who still works for the former employer.

Is There Still a "Higher Pension" Comparison?

Short answer: there is no longer a "higher pension" comparison; each pension is taxed under its own rule.

Older guidance asked retirees to compare pensions each year and treat the larger one as exempt. That comparison came from the omitted proviso to clause (8) and no longer applies. Declare each pension at its actual amount; pension from a former employer above Rs 10 million a year is taxed under Section 12(2A), and other pension, such as EOBI, is simply part of salary income.

EOBI Alongside a Government Service Pension

Short answer: declare both; the government pension is taxed only above Rs 10 million a year, and the EOBI pension is part of salary income.

A retiree with a government pension and an EOBI pension declares both. The government pension follows Section 12(2A). The EOBI pension is salary income; whether any tax arises depends on the total taxable income, which for most such retirees remains low.

EOBI Alongside a Former Employer's Private Pension Scheme

Short answer: the private pension from a former employer follows Section 12(2A); EOBI pension is declared alongside it.

Pension from a former private employer is treated the same way as government pension under Section 12(2A). Keep the employer's and EOBI's annual statements so the amounts in the return and the wealth statement match.

Distinguishing Pension From a One-Time Provident Fund or Gratuity Payout

It's worth clearly distinguishing an ongoing pension — a regular, recurring periodic payment continuing through retirement — from a one-time provident fund or gratuity payout received at the point of retirement, which follow their own entirely separate exemption rules covered in detail elsewhere on this site. A retiree receiving both an EOBI pension and a lump-sum provident fund or gratuity payout at retirement should apply the correct specific treatment to each — the pension-specific rules covered throughout this guide, and the separate provident fund/gratuity exemption rules covered in this site's dedicated guide on that topic — rather than conflating the two as if they followed identical treatment.

How to Handle Pension Declaration at Annual Filing

Short answer: declare every pension at its actual amount in the return and show it as a receipt in the wealth reconciliation.

Enter each pension in the return, add any other income such as bank profit or rent, and file the wealth statement. Filing keeps you on the Active Taxpayers List, which keeps the deduction on bank profit at 20% instead of 40%.

Pension Rules Change With Budgets

Short answer: pension rules changed in 2025 and can change in any budget, so check each year.

The Finance Act 2025 replaced the old pension exemption with the Rs 10 million rule. Budgets can change it again, so confirm the rule each year before filing.

EOBI Alongside an Armed Forces or Other Specific Pension Category

Short answer: armed forces pension follows the same Section 12(2A) rule as other pension from a former employer.

Family pension for dependants of members of the Armed Forces or public servants who die in service is exempt under clause (9)(ii) of the Second Schedule. Other armed forces pension is taxed only above Rs 10 million a year, and not from age 70.

Multiple Pensions Within the Same Household

Short answer: each spouse is taxed separately on their own pension and income.

Pension is assessed person by person. A husband's and wife's pensions are never combined; each declares their own in their own return.

Pension Beginning Partway Through a Tax Year

Short answer: declare the pension actually received in the tax year, from the month it started.

A pension that starts mid-year is declared for the months received. Keep the first payment advice and the annual statement.

Common Mistakes

Short answer: the costly ones are relying on the old "higher pension exempt" rule and not declaring pension at all.

  • Using the old multiple-pension rule: it ended with the Finance Act 2025.
  • Not declaring the pension: it must appear in the return and wealth statement even when no tax arises.
  • Ignoring bank profit: profit on savings is taxed, and filing keeps the deduction at 20%.

A Worked Example

Short answer: a retiree with a Rs 1.2 million government pension and a small EOBI pension owes no tax on the government pension and declares both.

The government pension is below Rs 10 million a year, so Section 12(2A) charges no tax on it. The EOBI pension is declared as salary income. Bank profit on savings is taxed at the deducted 20% under Section 7B. The retiree files to stay on the ATL and keeps the wealth statement reconciled.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is EOBI old-age pension taxable in Pakistan?
EOBI pension is part of salary income (Section 12(2)(f)). We found no specific exemption for it, but on its own it is normally well below the Rs 600,000 level at which salaried tax starts, so no tax arises.
What happens if I receive EOBI pension alongside a government pension?
Declare both. The government pension is taxed only above Rs 10 million a year (5% of the excess) under Section 12(2A); the EOBI pension is salary income. The old 'only the higher pension is exempt' rule ended with the Finance Act 2025.
Can the pension that's exempt change from year to year?
No longer. The yearly 'higher pension' comparison came from a proviso the Finance Act 2025 omitted; each pension is now taxed under its own rule.
Is a one-time provident fund payout at retirement treated the same as an ongoing pension?
No — a lump-sum provident fund or gratuity payout follows separate, distinct exemption rules covered elsewhere on this site, different from the ongoing-pension rules covered in this guide.
Do I need to declare an exempt EOBI pension on my tax return?
Yes. Declare it in the return even when no tax arises, and show it as a receipt in the wealth reconciliation.
Will the EOBI pension exemption always remain in place?
Pension rules change with budgets; the Finance Act 2025 already replaced the old pension exemption, so check the rule each year.
Does the multiple-pension exemption comparison apply jointly to both spouses in a household?
No. Each spouse declares their own pension and income in their own return; nothing is combined.
What if my second pension only started partway through the tax year?
Declare the amount actually received in the tax year, from the month it started.
