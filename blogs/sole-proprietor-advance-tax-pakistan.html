Advance Tax for Sole Proprietors: Who Pays Quarterly Under Section 147, How Much, and By When

10 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 9 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 10 November 2026
Business owners whose last assessed income was Rs 1 million or more must pay income tax in four quarterly instalments during the year, not only when they file.

Many sole proprietors meet advance tax for the first time as a notice or a default surcharge after a good year. The rule is mechanical once you know it: last year's assessed tax sets this year's instalments. This guide explains the test, the formula, the dates, and how to pay less when business has slowed.

TL;DR

Under Section 147 of the Income Tax Ordinance (amended up to 30 June 2026), an individual whose latest assessed taxable income, excluding salary taxed at source and income already taxed under final or non-adjustable deductions, is Rs 1 million or more must pay advance tax every quarter. Each instalment is one quarter of the tax assessed for the latest tax year, minus adjustable tax paid in that quarter (other than salary tax): (A/4) - B. Instalments are due on or before 15 September, 15 December, 15 March and 15 June. If this year's tax is likely to be lower, you may file an estimate before the last instalment, with supporting evidence, and pay the lower amount.

Who Has to Pay Advance Tax

Section 147(1) applies to every taxpayer whose income was charged to tax for the latest tax year. For individuals, Section 147(2) switches it off unless the latest assessed taxable income is Rs 1 million or more, after excluding:

  • income on which tax is deducted from salary under Section 149;
  • income under Sections 5, 6 and 7 (such as dividends and certain payments taxed separately); and
  • income from which tax was collected or deducted at source without being allowed as a credit (final or minimum tax income).

In practice: a shopkeeper, trader, consultant or other sole proprietor whose business and other adjustable income in the last assessed year was Rs 1 million or more pays quarterly.

How Each Instalment Is Worked Out

For individuals, Section 147(4B) sets the formula:

SymbolMeaning
(A/4) - BThe advance tax due for the quarter
ATax assessed to you for the latest tax year
BTax paid in that quarter for which credit is allowed under Section 168, other than tax deducted from salary under Section 149

Example. A sole proprietor's assessed tax for tax year 2026 was Rs 480,000. For each quarter of tax year 2027, A/4 is Rs 120,000. If Rs 30,000 of adjustable withholding tax (for example on electricity bills or bank transactions) was deducted in the September quarter, the instalment due by 15 September is Rs 90,000. The figures are illustrative.

Note that each instalment falls due in the last month of its quarter, before the quarter ends. Advance tax is paid through a payment slip (PSID) on IRIS and is credited against the final tax in your return.

If Business Has Slowed: Paying on an Estimate

Section 147(6) lets you pay less if you expect this year's tax to be lower than the formula amount. Before the last instalment falls due, you may file an estimate with the Commissioner and then pay the estimated amount, less what you have already paid, in equal instalments on the remaining dates. The estimate must show:

  • turnover for the quarters already completed;
  • estimated turnover for the remaining quarters, with reasons for any decline;
  • documentary evidence of expenses or deductions that lower the tax; and
  • a computation of estimated taxable income for the year.

Be careful with the number. Under Section 205(1B), if the tax you pay on an estimate under Section 147(6) is less than 90 per cent of the tax finally chargeable for the year, default surcharge at 12 per cent a year applies to the shortfall. File an estimate only when your records back it up.

Builders and Developers

Section 147(5C) has a separate rule: persons whose business is construction and disposal of buildings, or development and sale of plots, pay adjustable advance tax project by project at the rates in Part IIB of the First Schedule, in four equal instalments. If you are a builder or developer, this replaces the ordinary calculation for that business.

What If You Did Not Pay?

Under Section 205(1A), a person who fails to pay advance tax under Section 147 is liable to default surcharge at 12 per cent a year on the unpaid amount, from the due date until it is paid or until the return for the year was due, whichever is earlier. The shortfall is also visible to FBR when your return shows business income above the threshold with no instalments. Paying late is better than not paying: the payment is still credited in your return. If a notice has arrived, see our notice defence service.

Quarterly Checklist

  • ☐ Check last year's assessed taxable income (excluding salary and final-tax income). Rs 1 million or more means advance tax applies.
  • ☐ Take A, the tax assessed for that year, and divide by four.
  • ☐ Subtract adjustable tax deducted from you in the quarter (not salary tax).
  • ☐ Generate the PSID on IRIS and pay by the 15th of September, December, March or June.
  • ☐ Keep the payment slips; claim them in your return.
  • ☐ If business has dropped, prepare a supported estimate before the last instalment.

What Counts as "B"

B is the tax paid in the quarter for which a credit is allowed under Section 168, other than tax deducted from salary under Section 149. In plain terms, it is adjustable tax deducted or collected from you during that quarter that you will claim as a credit in your return. Tax that is final, or is a minimum tax that cannot be credited, does not reduce the instalment. Keep the deduction certificates or statements for every amount you include in B, because the same amounts are claimed in the return.

First Year in Business

Section 147 applies to a taxpayer whose income was charged to tax for the latest tax year, and for individuals it bites only when that latest assessed taxable income (outside salary and final-tax income) is Rs 1 million or more. A brand-new business with no such assessed income does not have a formula amount to pay in its first year. Once the first return with business income is filed and the income is Rs 1 million or more, instalments start in the following year. Plan cash for it: the first year of instalments often arrives at the same time as the balance of tax for the previous year.

Estimate Example: Using Section 147(6) Safely

A trader's formula instalments for the year total Rs 480,000, but by January turnover has fallen sharply. The trader prepares an estimate showing turnover for the completed quarters, estimated turnover for the remaining quarters with reasons for the decline, supporting evidence for expenses, and a computation showing estimated tax of Rs 300,000. Having already paid Rs 240,000, the trader pays the remaining Rs 60,000 in equal instalments on the dates not yet passed.

The check that matters: if the tax finally chargeable turns out to be, say, Rs 360,000, the tax paid of Rs 300,000 is below 90% of it (Rs 324,000). Under Section 205(1B), default surcharge at 12% a year then applies to the Rs 24,000 shortfall. An estimate built on real records avoids that.

Common Advance Tax Mistakes

  • Counting salary tax in B; Section 147(4B) excludes it.
  • Paying the same amount every quarter without deducting adjustable tax actually suffered.
  • Filing a lower estimate without evidence or reasons.
  • Missing the 15th, which is in the last month of the quarter, not after it ends.
  • Not claiming instalments in the return because the PSIDs were not kept.

Paying an Instalment on IRIS

  1. Log in to IRIS and open the payment section to create a payment slip (PSID).
  2. Choose income tax, the advance tax section (147), the tax year and the period.
  3. Enter the instalment amount worked out with the formula.
  4. Generate the PSID and pay it through your bank's branch, internet banking or app before the 15th.
  5. Download the paid challan (CPR) and file it with your records; you will claim it in your return.

Builders and Developers: Project-by-Project

Under Section 147(5C), a person whose business is constructing and disposing of buildings, or developing and selling plots, pays adjustable advance tax on a project-by-project basis at the rates in Part IIB of the First Schedule, in four equal instalments on the normal due dates. The project-based amount is adjustable against the final tax for the year, so the records for each project, including area and sale figures, need to be kept separately.

How This Differs for Partnerships and Companies

The (A/4) - B formula is only for individuals. Where the taxpayer is an association of persons or a company, Section 147(4) uses a turnover-based formula, (A x B/C) - D, in which the quarter's turnover is applied to the ratio of last year's assessed tax to last year's turnover, less tax paid in the quarter. AOPs and companies also have their own estimate rules under Section 147(4A). If your sole proprietorship becomes a partnership or company, the advance tax calculation changes with it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a sole proprietor have to pay advance tax?
Yes, if the latest assessed taxable income, excluding salary taxed under Section 149 and income taxed at source without credit, is Rs 1 million or more, under Section 147.
How is quarterly advance tax calculated for an individual?
Under Section 147(4B), each quarter's instalment is (A/4) - B: one quarter of the tax assessed for the latest tax year, minus adjustable tax paid in that quarter other than salary tax.
When is advance tax due?
For individuals, on or before 15 September, 15 December, 15 March and 15 June, under Section 147(5).
Can I pay less advance tax if my business is down?
Yes. Section 147(6) allows you to file an estimate before the last instalment is due, supported by turnover figures, reasons for any decline, evidence of expenses and a computation, and pay the lower amount.
Does salary count towards the Rs 1 million test?
No. Income on which tax is deducted under Section 149 is excluded when applying the threshold.
Is advance tax an extra tax?
No. It is a payment towards your final tax for the year and is credited in your return.
What is the default surcharge on unpaid advance tax?
Under Section 205(1A), 12 per cent a year on the unpaid amount, from the due date until payment or the return due date, whichever is earlier.
Do builders follow the same rule?
No. Under Section 147(5C), builders and developers pay advance tax project by project at the rates in Part IIB of the First Schedule, in four equal instalments.
Does a new business pay advance tax in its first year?
Usually not. Section 147 depends on income charged to tax for the latest tax year, and for individuals applies when that latest assessed income is Rs 1 million or more.
What happens if my advance tax is more than my final tax?
Instalments are credited against the tax chargeable in your return, and an excess can be claimed through the refund process.
What is the 90% rule for lower estimates?
Under Section 205(1B), if tax paid on a lower estimate is less than 90% of the tax finally chargeable, default surcharge at 12% a year applies to the shortfall.
Which withholding taxes reduce my instalment?
Only adjustable taxes for which credit is allowed under Section 168, other than salary tax. Final or minimum taxes do not reduce it.
How do I pay advance tax?
Create a PSID on IRIS for income tax under section 147 for the tax year and quarter, then pay it through your bank before the 15th and keep the paid challan.
Is advance tax paid on the 15th after the quarter ends?
No. For individuals the due dates are 15 September, 15 December, 15 March and 15 June, inside each quarter.
Do builders calculate advance tax with the (A/4) - B formula?
No. Under Section 147(5C), builders and developers pay project-by-project at the rates in Part IIB of the First Schedule, in four equal instalments.
Do partnerships use the same advance tax formula as individuals?
No. AOPs and companies use the turnover-based formula in Section 147(4), not the (A/4) - B formula for individuals.
