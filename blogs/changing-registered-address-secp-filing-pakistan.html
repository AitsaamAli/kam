Changing a Company's Registered Address — SECP Filing Process

September 2026 Kamboh Associates 9 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist

A company that relocates its office — moving to a larger space, shifting to a different city, or simply changing the address on record — has more to update than its own letterhead and business cards. The registered address on file with SECP is a legal record, and changing it involves its own specific filing, separate from just informing clients and vendors of the move.

TL;DR

Changing a private limited company's registered address with SECP requires a formal notification filed within the statutory window after the change takes effect, and the process differs somewhat depending on whether the new address is within the same city/province or involves a shift to a different jurisdiction. Until the filing is properly made and processed, the company's official record still reflects the old address, which can create its own complications for correspondence and compliance.

Why the Registered Address Filing Matters Beyond Just Paperwork

The registered address on file with SECP is the official address for legal correspondence, notices, and any formal communication regarding the company — it is not merely an administrative detail. A company that has physically moved but never updated this record risks missing important correspondence sent to the old address, and in a dispute or compliance review, the officially registered address is what governs, regardless of where the company actually operates from day to day.

The Statutory Window for Filing the Change

SECP requires notification of a registered address change within a set number of days of the change actually taking effect, and this window is measured from the effective date of the move, not from whenever the company gets around to filing the paperwork. Treating the address change as officially "done" once the physical move happens, without separately tracking the filing deadline that follows it, is a common gap.

Same-City Moves Versus a Shift to a Different Jurisdiction

A move within the same city or the same province generally follows a more straightforward notification process than a move that shifts the company's registered office to a different province or a different SECP jurisdiction entirely, which can involve additional procedural steps given the jurisdictional change involved. Confirming which category your specific move falls into before starting the filing avoids using the wrong process for your situation.

What Documents Are Typically Needed

  • A board resolution approving the change of registered address.
  • Proof of the new address — a lease agreement, utility bill, or ownership document for the new premises.
  • The specific SECP form for notifying an address change, completed and filed through the applicable channel.
  • Any supporting documents specific to a cross-jurisdiction move, where that applies.

Other Records That Need Updating in Parallel

The SECP filing is one part of a broader update that a genuine office move usually requires — the company's NTN profile with FBR, its bank account records, its sales tax registration if applicable, and any provincial registration all typically need their own address update, on their own separate timelines and through their own separate channels. Treating the SECP filing as the only thing that needs updating leaves these other records quietly out of date.

A move is a good natural checkpoint to review every registration your company holds and confirm which ones need a matching address update, rather than updating SECP and assuming everything else will simply follow.

What Happens If This Filing Is Delayed

A delayed address-change filing carries its own penalty exposure, similar in principle to other SECP filing delays, and beyond the direct cost, it leaves the company's official record inconsistent with its actual operating location for longer than necessary — which can complicate anything else that relies on that official address being current, from banking to a due diligence review by a prospective partner or investor.

A Note on Virtual or Shared Office Addresses

Some smaller companies operate from a shared workspace or use a registered-address service rather than a dedicated office, and a change in that arrangement — switching shared-office providers, for instance — still triggers the same registered-address filing obligation as a move to a fully owned or leased dedicated office. The nature of the space does not change the underlying SECP requirement to keep the registered address current.

Planning the Filing Alongside the Physical Move

Rather than treating the SECP filing as an afterthought once the move has already happened, preparing the board resolution and gathering the new address documentation in the weeks before the physical move actually takes place means the filing can be submitted promptly once the effective date arrives, rather than scrambling to assemble documents after the statutory window has already started ticking.

A Note on Temporary Versus Permanent Address Changes

A genuinely temporary relocation — a short-term move during renovation of the usual office, for instance — raises a different question than a permanent move: whether it actually constitutes a change to the registered address at all, or whether the company's permanent registered address remains unchanged while operations temporarily happen elsewhere. This distinction matters because filing a formal SECP address change for a genuinely temporary arrangement, only to file another change back once the temporary period ends, creates unnecessary filings — confirm which category your specific situation actually falls into before deciding whether a formal filing is even needed.

When an Address Change Coincides With a Director Transition

A company sometimes changes its registered address around the same time it is going through a director change — a new director bringing the company to new premises, for instance — and while these are two separate SECP filings with their own specific requirements, preparing them together as one coordinated exercise, rather than as two entirely disconnected tasks handled weeks apart, reduces the total administrative effort and the chance either one is forgotten.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is updating my company's letterhead and informing clients enough when I move offices?
No — the registered address on file with SECP is a separate legal record that needs its own formal filing, and until that filing is made, official correspondence and compliance still reference the old address.
How long do I have to file an address change with SECP after moving?
A set number of days from the effective date of the move — the window starts from when the move actually takes effect, not from whenever the paperwork gets filed.
Is the process different for a move within the same city versus a move to a different province?
Yes — a same-city or same-province move generally follows a more straightforward process, while a shift to a different jurisdiction can involve additional procedural steps.
What documents are typically needed for a registered address change filing?
A board resolution approving the change, proof of the new address (lease, utility bill, or ownership document), and the specific SECP form for the notification, plus any cross-jurisdiction documents where applicable.
Does changing my SECP registered address automatically update my FBR or sales tax records too?
No — these are separate registrations with their own address-update processes and timelines. An office move is a good checkpoint to review every registration and update each one individually.
What happens if I delay filing an address change with SECP?
It carries its own penalty exposure similar to other SECP filing delays, and leaves the company's official record inconsistent with its actual location for longer than necessary.
Does switching shared-office or virtual-office providers count as an address change that needs filing?
Yes — any change to the registered address, including switching shared-office arrangements, triggers the same SECP filing obligation as a move to a dedicated office.
Can the SECP filing be prepared before the physical move actually happens?
Yes — preparing the board resolution and gathering new-address documentation in advance means the filing can be submitted promptly once the move takes effect, rather than scrambling afterward.
Does a registered address change need shareholder approval, or just a board resolution?
Typically a board resolution is sufficient for a registered address change, though this can depend on your company's specific articles of association — confirm what your company's own governing documents require.
Is there a fee for filing a registered address change with SECP?
SECP applies its own current fee schedule for this filing — confirm the exact figure at the time of filing through SECP's own published rate card rather than an outdated estimate.
Does moving to a different city affect which SECP registrar my company falls under?
It can, particularly for a cross-jurisdiction move — this is part of why such moves involve additional procedural steps compared to a same-city relocation.
What if my company operates from more than one location — which one is the registered address?
The registered address is the one specific location formally on file with SECP for legal correspondence, regardless of how many other operational locations the company uses — only a change to this specific registered address triggers the filing.
Should I notify my bank about a registered address change even if my account details don't otherwise change?
Yes — banks typically require an updated registered address on file for a corporate account, and this is one of the parallel updates worth handling alongside the SECP filing rather than afterward.
Does a temporary relocation during renovation need the same formal SECP filing as a permanent move?
Not necessarily — confirm first whether a genuinely temporary arrangement actually constitutes a change to the registered address at all, since filing a formal change for a short-term situation may not be the right category of filing.
Can an address change and a director change be filed together in one coordinated submission?
They remain two separate filings with their own specific requirements, but preparing them together as one coordinated exercise reduces total administrative effort and the chance either one is forgotten.
Does switching to a serviced or co-working office space count as an address change for SECP purposes?
It does if the registered address itself changes as a result — the type of space matters less than whether the actual registered address on file with SECP is different from before.
Who should sign the board resolution approving a registered address change?
The company's directors, following whatever quorum and approval process the company's own articles of association specify for a board resolution of this kind.
Does the filing fee for an address change vary by how far the move is?
Generally no — the fee is typically tied to the type of filing itself rather than the distance of the move, but confirm the current figure against SECP's own published schedule.
