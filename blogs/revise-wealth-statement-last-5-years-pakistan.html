Revising Wealth Statements of the Last Five Years: What You Can Still Correct, and Why It Matters Now

11 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 11 November 2026
An error in one year's wealth statement carries into every year after it. Correcting the chain before FBR asks is far cheaper than explaining it afterwards.

FBR increasingly compares wealth statements with property, vehicle, bank and travel data. A plot bought in 2021 but never declared, or a loan from a relative never recorded, does not stay in 2021: it breaks every reconciliation after it. This guide explains how far back you can still correct, what the law allows, and the order to do it in.

TL;DR

Section 116(3) lets an individual file a revised wealth statement, with a revised reconciliation and reasons, at any time before receiving a notice under Section 122(9) for that year. FBR's own power to amend an assessment is limited by Section 122(2) to five years from the end of the financial year in which the assessment was issued or treated as issued, which is when the return was filed. In practice, years from about Tax Year 2021 onwards are still open in 2026–27, and a gap in any of them can be questioned as unexplained income or assets under Section 111. Fix the years in order, oldest first, so each closing figure becomes the next year's opening figure.

Why Wealth Statements Form a Chain

Each year's wealth statement opens with the previous year's closing net wealth. If an asset is missing in Tax Year 2022, then the 2022 closing figure is wrong, the 2023 opening figure is wrong, and the reconciliation in every later year either fails or has been forced to balance with an unrealistic expense or income figure. Correcting only the latest year rarely works; the chain must be fixed from the year the error started.

How Far Back Can FBR Look, and You Correct?

A filed return is treated as an assessment order under Section 120 on the day it is filed. Under Section 122(2), the Commissioner cannot amend that assessment after five years from the end of the financial year in which it was issued or treated as issued.

Dates depend on when each return was actually filed; a return filed after 30 June of the following year moves the limit by a year. Where an assessment has been amended, Section 122(4) allows further amendment within the later of five years from the original assessment year-end or one year from the amended assessment year-end. So, as of late 2026, roughly Tax Years 2021 to 2026 are the years that matter.

Revising a Wealth Statement: Section 116(3)

RuleDetail
WhoA person who has filed a wealth statement and discovers an omission or wrong statement
What to fileA revised wealth statement, a revised wealth reconciliation, and the reasons for revision, under intimation to the Commissioner in the prescribed form
DeadlineAny time before receipt of a notice under Section 122(9) for that tax year
SafeguardThe Commissioner may declare the revision void by written order if it is not to correct a bona fide omission or wrong statement

There is no 60-day limit for wealth statement revision; the cut-off is the 122(9) notice. That is the key reason to act early.

When the Return Must Be Revised Too

If the wealth statement change only reflects something already explained, such as an asset bought from declared savings but entered under the wrong head, revising the wealth statement may be enough. If the change reveals income that was not declared, such as rent from the omitted property, the return for that year also needs revision under Section 114(6), with the tax and default surcharge. For years where more than 60 days have passed since filing, return revision needs the Commissioner's approval, which is deemed granted if no order is made within 60 days of the request, or if the revision increases declared income.

The Right Order to Fix Several Years

  1. Find the first break. Compare each year's closing statement with the next year's opening statement, and each reconciliation with income and spending.
  2. Collect the evidence for that year: purchase documents, bank statements, gift or loan records.
  3. Revise the earliest affected year (wealth statement, and return if income changes).
  4. Carry the corrected closing figure forward and revise each later year in turn.
  5. Pay any tax and default surcharge with the revised returns before any notice arrives.
  6. Keep a file of each revision, its reasons and evidence, for at least six years under Section 174.

Example: A Plot Left Out in 2022

An individual bought a plot in Tax Year 2022 for Rs 4,000,000 from savings that were already declared, but did not list the plot. Savings in the 2022 statement were reduced, so the reconciliation balanced only by showing Rs 4,000,000 of household expenses that never happened. Every later statement then omits the plot. The fix: revise the 2022 wealth statement to show the plot and correct expenses, then revise 2023 to 2026 so the plot appears each year. No return revision is needed if no income arose from the plot; if it was rented out, the rent must be declared through revised returns. The figures are illustrative.

What Happens If You Leave It

Under Section 111, where a person owns money, an investment or a valuable article, or has incurred expenditure, and offers no explanation, or an explanation the Commissioner finds unsatisfactory, the amount can be included in income as income from other sources. Once a 122(9) notice arrives, the voluntary route under Section 116(3) is closed for that year, and penalties under Section 114(6A) rise to 50% of those leviable if a return is revised after the show cause notice. Questions Officers Usually Ask About Older Years

  • Where did the money for this asset come from, and was that source declared?
  • Why does this year's opening wealth differ from last year's closing wealth?
  • Why did household spending change sharply from one year to the next?
  • Who owns the asset registered in a family member's name?

A revision that answers these questions in advance, with documents, is the strongest position you can be in.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many years back can FBR amend my assessment?
Under Section 122(2), not after five years from the end of the financial year in which the assessment was issued or treated as issued, usually when the return was filed.
Until when can I revise an old wealth statement?
Under Section 116(3), any time before you receive a notice under Section 122(9) for that tax year.
Is there a 60-day limit for revising a wealth statement?
No. The 60-day rule applies to return revision without approval. Wealth statement revision is available until a 122(9) notice for that year.
Do I have to revise every year after the error?
Usually yes, because each year's closing wealth is the next year's opening wealth. Fix the earliest year first and carry the figures forward.
Does revising a wealth statement mean paying more tax?
Not always. If the change only corrects how already declared money was used, no tax may arise. If it reveals undeclared income, the return must be revised and the tax paid.
Can FBR reject my revised wealth statement?
The Commissioner can declare it void by written order if it is not to correct a bona fide omission or wrong statement.
What if I already received a notice?
The voluntary route under Section 116(3) is closed for that year; reply to the notice and consider the options in Section 114(6A).
Which years matter most in 2026?
Roughly Tax Years 2021 to 2026, depending on when each return was filed.
What evidence do I need to add an old property?
The sale deed, transfer letter or allotment, and bank records showing how it was paid for.
Can I correct household spending in an old statement?
Yes, through a revised wealth statement under Section 116(3) before a 122(9) notice, with realistic figures you can support.
Should I revise old years if FBR has never contacted me?
If you know an error exists, yes. Section 116(3) relief is available only until a 122(9) notice, and penalties under Section 114(6A) rise once FBR acts first.
