Electric Vehicle Tax Incentives Pakistan 2026 — What Buyers Actually Save

August 2026 Kamboh Associates 9 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN • SECP • Sales Tax Specialist

Pakistan's EV tax landscape shifted meaningfully in the 2026-27 budget — the sweeping exemptions of the previous few years partly expired, replaced by a more tiered, conditional set of incentives that a buyer needs to actually understand rather than assume based on older, now-outdated headlines about EVs being broadly tax-free.

TL;DR

The Automotive Industry Development and Export Policy 2021-26's broad EV tax exemptions lapsed on June 30, 2026. The 2026-27 budget introduced a tiered import duty structure (0% up to Rs. 20 million import value, rising to 30% and 40% at higher value bands) alongside a new sales tax of up to 25% on imported EVs — a meaningful change from the prior exemption-heavy environment. CKD kit sales tax exemption was extended to June 2027, and electric buses/trucks retain a reduced 1% sales tax. Why the EV Tax Picture Changed in 2026

For several years, Pakistan's Automotive Industry Development and Export Policy 2021-26 provided a wave of tax exemptions specifically designed to encourage early EV adoption — exemptions that lapsed on June 30, 2026, as that policy reached its scheduled end. The 2026-27 federal budget replaced this exemption-heavy environment with a more tiered, conditional structure, meaning buyers and importers relying on older articles or general impressions about EVs being broadly tax-free in Pakistan are very likely working from an outdated picture that no longer reflects the current rules.

The Tiered Import Duty Structure

Under the current framework, imported EVs face import duty calculated in value-based tiers: vehicles with an import value up to Rs. 20 million enjoy a zero-duty regime, EVs valued between Rs. 20 million and Rs. 30 million face a 30% duty, and vehicles exceeding Rs. 30 million in value are subject to a 40% duty. This tiered structure means the actual duty a buyer faces depends heavily on the specific vehicle's import value — a more affordable EV import can still access meaningful duty relief, while a higher-end imported EV faces a duty burden considerably closer to what a comparable conventional luxury vehicle import might face.

Key point: The zero-duty tier only applies below the Rs. 20 million import-value threshold — a common assumption that "EVs are duty-free" no longer holds uniformly across the full price range of imported electric vehicles.

Sales Tax of Up To 25% on Imported EVs

Alongside the tiered import duty, the 2026-27 budget introduced sales tax of up to 25% on imported EVs — a genuinely significant change from the more favorable sales tax treatment EVs previously enjoyed under the expiring policy framework. This sales tax applies on top of whatever import duty tier a specific vehicle falls into, meaning a buyer calculating the true landed cost of an imported EV needs to account for both the tiered duty and this separate sales tax layer, rather than treating either one as the complete tax picture on its own.

CKD Kits and Locally-Assembled EVs

Completely knocked-down (CKD) kits — components imported for local assembly rather than a fully-built imported vehicle — retain a sales tax exemption, extended through June 30, 2027 under the current Finance Bill. This distinction matters significantly for buyers: a locally-assembled EV built from CKD kits sits in a meaningfully different, generally more favorable tax position than a fully-built imported EV, which is exactly the kind of structural incentive designed to encourage local assembly investment rather than reliance purely on imports.

Electric Buses and Trucks — A Separate, More Favorable Category

Electric transport buses with 25 seats or more, and electric trucks in completely-built-up (CBU) condition, continue to benefit from a reduced 1% sales tax rate — a distinctly more favorable position than the up-to-25% rate applying to imported passenger EVs. This reflects a deliberate policy choice to prioritize commercial and public-transport electrification specifically, which matters for any business considering an electric commercial fleet rather than individual passenger vehicle purchases.

Incentives for EV Manufacturing Investment

Separately from consumer-facing purchase taxes, the government has proposed 0% customs duty on plant and machinery imported specifically for EV manufacturing facilities, available on a one-time basis for new projects and expansions, subject to Engineering Development Board approval. Inputs used by vendors and original equipment manufacturers for producing EV-specific parts also qualify for zero customs duty. This is a manufacturing-side incentive rather than something a vehicle buyer directly experiences, but it's relevant context for understanding the government's broader EV policy direction — investing in domestic capability rather than solely subsidizing imports.

The Proposed Motorway Toll Waiver

Pakistan's upcoming Auto Industry Development and Export Policy 2026-31 is set to waive motorway and highway toll taxes for electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles — a genuinely useful ongoing-cost incentive distinct from the one-time purchase tax considerations covered above, worth factoring into a buyer's total cost-of-ownership comparison between an EV and a conventional vehicle, particularly for someone who drives frequently on toll roads.

How This Compares to Hybrid Vehicle Tax Treatment

Reports around the 2026-27 budget process indicated that tax treatment for hybrid vehicles was expected to remain largely unchanged even as the pure-EV framework shifted — meaning a buyer comparing a fully electric vehicle against a hybrid alternative should specifically check current rates for each category separately rather than assuming they move in lockstep, since the policy direction for pure EVs and hybrids hasn't necessarily tracked identically through this particular budget cycle.

Provincial Registration and Token Tax Considerations

Beyond the federal import duty and sales tax framework covered above, provincial vehicle registration and annual token tax rates can also carry EV-specific treatment, varying by province — some provinces have offered reduced or waived token tax for electric vehicles as their own separate incentive layered on top of the federal tax picture. A buyer should check their specific province's current motor vehicle tax rules for EVs, since this is a distinct, provincially-administered layer separate from the federal import and sales tax considerations that make up the bulk of this guide, and provincial policies don't necessarily move in lockstep with federal budget changes.

Used vs New EV Imports

Pakistan's vehicle import policy generally treats used and new vehicle imports differently across various categories, and this distinction can apply to EVs as well — a used imported EV may face a different duty and tax calculation basis than a new one, tied to vehicle age and applicable depreciation-based valuation rules under Pakistan's broader vehicle import framework. A buyer specifically considering a used EV import, rather than a new one, should confirm the applicable rules for that category directly, since the tiered duty figures discussed earlier in this guide are generally oriented around new vehicle import values rather than the separate, age-adjusted calculation basis a used-vehicle import typically follows instead.

Tax Treatment of Home and Commercial Charging Infrastructure

A buyer installing home EV charging equipment, or a business installing commercial charging infrastructure, faces a separate tax question from the vehicle purchase itself — charging equipment and installation costs follow their own applicable sales tax and duty treatment, distinct from the vehicle-specific incentives covered throughout this guide. A business specifically investing in EV charging infrastructure as part of a broader fleet electrification strategy should get this specifically confirmed rather than simply assuming the same incentive structure that applies to the vehicles themselves automatically extends in full to any supporting charging infrastructure they're also installing alongside a new EV fleet.

Why Confirming Current Rates Directly Matters So Much Here

Perhaps more than any other tax topic covered across this site, EV incentives in Pakistan have moved through several genuinely distinct policy phases in a relatively short span — a broad-exemption era, a tiered-transition period, and now a more conditional structure calibrated around import value and vehicle category. A buyer researching EV taxes should treat any single article, including this one, as reflecting the position at the time it was written rather than a permanently fixed rule, and should specifically confirm current rates with a tax professional or customs broker before finalizing an actual purchase decision, given how much this specific area has continued to shift.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming EVs remain broadly tax-exempt based on older reporting: the 2021-26 policy's exemptions lapsed June 30, 2026, replaced by a more tiered, conditional structure.
  • Overlooking that the zero-duty import tier only applies below Rs. 20 million import value: higher-value imported EVs face meaningfully different duty rates.
  • Forgetting the new sales tax applies on top of import duty, not instead of it: both layers need to be accounted for in the total landed cost.
  • Not distinguishing between fully-imported EVs and locally-assembled CKD-kit EVs: these sit in genuinely different, and differently favorable, tax positions.
  • Assuming hybrid vehicle tax treatment moves identically with pure-EV policy changes: check current rates for each category specifically rather than assuming parallel treatment.

A Worked Example

A buyer comparing two imported EV options — one valued at roughly Rs. 18 million and another at Rs. 25 million — finds the two vehicles land in genuinely different tax tiers: the Rs. 18 million EV qualifies for the zero-duty import tier, while the Rs. 25 million EV faces the 30% duty tier, on top of which both vehicles face the new sales tax of up to 25%. After calculating the full landed cost including both duty and sales tax for each option, the buyer also investigates a locally-assembled alternative built from CKD kits, finding its overall tax position more favorable than either fully-imported option once the CKD exemption is factored in — a genuinely different total cost picture than a quick glance at "EV tax incentives" headlines from a year or two earlier would have suggested. The buyer also checks their specific province's current token tax treatment for electric vehicles, finding a modest additional reduction available there too, layered on top of whichever federal import and sales tax path the eventual purchase decision follows.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are electric vehicles still tax-free to import into Pakistan?
No, not uniformly — the broad exemptions under the 2021-26 Automotive Industry Development and Export Policy lapsed on June 30, 2026. Current treatment is a tiered import duty structure plus a new sales tax of up to 25%, depending on the vehicle's import value.
What determines which import duty tier my EV falls into?
The vehicle's import value: 0% duty up to Rs. 20 million, 30% duty between Rs. 20-30 million, and 40% duty above Rs. 30 million.
Does the new EV sales tax replace the import duty, or apply on top of it?
On top of it — a buyer needs to account for both the tiered import duty and the separate sales tax of up to 25% to calculate the true total tax cost on an imported EV.
Is a locally-assembled EV taxed differently from a fully-imported one?
Yes — CKD (completely knocked-down) kits imported for local assembly retain a sales tax exemption extended through June 2027, putting locally-assembled EVs in a generally more favorable tax position than fully-imported vehicles.
Do electric buses and trucks get the same tax treatment as electric cars?
No — electric buses with 25+ seats and electric trucks in CBU condition benefit from a reduced 1% sales tax, a distinctly more favorable rate than the up-to-25% rate applying to imported passenger EVs.
Do I get any provincial tax benefit for owning an EV beyond the federal import/sales tax picture?
Possibly — some provinces offer reduced or waived token/registration tax for EVs as a separate incentive layer. Check your specific province's current rules, since this is administered separately from federal policy.
Is buying a used imported EV taxed the same way as a new one?
Not necessarily — used vehicle imports generally follow a different duty and valuation basis tied to vehicle age under Pakistan's broader import framework. Confirm the applicable rules specifically for used EV imports rather than assuming the new-vehicle figures apply directly.
