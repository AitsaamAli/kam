Form ATL-U

The Form ATL-U Undertaking: What You Cannot Buy or Receive for Six Months, and What Happens If You Do

19 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 19 November 2026
Form ATL-U saves Rs 25,000 in exchange for one promise. Before you make it, know exactly what the promise covers.

The undertaking takes a minute to furnish. Keeping it means thinking about every way property could come to you, or to someone holding it for you, over half a year. This guide walks through the situations our clients actually ask about.

TL;DR

Under Section 182A(3) of the Income Tax Ordinance, an individual using Form ATL-U declares that he 'shall not purchase, acquire, or otherwise obtain ownership or beneficial interest in any property' for six months from the date of the undertaking. The words are wide: buying, acquiring by any other means and obtaining any ownership or beneficial interest are all covered, and the law does not define 'property' narrowly for this purpose. Plots, houses, flats, shops, allotment files and bookings are clearly within it; gifts, inheritance, vehicles and shares are not addressed expressly, so the careful approach is to treat them as covered until FBR clarifies. As notified in SRO 2097(I)/2026, a breach leads to withdrawal of the benefit from the date of breach after a hearing, and the surcharge rule then applies.

The Words That Bind You

Section 182A(3) has four working parts:

Words in the lawWhat they cover
"purchase"Buying for a price, in cash or by instalments
"acquire"Coming to own something by any route, not only by buying
"or otherwise obtain ownership"A catch-all for any other way of becoming the owner
"or beneficial interest"Property in another person's name that is held for your benefit

Then the object: "any property". The undertaking does not say "immovable property", even though Sections 236C and 236K, which deal with property transfers, do. Until FBR issues guidance, read "any property" in its ordinary, wide sense.

Clearly Covered

  • Plots, houses, flats, shops and agricultural land bought in your name.
  • Allotment files and bookings in housing schemes, including the first instalment of a plan.
  • Transfer of a file into your name from another person.
  • A share in jointly bought property: you are acquiring your share.
  • Property in a spouse's or relative's name paid for by you and held for you. That is a beneficial interest.

Grey Areas: Treat as Covered Until Clarified

SituationWhy it is uncertainSafe course

These are honest uncertainties. The law does not answer them and the notified rules, as reported, do not either. We would rather you pay Rs 25,000 than lose it later in a dispute.

Not Restricted

  • Selling property you already own. See our guide on selling as a late filer.
  • Renting property as a tenant. A tenancy is not ownership.
  • Repairing or building on land you already own. You are not obtaining new ownership of land; take advice if the construction creates a separate unit for sale or transfer.
  • Your adult family members' own purchases from their own funds, in their own names and for their own benefit.

Counting the Six Months

The law says six months "commencing from the date of furnishing such undertaking". The date on the IRIS acknowledgement for Form ATL-U is your start date. Six months from 25 November 2026 run to 24 May 2027; do not book anything until the following day. The return's filing date and the date your name appears on the ATL are not the start date.

What a Breach Costs

As notified, FBR can check against information from registries, financial institutions and other lawful sources. If a breach is found, you are given an electronic opportunity to explain. If it is established, the benefit is withdrawn from the date of breach and your ATL position follows the ordinary rule in Section 182A(1): you need to pay the surcharge to be included.

The real cost is the timing. A breach usually happens during a property purchase. If you are off the ATL at that moment, the buyer's advance tax under Section 236K is 10.5% to 18.5% by value instead of 1.25%. On a Rs 1 crore plot, that is Rs 1,050,000 instead of Rs 125,000.

If Your Plans Change

Life changes. If a property opportunity appears in month three, do not proceed and hope. Talk to us first. Depending on the facts, the answer may be to wait out the six months, to pay the surcharge before the transaction so you are on the ATL on your own terms, or to structure the deal so that it completes after the end date.

A Quick Self-Check

  1. Is any property, file or booking going to come into my name in six months?
  2. Is anyone holding property for me, or buying with my money?
  3. Am I expecting a gift or inheritance of property soon?
  4. Do I need to buy a car in the next six months?

Four honest "no" answers: Form ATL-U is likely right for you. Any "yes" or "maybe": talk it through, or read our surcharge vs Form ATL-U comparison.

Keep Evidence During the Six Months

If a family member buys property during your six months, keep simple proof that it is theirs: their own bank transfer, their own name on every paper, and no payment from your account. If FBR's data ever links you to a transaction, this is what lets you explain it in the electronic hearing that the rules provide for before any withdrawal of the benefit.

Mark the Calendar

Write the end date on the acknowledgement, put it in your phone, and tell your spouse. The most common breach we hear about is a booking made on a property expo day in the fifth month, when the person had simply forgotten. A two-week buffer after the end date costs nothing and removes any argument about counting.

A Note on Vehicles

Vehicles deserve a separate word because they are the commonest purchase. The Ordinance treats them in their own sections (231B for registration), and the undertaking does not mention them; but it does say "any property", and a car is property in ordinary language. Until FBR says otherwise, a car purchase in the six months is a risk we would not advise taking.

If You Think You Have Already Breached

Do not ignore it and do not hide it. Three practical steps:

  1. Write down the facts: what was acquired, on what date, in whose name and from whose money.
  2. Get advice on whether it is a breach at all. A family member's own purchase, or an instalment on an interest you already held, may not be.
  3. If it is a breach, consider the surcharge. The rules, as notified, withdraw the benefit from the date of breach and apply the ordinary rule, which is the surcharge. Being ready to show you understood and acted promptly is better than waiting for FBR to raise it.

The electronic hearing the rules provide for is your chance to explain. Go into it with documents, not memories.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Form ATL-U stop me from buying?
Section 182A(3) covers purchasing, acquiring or otherwise obtaining ownership or beneficial interest in any property for six months. Plots, houses, flats, shops, files and bookings are clearly covered.
Can I buy a car after signing Form ATL-U?
The law says 'any property' and does not mention vehicles. Until FBR clarifies, the careful course is to wait or to pay the surcharge instead.
Does inheritance breach the undertaking?
The law does not address it. Succession is not your act, but transferring the property into your name is a way of obtaining ownership. Seek advice before mutation within the six months.
Can my wife buy a plot while I use Form ATL-U?
If she buys with her own money for herself, it is her purchase. If you fund it or she holds it for you, that is a beneficial interest and a breach.
When do the six months end?
Six months from the date you furnished the undertaking, shown on the IRIS acknowledgement.
What happens if I breach the undertaking?
As notified, after an electronic hearing the benefit is withdrawn from the date of breach, and the surcharge rule in Section 182A(1) applies.
Can I sell property during the six months?
Yes. Selling is not one of the restricted acts.
Can I pay the surcharge later instead?
Yes. If your plans change, paying the surcharge puts you on the ATL without depending on the undertaking. Do it before the purchase.
Can my family buy property while my undertaking runs?
Yes, from their own money and for their own benefit. Keep evidence that the purchase is theirs.
Is a two-week buffer after six months necessary?
Not legally, but it removes any dispute about how the six months are counted.
I booked a plot by mistake in month four. What now?
Write down the facts and get advice. If it is a breach, the benefit is withdrawn from the breach date and the surcharge rule applies; acting early is better than waiting for FBR.
