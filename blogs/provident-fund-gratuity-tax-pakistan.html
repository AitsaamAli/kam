Retirement benefits — provident fund, gratuity, and pension — are among the most tax-advantaged income types in Pakistan when received from the right sources. An employee who retires from a company with an FBR-approved provident fund trust pays zero income tax on lump-sum benefits. But employees receiving gratuity from unapproved arrangements face full slab-rate taxation. Knowing the difference before retirement can save hundreds of thousands of rupees.

Approved vs Unapproved Provident Fund

Short answer: the balance of a recognised (approved) provident fund is exempt when paid; an unapproved fund is taxed as salary.

The most important distinction for private sector employees is whether their employer's provident fund is approved by the Commissioner of Income Tax under Part III of the First Schedule to the Income Tax Ordinance 2001.

Approved Provident Fund:

  • The trust is registered with FBR and files separate annual returns
  • The accumulated balance due and payable to the employee is exempt (Second Schedule, clause 23)

Unapproved Provident Fund:

  • No FBR trust registration — funds are held in a company bank account or informal arrangement
  • Employer contribution is taxable as a perquisite in the year credited
  • Interest earned in the fund is taxable each year
  • Lump sum at withdrawal is taxable as salary — can push income into high slab in the year received

Before joining a company, ask HR if their PF is FBR-approved. This single difference can mean tens of thousands of rupees difference in your retirement tax. Many small and medium companies have unapproved funds because setting up an approved trust costs Rs. 50,000–150,000 in legal fees — but for employees, the cost to them is much higher over a career.

Gratuity Tax Rules — Approved vs Unapproved

Short answer: approved-fund and Government gratuity are exempt; a Board-approved scheme gives Rs 300,000; otherwise only the lesser of 50% or Rs 75,000.

Gratuity is a one-time payment on retirement. Clause (13) of Part I of the Second Schedule to the Income Tax Ordinance (FBR, amended up to 30.06.2026) exempts it as follows (checked 20 November 2026):

Who pays the gratuityExempt amountClause
Government, Local Government, statutory body or corporationAmount receivable under the employee's service rules13(i)
Gratuity fund approved by the Commissioner (Sixth Schedule, Part III)Amount receivable from the fund13(ii)
Any other employer, under a scheme for all employees approved by the BoardUp to Rs 300,00013(iii)
Any other case50% of the amount or Rs 75,000, whichever is less13(iv)

Sub-clause (iv) does not apply to a payment not received in Pakistan, to a director who is not a regular employee, to a non-resident, or to someone who has already received gratuity from the same or another employer. Anything above the exempt amount is taxed as salary in the year received.

Worked Example — Private Sector Gratuity

Tariq retires and receives Rs. 3,000,000 gratuity. If it is paid from a gratuity fund approved by the Commissioner, the whole amount is exempt under clause 13(ii). If his employer pays it directly with no approved fund or Board-approved scheme, only Rs. 75,000 (the lesser of 50% and Rs. 75,000) is exempt, and Rs. 2,925,000 is added to his salary for the year. Checking the fund's approval before retirement is worth a great deal.

Government Employees — GP Fund and Pension

Short answer: GP Fund and commuted pension are exempt; monthly pension is taxed only above Rs 10 million a year, and not at all after age 70.

Federal and provincial government employees have the most tax-advantaged retirement arrangements in Pakistan:

  • General Provident Fund (GP Fund): Mandatory government savings scheme. Both the employee contribution and accumulated interest are fully exempt from income tax. Final lump sum at retirement is also fully exempt under the Second Schedule.
  • Pension: the general pension exemption (Second Schedule clause 8) was omitted by the Finance Act 2025. Under Section 12(2A), pension from a former employer is charged to tax where it exceeds Rs 10 million in a tax year, as a final tax at the rate in Division I of the First Schedule; an individual aged 70 or over is not taxed on pension income; and a pensioner who still works for the former employer is taxed at the normal rates.
  • Commuted Pension: commutation of pension received from Government or an approved pension scheme is exempt (Second Schedule, clause 12).
  • Gratuity: Government gratuity is exempt to the amount receivable under the service rules (clause 13(i)).

Government employees should still file returns. Even though pension and GP Fund withdrawals are exempt, filing keeps you on ATL and reduces non-filer WHT rates on banking and property. Many retired government employees mistakenly stop filing — this costs them every time they receive bank profit or sell property.

VPF — Voluntary Provident Fund Contributions

Short answer: voluntary contributions grow inside a recognised fund and the balance is exempt when paid; there is no separate yearly deduction for them.

Employees of companies with approved PF trusts can make voluntary additional contributions (VPF) above the mandatory amount. Benefits:

  • The accumulated balance of a recognised provident fund, including voluntary contributions, is exempt when it becomes payable (clause 23)
  • Earnings accumulate inside the fund

We could not find a separate deduction for an employee's own voluntary contributions in the Ordinance as amended up to 30 June 2026, so the benefit is the exemption of the balance on payment, not a yearly deduction.

SECP Voluntary Pension System (VPS)

Short answer: VPS contributions earn a Section 63 tax credit on up to 20% of taxable income, and up to half the balance is tax-free at retirement.

For freelancers, self-employed professionals, and employees whose employer lacks an approved PF, SECP-approved VPS accounts offer similar benefits under Section 63 of the Income Tax Ordinance:

Section 63 gives a tax credit, not a deduction: (A/B) × C, where A is the tax assessed before credits, B is taxable income, and C is the lesser of your contribution and 20% of taxable income. The extra allowance for people aged over 40 applied only up to 30 June 2019, so the limit is 20% for everyone today.

Example: taxable income Rs 2 million, tax Rs 300,000, VPS contribution Rs 400,000 (20% of income). Credit = (300,000 / 2,000,000) × 400,000 = Rs 60,000.

At retirement, up to 50% of the accumulated balance can be withdrawn tax-free (Second Schedule, clause 23A); early withdrawals and amounts above 50% are taxed at the rate in Section 12(6), deducted by the pension fund manager.

How to Declare Provident Fund and Gratuity on IRIS

Short answer: put taxable gratuity in salary, show exempt amounts under exempt income, and claim the Section 63 credit for VPS contributions.

  1. Log in to IRIS, open Form 114(I) for income tax return filing 2026.
  2. Under Income from Salary, include any taxable portion of provident fund or gratuity in the gross salary figure.
  3. If the provident fund is approved, claim the exemption under Exempt Income section — select the relevant exemption clause from Second Schedule.
  4. For government employees, both pension and GP Fund withdrawals go under Exempt Income.
  5. For VPS contributions made during the year, claim the Section 63 tax credit for the contribution to an approved pension fund.
  6. Your employer's WHT certificate (salary certificate) should show the taxable vs exempt breakdown. Verify the figures match what your employer reported.

How to Verify Your Employer's Provident Fund Is FBR-Approved

Short answer: ask HR for the Commissioner's approval order and the trust deed before you retire.

Most employees never check this until retirement — by then, it is too late to change anything. Approval status determines whether you keep the full lump sum or lose a third of it to tax, so verify it early:

  • Ask HR for the trust deed. An approved fund is registered as a separate legal trust with its own bank account, trustees, and an approval order issued by the Commissioner of Income Tax (or the relevant Chief Commissioner). HR should be able to produce this order on request.
  • Check the salary slip structure. If "employer PF contribution" appears as a separate line that is not added to your taxable salary each month, the fund is almost certainly approved — unapproved employer contributions are usually taxed as a perquisite immediately.
  • Look for a separate PF account statement. Approved trusts issue annual statements showing your balance, employer contribution, and interest credited — separate from your salary certificate.
  • Ask if the trust files its own return. Approved PF trusts are separately registered with FBR and file their own annual income tax return as a trust, distinct from the company's corporate return.
  • When in doubt, get written confirmation from HR before your final year of service — this gives you time to plan withdrawal timing, VPS top-ups, or a change of employer if the exposure is large.

Startups and small private companies most often run unapproved funds simply because setting up an approved trust involves legal and compliance cost. If you work at a smaller employer, do not assume approval — confirm it in writing.

Common Mistakes Employees Make With Retirement Benefit Tax

Short answer: the costly ones are assuming all gratuity is exempt, reporting it in the wrong year and forgetting the VPS credit.

  • Assuming all gratuity is automatically exempt. Exemption depends on clause 13: full from an approved fund or under Government service rules, Rs 300,000 under a Board-approved scheme, and otherwise only the lesser of 50% or Rs 75,000.
  • Reporting gratuity in the wrong tax year. Gratuity is taxable in the year it is actually received, not the year you resign or retire if payment is delayed. Check the payment date on your bank statement, not your last working day.
  • Forgetting to claim the VPS tax credit. VPS contributions earn a Section 63 tax credit that must be claimed in the return — they are not automatically pulled from your salary certificate the way employer PF contributions sometimes are.
  • Mixing up GP Fund and EOBI on the return. These sit in different sections of IRIS (GP Fund under Exempt Income for government employees; EOBI pension also under Exempt Income but reported by private-sector filers) — using the wrong head can trigger a system flag.
  • Not adjusting advance tax after a large gratuity year. A one-off gratuity payment can push you into a higher slab for that year only. Filers sometimes carry that higher slab assumption into next year's salary WHT calculation and overpay — recompute WHT with your employer once the retirement year return is filed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is provident fund withdrawal taxable in Pakistan?
It depends on whether the fund is FBR-approved. Withdrawals from an FBR-approved provident fund trust are fully exempt from income tax. Withdrawals from unapproved funds (company accounts without a registered trust) are taxable as salary income in the year of withdrawal, which can cause a significant spike in your tax liability for that year. Ask your employer HR whether their PF is approved by FBR.
Is government pension taxable in Pakistan?
Since the Finance Act 2025, pension from a former employer is taxed under Section 12(2A) only where it exceeds Rs 10 million in a tax year, and not at all for individuals aged 70 or over. Commutation of pension is exempt under clause 12 of the Second Schedule. Pensioners should still file to stay on the ATL.
How much gratuity is tax-free in Pakistan?
Under clause 13 of the Second Schedule: the full amount from a gratuity fund approved by the Commissioner or under Government service rules; up to Rs 300,000 under a Board-approved scheme; otherwise the lesser of 50% or Rs 75,000. The rest is taxed as salary.
What is the difference between GP Fund and EOBI in Pakistan?
The GP Fund (General Provident Fund) is a savings scheme for government employees only — they contribute a mandatory percentage of salary, which accumulates with interest and is paid as a lump sum at retirement. EOBI (Employees Old-Age Benefits Institution) is for private sector employees and provides a monthly pension from age 60. Employers contribute 5% of minimum wage monthly.
Can I deduct VPS contributions from income tax in Pakistan?
Section 63 gives a tax credit, not a deduction: (A/B) x C, where C is the lesser of your contribution and 20% of taxable income. The age-based extra limit ended on 30 June 2019.
I received both gratuity and PF in the same year — how do I file?
Declare both in the same return. The accumulated balance of a recognised provident fund is exempt (clause 23); gratuity is exempt as clause 13 allows, and any excess is added to salary for the year.
