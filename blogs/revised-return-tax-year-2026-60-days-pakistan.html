Revised Return for Tax Year 2026: How the 60-Day Window Works, the Conditions, and What Happens After

11 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 11 November 2026
Most Tax Year 2026 returns were filed close to the 31 October 2026 deadline. If yours has a mistake, the next 60 days are the simplest time to fix it.

The Tax Year 2026 deadline was extended twice and closed on 31 October 2026, and many returns went in during the last week. Rushed returns often leave out a bank account, a plot, a source of income or a tax credit. This guide explains exactly how the law lets you correct a 2026 return, why the next 60 days matter, and what to prepare.

TL;DR

Under Section 114(6) of the Income Tax Ordinance (amended up to 30 June 2026), a person who discovers an omission or wrong statement in a filed return may file a revised return, with revised accounts where relevant and written, signed reasons. Commissioner approval is normally required, but not if the revised return is filed within 60 days of filing the original. A return filed on 31 October 2026 can therefore be revised without approval until about 30 December 2026. After that, approval is deemed granted if the Commissioner does not decide within 60 days of the request, and must be granted for a bona fide omission. A revised return that misses a condition is treated as invalid.

What Section 114(6) Says

Any person who, having furnished a return, discovers an omission or wrong statement in it may file a revised return, subject to these conditions:

ConditionWhat it means in practice
(a) Revised accountsBusiness taxpayers attach revised accounts or revised audited accounts. The Commissioner may waive this if they are not necessary.
(b) Written reasonsThe reasons for revision, in writing and signed by the taxpayer, are filed with the revised return.
(ba) Commissioner's approvalWritten approval of the Commissioner is required, except in the cases below.
(c) Not below an orderTaxable income declared must not be less (and loss not more) than income or loss determined by an order under Sections 121, 122, 122A, 129, 132, 133 or 221.

If any condition is not met, the revised return is treated as an invalid return, as if it had not been filed. That is why the reasons, the figures and the timing all matter.

The 60-Day Rule: When No Approval Is Needed

The provisos to Section 114(6) remove the approval requirement in three situations:

  1. Within 60 days of filing the return. A revised return filed within sixty days of filing the original does not need the Commissioner's approval.
  2. When the Commissioner does not decide in time. If you seek approval and the Commissioner makes no written order within 60 days of your request, approval is deemed granted.
  3. When you declare more income. If the revised taxable income is more than (or the loss less than) the amount assessed under Section 120, approval is deemed granted.

The law also says the Commissioner shall grant approval for a bona fide omission or wrong statement. The mode and manner of seeking revision are prescribed by FBR, through IRIS.

Common Reasons to Revise a 2026 Return

  • A bank account, savings certificate or profit on debt left out.
  • A plot, house, vehicle or gold not shown in the wealth statement.
  • Rental, freelance or business income omitted.
  • Adjustable tax collected at source not claimed, for example tax on monthly electricity bills above Rs 30,000, which Section 235(4) makes adjustable for taxpayers other than companies.
  • A wealth reconciliation that does not balance, or household expenses understated.
  • Wrong figures carried from the employer's certificate or from last year's statement.

What Happens When the Revised Return Is Filed

A complete return is treated as an assessment order under Section 120. When you file a valid revised return, Section 122(3) says the Commissioner is treated as having made an amended assessment of the income and tax set out in the revised return, and the revised return is taken to be an amended assessment order issued on the day it is filed. In plain terms, the revised return replaces the original as your assessment for the year.

The Commissioner can still amend an assessment later, within the time limits in Section 122(4): the later of five years from the end of the financial year in which the original assessment was issued, or one year from the end of the financial year of the amended assessment.

If the Revision Increases or Reduces Tax

Tax increases: pay the additional tax with the revision. Under Section 114(6A), a person who voluntarily files a revised return with the tax short paid and default surcharge, before receiving a notice under Section 177 or 122(9), pays no penalty. Our guide to revising before a notice explains the penalty relief in detail.

Tax reduces or a refund arises: the revised return can create a refund. Refund applications under Section 170 must be made within three years of the later of the assessment date or the date the tax was paid; see our guide to claiming refunds and missed credits.

Revising the Wealth Statement at the Same Time

Most return revisions also change the wealth statement. Section 116(3) allows a revised wealth statement, with a revised reconciliation and the reasons, before you receive a notice under Section 122(9) for that year. The Commissioner can declare a revision void if it is not a bona fide correction. Revise both together so the figures agree.

What to Prepare

  1. The IRIS acknowledgement of the original return, to fix your 60-day date.
  2. Documents for each change: bank certificates, property papers, salary or rent records, withholding certificates.
  3. Revised figures for income, deductions, credits and the wealth statement.
  4. A revised wealth reconciliation that balances.
  5. A short, honest written reason for each change, signed by you.
  6. Payment of any additional tax before submitting.

If Your 60 Days Have Already Passed

You can still revise, but with the Commissioner's approval. Apply with your reasons; if no written order is made within 60 days of your request, approval is deemed granted. If you are increasing your income above the assessed figure, approval is deemed granted anyway. For older years and the five-year limits, see our guide to revising wealth statements of the last five years; for the general background in Roman Urdu, see revised return kya hai.

When You Do Not Need a Revision

  • You have not filed yet. A return filed for the first time after 31 October 2026 is a late return, not a revision; late filing has its own consequences under Sections 182 and 182A.
  • The difference is a typing error with no effect on income, tax or wealth. Keep a note on file; a revision is not always worth it.
  • FBR has already amended your assessment. Section 114(6)(c) bars a revised return that declares less than the income determined by such an order; respond to the order instead.

Writing the Reasons Properly

Section 114(6)(b) requires the reasons, in writing and signed by you. Good reasons are short, specific and verifiable: "Profit of Rs 85,000 on a savings account at [bank] and the tax deducted on it were omitted; bank certificate attached." Vague reasons such as "some corrections" invite questions and do not show a bona fide omission, which is the test the Commissioner applies when approval is needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I revise my 2026 income tax return?
Yes. Section 114(6) allows a revised return when you discover an omission or wrong statement, with written reasons and, for businesses, revised accounts.
Do I need the Commissioner's approval to revise?
Not if the revised return is filed within 60 days of filing the original return. After that, approval is needed, but it is deemed granted if the Commissioner does not decide within 60 days of your request.
I filed on 31 October 2026. Until when can I revise without approval?
Within 60 days of filing, which is about 30 December 2026. Count from the date on your IRIS acknowledgement.
What happens if my revised return misses a condition?
Section 114(6) says it is treated as an invalid return, as if it had not been filed.
Will a revised return trigger a penalty?
Not if you file it voluntarily with any tax short paid and default surcharge before a notice under Section 177 or 122(9), under Section 114(6A).
Does the revised return replace the original?
Yes. Under Section 122(3) the revised return is treated as an amended assessment order issued on the day it is filed.
Should I revise my wealth statement too?
Usually yes. Section 116(3) allows a revised wealth statement with a revised reconciliation and reasons before a notice under Section 122(9).
Can a revision reduce my tax?
Yes, if the original overstated income or missed credits. A refund can then be claimed within the time limit in Section 170.
Is a return filed after 31 October 2026 a revised return?
No. A first return filed after the due date is a late return. A revised return corrects a return you have already filed.
What should the written reasons say?
Each change, why it was missed and the document that supports it, signed by you, as Section 114(6)(b) requires.
