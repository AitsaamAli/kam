W-8BEN Form for Pakistani Freelancers: What It Is, Who Needs It, and How to Fill Each Line (2026)

20 November 2026 Kamboh Associates 12 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Author · Figures from FBR’s official website · Facts checked 20 November 2026
The W-8BEN tells a US payer you are not a US person. It does not file anything with the IRS, and it does not change your Pakistani tax.

Almost every Pakistani freelancer meets the W-8BEN on the first payout from a US client or platform. It looks like a tax return, so people either panic or click through it carelessly. Both are mistakes. Here is what the form does, line by line, based on the IRS's own instructions, and how it fits with your FBR obligations.

Short answer

Form W-8BEN (Certificate of Foreign Status of Beneficial Owner for United States Tax Withholding and Reporting) is the IRS form a non-US individual gives to a US payer or platform to show they are not a US person. It is given to the payer, not filed with the IRS. Without a valid form, the IRS instructions say a withholding agent may have to treat the payment as made to a US person or apply withholding; the standard rate on US-source fixed or determinable income of a foreign person is 30%. For a freelancer in Pakistan, the key lines are name, country of citizenship, permanent residence address in Pakistan, and the foreign tax identifying number, which for an individual is the NTN (the CNIC number). Part II (treaty claim) is only for a reduced rate on US-source income. A form generally stays valid until the last day of the third calendar year after it is signed, unless your circumstances change. Your Pakistani tax on the same earnings is settled separately, in your FBR return.

What the W-8BEN Is

Short answer: Form W-8BEN certifies to a US payer that you are a non-US individual and the beneficial owner of the income.

The full name is "Certificate of Foreign Status of Beneficial Owner for United States Tax Withholding and Reporting (Individuals)". According to the IRS, a foreign person gives it to the withholding agent or payer when requested, to establish that they are not a US person, to claim that they are the beneficial owner of the income, and, where relevant, to claim a reduced rate of withholding under an income tax treaty.

Who Needs a W-8BEN

Short answer: Pakistani individuals paid by US clients, platforms or brokers need it; companies use W-8BEN-E.

  • A Pakistani freelancer paid by a US client or a US-based platform.
  • A Pakistani investor holding US shares through a broker, for dividend withholding.
  • Anyone a US payer asks for proof that they are not a US person.

You do not use a W-8BEN if you are a US citizen or US resident for tax purposes (that is Form W-9), or if you are paid through a company you own (that company uses the W-8BEN-E).

Why Most Freelancers Owe No US Tax

Short answer: Services performed in Pakistan are generally not US-source income, so no US tax is usually withheld.

US withholding on a foreign person applies to US-source income. The IRS treats income from personal services as sourced where the services are performed. Work you do while physically in Pakistan is therefore generally foreign-source income for US purposes, even when the client is in the US. That is why, for most freelancers, the W-8BEN simply confirms foreign status and no US tax is withheld. It is also why most freelancers do not need the treaty section at all.

If you travel to the US and work there, or earn US-source income such as US dividends, the position changes and Part II may matter.

How to Fill Each Line

Short answer: Enter your name, Pakistan, your home address and your NTN (CNIC) as foreign TIN; leave Part II blank unless claiming a treaty rate.

Part II: The Treaty Claim

Short answer: Part II claims a US-Pakistan treaty rate on US-source income such as dividends; most freelancers do not need it.

The IRS lists an income tax treaty between the United States and Pakistan on its "United States income tax treaties A to Z" page. Part II is used to claim that treaty's reduced rate or exemption on a specific kind of US-source income, such as dividends. You name the country of residence, and for a special rate you cite the treaty article, the rate and the type of income, with the conditions you meet.

Two cautions. First, if your income is not US-source (services performed in Pakistan), there is nothing to reduce, so a treaty claim adds risk without benefit. Second, the IRS instructions say a US TIN or foreign TIN is generally required for treaty claims, and the certification is signed under penalties of perjury. Do not cite an article you have not read. When in doubt, leave Part II blank and ask.

Mistakes We See

Short answer: Common errors are a missing NTN, a business address, a wrong date order and an unneeded treaty claim.

  • Writing "N/A" in line 6a when you have an NTN. Enter the number.
  • Putting a business address in line 3. It must be your permanent residence.
  • Using W-8BEN for your company. A private limited company or AOP uses W-8BEN-E.
  • Claiming a treaty rate on service income earned in Pakistan, often copied from a template online.
  • Forgetting to update after you move abroad or become a US resident. The instructions say that after a change in circumstances that makes the form incorrect, you must tell the payer within 30 days and give a new form.
  • Date order: 03-07-1995 means 7 March, not 3 July.

Your Pakistani Tax Is Separate

Short answer: The W-8BEN does not change your Pakistani tax; freelance income is reported in your FBR return.

The W-8BEN only deals with US withholding. A resident individual in Pakistan is taxed on worldwide income under the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, so your freelance earnings are reported in your FBR return. How freelance and IT export income is taxed, and what records banks issue for foreign remittances, is covered in our tax return guide for freelancers. Remittances that build up in your accounts should match your wealth statement.

Staying on the Active Taxpayers List also matters for a freelancer who keeps money in Pakistani banks: bank profit is taxed at 20% instead of 40%, and there is no cash withdrawal tax.

Validity and Renewal

Short answer: A W-8BEN is generally valid until the end of the third calendar year after signing.

A W-8BEN generally remains in effect until the last day of the third calendar year following the year it is signed. A form signed on 15 March 2026 is valid until 31 December 2029, unless a change in circumstances makes any information incorrect. Platforms usually remind you to renew; keep a copy of each signed form.

W-8BEN, W-8BEN-E or W-9?

Short answer: Individuals use W-8BEN, non-US entities use W-8BEN-E, and US citizens or residents use W-9.

FormWho uses itTypical Pakistani case
W-8BENNon-US individual (beneficial owner)Freelancer, consultant or investor paid in their own name
W-8BEN-ENon-US entityA Pakistani private limited company or other entity receiving the payment
W-9US person (citizen or resident)A Pakistani who is also a US citizen or green card holder

If you are a US citizen as well as Pakistani, the W-8BEN is the wrong form however long you have lived in Pakistan; US citizens are US persons for tax purposes.

Filling It on a Platform: Step by Step

Short answer: Choose individual and non-US person, enter your CNIC name and address, your NTN as foreign TIN, and skip the treaty claim unless needed.

  1. Open the tax information section of the platform and choose "individual" and "not a US person".
  2. Enter your legal name exactly as on your CNIC or passport.
  3. Enter your permanent residence address in Pakistan, with city and postal code.
  4. Enter your NTN in the foreign TIN field. For an individual, FBR uses the CNIC number as the NTN.
  5. Answer the question on US activity honestly. If you performed no work while physically in the US, say so.
  6. Skip the treaty claim unless you have US-source income it applies to.
  7. Sign electronically, then download and keep a copy.

Keep the Pakistani Paper Trail Too

Short answer: Keep the platform earnings statement, bank remittance records and the W-8BEN together for FBR.

The payer keeps your W-8BEN; FBR never sees it. What FBR does see are the remittances arriving in your Pakistani bank accounts. Keep the platform's annual earnings statement, the bank's foreign remittance records, and your W-8BEN together. If FBR asks how your wealth grew, those three documents answer it in one go. They also support the income figure in your freelancer return.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a W-8BEN form?
An IRS form a non-US individual gives to a US payer to certify foreign status and beneficial ownership, and optionally to claim treaty benefits. It is not filed with the IRS.
Who needs a W-8BEN in Pakistan?
A Pakistani individual paid by a US client, platform or broker who asks for proof of non-US status.
What is my foreign tax identifying number on the W-8BEN?
For an individual in Pakistan, the NTN, which is the same as the CNIC number.
Do I need to fill Part II (treaty claim) as a freelancer?
Usually not. Services performed in Pakistan are generally not US-source income, so there is no US tax to reduce.
What happens if I do not submit a W-8BEN?
The payer may have to presume you are a US person or withhold tax; the standard rate on US-source fixed or determinable income of a foreign person is 30%.
How long is a W-8BEN valid?
Generally until the last day of the third calendar year after it is signed, unless a change in circumstances makes it incorrect.
Is the W-8BEN the same as a W-9?
No. A W-9 is for US persons; a W-8BEN is for non-US individuals.
Does submitting a W-8BEN affect my Pakistani tax?
No. Your Pakistani tax on freelance income is settled in your FBR return.
