Peshawar's Cross-Border Trade Economy

Peshawar's position as Pakistan's main gateway to Afghanistan gives it a taxpayer profile unlike most provincial capitals — a significant share of its commercial activity runs through import-export trade, customs clearing agents, and transport operators linked to the Torkham and Afghan transit trade routes, alongside a more conventional base of government employees, university faculty, and professionals in the city's planned residential areas.

Karkhano Market & Import-Export Trade

Karkhano Market has long been one of Peshawar's best-known trading hubs, historically associated with imported goods and now home to a broader mix of wholesale and retail traders, alongside customs clearing agents and transport operators serving the Afghan transit trade corridor.

Common tax situations: Import-linked traders need NTN registration and, once turnover crosses the threshold, sales tax registration with correct documentation of import duties already paid at customs, since these interact with how sales tax is calculated on resale. Customs clearing agents and freight forwarders earn commission-based income that must be declared as business income, not treated as reimbursed expenses. Traders who deal partly in cash across the Afghan transit trade corridor face particular scrutiny from FBR's data-matching systems when declared income looks low relative to visible business scale — formal registration and consistent filing is the most effective protection against this.

University Town — Academic & Professional Community

University Town is home to Peshawar University, Khyber Medical College, and a concentration of university faculty, doctors, and senior professionals, alongside consulates and NGO offices that make this one of Peshawar's most institutionally dense neighborhoods.

Common tax situations: University faculty with consulting income, research grants, or foreign lecture fees alongside their salary need these declared as separate business or professional income, not folded into salary. Doctors at Khyber Medical College's affiliated hospitals with a private practice need combined salary and practice income treatment. NGO and international-organization employees sometimes receive partially tax-exempt allowances under specific agreements — this needs careful, case-by-case verification rather than a blanket assumption of exemption.

Hayatabad — Peshawar's Modern Residential & Commercial Hub

Hayatabad is Peshawar's largest planned housing scheme, with a mature commercial sector along its main boulevards including retail showrooms, private hospitals, and a growing number of registered small businesses, alongside a substantial resident population of professionals and returning overseas Pakistanis.

Common tax situations: Hayatabad property owners must declare every phase-sector plot or house in their wealth statement at fair market value — a growing area of FBR review as Hayatabad property values have appreciated significantly. Retail and healthcare businesses along Hayatabad's commercial boulevards need NTN and, once eligible, sales tax registration. Overseas Pakistanis who have returned to settle in Hayatabad, or who own property there while still working abroad, need their foreign-earned assets and any remaining foreign income correctly reconciled with their new resident or non-resident filing status.

Peshawar Cantt & Saddar

Peshawar Cantt and Saddar form the city's older commercial and institutional core, housing government offices, established trading firms, and a mix of military-linked and civilian professional households.

Common tax situations: Government employees in Cantt need standard advance tax reconciliation and asset declaration. Established trading firms in Saddar with decades of operating history sometimes carry undeclared legacy assets that were never formally added to the wealth statement — bringing these into current-year compliance without triggering unnecessary scrutiny requires careful handling, which Kamboh Associates manages directly with clients.

Ring Road Industrial Zone & Small Manufacturing

Peshawar's Ring Road corridor and the adjacent industrial estates host a growing base of small manufacturers — marble processing, furniture, plastics, and food processing units — supplying both the local KP market and, increasingly, buyers across Punjab.

Common tax situations: Manufacturers here frequently start as unregistered family businesses and only formalize once a bank facility or a larger buyer's compliance requirement forces the issue — at which point Kamboh Associates registers the unit for NTN and sales tax and helps assess whether prior years' activity needs to be brought into compliance. Marble and stone processing units, a distinctive part of this belt given KP's mineral resources, need correct treatment of both extraction-linked provincial levies and federal income tax on processing and sale margins — the two are separate obligations that are often confused.

DHA Peshawar & Regi Model Town — Newer Property Development

DHA Peshawar and Regi Model Town represent the city's newer wave of planned housing development, drawing both resident buyers and overseas Pakistani investors attracted by lower entry prices compared to Lahore or Islamabad DHA phases, alongside strong appreciation potential as the schemes mature.

Common tax situations: Every plot booked in DHA Peshawar or Regi Model Town must be declared in the buyer's wealth statement from the year of booking, not just from possession — a distinction many first-time investors get wrong. Section 236C and 236K withholding tax applies on transfers within these schemes the same as any other property transaction nationally. Investors who purchased through an installment plan need each installment year's declaration to stay consistent with the cumulative amount paid, since FBR can cross-reference developer records against declared wealth.
