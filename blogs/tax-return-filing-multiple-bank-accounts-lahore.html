Tax Return Filing for Multiple Bank Account Holders in Lahore

September 2026 Kamboh Associates 10 min read
By Aitsaam Ali, Tax Consultant | NTN & Compliance Specialist

Having accounts at several different banks — common for anyone who's changed jobs, maintains a separate savings account, or keeps a business account alongside a personal one — creates a specific filing consideration: every account needs to be declared, not just the one you use most actively, since FBR increasingly cross-checks bank-reported balances against what's declared.

TL;DR

Every bank account you hold needs to be declared in your wealth statement, not just your primary or most-used account. FBR receives bank data directly and cross-checks it against declared wealth statements — an undeclared account, even a dormant one with a small balance, creates exactly the kind of inconsistency that can trigger a notice. Why Every Account Needs Declaring, Not Just the Main One

A common, understandable mistake is declaring only the bank account someone actively uses — their salary account, say — while forgetting or deliberately omitting an old savings account from a previous job, a dormant account rarely used, or a joint account maintained for a specific purpose. FBR's data-matching increasingly draws directly from banks, meaning any account with a balance that isn't reflected in your wealth statement creates a visible gap between what banks report and what you've declared.

How FBR's Bank Data Cross-Check Actually Works

Banks in Pakistan report account and transaction data to FBR as part of the broader tax-base documentation framework — this isn't a hypothetical risk but an active data-sharing relationship. A wealth statement that reflects only one account, when bank data shows several accounts with meaningful balances, presents an obvious inconsistency that FBR's systems are specifically designed to catch.

A Note on Dormant or Rarely-Used Accounts

An account you opened years ago and rarely touch still needs to be declared if it holds any balance, however small. "I forgot about that account" is understandable as an honest oversight, but it doesn't prevent the inconsistency from being flagged — the fix is proactively declaring every account you actually hold, including ones that feel irrelevant because they're inactive.

Business Accounts Alongside Personal Accounts

If you run a business alongside personal employment or other income, both the business bank account and your personal accounts need to be correctly reflected — the business account typically feeding into your business income declaration and the personal accounts into your overall wealth statement, with both pictures needing to be internally consistent rather than treated as entirely separate, disconnected filings.

What If You Want to Simplify by Closing Old Accounts

If you genuinely no longer need several old accounts, formally closing them (rather than just leaving them dormant) can simplify future filing — but closing an account doesn't retroactively remove the need to have declared it while it was open and held a balance. This is worth doing prospectively to reduce future complexity, not as a way to avoid declaring accounts that already exist.

A Worked Example: Four Accounts Across Three Banks

A business owner in Lahore held a personal salary account at one bank, a savings account at a second bank he'd opened years earlier for a specific fixed deposit, a joint account with his wife at a third bank, and his business current account — four accounts across three banks, only the first and last of which he actively used day to day. When preparing his wealth statement, only the actively-used accounts were initially mentioned, with the older savings account and joint account nearly left out simply because they didn't come to mind as readily.

A direct question — "list every bank account you currently hold, not just the ones you use regularly" — surfaced both forgotten accounts, which were then correctly included with their actual balances. This single question avoided what would otherwise have been an incomplete wealth statement, inconsistent with what FBR's bank-data matching would eventually show across all four accounts.
