Two Separate Registration Tracks — Regulatory and Tax

Running a pharmacy or medical store in Lahore involves two genuinely separate registration tracks that shouldn't be confused. The regulatory track — Drug License from the relevant health/drug authority, pharmacist registration if a qualified pharmacist is required to be on staff or supervising — governs whether you're legally permitted to operate as a pharmacy at all. The tax track — NTN registration with FBR — governs your tax compliance as a business, entirely separate from whether your drug license is in order.

A pharmacy can have a perfectly valid Drug License and still be non-compliant on the tax side if NTN registration was never completed — these two tracks need to both be handled, but neither substitutes for the other.

Documents Needed for NTN Registration

  • CNIC of the business owner
  • Business name — the pharmacy/medical store's trading name
  • Business address — the pharmacy's physical location
  • Drug License (if already issued) — useful supporting documentation, though the NTN registration itself is a separate FBR process from the license issuance

Sales Tax Considerations for Medicine Retail

Medicine and pharmaceutical retail has specific sales tax considerations that differ from general retail — certain categories of medicines and drugs can carry different tax treatment than standard goods under Pakistan's sales tax framework, including exemptions or reduced-rate treatment for specific essential medicine categories in some cases. This is genuinely more nuanced than a typical retail STRN registration and deserves a specific assessment based on exactly what products a given pharmacy or medical store stocks and sells, rather than a generic retail assumption.

How This Differs From Pharmaceutical Manufacturing/Distribution

It's worth distinguishing this guide from broader pharmaceutical sector tax content that covers manufacturing and large-scale distribution — a retail pharmacy or neighborhood medical store selling to end customers faces a genuinely different, smaller-scale compliance picture than a pharmaceutical manufacturer or a large distribution company. If you're specifically a retail pharmacy or medical store owner, this guide's registration process applies to you directly; larger-scale manufacturing or wholesale distribution involves additional considerations beyond this page's scope.

If You Employ Pharmacists or Sales Staff

A pharmacy employing a pharmacist, sales counter staff, or a delivery person has the same additional considerations as any small business with employees — potential EOBI registration (see our EOBI fee guide) and salary withholding tax obligations, worth assessing alongside the initial NTN registration rather than separately later.

Pharmacy Owners With Multiple Branches

A pharmacy owner operating more than one location across Lahore — a common growth pattern for successful medical stores — should consider whether continuing as a single sole-proprietor registration covering multiple branches, or restructuring as an AOP (if a partner is involved in expansion) or a formal company, best fits the actual business structure. This is worth a direct conversation rather than defaulting to whichever structure the first branch happened to register under years earlier.

A Worked Example: A Family-Run Medical Store With an Existing Drug License

A medical store in Township, Lahore had held a valid Drug License for over a decade, run by the current owner's father before him — a well-established, legitimate business by any regulatory measure. What had never happened was a formal NTN registration separate from the Drug License, since the family had always assumed the Drug License itself constituted "being registered." Once this was clarified, NTN registration was completed the same day using the existing Drug License as supporting business documentation, followed by an assessment of which specific medicine categories the store stocked to determine sales tax applicability.

This is a genuinely common pattern among longer-established, family-run pharmacies specifically — the regulatory side was handled properly from the start, but the tax side was never separately addressed because the two were conflated as one requirement.

A Note on Insurance Panel and Corporate Account Requirements

Pharmacies seeking to join an insurance company's approved panel, or supply to corporate clients under a billing arrangement, are increasingly asked to provide an active NTN and filer status as a condition of that relationship — similar to the developer/registration-authority pattern seen with property transactions elsewhere on this site. A pharmacy without proper tax registration can find this kind of business-growth opportunity blocked entirely, not because of any regulatory issue with the Drug License, but purely because the tax side was never addressed.

A Note on Family Succession in Pharmacy Ownership

Pharmacies passed down within a family — as in the example above — sometimes continue operating under a deceased or retired original owner's NTN and Drug License for a period after an actual change in who runs the business day-to-day. This creates a mismatch between the legal registration and actual operation that's worth formally addressing (updating ownership records, or registering a fresh NTN under the new operator) rather than continuing indefinitely under an outdated registration, since this can create complications later — both regulatory and tax-related — if not addressed as the actual transition happens.
